PAC Submissions:
●​    A parent/caregiver alleges that the Guidance Department has a policy to not send student
     information to colleges (e.g., transcripts, letters of recommendation) before October 1st. The
     parent also alleges that they have experienced the detrimental effects of this policy. They claim
     that many schools, even if unwritten, have policies of reviewing applications on a first-come
     first-served basis. They present as proof the alleged deferral of some early-action candidates by
     some colleges that claim that they simply had too many applications already.

     The parent goes on to allege that guidance counselors vary widely on when they submit
     materials. This is claimed to further exacerbate the problem of arbitrarily burdening some
     students. Ultimately they claim that student’s educational futures are subject to the ‘luck of the
     draw’ from these arbitrary dates and arbitrary assignment of counselors who submit materials at
     different times.

     They request the administration allow counselors who receive all of an individual student’s
     materials before the October 1st date, nonetheless, be allowed to forward that information to
     colleges as they receive it.

     RESPONSE: The administration has dealt with this issue in the past. They gave the example of
     Vanderbuilt as a university that receives this type of student information as early as perhaps
     August. However, the administration has confirmed with this university, and others, that merely
     receiving a student’s application and other supporting information does not trigger an early
     review. The universities have likewise confirmed that an October 1st deadline would not
     disadvantage any student because that deadline is early enough that all completed applications
     meeting it would be considered together.

     The administration asks that if there is some proof from a university that the October 1st deadline
     does disadvantage students, please contact them with such proof.

     The administration also disagrees that submission of student materials varies widely from
     counselor to counselor. However, they note that one of the reasons there is a deadline is so that
     all submissions will nonetheless be timely reviewed by the universities that receive them.



●​    A parent/caregiver alleges that Irvington has a history of choosing to celebrate athletics at the
     cost of academics. In their words:

              For years there has been a senior awards dinner and a separate sports
              banquet - similar events held often on consecutive nights at the same
              location. These are rare opportunities for students and parents to mingle
              and schmooze while students receive recognition for achievements. And
              for seniors, it's quite likely their last opportunity for this.
            But this year, apparently in response to concerns about the
            overburdensome combination of those two events plus the prom, the
            administration decided to "revisit" the scheduling. It is unknown just how
            deep and broad these concerns actually were, but regardless, the
            administration arbitrarily chose to keep the sports banquet as is, and
            push the senior awards out and into the campus theat[er] where it will
            become something that will resemble a school assembly not a dinner.

            Once again, academic achievement is trumped by athletic participation
            [in] our school. During the year there are many events that (rightfully)
            celebrate athletic achievements. Homecoming days. Pep rallies, etc. But
            there are NO events that broadly celebrate academic achievement. Yes,
            there are Oley awards, and the cum laude events, but those are
            relatively limited in scope .

            Why does our high school continue to exalt athletics over academics?
            And is there time for the PTA and other organizations to find a better
            program for this year?

    RESPONSE: The administration disagrees with the underlying premise that IHS
    celebrates athletics at the cost of academics. The administration states there
    were several valid reasons for the change in venue this year. They cited:

        1.​ The high overlap in attendance between the two events
        2.​ A feeling of being duplicative or feeling like ‘we were just here’
        3.​ Mistakes by the conference center that necessitated this change
        4.​ The cost to seniors becoming burdensome because in the past the PTSA
            had been able to pay for one of dinners in full
        5.​ Holding events in the theater allows teachers to attend, and more
            attendees, in general

    The administration goes on to share that they have a strong belief that the senior
    awards event will be lovely. The administration also states that this submission
    underplays the many academic celebrations that IHS does have. Nonetheless,
    the administrators do consider feedback from all stakeholders and may have a
    future audit to consider the celebrations further.
