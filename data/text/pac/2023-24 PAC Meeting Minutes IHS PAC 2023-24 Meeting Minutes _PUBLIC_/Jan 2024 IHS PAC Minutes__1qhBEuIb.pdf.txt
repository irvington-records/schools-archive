                            Irvington High School PAC Meeting
                                                Minutes
                                            Jan. 11, 2024
In attendance(in person): Jon Hirsch (IHS Principal), Sara Rust (IHS Assistant Principal), Jorge Magllón
                                           (PTSA IHS VP)
 (via teleconference): Mavis Edme (PTSA Grade Parent - 9th), Tricia Kim (PTSA Grade Parent - 10th),
          Maggie Kadro (PTSA Grade Parent - 11th), Kerri Warner (PTSA Grade Parent - 12th)


   PAC Submissions:
           1. Adult Visitors - Student Rights: A parent asks, what the policy is regarding the rights of
               students vis a vis adult visitors who are on the campus? The parent alleges that an
               administrator and adult visitors entered a class during instruction and interrupted
               instruction to elicit responses from students for the benefit of the visitors. The parent
               suggests that as partners in education, students should be introduced to visitors upon
               entry and advised in advance about interruptions.
           RESPONSE:
           ● The administrators began by stating that, as a rule, they introduce visitors when they enter
              classes, as well as give advance notice to staff if they will be visiting class with outside
              visitors. However, staff do not necessarily formally introduce themselves if it is only
              permanent HS staff that is entering a room where active student instruction is going on.
              The administrators posited that perhaps this parent’s concern may have arisen by a visit
              from a consultant in World Languages, Lea Kennedy. Lea is known and recognized by
              many students, and so feels like “part of our team”, in the words of the administrators.
              They went on to state that they will make the effort to make sure that every student
              knows who is in their classroom and why.
           2. Gettysburg Class Visits: A parent asks, why is the trip to Gettysburg open only to AP
               American History? The American Civil War is a central topic to all levels of US History.
               Building real life access to experiences and enrichment beyond our school district seems
               unfairly limited by this.
           RESPONSE:
           ● The administrators began by noting that this different treatment may seem notable because
              in middle school students ALL attend a Washington D.C. field trip. In IHS, however, the
              Gettysburg trip is linked to and tailored to the specifics of Mr. Barry’s AP class curriculum.
              Therefore, opening up the trip to a wider audience would necessarily limit the depth that
              each student receives from the experience. The administration also noted that AP History
              is an “open enrollment” class. So if any particular student especially values the trip, they
              can apply for the class.
           3. Coordinating on skills necessary for success at IHS with K-9: A parent suggests that
               IHS coordinate/communicate with the District on what practices allow students to
               succeed in IHS. The parent uses penmanship as an example. Penmanship instruction
               stops in grade 2. Cursive is no longer taught in grade 3 (unless a teacher decides to do
               so). No grade offers mechanical/technical drafting, which the parent alleges also
               improves related skills in making one's thoughts/work clear on the page. Now at high
               school, the parent says “you have math department and science department teachers
               complaining about students handwriting in lab reports or in ‘showing their work’ on math
               exams.’ This brings us back to the parent’s opening point, if a skill is important at the
               high school level, it should be begun to be developed throughout the K–9 career.
           RESPONSE:
● The administration acknowledged the parent’s concern. However, they noted that IHS does
   currently frequently coordinate student skills development within the departments in the
   school as well as with the other campuses. For example, once a month there is a
   meeting between the High School and Middle School divided by departments. Likewise,
   the schools meet regularly on a District-wide basis to discuss student skills alignment.
   Also, within the High School’s departments they meet regularly where these topics are
   also coordinated. Finally, one more check on coordinating skills across campuses, are
   the shared staff members between those campuses in subjects such as Math, World
   Languages and Art, for example.

   Regarding the specific extended example of cursive, the administration both
   acknowledged the ‘lost art of cursive’ and noted that that lack of ability should not be a
   reason for a staff member to deduct grades from any student. Also, the administration
   noted that if cursive should ever become an issue with a particular grade or staff member,
   that particular instance can be brought to the attention of administration, for example.
   Moreover, it is possible that while an instructor may have commented on the cursive, that
   does not necessarily mean that the student's grade was impacted.
4. Curriculum - War in Israel, Creation of Israel etc.: A parent/caretaker asks:
            In light of the war in the Middle East, rising antisemitism
            worldwide, and specifically on college campuses, and the spread
            of disinformation on Tik Tok and other social media, I would like
            to know what education IHS (and IUFSD as a whole) provides
            surrounding the Holocaust and the history of the Middle East /
            creation of Israel. What is the set curriculum on these topics and
            where is this information discussed? My sincere hope is that our
            kids coming out of IUFSD are equipped with the facts and
            enough information that they can be informed global citizens and
            can combat antisemitism as they go out into the world,
            regardless of their religious background.
    RESPONSE:
 ● The administration began by noting that the negative impacts of social media mentioned
   by the parent will be part of the editorial of a January Bulldog Blast describing the
   problem of classroom instruction, students and smartphones.
   The administrators went on to discuss the many instances where the topics mentioned by
   the parent/caretaker are woven into the curriculum in 8th grade and then at IHS. In 8th
   grade the entire class has the opportunity to visit the Holocaust Museum.

   Later, at IHS in AP World and Global History classes students learn extensively about the
   Middle East, generally, and the creation of Israel specifically. Likewise, in American
   History there are extensive discussions of Israel through the lens of US Foreign Policy.
   Also, in Current Events, an elective class, there is a large focus on human rights which
   include those that touch on Israel.
   The administration noted that all its instruction is research based and NY State
   Educational Standards based.
   Circling back to the parent’s/caretaker’s concern about disinformation, the administration
   did share some concerns. While they note that they particularly work hard to cultivate
   critical thinking in classes like English and Social Studies, they are learning that exposure
   to social media has some troubling implications for our students.
5. Graduation and Prom - Proximity of Dates: A parent asks why the prom date is now so
    close to the date of graduation?
    RESPONSE:
 ● The administration notes that there are many benefits to holding the prom at the Ardsley
   Country Club. Of particular note is that its closeness to IHS makes it particularly
appealing because transportation costs are an outsized proportion of prom ticket costs.
As a result, its geographic closeness makes tickets far cheaper than they otherwise
would be. However, it does not allow IHS to plan a full year in advance. The country
club opens its bookings up first to members and then to outside organizations.
Therefore, by the time IHS is able to book for the prom there is limited availability. This
year, unfortunately, an appropriately earlier prom date was not available. The
administration is still actively monitoring to see if a better date opens up, however.

The administration cast a wide net regarding how early it would consider prom. However,
it ultimately found suggestions that included dates as early as April as not in the best
interest of the students. While perhaps not ideal for some, the relatively closer dates
between prom and graduation is better for most seniors. It helps them feel invested and
engaged for the entire year instead of receiving signals, like an early prom, that their year
is already over.

Moreover, the hope of the administration is that by having prom and graduation close
together it might create a truly special end of year. The administrators expect that in this
way the week of June 10 should be one that seniors remember for a long time. The
administration does reiterate that it acknowledges, this moving prom date does make it
difficult for parents and students to plan their year, however.
