   PAC Submissions:

   1. A parent/caregiver, submitted the following (only formatting changes):
   I am wondering if IHS building leaders would consider talking to teachers about using the word
   “entitled” when addressing students that ask to discuss a graded assignment, assessment, or other
   issue that concerns them.
   We want our kids to advocate for themselves and when they do, they should not be told that they are
   “entitled” for doing so. The word is offensive when it is used in this way and it makes assumptions
   about students that may be far from true.
   Even if a student fits the description of “entitled,” I believe that it is hurtful[.] [I]f a teacher feels that a
   student is wrong to ask questions about their grade, this can be communicated in a way that does not
   stereotype a child.
   There are many privileged students and families in the district and there are many that are
   marginalized. A welcoming and affirming environment means that educators are mindful of their
   interactions with students and families and checking their own bias when it comes to all types of
   students. Most teachers seem to welcome students that advocate for themselves, but there have
   been several instances over the years with teachers in different departments that have done this.
   Thank you so much for considering this.


   Response:
   The administration agrees with the parent/caregiver that students should not be spoken to in this way
   by staff. The administrators go on to say that the topic of speaking to students in a respectful way has
   been previously addressed in faculty meetings. However, it will be reiterated. The administration
   does encourage students/parents to reach out directly to administrators in these situations. The
   administrators think these types of conversations can be more impactful when done directly with
   administrators or staff, rather than through the more indirect submissions route. Another option
   suggested is that the parent/caregiver is also encouraged to communicate directly with the staff
   member. However, the administrators understand that this may be an uncomfortable dynamic.


   2. Submitted directly to PTSA VP to IHS, Jorge Magallón

   A parent shared a concern that a major religious holiday for her student was not considered by a
   particular staff member in a request to reschedule an exam. Aside from contacting administration,
what should this parent and student do in the future if theirs is a minority religious group in this area of
the United States?



Response:

The administration always encourages staff to be respectful of religious holidays (The example of
Color Wars being moved because it conflicted with Holi was given as an example).The fact that some
holidays are commonly observed on local calendars and many others are not is true. Nonetheless,
the administration will work with the staff to make accommodations. Faculty do have many competing
pressures. Therefore, they may at times not be able to address a conflict regarding a religious holiday
and a test day, for example, on their own. Staff members should in these cases reach out to the
administration to help them come to a mutually agreeable outcome with the concerned families. If a
parent/caregiver or student do not have their needs addressed satisfactorily by a faculty member, they
are encouraged to contact the administrators directly.

3. Submitted directly to a PTSA Grade Parent, Tricia Kim

A parent suggests that students should be allowed to go home after finishing AP tests.

Response:

A parent/caregiver has the right to excuse their students, but should plan to make their own
transportation arrangements. However, the administration feels the balance of the school day is
nonetheless important for a myriad of reasons. For example, they point out that there are equity
issues in announcing that students taking the AP exams may leave afterwards. They specifically cite
the example of complicating the bussing schedules. The District’s bussing could not accommodate
the more complex dismissal schedule that would result. However, the administration will reiterate, in
upcoming correspondence, that a parent/caregiver can excuse their student in such cases, even
though the school day is not officially over. The administration also noted that faculty are aware of the
AP schedules and, to the extent possible, lessen students' workloads on those days to help
compensate.
