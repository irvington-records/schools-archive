                            Irvington Dows Lane PAC Meeting

In attendance: Dr. Andrea Kantor (DL Principal), Tom Chickery (DL Asst. Principal), Katherine
Cain (PTSA DL VP), Alison Lang (PTSA DL VP), Amy Suehnholz (PTSA DL VP), Emily Burley
(PTSA Grade Parent), Katie Bauer (PTSA Grade Parent), Brittany Marcus (PTSA Grade

PAC Submissions

   1. Reading Curriculum: With the rise of ‘the science of reading’ gaining traction
      nationwide & school districts across the country leaving behind the Lucy Caulkin’s
      reading approaches, what direct steps is Dows Lane taking to move more towards
      ‘explicit’ teaching of reading and writing for general edu students to better equip them to
      manage academics as they approach 5th/6th grade? As opposed to just accommodating
      students who are at risk of learning issues and/or have an IEP. Are teachers being
      trained on more explicit teaching strategies?
       RESPONSE: Dows Lane has a strong instructional reading foundation that uses a
       Balanced Literacy approach that includes workshop model, strategy groups and
       resources with Fundations, Orton-Gillingham, Heggerty Reading Program, and Writing
       Revolution as resources that all follow state standards. Kindergarteners through third
       graders are supported by a balanced approach which includes explicit instruction. More
       details about literacy at Dows Lane, can be found on the website.


       Children who qualify, based on data, and/or are unable to read at grade level are
       provided resources such as MTSS (Multi-Tiered System of Supports). Additionally
       children are screened by the Aimsweb standard tests in the fall, winter and spring. These
       tests identify a child’s ability to determine different letter sounds, letter combinations,
       etc... The school specifically focuses on children getting reading support before third
       grade, which is where the gap widens.
       The teachers and school administration are constantly reflecting on how the reading
       program can be improved, adding to existing approaches. The administration is
       committed to staying current on latest trends and research, implementing faculty
       workshops to get teachers up to speed. Nancy Deakin is the school’s literacy coach and
       Gail Duffy is also involved in high level decisions about the reading program.


   2. Hygiene: Is it possible to have teachers encourage students to do hand washing or use
      hand sanitizer before they eat snacks and go to lunch? Think it would be something
      really helpful that could help prevent illness, especially as we enter flu season.
       RESPONSE: There are sinks in the classrooms that are used for hand washing.
       Teachers typically encourage students to use the restroom before lunch, where they can
       wash their hands. There are hand sanitizer stations in the cafeteria and throughout the
building that some children choose to use before eating. Finally parents can put their
own wipes or sanitizer in their child’s lunch bag.
