      ●   Snowy weather recess Many parents would like to know whether their kids this year will
          be able to go play in the snow at recess or will they be only allowed on the blacktop?
          When there is snow on the ground, our students go outside for recess on a rotating
          schedule of three classes at a time. The custodians plow the snow from the blacktop so
          that students are able to play there. Other outdoor areas typically have too much snow
          from plowing for students to play. On extremely snowy days, recess is held indoors. Any
          day that recess is held indoors due to inclement weather includes a nature walk of the
          school grounds outside on the plowed pathways so that students can get some fresh air.

●   Socialization Is there an opportunity to add more socialization into the curriculum? Many
    parents have noted that this is something their children have been struggling with, both in
    class and highlighted especially during recess. Considering this age group was isolated
    and masked during critical socialization development, it seems appropriate to provide a
    little more support.
    Socialization is something that is woven throughout the day for all students. DEI,
    Character Education and other tools are always in the classroom, and at recess this year
    in addition to the aides and monitors that are always outside with the children, we have a
    teacher outside everyday as well to assist any student who may need some extra
    assistance. In addition, our PE teachers do a wonderful job of teaching conflict resolution
    in PE via games such as rock paper scissors. If you have specific concerns about your
    child, please don’t hesitate to contact their teacher, with the next level of contact being Dr.
    Kantor and Mr. Chickery.


●   MTSS (Multi-Tiered System of Supports) support Can there be more clear
    communication regarding what is being worked on in the MTSS support sessions - what
    the goals are, barriers to reaching those goals and what the strengths are. Can parents
    be included in the meetings when MTSS meets to assess the need for ongoing support
    (or not) the way we are brought into an IEP meeting? The goal/progress note to parents
    doesn’t feel like adequate collaboration with parents nor are parents explicitly asked how
    they feel their child is doing in regards to these goals. For those parents who may be less
    aware of how they can be activated as it pertains to their child’s support needs can they
    be brought in more explicitly to actively engage?
    MTSS is a department of education government initiative that allows for us to provide
    proactive support for children who need some extra help. Every student in the school is
    screened for math and reading, and parents will receive a consent letter to fill out if the
    school feels their child should be screened for anything additional - for example, speech
    therapy or occupational therapy. These screenings are all done throughout the year, and
    families are notified via email or phone call in September / early October if their child
    qualifies. The support is given on a rolling 4-6 week cycle, where progress is monitored
    and specific goals are reached. Parents can always contact their child’s MTSS provider if
    they would like more information on what is happening at school, as well as things they
    can do at home to assist their child. About 10% of Dows Lane students receive MTSS.

●   Bullying policy What protocol has been provided to parents when they think their child is
    being bullied? At what point should the parent be involved if the child cannot effectively
    advocate for themself? If the child does advocate for his or herself, is there transparency
    for how the situation is handled and next steps for how the school follows up?
    The district has a policy to deal with the issue of bullying, called DASA (Dignite for All
    Students Act). The key and first step is understanding. At the K-3 level, bullying is very
    rare, and more often what we deal with is unkind behavior that then become teachable
    moments. Bullying is defined as targeted behavior with a power imbalance, and a
    repetitive nature. If you suspect your child is being treated unkindly or bullied, please first
    contact their teacher, and after that if you have additional questions or concerns, please
    contact Dr. Kantor and Mr. Chickery.
    If bullying is suspected, a full DASA investigation is launched within 24 hours. This
    includes forms to be filled out by all parties, and interviews with students, teachers and
    parents. A hand delivered letter is given to the parents at their home when an
    investigation starts, and there is contact communication throughout the process. The final
    determination is then given by the district to the families involved when the investigation
    is complete.
    More information can be found on our district website here
    https://www.irvingtonschools.org/domain/2228 and we are also working on creative
    additional resources for parents which will be available shortly on the PTSA website.
●   PE Some kindergarten parents would like to know why there is such limited recess time
    as it does not seem like enough time to run around and get physical energy out every
    day, instilling sedentary habits early on in life.
    Our students have just under an hour for lunch and recess, and in addition, they receive
    many ‘brain breaks’ throughout the day. For our youngest students, they are not expected
    to stay seated for longer than 10-15 minutes at a time without being given a movement
    break.
