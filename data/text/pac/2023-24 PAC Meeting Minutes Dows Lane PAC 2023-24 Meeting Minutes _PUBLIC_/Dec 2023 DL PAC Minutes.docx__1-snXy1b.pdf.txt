●   Smart Watches: Studies show that smart phones and watches can have detrimental effects on children.
    The U.S. surgeon general has released an advisory
    (https://www.hhs.gov/sites/default/files/sg-youth-mental-health-social-media-advisory.pdf) warning of the
    dangers associated with social media use which likely can be expanded to include smart device use in
    general (texting peers, sending photos, etc.). "No-phone policies come amid rising concerns about kids'
    mental health and technology. The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention says that in the 10 years
    before the COVID-19 pandemic, feelings of persistent sadness, hopelessness and suicidal thoughts
    surged by approximately 40%. And test scores, particularly in math for grades 4 and 8, saw the biggest
    decline on record." "Proponents say the bans prevent students from scrolling through social media and
    sending bullying text messages, reducing classroom distractions." Has the Irvington school district
    considered implementing a no smartphone/smartwatch policy in hopes of improving children's mental
    health, reducing distractions and bullying? My hope is that we can open this topic for ongoing
    consideration and discussion to ultimately develop a policy prohibiting use of smart phones and watches
    in schools to benefit our children by reducing distraction and bullying and improving mental
●   RESPONSE: Based on the code of conduct, no device that can record or photograph children is allowed
    for privacy reasons. For this reason, Apple Watches are not permitted and if brought to school must stay
    in the student’s bookbag. Apple Watches and similar devices brought to school may be put back on after
    the instructional day if a parent needs to track their child on the commute home. Devices such as Fitbits
    are allowed only if they do not have the recording components.
    Technology is used in classrooms to enhance learning. Technology Director Emerson Segara researches
    all IT programs, apps, social media sites, etc… to determine what teachers and students are allowed to
    use based on Ed Law 2D. Dows Lane and the Irvington School District in general must adhere to these
    strict policies by state law. Teachers are allowed to use different tools than the students. For example, a
    teacher may determine that a YouTube video about math is acceptable to use in a relevant lesson.
    Students would not be allowed to independently use this same interface on their Chrome Books due to
    data collection, privacy and safety issues.
    To reiterate the importance of being in compliance with the law, please note that all classes are consistent
    in the technology available to teachers and students across all grades. Teachers collaborate with
    Instructional Coaches and Administrators to decide what programs or apps are safe and appropriate to
    use for an entire grade level.
●   Single Use Plastic: Can the school phase out single use plastic water bottles (my kid likes to buy them
    bc they are extra cold but I’ve gotten them to stop).
●   RESPONSE: Carol Stein heads up the Wellness Committee which discusses and helps to decide
    beverage, lunch and snack choices in the cafeteria. Any parent is welcome to join this committee or find
    out more by emailing Carol Stein, carol.stein@irvingtonschools.org.
    The only single-use plastic beverages in the school are sold by Aramark. Free cups are available by a set
    of water pitchers in the cafeteria for all children. Free paper cups of water are also available in the
    administrative office area for students as well. The school and PTSA have phased out using single-use
    plastic beverages for all events.
●   Lunch: Are students only allowed to go up one time for food? For example, if a child wants lunch and
    then decides they also want to purchase a snack, are they able to make two trips? If not, what is the
    reason? My concern is that my son purchases lunch sometimes and ends up disliking the food. He then
    wants to buy a snack so he is not starving (he is telling me this is not allowed).
●   RESPONSE: Students can only come through the line once as otherwise there would not be enough time
    for everyone to eat their food. There are two hot entree options and snacks for children to select when
    going through the line and cold options like bagels, salads, sandwiches. If your child seems to be having
    trouble, you could review the entree options together ahead of time and email the teacher, so they can
    speak with the Teacher Aide who supervises lunch and recess.
●   Recess: I have some concerns about the rules for recess. I would like my child to be able to play soccer
    and other sports during recess. My son has been coming home telling me that he is no longer allowed to
    play soccer, two-hand touch football, or other sports games. I think this is a crucial part of their day and
    development, especially in third grade when the playground is not as enticing. I would love to know more
    about why these activities are no longer permitted.
●   RESPONSE: Recess is for fun and socialization, aggressive competition is discouraged. It strains the
    ability for monitors to stay on the perimeter of the playground, which is important for safety, supervision,
    and security reasons. When related conflicts arise, the children are asked to take a break from those
    respective games and find other creative ways to socialize and have fun. Creating new and interesting
    ways to play can make way for new opportunities and experiences.
