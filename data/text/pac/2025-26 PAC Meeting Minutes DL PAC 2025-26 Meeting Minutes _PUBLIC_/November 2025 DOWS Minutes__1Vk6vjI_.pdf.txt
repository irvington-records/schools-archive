ENRICHMENT /CURRICULUM:

   ●​ Question: There does not seem to be a lot of enrichment opportunities for students who
      quickly pick up the material that the whole class is learning. I've noticed this throughout
      my son's time at Dows. Sure he reads books at his "just right" level which allows for
      differentiation, but in Math, he seems to be doing the same thing as everyone else and
      there doesn't seem to be opportunities for enrichment/ways for students to still do the
      same concepts but at a higher level of thinking. There isn't a ton of communication on
      how kids can be challenged when they already have a basic knowledge of these
      concepts and I wish as a school there was just as much emphasis on students who need
      enrichment opportunities as kids who need extra help. A suggestion- WIN time has been
      on parent presentations and I've interpreted this as something that could be a good
      opportunity for enrichment but I have yet to see anything come home from WIN time/
      anything that has shown any enrichment material.
   ●​ Question: It seems like kids with special needs are very well accommodated and cared
      for through IEPs, extra classroom aides or specialists, etc. (hurray!!) but the kids who are
      advanced and require more challenging work to stay interested and passionate about
      learning don’t have their needs met in the same way. We are blessed to have VERY
      small class and grade sizes. Why can’t we find a way to accommodate kids who need
      more challenging work (e.g., differentiated lesson plans for children who are advanced in
      math, independent science research projects where kids are paired with a high schooler,
      etc.)? It seems like there is room for creativity in how we challenge students above and
      beyond the state mandated lesson plans.

       Answer: There's a differentiation to determine each child's learning needs. ELA (English
       Language Arts) and Math come from Great Minds, Eureka Math 2 and Arts and Letters.
       In math there are challenging questions embedded in the lessons. For ELA each student
     has an opportunity for higher level text complexity, there could be a 2 year span in each
     class. The District has instructional coaches to help teachers differentiate. For example,
     in Amplify Science, children have journals, can always write more, delve deeper.
     Differentiated work, this is the core of what DL does in order to meet the needs of all
     levels. Any parent that has more questions should talk to their classroom teacher, to
     understand where the child is and what they are working on. Parents do not need to
     wait until the first teacher conference to talk to the teacher.

  ●​ Question: Does Dows offer any “life skill teaching"?” For example, tying shoes, ordering
     food at a restaurant or counter?

     Answer: This happens in the classes through teaching, reading books, play and through
     conversations EX: Kindergarten classrooms have kitchens; in all classes teachers are
     teaching students how to advocate for themselves which would translate to ordering
     food at a restaurant.

  ●​ Question: Is there any talk of putting foreign language back into the curriculum at Dows
     Lane?

     Answer: Years ago 3rd-5th grade had world language (Spanish) as part of the
     curriculum. This was for 3rd grade students and Main Street School students. At Dows
     Lane, it shared the room with PLTW. This is a much broader conversation for the
     district. As there is limited time in the instructional day, if it was implemented it would
     have to take instructional time from somewhere. As a community we would have to look
     at the research and see if 45 min 1/week is worthwhile, or even if 45 min 1/day. Another
     option to discuss with the District Office is a dual language program.

     The afterschool program could be a place to add world language – Ms Shortino was just
     appointed as the afterschool coordinator for Dows Lane. The program is organized by
     Scott Wynne, District Office and starts in Dec.

  ●​ Question: How much time does a typical kid in Kindergarten spend on screens in their
     classrooms?

     Answer: DL is a hands-on environment primarily, teaching through books and papers,
     activities. But technology and screens are a part of young learners' lives. They will have
     to know technology. Each teacher uses their discretion to ensure the balance is
     appropriate. There is no “downtime” where videos are being shown but videos may be
     used for movement breaks (GoNoodle, Yoga movement, songs, dance). If a parent has
     a more specific questions, they should speak with their student's teacher.



ACTIVITIES AT DOWS:
   ●​ Question: It would be nice if Dows Lane had more activities to encourage engagement
      for working parents (who are unable to volunteer during the day) and families to create a
      sense of community. For example a Literacy Night/Trunk or Treat event/Valentine’s Day
      dance etc. I work at an elementary school that holds many events like this and it really
      creates an established community. The teachers have more opportunities to create
      meaningful relationships with the students and their families.

      Answer: We have a Literacy Night, an IEF event for 1st grade and Math Night with 3rd
      grade, and new this year will be a multicultural night for 2nd grade. The PTSA also runs
      bingo, movie nights, preschool carnival, Saturday book fair hours, DL also has a 3rd
      grade concert in the evening. The PTSA is exploring a weekend event with Gate Hill for
      the winter.

   ●​ Question: Growing up I loved that there were so many activities that allowed my mom
      and dad or grandparents/guardians to come into my school and Dows seems to lack
      those opportunities. Suggesting “Muffins with Mom” for Mother’s Day or “Donuts with a
      Dad” for Father’s Day, also would love to see a career day where parents go in and
      share about their careers.

      Answer: Being inclusive of all, we do celebrate have mothers/fathers/special persons
      day – K/1st typically invites parents in more (EX: to read books, breakfasts in November)
      than 2nd/3rd. 2nd/3rd has plays, international day, 3rd grade concert. If families want to do
      something with the class, talk to the teacher.

   ●​ Question: Why aren’t there winter and spring concerts for all grades?

      Answer: 1st/3rd have concerts – 2nd has the play. The school is mindful to be inclusive of
      having people come in during the day, and the 3rd grade concert night. Based on
      capacity in the gym, students are able to bring 2 guests to a concert. Any additional
      family members are welcomed to dress rehearsals to see the performances. Families
      should contact their child’s teacher for questions. The idea of moving 3rd grade Moving
      Up to the HS was raised, we will talk about it with Genevieve Lotze, Music teacher. We
      can also check with Jerrod Blair, Technology Director, for live streaming option. And we
      will always continue inviting people to the dress rehearsals.



DOWS LANE CAFETERIA:

(From PAC: we received approximately 17 submissions from 17 different parents with
concerns around the Dows Lane cafeteria, below is a summary of EACH topic of
concern)

   ●​ Quality: Parents expressed concern about the general quality of the food being served
      at Dows Lane. It is highly processed, institutional in nature, can we explore changing
      vendors? Our students deserve fresh, locally sourced ingredients that cater to modern
       student preferences and different cultural backgrounds. How can we make change in
       the cafeteria?

       Answer: The district makes the decision to be a part of the national lunch program or
       not which is largely based on the number of families who need free lunch – if you don’t
       have students who need that service, you are likely to not need that option which will
       open more options with that vendor. If you do, there are broad federal requirements to
       be part of that service. So, the vendor is not the issue around some of the limitations, the
       limitations are based around being part of the national school lunch or not. Irvington
       made the decision to be part of the national lunch program a long time ago. We get
       money from the govt to offer free breakfast and lunch, Carol Stein could put together the
       cost of this and what it would mean financially to families, if we would remove it. The
       district made a choice and that choice dictates this but it can be better in the confines
       which we have.

       There is a wellness committee discussing changing items but some of guidelines to be
       followed are strange, EX ketchup has to be sugar free. Additionally, in a recent meeting,
       it was decided to pull Capri Sun (to be replaced with Honest, the cost differential will be
       covered by the district) and Rice Krispies.Anyone interested in joining the Wellness
       Committee, should contact Carol Stein, Assistant Superintendent for Business.

The district is working with Aramark Food Services, about their response time to families about
questions regarding the cafeteria, this a known issue.

   ●​ Low Food Supply: Food is running out, can we be told which items / days this is more
      likely to happen so we can pack on those days? Why is this happening and will the
      situation improve?

       Answer: This is an Aramark Food Services management issue. The person managing
       this works for Aramark, many districts hire their own manager. If it was our employee –
       this person could ask Aramark to get more food, if the manager works for Aramark, its
       harder. But, there is always food for the children, it just may not be what’s on the menu.
       All families should make sure their child knows that they need to be flexible when getting
       school lunch- as there will always be food, but it might not be what they think it is, since it
       is free this year.

   ●​ Snacks: (PAC received approximately 12 submissions from 12 different parents in
      regards to snack quality and wrote feedback that students (a lot in K) are
      procuring snacks with NO money on their accounts): For example, I’ve put $0
      dollars on my daughter’s lunch account and have asked Gloria Estrada to put a
      restriction on preventing my daughter from getting any à la carte items. My daughter is
      still getting chips and ice cream. How can I actually prevent her from getting à la carte
      items?
       Answer: The district and Aramark are not giving food to children who have restrictions.
       Per Andrea and Meg’s investigation these were kindergarten students who were taking
       food. Communication with families and reminding students, that they can only take food
       that their parents approve and snacks that are paid for. The problem has been found and
       addressed.

       The overall experience of the cafeteria won't change unless we make different decisions
       from the District and Wellness Committee. The food contract is not school based, but
       District facilitated.




   ●​ Ingredients: Nutrislice does not give full ingredients on all foods. My child specifically
      has food allergies and isn't even able to eat some of it (egg and sesame allergies). Since
      about 30% of the food is NOT labelled in Nutrislice it is very hard to supplement food that
      we bring from home along with food from the cafeteria. Can we get a more
      comprehensive breakdown of the ingredients?
   ●​ Cafeteria Time: I recognize there are many kids and grades that need to use the
      lunchroom but it would be nice if kids had a bit more time for lunch. Both of my kids
      complain that they don’t have enough time to finish their lunches. Can the time be
      extended?



TEACHER COMMUNICATION:

   ●​ Is it possible that parents receive bi-weekly or monthly email updates from the head
      classroom teacher? As a parent, I’d love to know a little bit more about her life too.
      Currently, ‘no news is good news’ has been what I experience.
   ●​ I feel I get no information on what’s going on in the classrooms, and my kids tell me
      nothing. Has the school considered a school-wide app to enhance teacher-parent
      communication? In my experience, most schools/districts have apps or digital platforms
      for this.

Answer: All teachers communicate on a monthly basis, it could be via newsletter, Instagram,
Class Dojo, email. The frequency of the communication is at least monthly for every class, and
in between there are letters from Fundations, Eureka2 math, and Arts and Letters about student
work. The class communication is up to the teacher. Dr. Kantor likes the creativity of each
teacher having their own strengths, some take pictures, some write more, some do newsletter.
Parents should feel encouraged to reach out to the teachers if they have additional questions.
