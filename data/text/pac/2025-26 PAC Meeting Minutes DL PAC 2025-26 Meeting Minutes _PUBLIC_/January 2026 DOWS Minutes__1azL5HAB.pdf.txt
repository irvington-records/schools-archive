· Question - CAFETERIA: Is there a plan to improve the lunch service to healthier,
more nutrient rich and varied meals? Hot dogs, burgers, corn dogs should not be on
school menus and especially not every week for little kids. Some days there are only
meat options. The NYC public schools menu can provide inspiration - hummus, veggie
burgers, tuna wraps, falafel, chilli. It is interesting, varied, and nutritious. A menu that
prioritizes fried foods, meat, and unhealthy meals is doing the kids a disservice and
negating all the excellent work the school does in other areas. We know these foods
damage bodies, why are they served?

Answer – The school always has nonmeat options like salad, bagels, cheese. For the
answers to the rest of the question, please look through past PAC meeting minutes,
especially the November 2025 minutes which can be found here. Carol Stein, Assistant
Superintendent for Business, is the liaison for the cafeteria and can answer detailed
questions about the food choices. Additionally, if you would like to make further change,
please consider reaching out to or joining the wellness committee.

· Question - PLRD(Professional Learning Day) :Why are there so many PLRD days?
It's incredibly disruptive and challenging for parents, especially working parents.
Additionally, it seems very unnecessary to have early dismissal the weeks where there
is already no school (e.g. no school on a Monday and a short day on Wednesday), as
well as the week right after a 1-2 week break. It's very hard on families, and we've never
been told what exactly is happening during all those short days. What is the outcome?
How is it helping kids?
Answer – The district office creates the school calendar with the PLRD dates. PLRD
dates are used for professional learning and to better instructional practices. They can
answer detailed questions about how the calendar is created. Additionally, this topic has
been discussed in the past in previous PAC meetings, the minutes can be found here.
      Similar question posed at 12/11/24 PAC:
      PLRD Scheduling: Has there been any discussion about NOT having PLRD days
      immediately before or after long breaks? And, why is our winter break so long? Other
      local districts are back in school on Jan. 2nd. That, coupled with all of the early
      dismissals, have a lot of parents very angry and frustrated.
      12/11/24 RESPONSE:
      Those dates come from the district and not Dows Lane administrators. It is worth
      reaching out to the superintendent for comment if you have concerns about
      scheduling. Please note that for the purpose of receiving NYS funding, the District
      needs to have a certain number of school days, and 1/2 days count as a "day" as
      per NYS State.



· Question - “DUMB” WATCHES: We are trying to teach our child how to tell time
on a clock/watch as well as responsibility for his own things therefore we bought him a
“dumb” watch. It has no other capabilities other than to tell time. He is being asked to
remove it daily at school. Is this a general watch rule or is this allowed?

Answer – The school is following Governor Hochul’s bell to bell policy effective on
school grounds. If there are concerns about the particular watch your child is wearing,
please contact their teacher.

playground)? Our current sign seems outdated and people have to manually update it?

Answer – Yes, Dows Lane looked into this last year. The cost of the electric sign was
$6K. The PTSA will discuss fundraising or donating for this sign.

· Question - LIBRARY BOOK RECOMMENDATIONS:I am receiving feedback from
my child that graphic novels are being recommended for independent reading over
traditional prose novels or chapter books. What is the school’s philosophy for the use of
graphic novels in the elementary library program? What is the recommended balance
between graphic novels and traditional prose novels for students in 3rd grade? Are
there specific reading benchmarks tied to reading longer prose that the library program
supports?

Answer from December PAC meeting: The Dows Lane librarian, Gina Samaniego
does not recommend books. The library program is to offer children a free choice to
select books. Dr Kantor is wondering about the balance of graphic novels vs fiction vs
nonfiction in the library and will follow up with Ms. Samaniego. She will discuss her
findings with Nancy Deakin, the school's literacy coach. More traditional prose are being
read in the classrooms

Answer - 3.5% or 1,717 of the 20,000 books in the Dows Lane library are graphic
novels– Gina and the library aide do not recommend books to students – if a child
taking out 2 graphic novels they do suggest taking 1. Many of the graphic novels are
historical fiction, biographies and in general are meatier than what they used to be. Also
note this is a free reading opportunity, there is a lot of reading happening in the class.
Here is an article that supports this.
