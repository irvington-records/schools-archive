      PAC Submissions


          ●   PLRD Scheduling: Has there been any discussion about NOT having PLRD days
              immediately before or after long breaks? And, why is our winter break so long? Other
              local districts are back in school on Jan. 2nd. That, coupled with all of the early
              dismissals, have a lot of parents very angry and frustrated.
              RESPONSE:
              Those dates come from the district and not Dows Lane administrators. It is worth
              reaching out to the superintendent for comment if you have concerns about
              scheduling. Please note that for the purpose of receiving NYS funding, the District
              needs to have a certain number of school days, and 1/2 days count as a "day" as
              per NYS State.

          ●   Cafeteria Menu:
              ENTRY 1: The lunch menu offered to students is beyond poor, and I'm truly horrified that
              packaged snacks are available for purchase by children so young. Overall what's offered
              at DL has a massive amount of sugar (which until I banned my son from buying was
              impacting his ability to learn and sit & focus in class) - a rice krispy treat contains 16% of
              the total amount of sugar per day suggested for an adult, combine that with a Capri Sun
              (10g), and a bag of Lindenman Cookies (13g). These alone equals the amount of sugar
              adults should be having in one entire day, not just one meal. At what age will nutrition be
              integrated into the classroom? And what can I do as a parent to enable the school to
              eliminate packaged snacks and provide healthier options to all students. What would
              Michelle Obama suggest with her healthy moves program?
              ENTRY 2: Could the school undertake an audit of the canteen food to determine what
              percentage of the meal options and snacks contain Ultra Processed Food (UPFs) and
              consider a plan to reduce these and replace them with alternatives where possible?
              ENTRY 3: I'm concerned about the sugary and generally unhealthy options in the school
              cafeteria. Kids at this age cannot make the "right" decision related to sweets and they
              should not be offered at all. What are we doing to eliminate the processed snacks
              offered?
              RESPONSE:
              Carol Stein heads up the Wellness Committee which discusses and helps to decide
              lunch and snack choices in the cafeteria. Any parent is welcome to join this
              committee or find out more by emailing Carol Stein,
    carol.stein@irvingtonschools.org Please note that there is a “no snack option” on
    the My School Bucks account. Parents are encouraged to speak with their children
    about how to make decisions regarding their food choices when purchasing the
    school provided meals.
●   Sickness and Lice Notifications: In the pre Covid world, parents would be notified
    when there was a case of strep, lice, flu etc in our child’s class, so we could be on the
    lookout for symptoms. This practice stopped during Covid. When I’ve inquired why, I was
    told it was a privacy concern. I have not been able to find any state regulation or BOE
    policy forbidding this practice. I know other parents were given the same reasoning. Can
    we please have more clarity on this?
    RESPONSE:
    Per the district’s medical director, it is a family’s responsibility to mitigate
    individual lice problems. The school communicates to parents about any sort of
    lice problem when three or more students report having it in a classroom. Feel free
    to purchase the preventative sprays on your child’s hair or book bag. Families are
    encouraged to reach out to Nurse Flemming if they detect lice at home. The school
    also seeks the advice of Dr. Huff, District Doctor, with Tribeca Pediatrics with
    regard to contagious illnesses.


●   Gender Education: What is the Irvington District's response to the Hastings school
    curriculum announcement on the changes to gender curriculum for K and subsequent
    media attention/school lock down? What implications/learnings are there for neighboring
    districts?
    RESPONSE:
    Dows Lane and the District, use the Casel K-3 framework, which focuses on
    honoring the differences of others without calling out specific differences. At Dows
    Lane, the focus is to teach kindness and respect for all.
●   Eureka 2: How is the new Math program (Eureka 2) being received by teachers &
    students since implementation? Will parents receive any feedback on how it went for the
    first year of the teachers teaching it and the students learning from it? Thanks.
    RESPONSE:
    Classroom teachers and also Molly Stern, Dows Lane’s instructional math
    professional, are faculty members to direct math related questions. Eureka 2
    seems to be well received. Parents in particular seem to appreciate the homework
    books as there is an explanation section for families at the beginning of each
    lesson so that the lessons can be further reinforced at home.

    ENTRY 1: I see a lot of communication about celebrating neurodiversity, which is great,

    further in the areas in which they show academic strength? Surely the majority of your
    students fit into this category and we'd like to hear more about your efforts to give them
    an individualized, above-average educational experience.
    ENTRY 2: In the Nov. 14th Dows Lane Buzz, Dr. Kantor shared the school's MTSS
    program. While I think it's an important program and I appreciate her sharing the details,
    her repeated italicized emphasis of the word "all," in conjunction with the description of
    the MTSS program, made me pause and has impelled me to write the following question:
    What is Dows Lane doing to support those students who are above grade level? MTSS

●   Technology: Is the third party content that schools sign up for and kids have access to
    via their Chromebooks (e.g. Razzkids and Class link) being properly vetted to ensure its
    age appropriate? I ran into a situation last year where my then 2nd grader came home
    asking me about fentanyl and steroids because he saw a Razzkids video telling him
    about it. I addressed this with the school administration and they pulled the content, but
    then several weeks later my child came home telling me about another video they saw
    about Ebola and its effects. Luckily the teachers got wind of that one and managed it
    appropriately, but was surprised the kids have access to that type of subject matter
    without adult supervision to provide context. Similarly, why are my kids learning how to
    play video games at school? I understand the value of learning how to code video games
    (e.g Scratch Kids), but don’t think they need access to video games like Geometry Dash
    where there isn’t any coding involved.
    RESPONSE:
    Computer safety is of great importance to the district. Though all programs used
    for educational purposes in the classroom must comply with Ed Law 2D privacy
    law, that does not ensure that the content is safe. It is the responsibility of the
    school to oversee each program or software. Parents are encouraged to reach out
    to your child’s teacher if they are concerned about his or her philosophy around
    technology, including the amount of screen time used in the classroom. Please
    look out for Dow’s Lane’s next Coffee with Principal event in February that focuses
    on technology in classrooms. You can attend either remotely or in person. Parents
    will be encouraged to express their concerns at this time with the director of
    technology, Dr. Jerrod Blair.
●   Playground:
    ENTRY 1: I am writing to share my feedback regarding the new playground equipment
    that was recently installed at Dows Lane School. As a member of the school community, I
    was excited to see improvements to the playground, but I must express my
    disappointment with the size and overall design of the new installations. Compared to the
    previous equipment, the new structures seem much smaller and less varied, which is a
    significant downgrade in terms of both play options and overall space. Many children who
    used to enjoy the previous playgrounds are now left with less room and fewer engaging
    activities, which feels like a missed opportunity to enhance the play experience. I
    understand that there are many factors involved in selecting new playground equipment,
such as budget, safety regulations, and space constraints. However, I believe that the
community would have benefited from more robust engagement in the planning process.
It would have been helpful to gather input from parents, teachers, and students to ensure
the new equipment meets the needs and expectations of those who use it most. I would
appreciate it if you could provide some insight into the process that led to the selection of
this new equipment. Additionally, I would be very interested in knowing if there are any
opportunities for future upgrades or expansions to improve the playground experience for
all students.
ENTRY 2: Why wasn't there any news shared in the past 2 years (since my child started
at Dows Lane) about the playground equipment procurement process or funding
process? Why isn't there somewhere for site-based committees to share their ongoing
projects with the Dows Lane community and solicit input or feedback from the larger
parent community? My child is currently in 1st grade, and I don't recall seeing a single
item asking for input about the selection process for the playground equipment (or even
making parents aware that there was a new playground equipment procurement process
ongoing). Because, while I do appreciate there being new playground equipment, the
newly installed equipment seems insufficiently sized for the number of students on the
playground during recess. These kids only get a measly 25 minutes of recess (which, I
know, is in line with most American public schools, but is pathetically insufficient from a
developmental standpoint), so at the very least the playground equipment should be
sufficient for the short time they have. This was an opportunity to provide our children
with some really great, innovative playground equipment, and while I appreciate the work
of the site-based committee to provide Dows Lane students with updated playground
equipment, I find the lack of transparency and communication about the process
perturbing, and the decisions made regarding the new equipment disappointing.
ENTRY 3: The new playground appears to be much smaller than the previous one
(south) is this intentional or is it still in progress? Also, why are there no longer monkey
bars? This is something the older kids looked forward to. If this was to avoid risk, seems
like a shame--risk taking is an important part of childhood development.
RESPONSE:
Since 2021 there has been a Site Committee composed of parents and school
employees that has provided the administration with input about the new
playground. Some of the concepts behind the new play structure were more room
for running and playing and having separate areas for kids who were struggling
with sensory overload to have a quiet place to play. The school would like to see
the playground open and evolve, and supports fundraising for a second phase of
structures to be added. If you would like to spearhead this collaboration, please
contact the PTSA or Dr. Kantor.
