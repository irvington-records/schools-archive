                         Irvington Middle School PAC Meeting
                                         MINUTES
                                      October 17, 2024


In attendance: David Sottile (IMS Principal), Allyson Daley (IMS Asst. Principal), Mavis Edme
(PTSA IMS VP), Mara Gredick (PTSA IMS VP), Oda Sham (PTSA IMS VP), Sarah Harris Weiss
(PTSA Grade Parent – 6th), Barbara Trencher (PTSA Grade Parent – 7th) and Janet Kovacs
(PTSA Grade Parent – 8th)

                                     PAC Submissions

       1. My question is basically why is there so much homework? My son did an
          average of 2-2.5 hours per night in 6th grade and about the same this year in
          7th grade. There was a brief period this year, where it improved and it wasn't
          taking that long, but now he's back to 2-3 hours most nights. This is bizarre to
          me. I work as an Occupational Therapist in the NYC schools in 6 different high
          schools and I don't know of any student who spends this much time on
          homework in all of the schools I work in. Our family friends in Hastings who
          have a 7th grader say that he *never* has homework. My understanding of the
          current research on this topic is that there's no proven benefit to homework.
          I'm not necessarily advocating for no homework, but there's got to be some
          middle ground here.

          60-70 minutes for 7th grade benefit 1 ½-2 hours a night from site committee for
          7th.no homework during breaks

          I have spoken with a handful of other parents and about 80% of them reported
          a similar experience with their 7th graders. My son has given up or scaled back
          some extracurriculars and they reported the same. This is obviously not a
          significant sample and I'm sure some 7th graders are getting their homework
          done in less time. I'd actually really love to see a survey of families in the
          middle school about how much time is being spent on homework and whether
          others are facing similar difficult choices about how to spend after school
          time.

          RESPONSE:
          ● Homework is meant to advance and reinforce what was learned in the class. In
            the IMS Handbook, there are guidelines on what is a reasonable expectation of
            homework. The recommendation for a 7th grader is 1 ½-2 hours per night based
            on research and consulting with other middle schools in the Rivertowns. But
            accelerated classes may have more homework. Homework is also not assigned
            during breaks.
          ● If a student is experiencing longer time for homework, students can email their
            teacher with questions or other difficulties regarding homework. If it is
            consistently taking time in excess, parents/students are advised to contact their
            counselor and share areas of difficulty.

       2. I have always heard about how middle school girls are so mean and now I am
          living through it with my daughter. I wonder if there is anything that the school
          can do to help girls to make choices to include those who may feel left out,
          especially at lunch. The whole friend group thing is crazy, I have heard from
          other girls besides just mine that they don't feel welcomed to sit with others at
          lunch if they aren't in the same friend group, even if they are friends with some
          of them outside of school. My daughter reached out individually to a few girls
          who are supposedly her friends to carpool to a party for another child from
          IMS. All of them said no and not because they didn't have room in their car but
          because they were each already going with one other girl or in one case her
          friend group was all going together. In a small town like this it would be nice if
          the girls would try to be kind to each other. I know MSS worked on this, not
          sure if IMS could as well as it only gets worse as they get older. (Perhaps this
          applies to boys as well, I just don't know about that.)

          RESPONSE:
          ● The IMS community strives to help our students develop:
            o a positive and accepting attitude toward self and others
            o respect for the rights of others
            o a capacity for self-motivation and self-discipline
            o a high level of self-esteem and self-respect
          ● IMS focuses on restorative practices, to proactively build relationships and
            community. It also offers a path for responding to ruptured relationships in a way
            that centers around accountability and the repair of harm.
          ● Ms. Daley and the student’s guidance counselor have partnered to co-facilitate
            restorative circles as appropriate to address student conflict. The conversations
            are centered around intention, having an open and honest conversation, and the
            idea that the students involved are willing and want to be part of the
            conversation.
          ● IMS also has Good News Referral as a way to celebrate and support students.
            Each week, the team of teachers can nominate students that exhibit one of the
            core social emotional competencies. Ms. Daley meets with the students in a
            small group to acknowledge their nomination.
