                             Irvington High School PAC Meeting
                                              MINUTES
                                             Nov. 15, 2024


In attendance: Jon Hirsch (IHS Principal), Sara Rust (IHS Assistant Principal), Jorge Magallón
(via video teleconference): Joanna Anemodouras (1st Year PTSA VP to IHS), Jorge Magallón
(2nd Year PTSA VP to IHS), Amy Friedman (PTSA Grade Parent - 9th), Mavis Edme (PTSA
Grade Parent -10th), Gayle Katzman (PTSA Grade Parent - 11th)


   PAC Submissions (PAC Submissions from Oct. canceled Meeting. None submitted for this Month.):

   1. Submission: Hello! College visits to IHS are beginning and I find it very short-sighted by the
   guidance that 11th graders are not allowed to attend these visits unless they do not have a class. I
   would argue junior year is just as important, if not more important of a time to start exploring college
   options. Can this be addressed asap as the visits have begun. Thanks!

   Initial Response via email(Between the Administration and the PTSA VPs):

   While the administration is not opposed to juniors attending, they don't want their increased
   participation to rob seniors of this face-to-face opportunity with a college rep who may be reading
   their application in the near term.

   Response (From 11/15 PAC meeting):
   Jon Hirsch mentioned that this issue hits very close to home because he also has a child who is a
   junior high school student.

   PTSA: Can you also address the related concern that perhaps the IHS counselors may not be familiar
   enough with all the schools attending this event to answer the questions of juniors?

   Response: The college reps first meet with the IHS counselors to get to know IHS, answer each
   other’s questions and concerns and for the reps to distribute information about their respective
   colleges/universities. So, counselors should be familiar with the schools that attend the event.

   The reason for this event is more than to distribute information. Instead, it is a chance for IHS
   students to “meet and greet” in a more intimate way. (ed. see the initial response via email above)

   Another reason for the current policy, is that managing the number of class absences due to these
   visits would be burdensome. If excused for this event, juniors could miss somewhere between 10-15
   classes each. Our policy is also consistent with surrounding school districts that we reached out to.
PTSA: What if juniors were eligible for excused absences only during elective classes?

Response: This could put the teachers of elective classes in an uncomfortable situation. We will
nonetheless meet with them to discuss.

PTSA: Another reason to consider opening up this event to more juniors is that it would help juniors
whittle down the group of schools they will be applying to. This is because many students don’t have
a clear idea of what colleges are best suited for them at this point. The idea of a library of college
information at IHS was raised at this point.

Response: The idea of organizing a library of college information at IHS that students could use to
become more familiar with the colleges is a good one. We will look into this and will follow up.

PTSA: For many students it is not so easy to visit college campuses, so an event like this might be
their only opportunity to meet with these school reps.

Response: Again, the counselors are familiar with the schools that attend the event because they
meet with the college reps. This is another reason to put together a library of college information.
Also, there is a local college fair at the Westchester County Center later in the year that IHS
participates in and promotes.

2. Submission: I realize that parents should address things with the teacher first or their guidance
counselor, however, can there be some sort of anonymous suggestion box that students can address
their thoughts or feelings without feeling like they will be penalized. My son never wants to speak up
or advocate for himself when he disagrees with a teacher because he is afraid of being penalized with
his grades. I am sure this is common.

Response: First off, we hate that a student feels this way. One possibility is for the student to meet
with an administrator first to see what a conversation with the teacher might look like.

In thinking about retaliation by a teacher, we aren’t aware of an instance of this happening.

About something like a suggestion box, one weak point about a totally anonymous submission is the
example of the IHS Anonymous Alert program. While it has its place, it is often difficult to get
meaningful outcomes with anonymous information.

Generally, a fix is more meaningful if both parties are involved. But, sometimes people are only
comfortable with being anonymous and that’s fine. But, trying to have them find their agency is a
more impactful response. In these cases, a student can meet with a counselor or an administrator
who can give feedback and discuss what actions can be taken. We hope we have earned the
students’ trust enough so they feel comfortable reaching out to us in these situations. Generally, in
working with a student in this way, we can reach a better outcome.
PTSA: Maybe a generalized survey of the teachers and classes could at least flag consistent
questionable behavior.

Response: We do send out evaluations at the end of each semester to solicit feedback on the
academic and social experience of students. But the evaluations do suffer in that they come at the
end of a course.

PTSA: It would be helpful if teachers affirmatively reminded students regularly to please come
forward when students feel the teacher has done something wrong or made some mistake.

Response: We do try to create a welcoming and affirming environment and work with students on
this goal. It is a great suggestion and we will implement it.
