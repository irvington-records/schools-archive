                              Irvington High School PAC Meeting
                                              MINUTES
                                             Dec. 20, 2024


In attendance: Jon Hirsch (IHS Principal), Sara Rust (IHS Assistant Principal)
(via video teleconference): Joanna Anemodouras (1st Year PTSA VP to IHS), Jorge Magallón
(2nd Year PTSA VP to IHS), Amy Friedman (PTSA Grade Parent - 9th), Mavis Edme (PTSA
Grade Parent -10th), Gayle Katzman (PTSA Grade Parent - 11th)


   PAC Submissions:

   1.​ Submission: ​
   A parent states:​
   The students who participated in [the] fall high school play work[ed] extremely hard and​
   dedicate[d] many hours and there was no announcement in the weekly school or district newsletter
   or anywhere about the play that was performed November 15th and November 16th. There also was
   no preview to the middle school which had been done in the past. There was very low audience
   turnout and it was disappointing to the theater students. The school should have made more of an
   effort to promote the production.

   Response: ​
   We agree that the students do work very hard on these plays. We think this play was one of the
   more creative productions we have seen from high schoolers. ​
   ​
   As far as attendance goes, in our opinion, it seemed in keeping with past productions. ​
   ​
   As far as how well the play was publicized, the theater program instructors spearhead the publicity
   details. Perhaps, it could have been publicized better, so this type of constructive advice on how to
   improve is always welcome. ​
   ​
   Specifically, about the decision not to do a middle school preview, we don’t know why it wasn’t
   done. However, we will take this input and make sure the upcoming spring play is better publicized.

   Thank You.

   2. Submission:

   A parent/caregiver states:​
   My nieces attend private school in the city so [I’ve] been able to compare certain approaches to
   certain time honored traditions. Obviously the money in private [schools] is always a factor. I get it.
   However, there are two areas where we can see improvement in sophistication that do not
necessarily cost a ton of money.​
​
1. Student photos. The quality of photographers we use is… [artistically sub par].​
​
2. Graduation ceremony. There is so much we can do that’s better than our current graduation
ceremony. Cap and gowns and speeches are ok, but the experience can be elevated so that the pride
in our school elevates. [I’ve] seen it done. It just takes planning and commitment. I’m happy to discuss
if there’s any room to try to improve this experience….​
​
Response: ​
Administration: Having worked in various districts in the area, from our experience with other
vendors, we don’t see a meaningful difference in the quality of the photography. Still,
recommendations for other vendors are welcome. However, money is obviously an issue because
we don’t want our yearbooks and photographs to cost too much. ​
​
PTSA: Several grade parents in attendance at this meeting shared that they personally and other
caregivers had no digital previews of their students’ photographs. One grade parent commented
that when they contacted the vendor in this regard, they were told that the vendor ‘didn’t contract
for that.’ Likewise, another PTSA member mentioned they heard multiple complaints from the
various campuses last year about this same vendor.

Administration: We were not aware of this issue. Providing a digital preview seems like something
that should be done. Thank you for bringing this to our attention. Can the PTSA please gather a list
of related complaints?

PTSA: We will and will respond with our results.

 Administration: As far as the graduation ceremony is concerned, if someone has specific actionable
suggestions, they are welcome to come forward with them.

PTSA: One grade parent mentioned that the ceremony was beautiful last year. However, they were
disappointed in the physical program because it did not list the various academic achievements of
particular students. Likewise, unlike in many other graduation ceremonies, students aren’t allowed
to wear chords, stoles, sashes etc. that announce their academic and other honors. Because these
honors aren’t highlighted in this way at graduation, family and friends who aren’t present at other
IHS ceremonies aren’t aware that particular students have received these hard won honors.

Administration: Different events have different purposes. At IHS we have chosen not to use the
graduation ceremony to honor specific honors. Rather, we emphasize the communal aspect of IHS at
graduation. For example, we have students participate in announcing the graduates and we enter
and exit the ceremony as one. However, to recognize particular students’ achievements, we have
many other events throughout the year that honor various academic awards, service awards,
National Honor Society awards, Cum Laude awards and sports awards.
