    PAC Submissions:

    1.​ Submission: ​
    A parent asks:​
            ●​ Does IHS have a clear fact sheet or manual on how Sec. 504 operates at IHS from the
                application process, what their rights are at a 504 Committee meeting, and all the way
                through the appeal process with estimated time frames for how long major milestones
                take like when a family can expect to receive a ‘504 meeting’ for their particular student?
​       Response:
                Currently, a good deal of the information is located on the district’s website
                (https://www.irvingtonschools.org/Page/4434). The administrators have spoken with
                Heather Shaughnessy, the Director of Special Projects, who currently oversees the 504
                program. They have discussed developing a 504 handbook throughout this year. The
                administrators have also connected her with an expert in the field who helped Scarsdale
                streamline their process and develop their handbook. ​
                ​
                The administrators acknowledge that currently it requires some hunting to find the
                necessary 504 information. They likewise acknowledge that this is particularly difficult if a
                parent is not comfortable with the amount of educational jargon the information contains.
                In the meantime, the administrators forwarded a hyperlink to the Department of
                Education’s 504 handbook
                (https://www.ed.gov/media/document/parent-and-educator-resource-guide-section-504-p
                ublic-elementary-and-secondary-schools-2016-21262.pdf).


                Jon Hirsch offered that if the parents are comfortable reaching out to him to discuss this
                situation, he is happy to speak with them.


                The administrators want to mention that this year’s budget is seeking a new position that
                could enable much of the 504 process to return to the individual campuses, instead of
                being handled at the district level. They think some of the initial process may begin with
                Pupil Personnel Services (“PPS”) and then the maintenance of the 504 process would be
                handled at the building level (e.g., IHS) , most likely overseen by the assistant principal.


​       The same parent also asks:
   ●​ Can the administration help set a more collaborative tone in the 504 committee? ​
      ​
      The parent goes on to say that when clarification was asked for on how the 504
      committee process works or if there was a manual explaining it, no clear answers were
      given.
      The parent also is distraught at the amount of time it took for the family to receive their
      504 meeting, claiming it took about 7 months. Likewise, the parent is concerned that
      reviews of implemented protocols only occur on a yearly basis.
      Also, the parent singled out one member of the committee as a source of particular
      frustration. The parent claims that this committee member was dismissive, had an “the
      answer is no attitude” from the start, assumed the parent was merely seeking more time
      in what the parent feels like was an attitude that they were trying to “game the system”,
      the committee member lacked any sense of urgency, and in short that working with the
      student and the parents was not important.​
      ​
      Lastly, the parent states that extra time may be necessary at times for the student, the
      parent is not sure. However, the parent feels that because of the current tone of some of
      the members in the committee, no fair assessment of any such need would be given.
      The parent explains that in this student’s case, the ability to mask ADHD symptoms,
      partially because of intelligence, led to a late diagnosis, but also may make the student
      appear a less than prime case for the assistance needed to the untrained eye.
Response:
      In regards to the parent’s claim that their student’s 504 meeting took 7 months, without
      knowing if there were some extenuating circumstances, the administrators agree that this
      sounds too long. The process is not designed to work like this.
      As far as reviewing protocols that are implemented for a student in response to a 504
      meeting, our district follows similar practices to surrounding districts of reviewing these on
      a yearly basis. However, either the school or the parent can initiate a review of these
      protocols at an earlier point if it is felt that part of the plan is not meeting the student’s
      needs.
      As far as the tonal issues and lack of congeniality, the administrators have not heard this
      type of feedback in the past regarding the current 504 process. While the administrators
      find it difficult to believe the committee members intended any harm towards the parent
      or student, the impact was clearly felt. The administrators reiterate that they are a
      resource for the parents to help manage conflicts. Another good resource for the parents
      in this situation would be Celia Strino, the Director of PPS.
      As far as the denial of extra time, administrators said that if this student is so bright and
      otherwise able to do well academically as it appears from the parent’s statements, it is
      difficult to meet the standard of ‘demonstrated academic impact’ that is required to trigger
      the need for extra time on tasks like tests. The administration understands that perhaps
      the parents and the student feel and see a real impact. The administrators do not deny
      that. However, it is only if there is some negative impact on the student’s academic
      performance that extra time can be granted.


      PTSA: It should be noted, that two grade parents attending PAC have had experience
      with the 504 process. Both said that their experiences were positive. The PTSA hopes to
      help bring the parent of this submission a more positive outcome as well.
2. Submission: ​

    ●​ A parent asks:
        How often are teachers supposed to update grades in Infinite Campus? The parent
        claims that many teachers are updating grades very infrequently and sometimes not until
        the end of the marking period, leaving students unsure of their grades.
Response:
        The administration states that currently there is no hard and fast rule. However, the
        expectation is that they are updated frequently. Since this is not the first time the
        administration is hearing this complaint, they are contemplating implementing a more
        formal policy.
        Also, if there is a specific class a parent is concerned about they can reach out to the
        individual instructor. Alternatively, they can reach out to the administration. Still, the
        administration recognizes a parent’s frustration when this information portal is being
        underutilized.
3. Submission:
    ●​ A parent states:
        At the Academic Awards Ceremony, many awards and scholarships are given, more than
        from the list students are encouraged to apply for. Additionally, last year, many of the
        awards/scholarships were won by the same 1 or 2 students. The parent asks, is there a
        list of the available awards/scholarships that will be given at the Academic Awards along
        with the criteria? Also, who decides which students win the awards/scholarships?
Response:
        The administrators state that these are great questions. There is a Google list shared
        with the seniors. The administrators will double check with the 2 awards coordinators to
        make sure all the awards are listed there.
        The issue that many awards are going to the ‘same 1 or 2 students’ is a bit of a challenge
        to address. The reason is because a number of the awards are not determined internally.
        Moreover, in the past we have received the name of the recipients late in the year, past
        the time IHS’s own internal awards have been determined. This has resulted in more
        overlap in awards than might otherwise have occurred.
        Another factor that limits IHS’s ability to diversify its pool of awards recipients is that some
        awards have criteria that greatly limit who can receive them (e.g., the highest GPA in
        math classes). However, this year if the criteria is strictly tethered to something like
        GPA, we are looking at other factors for multiple reasons, including to ‘spread the wealth’.
        One other way the administration is hoping to increase the pool of awards recipients is by
        coordinating with the PTSA. The awards that come through the PTSA will now come
        earlier in the year so that IHS can now take those recipients into consideration when
        making decisions on its own internal awards.
        At the same time, the administration states that some students have done so well
        academically that they have put themselves in a position to receive a disproportionate
        amount of awards. IHS would like to find a fair balance.
4. Submission:
   ●​ A parent states:
       A few years ago, the question of students wearing stoles and cords at graduation was
       raised. The parent claims that the answer given as to why students were not allowed to
       wear them was that there was 1) a separate awards ceremony and 2) that recipients
       were acknowledged in the graduation program. At last year’s graduation, the program
       contained no mention of any awards or honors. The program contained only the names
       of the graduates. Why were scholarships/awards/honors not included in the graduation
       program? Who made the decision not to include it last year? The parent finishes by
       saying “our students work hard and deserve to be recognized at graduation for their
       accomplishments.”
Response:
       The administration begins by agreeing with the parent’s closing sentence that the
       students work hard and deserve to be recognized at graduation. The administration
       however feels that the decision that was made several years ago with many stakeholders
       as a result of Culturally Responsive-Sustaining Educational (“CRSE”) was that the ‘thesis
       statement’ that graduation would center around ‘community and cohort’. It was felt that
       individual accomplishments were already celebrated more than sufficiently at the many
       academic and sports award ceremonies throughout the year. The administrators also
       noted that this was not the first year that the graduation program did not note academic
       or other honors.
