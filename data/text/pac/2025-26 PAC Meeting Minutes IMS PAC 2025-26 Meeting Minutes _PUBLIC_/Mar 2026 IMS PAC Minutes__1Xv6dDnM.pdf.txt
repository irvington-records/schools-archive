                                       PAC Submissions

   ●​ Studying for tests has been challenging for some classes without having text
        books to reference. Trying to collate the host of disparate materials from various
        online sources and on paper can be difficult - sometimes kids have to resort to
        googling it. I understand part of this middle school process is learning how to
        study but it can be really stressful to prepare for exams when there is not a readily
        accessible, definitive, source for answers. Can teachers provide study guides or
        at least a list of the relevant sources that students can rely on when preparing?
   ●​   RESPONSE: There are fewer traditional textbooks in use today than in the past.
        Instructional standards and best practices have evolved, and many textbooks are no
        longer fully aligned with current learning goals. As a result, teachers often find it more
        effective to use a combination of authentic and original materials, either created by them
        or carefully compiled from trusted sources, to better meet students’ needs. We
        understand that this can sometimes feel challenging, as there is not always a single,
        consistent resource for students to reference.
   ●​   Students should be using study guides that are created collaboratively in class, under
        the supervision and direction of their teacher. These guides are built from notes taken
        during class, and while having a greater quantity of notes can be helpful, the focus
        should always be on clarity and accuracy. We will continue to follow up with teachers on
        this process and reinforce the importance of students having reliable, well-organized
        resources so they feel confident in how to prepare.
   ●​   We are also working to better understand and address the gap that can sometimes
        occur between what a teacher communicates and what a student hears or interprets.
        Helping students reflect on instructions and expectations is an important part of this
        process.
   ●​   At home, you can support your child by encouraging them to look ahead, not just to the
        next day, but to the upcoming several days. Tests and major assessments are not
        announced one day for the next, so having an awareness of what is coming up allows
        students to plan ahead. If there is any confusion, this also gives them time to seek
        clarification before it becomes urgent.
   ●​   We strongly encourage students to reach out when they have questions. If they are
        unable to explain a concept to you at home, that is a good indication that they should
        follow up with their teacher, either in class or via email. Taking this initiative is an
        important demonstration of their commitment to learning.
