                         Irvington Main Street School PAC Meeting
                                             MINUTES
                                          November 6, 2025


In attendance: Joyce Chapnick (MSS Principal), Heather Shaughnessy (MSS Asst
Principal), Katherine Cain (PTSA MSS VP), Amy Suehnholz (PTSA MSS VP), Emily
Besidski (PTSA Grade Parent - 5th), Lauren D’Amico (PTSA Grade Parent - 4th)

PAC Submissions

    1. What is the general impression among teachers and administrators of the new ELA and
       Science programs? How long are the contracts for the programs, and how will the
       effectiveness of these programs be evaluated?

         The general impressions of both programs are positive, although new programs always
take some time to get used to. Both programs are constantly being evaluated by teachers and
administrators. They will also be assessed based on student test scores - both internal and NYS
tests. Additionally, administrators are planning to conduct some student focus groups in the
future, to get direct feedback from students about the programs.

         ELA: Administrators and teachers both feel that all students are benefiting from explicit
instruction in grammar, sentence structure, and vocabulary. The program has a rigorous and
uniform approach to writing that is beneficial to all students. Additionally, all parents are invited to
attend a presentation on the Arts and Letters program on Monday, November 24th at 9:15 am at
MSS.

       Science: In the coming weeks, science classes will transition to one instructional period a
week and one lab period per week. The lab will be hands-on and geared toward giving students
a more engaging and fun look at the various science concepts they are learning.

   2. Can there be a science based recess club (something like robotics, engineering or
       tinkering?) where children can get more enrichment?

         Great idea! The administration is going to look into possible opportunities for partnering
with the IHS robotics club. Students are also always encouraged to make an appointment to
speak with Mrs. Chapnick if they have specific ideas for recess clubs. Also, the board games
club does have some games that encourage “tinkering” - if a student is looking for a particular
tool or game to be added to the board games club, we could also look into that.

    3. How should a complaint of bullying / non-inclusive behavior be handled, particularly
        when it occurs during the recess period? How much adult supervision do students have
        while outside at recess?

         If an issue occurs at recess, in the immediate aftermath, a student can speak to a recess
monitor. At any given time, there are 6 adults out on the playground at recess. If a student would
 like to speak further or to someone else, they can ask to go to the main office to speak with Ms.
  Chapnick or Ms. Shaughnessy (or they can also speak to their teacher back in the classroom).
        Students are always welcome to have their parents reach out to their teacher or
administrators, if the student doesn’t feel comfortable bringing up the issue. There are also a few
other channels that have been discussed throughout the school year: there is a GoogleForm that
students fill out on Class Link in order to request a meeting with the school counselor, and
students have also discussed in their classes who their “trusted adults” are both in and out of the
building. The administration encourages students and parents to reach out with any issues of
bullying or non-inclusive behavior.
