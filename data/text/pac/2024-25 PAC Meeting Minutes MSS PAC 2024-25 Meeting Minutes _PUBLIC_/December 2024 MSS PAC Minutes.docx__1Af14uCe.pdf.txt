                      Irvington Main Street School PAC Meeting
                                         MINUTES
                                     December 11, 2024


In attendance: Joyce Chapnick (MSS Principal), Heidi Rhodes (PTSA MSS VP), Katherine
Cain (PTSA MSS VP), Heather Goodman (PTSA Grade Parent - 5th), Emily Besidski (PTSA
Grade Parent - 4th)

PAC Submissions

      1.​ Chromebooks: My daughter brings home her chromebook regularly (we don’t have
          a home computer she can use) and I was under the impression that this was to
          complete school work. While it occasionally has been for school work, it has come to
          my attention that she is using the chromebook to go on YouTube and and Google to
          browse videos (e.g. Jimmy Fallon interviews, Makeup tutorials, pop music videos). I
          was under the false impression that this kind of content would be blocked on school
          computers and therefore was not concerned about her usage. I am unable to view
          her browser history and am only aware of this because I went to Youtube and can
          see history there. This might be OK in some households and in those households
          children usually have access to their own screen or can use a family computer. This
          is not something I willingly brought into my home. I have seen groups of kids
          gathered around their school issued Chromebook outside of MSS looking at this kind
          of inappropriate (in my mind) content.

          A few thoughts:
          -Is there a reason that children in 4th and 5th grade need to bring their school
          computers home? Could this be something that could be reconsidered?
          -Can parents be given some kind of provisioned access to their child’s Chomebook
          so we can have conversations with our children about what they are seeing?
          -Is non-educational content OK for children to use Chromebooks to view during
          school hours (because as of now this is something they are doing)?
          -Do children at this age all really need to have a Chromebook in order to complete
          their school work?

          It seems to me like this was a Covid era decision that was made that has had lasting
          consequences, and we should take a minute to make sure this is really
          what is best for our kids. I understand by the time they reach middle school much
          of this control goes out the door, but 9-11 year olds are a different story.
          Thanks for considering my issues/questions.


          RESPONSE:
          ●​ Chromebooks should be brought home only when related to a school
             assignment. Parents can log in with their child’s student ID to see what their child
             is accessing on their Chromebook.
          ●​ YouTube is restricted to only educational portions of the site.
   ●​ If you have additional concerns about your child’s Chromebook usage at home,
      please feel free to contact their teacher directly.

2.​ 5th Grade Activity Fee: The request for the $195 5th Grade Activity Fee raises
    several concerns:
       1. Since the field trips occur during regular school hours and are effectively
       mandatory, they should be funded through the voter-approved school budget.
       Optional activities like the school play or the BBQ understandably warrant
       additional fees, but mandatory activities do not.

       2. Given that the activities are effectively mandatory, the $195 fee resembles a
       regressive tax, which feels somewhat insensitive, especially considering the
       21.97% cumulative inflation rate since 2020.

       3. Parents experiencing financial hardship may feel uncomfortable approaching
       school staff for assistance due to the sensitive nature of such requests.
       Additionally, this issue goes beyond financial hardship—it’s about respecting
       families’ ability to decide how they allocate their hard-earned money. For
       instance, a family might prefer to devote the $195 to a private tutoring session, a
       529 Plan contribution, or a family vacation.

       4. Regarding the end-of-year celebratory event, while the Elmwood Day Camp
       trip aims to provide team building and reflection opportunities, could the same
       goals be achieved using local resources? Irvington’s riverfront parks and other
       venues could offer a cost-effective and equally enjoyable alternative.

       5. On the compulsory yearbook purchase, parents should have the option to
       decide whether they wish to buy a yearbook. If the administration feels strongly
       that every 5th grader should receive one, the cost should be incorporated into the
       school budget rather than being compulsory.

       Many of us have already paid the fee, likely with varying degrees of difficulty and
       comfort. We encourage the school to reconsider the programming and funding
       model for these activities. Field trips, in particular, should be included in the
       regular school budget. Additionally, we urge the return of fees to families who
       have already paid, with future adjustments to better reflect these considerations.
   ​

   RESPONSE:
   ●​ This is an ongoing discussion that Joyce is having with the district, PTSA and IEF
      on how to balance the funds at costs for grade-wide activities and in particular,
      field trips. There are requests for funds in every grade level, which are spread out
      over the year.
   ●​ Families in financial need can always reach out to Joyce or Gina Menendez.
      Families do this, and it is always confidential.
   ●​ Reasons for the one-time payment, as opposed to smaller payments throughout
      the year, include:
●​ Families only need to handle one payment, reducing the hassle of multiple
   transactions throughout the year.
●​ Provides families with a clear understanding of all costs associated with student
   activities upfront.
●​ Reduces administrative time spent collecting, tracking and following up on
   multiple payments.
●​ Helps teachers and staff plan activities, field trips and events with confirmed
   funding at the start of the year.
●​ Guarantees all students have access to activities, field trips and other
   opportunities without delays caused by unpaid fees.
●​ Ensures all students receive the same experiences (e.g., yearbook, T-shirt,
   end-of-year events), fostering a sense of inclusion.
●​ Families avoid unexpected expenses or forgotten deadlines for payments later in
   the year.
●​ Enables families to plan for the expense and prioritize it as part of their
   back-to-school preparations.
●​ Decreases the risk of missed payments, confusion about amounts owed or
   misplaced money.
●​ Creates a seamless experience for students and families, reinforcing a positive
   and organized school environment.
