Curriculum
   -​ MATH - Have we considered other math curricula? My child is bored by the current one and I
       hear similar feedback from other parents. I have suggestions if the district is open to it.
           o​ This is our third year using Eureka squared. It was chosen by the Math committee that
               reviewed and considered multiple programs over the course of the year before choosing
               this program. A reason why they chose this program is because it requires thought
               behind solving a problem. It can “slow down” some of the mathematicians who arrive at
               answers quickly and that can be frustrating for the kids who just get to the answer
               because it forces them to go back and explain their thinking.

            o​ There’s differentiation within the program- for those who are advanced it has the kids go
               deeper into the content. Anyone looking for more of a challenge should go to the teacher
               to ask how they are differentiating. If the teacher doesn’t answer, then go to Andrea and
               Meg to see how differentiation is happening within the classroom. We still use IXL and
               other tools for automaticity. Differentiation starts in Kindergarten.

            o​ Dows has differentiation training for teachers- Diane Cunningham is an outside
               consultant who runs sessions with the teachers that focus on differentiation. The
               Instructional Leaders meet with Diane as well- all teachers are sharing their experiences
               with each other on how they are differentiating, and it’s an ongoing conversation and
               focus.
             o​ Please feel free to email the teachers and ask them how your child is getting
                differentiation within the classroom- teachers want to communicate with you about your
                child.

    -​   We had an issue in 3rd grade where my child was required to already know/was being tested on
         multiplication facts but was not taught these sufficiently in 2nd grade nor were we aware that we
         had to teach our child this over the summer. It would be helpful for future families if this could be
         emphasized.
             o​ This was likely the pre-test for the unit of study to see where the children are at, so the
                  teacher knows where to meet the class where they are. Math curriculum has a pre and
                  post test. This helps with differentiation within the classroom.

    -​   How does Dows Lane incorporate problem-based learning (PBL) into their curriculum? PBL
         creates motivation, forces kids to think outside the box and work collaboratively. It also makes
         learning more fun and relevant to our kiddos. We should be nurturing their budding critical
         thinking skills in these formative years.
             o​ PBL is Project Based Learning, and the focus is always on “Authentic projects” - We do
                  this across every class/grade level. The teachers work closely with Diane Cunningham to
                  figure out the projects that are appropriately linked to the curriculum. PBL gives choice
                  and allows students to use creativity and quick feedback with the teachers. Projects
                  promote collaboration and excitement and student engagement, and we are very proud
                  of using PBLs in each class.
