PAC Submissions
   1.​ Question re: Assessments: Why aren’t parents told when Aimsweb assessments are
       being given? Additionally, why aren’t these scores transparently shared with parents,
       and only to those who know to request them? It seems like we’re waiting for children to
       require MTSS and that’s when it is shared they’re having difficulties. This could be
       prevented in some cases if parents are aware and can focus on that skill at home or over
       the summer.
        Response: This is the final year with aimsweb as we move to a more user-friendly
        universal screener, Renaissance Star. Scores will be automatically uploaded to the
        portal. Multi-Tiered System of Supports (MTSS) uses multiple measures to assess
        children. Aimweb results is one of a mosaic of assessments. The school takes into
        account many pieces of data (fundations test, Eureka2 math module, progress
        monitoring, and other information for children to receive extra support).


   2.​ Question re: Report Cards: Follow-up to the October report-card answer: What was

3.​ Question: Re: Curriculum - The second grade math curriculum is, up until October
    23rd at least, disappointing. My 2nd grader is bringing home single-digit addition that he
    can do easily- he has brought home at least 8 "mad minute" worksheets that are all
    single digit addition, always 30/30 and completed with ease. His kindergarten brother
    does the extra worksheets, and for the kindergartener they are at an appropriate
    learning level. Can we please offer some differentiation in the math options? I did
    approach the teacher who noted that the curriculum would advance in the coming
    months. I know that it must be difficult for the teachers to teach at different levels. Could
    there be an extra math book and lesson for kids who are bored with this? I feel we are
    not serving him educationally, and I believe he isn't the only one. He is getting bored and
    wants to know why he has to keep doing single digit addition. For the record we felt this
    same frustration in first grade math, and did not know what to do about it then either.
    Would love to hear teachers' suggestions, or some from the administration. ​
   Response: Andrea met with Molly Stern (K - 5 Math and Science Instructional Coach) to
   discuss this question/concern. The Eureka math curriculum is designed to build math
   skills and mathematical reasoning. Repetitive “sprint” exercises help students develop
   fluency and recall of basic math facts through repeated practice. This foundational
   skill-building is important as it prepares students to approach more complex concepts
   with confidence. (Similar to how basketball players practice thousands of free throws
   repeatedly to build consistency and skill, these exercises are intended to strengthen
   foundational math abilities through regular repetition.) At this elementary level, the goal
   is for students to develop automaticity across the four operations. Building a strong
   foundation/confidence in completion of these exercises helps prepare students for future
   opportunities, including access to Algebra in 8th grade and the potential to pursue
   higher-level mathematics in high school such as calculus AB/BC.) The curriculum does
   offer opportunities for differentiation (Eureka Squared and Arts & Letters). Parents are
   encouraged to speak directly with their child's teacher. Molly Stern will join the January 8
   PTSA Coffee to provide additional information about math instruction. Jerrod Blair,
   Technology Director, will also attend to discuss technology.

4.​ Question: Re: Tech – Is there a way to opt out of Chromebook use in the lower grades?
    The tech guidelines are great for addressing some use, but there are other purportedly
    educational uses that are not specifically addressed. In first grade we have been told
    there are some Chromebook projects coming up that could still be done analog (aka why
    make a presentation on a Google slide instead of a poster presentation? Why do they
    read articles on the computer instead of printed?
   Response: We cannot opt out of Chromebook use as we have to adhere to NYS
   standards. The school and administration are actively listening to the community and
   have changed practices to ensure we are following the Learning First: Balanced
   Technology Integration model.
5.​ School Halloween parade - is it possible to have more than one parent attend?
   Yes, we will have space for more than one adult next year.
6.​ Why isn’t the amount of homework consistent amongst teachers on the same grade
    level? Some kids have 4 assignments per night, others have 2?
   Grade levels are consistent in the amount of reading each child is expected to do;
   depending on grade level could be math sheet or extension of something done in school.
   Classwork and homework are consistent and are paced in each grade level within 1
   week of each other.
7.​ Would the school ever consider having a grandparents day or a special person day?
    Could Dows Lane pilot a once-a-year "lunch guest" day, where a parent, grandparent, or
    other special grown-up joins their child in the cafeteria, staggered by grade/class on a
    sign-up schedule?
   School leadership is committed to re-establishing an “in the school” culture adding
   events where families can be in-person to better understand the curriculum, see what
   the kids are doing academically, and experience the classroom. There are many
   volunteer opportunities for parents (in the garden, library, etc.) but the school cannot host
   guests for lunch in the cafeteria– given size and capacity limitations and the importance
   of prioritizing this time for kids to focus on eating and socializing. We can continue to talk
   about and explore a grandparents/special persons day (likely spring); Andrea will talk to
   her team to get more input from staff.
