	Action (Consent): 10.3 Patient Protection and Affordable Care Act Resolution -new variable hour employees

	Resolution: WHEREAS, the Patient Protection and Affordable Care Act requires the Irvington Union Free School District to determine full-time status of new variable hour employees in order to establish eligibility for health insurance coverage; and WHEREAS, the proposed regulations under the Patient Protection and Affordable Care Act allow for look-back measurement periods to be used for new variable hour employees to determine whether an employee is a full-time employee; and WHEREAS, the Irvington Union Free School District desires to establish a look-back initial measurement period, administrative period and stability period for its new variable hour employees; and WHEREAS, the Irvington Union Free School District provides a health plan and is a participating municipality under the Southern Westchester School Cooperative Health Plan (SWSCHP); and WHEREAS, the Irvington Union Free School District’s health plan year is a fiscal year which runs from July 1st to June 30th; and NOW THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED, that the initial measurement period for new variable hour employees shall be a period of 12 months which shall begin on the first day of the first month following the employee’s start date; and BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED, that the administrative period for new variable hour employees shall begin at the end of the initial measurement period and shall continue through the end of the first full calendar month beginning on or after the end of the initial measurement period; and BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED, that the stability period for new variable hour employees, determined to have averaged at least 30 hours of service per week during the initial measurement period, shall be a period of 12 consecutive calendar months to begin immediately after the administrative period; and BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED, that the stability period for new variable hour employees, determined not to have averaged 30 hours or more per week during the initial measurement period, shall be a period of 12 consecutive calendar months to begin immediately after the administrative period.

	Motion by Phil Whitney, second by Robyn Kerner.

	Final Resolution: Motion Carries

	Yes: Robert Grados, Phil Whitney, Robyn Kerner, Maria Kashkin, John Montgomery

	Action (Consent): 10.4 Patient Protection and Affordable Care Act - on-going full-time employees

	Resolution: WHEREAS, the Patient Protection and Affordable Care Act requires the Irvington Union Free School District to determine full-time status of ongoing employees in order to establish eligibility for health insurance coverage; and WHEREAS, eligibility must be determined prior to the start of the health plan year in 2015; and WHEREAS, the proposed regulations under the Patient Protection and Affordable Care Act allow for look-back measurement periods to be used for ongoing employees to determine whether an employee is a full-time employee; and WHEREAS, the Irvington Union Free School District desires to establish a look-back standard measurement period, administrative period and stability period for its ongoing employees; and WHEREAS, the Irvington Union Free School District provides a health plan and is a participating municipality under the Southern Westchester School Cooperative Health Plan (SWSCHP); and WHEREAS, the Irvington Union Free School District’s health plan year is a fiscal year which runs from July 1st to June 30th; and NOW THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED, that the standard measurement period for ongoing employees shall be a period of 12 months to be measured from November 1, 2013 to October 31, 2014; and BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED, that the administrative period for ongoing employees shall be a period of 61 days to be measured from November 1, 2014 to December 31, 2014; and BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED, that the stability period for ongoing employees, determined to have averaged at least 30 hours of service per week during the standard measurement period, shall be a period of 12 months to begin immediately after the administrative period on January 1, 2015 and to continue until December 31, 2015; and BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED, that the stability period for ongoing employees, determined not to have averaged 30 hours or more per week during the standard measurement period, shall be a period of 12 months to begin immediately after the administrative period on January 1, 2015 and continue until December 31, 2015.

	Motion by Phil Whitney, second by Robyn Kerner.

	Discussion: 11.1 Space Utilization Study (Presentation)

	Dr. Harrison began by noting that space utilization within the District has been a discussion in the community for many years. In the 2012-13, the Board wanted to explore the topic further and an analysis was completed at the end of the school year, which resulted in a list of possible scenarios, including

-
		maintain the status quo;

-
		turn Main Street School into a pre-K and Kindergarten school only;

-
		renovate space at DL to accommodate grades 4 and 5; or

-
		relocate grade 4 to DL and grade 5 to the middle school, which would require moving the 8th grade to the high school.

	Dr. Harrison also provided financial implications for each of the scenarios and noted that the five year facilities plan notes the prospect of $3.1M of capital improvements that will be needed at MSS. Dr. Harrison also highlighted the potential costs of renovations associated with the options as well as potential savings.

	The options for District Office space included:

-
		replacement of current modulars, costing approximately $1M;

-
		relocating to DL would require $385K to renovate the space;

-
		rental of commercial property and selling MSS with an approximately yearly cost of $60,000.

	Dr. Harrison reported that there are significant structural problems with the current modulars and a $1M replacement is not a viable solution. Moving DO to DL has already received SED approval; would reclaim additional green space and make more efficient use of DL and allow the school to operate more effectively as the school office would be more centrally located. This option would result in the loss of one classroom. Any construction would be done during the summer but might carry over to the school year.

	Dr. Harrison highlighted the impacts on the possible school organization scenarios, both opportunities and challenges, including financial, logistics and educational challenges. Of note would be the significant impact of moving 5th grade to the middle school and 8th grade to the high school given the wide age continuum that would be present. Dr. Harrison again noted that if MSS was decommissioned and it became necessary to reopen the school at a later time, there would be necessary to modify the space to be in compliance with ADA and SED requirements. As an aside, there would be little if any transportation cost savings with any of the scenarios.

	The District-wide 5 year building maintenance plan identifies $9.7M of recommended work to District facilities and if any of the scenarios are considered, Dr. Harrison believes that it will be necessary to sell MSS in order to balance the needs of other district facilities.

	From an administrative point of view, it is a complex matter that will impact the education of all students and at this point, administration does not recommend the closing of MSS but rather to develop a strategy to finance the maintenance needs of the district and pursue a plan to move District offices to DL and eliminate the modulars.

	Next steps will include additional presentations, reports and meetings as needed in addition to tonight's discussion.

	The Board concurred with administration’s assessment to maintain the status quo, develop a strategy to address the maintenance issues as outlined in the five year plan, and find an optimal solution for the district offices.

	In response to Mr. Montgomery's question on the types of capital spending that would be needed at MSS, Mrs. Miller reviewed the items that are included in the five year maintenance plan, both short and long term. The MSS gym is included in the MSS capital needs projections and it was noted that perhaps it is best to look at the gym as part of the fields/athletic needs discussion. In addition the Board discussed savings in relation to the scenarios and implications to staffing. Mrs. Kerner noted the cyclical nature of Irvington's enrollment and must be able to accommodate the higher enrollments and should the enrollment surpass the space available, it might become necessary to increase class sizes in order to reduce the number of sections, as there would be no flexibility with existing space.

	Mr. Whitney noted that it seemed nearly impossible to contemplate the movement of 5th grade to the MS and he doesn't feel that the district can commit to a scenario that does not provide for any increase or flexibility should there be growth within the District. The savings would lower the cost base but the reality is that it is not feasible.

	Mrs. Kerner noted that she could not consider closing MSS and must ask what is optimal to student learning and what is the optimal solution for the District Office, and maintain the current organization of the schools. She also thought that any costs to address district office might be folded into any bond that might be considered.

	Mr. Grados stated that he thought a space study would finally satisfy the discussions that occur during budget time that there was money to be saved by closing MSS to the point that it has become lore and having a professional study of how our space is utilized and what might be viable options would  put to rest the idea, and noted that decisions are not made for purely economic reasons but that the impact on students, student learning and the community, especially with any consideration of closing MSS are must be factored into any decision. He also noted that there doesn’t appear to be any significant savings and there are significant costs to reorganization.
