	11. Presentations/Discussions

		Discussion, Presentation: 11.1 2013-14 Budget Discussion: Instruction, Technology, Central Data, Benefits and Revenue (Presentation)

		Dr. Harrison stated that the presentation by Dr. Kor and Ms. Miller will begin to delve into instruction in tonight's presentation, including benefits and revenues anticipated for the 2013-14. He also noted that in December, budget presentations began with a "maintenance" budget", meaning the continuation of all programs currently in place, and the January 22nd meeting focused on buildings, athletics and special education programs. Also noted was that there are areas where efficiencies might be found, but many costs are well beyond District's control, i.e. pension costs, health insurance and tax certiorari settlements which affects the tax base going forward but also there is exposure for past years with settlements that might exceed $3 million next year. Dr.  Harrison also pointed out that requirements from NYS and the Federal government affects the budget and that New York State has possibly 300 mandates more than the Federal government. All these factors drive where we are going and it does present a gloomy picture.

		Dr. Kor and Ms. Miller presented Central Data Processing which supports infastructure and operational, such as eSchool Plus which maintains student data, and Edline, the District's web hosting system. Dr. Kor reviewed the individual codes which covers other expenses that fall under the curriculum budget such a staff development, membership to the Tri-State Consortium as well as BOCES resources such as Teachscape, which is the administration and teacher evaluation program, professional learning services as well as other membership fees. New this year is the Data Warehouse tools and Rubicon Atlas-Curriculum Mapping which is web-based software that holds the District's curriculum that teachers and parents have access to, noting that it is a time-saving tool that allows for regular updating to keep curriculum current. Building-based expenses such as membership fees, equipment, as well as DASA training were also addressed.

		Within the Teacher salary code which includes 35 positions, is the addition of a K-3 teaching assistant for Dows Lane which is a reinstatement of the position, with a salary budget of $35,000. In Grades 7-12, a .6 position is being requested to add position for French and Social Studies to avoid exceeding the contractural limit and to provide flexibility in scheduling students, totaling $37,980. Three non-instructional positions have been added to provide security at each building, but no exact title has been given, but budgeted at $51,000 for the three positions. Other expenses covered are home tutoring, test scoring, and professional development. expenses connected to high school students, such Model UN, yearbook, literary magazine and Paw Print and the Academic Challenge team. A targeted, in-service program, with an expense of $21,000 to cover courses offered in-district is being proposed for the 2013-14 school year, thus saving money over time and is only a pilot program offering no more than five courses initially. Mr. McCann asked how the District will evaluate the effectiveness and savings of this program and can the money be found in reducing BOCES services. Dr. Kor again noted that the codes also include principals' requests and needs. The District also switched to an automated sub system called AESOP and Dr. Kor reported that the District has realized savings by utilizing the system, but not having teachers cover classes and pay for sub callers.

		Mrs. Kerner asked how the District measures the effectiveness of the consultants and requested if the Board that the Board be provided with the schedule of professional development.

		With respect to benefits, Mr. McCann asked if the student accident insurance could be bidded out, but Ms. Miller noted that there are only two providers for such services. Ms. Miller will call to get costs.

		Mr. McCann questioned if the District shouldn't be looking to use iPads in the District which would eliminate the costs of supplies, etc. He noted that he had previously asked to have representatives come in to audit our technology. Dr. Kor also noted that beginning in the 2014-15 school year, students will need to have access to computers to take the new assessments and therefore will hinder the ability for the district to go towards using tablets.

		With respect to the co-curriculur budget, a $11,200 or 10.34% increase is proposed due to three new requests from IMS as well as contractural increases.

		Ms.Miller reported on employee benefits projected budget, which are mostly out of the district's control, and the increase is $1,368,337 or a 13.27% increase, primarily due to an increase in the employees' and teachers' retirement system, health insurance and social security. Ms. Miller provided an overview of the NYS Employees Retirement and Teacher Systems and how the increases are calculated, noting that all figures are projected as the State will not provide exact increases until the summer.

		All presentations represent a budget to budget increase of 4.64%. Ms. Miller also proposes to increase the budget by $700,000 to cover expenses connected with issuing a BAN to cover tax certioraris, budget to budget is 6.01% increase, which brings the District is over the tax cap by approximately $2 million dollars. In reviewing anticipated revenue for the 2013-14 school year, Ms. Miller is anticipating an increase of $294,545, and noted that state aid is anticipated to be increased approximately $128,219, and increases in day school tuition, health services, and BOCES aid. Mrs. Kerner voiced her concern that the fund balance is at $2M, the maximum allowable, but doesn't know what the amount will be at the end of the year.

		In conclusion, Dr. Harrison asked the Board to provide guidance to the administration as what might be a realistic increase by February 12th. Administration will need to strike a balance between what children need but also being mindful of community and agai called for pubic involvement in the process.

		Board comments included:

		Mr. Grados noted that the Board's hands are tied and all members have ideas as to how they would rearrange budget or to cut, but budget is 2.+ over cap based upon over $2M of costs that have no control over, He also noted that three years ago, cut nearly to bone, two year 0% increase, and over four years it has been an annualized 2% increase.

		Mr. McCann noted all districts are faced with the same increases that we have in benefits, etc. and asks to attempt to try to not spend more than we take in with regard to paying back tax certs. He is willing to present a budget over the tax cap,with making some fine tuning and perhaps better gage the communities reaction but will need to determine how much over the tax cap. It would not include cutting sports or music, so it would be teachers and increase in class sizes.

		Maria Kashkin noted that 20 teachers would need to be cut to stay within the tax cap and is not sure that the community will accept that. She also stressed the need to hear from community of what they would accept because getting to tax cap would be hurtful to the district.

		Mrs. Kerner noted that long term planning was lacking over the years and as a District must think about what kind of District we want Irvington to look and make wise decisions and ask the community that when voting for budget,the importance of keeping in mind to vote for Irvington over the long haul, and when put in that context, will have the right direction.
