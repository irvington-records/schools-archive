5. Presentations/Discussions

The administrative team and the Board engaged in a conversation as to what a successful school experience looks like in Irvington and how individual and school-wide or systemic success is measured.  To help frame the discussion Dr. Harrison in advance shared an article entitled "The Significance of Grit: A Conversation with Angela Lee Duckworth” which speaks to the relationship between grit and achievement.

Administrators shared their thoughts on how they define grit, how to build it, and what opportunities are provided to students in the Irvington schools that would foster grit. Mr. Cohen shared the focus at the high school has been on looking for opportunities to provide extended learning over time through multiple sequences, such as Project Lead The Way as a means for students to realize a sense of mastery. He also noted that experiences out of the classroom that recognize students’ successes are important as well, with programs such as the new Core Value recognition awards. Mr. Montgomery spoke to another article co-authored by Angela Duckworth linked to the subject, but which spoke of the differences between self-control and grit, but raised the question if grit can be taught in any way, and he wonders if it’s realistic that schools can teach grit and if so, how might schools be structured. Dr. Kor noted that our goal is to have students be passionate, and motivated with a “can do” mindset and noted that along with the teaching of the Habits of Mind, important to build environments in classrooms where instruction is designed to push kids to do their best, to provide students with feedback and to demand that students be persistent, all which work together to influence success. Mr. Palermo queried how do we define success and does it look the same for all students or do we allow success to be different for every student and in the manner that they choose. Mr. Whitney raised the notion of mastery and how to navigate mastery as a goal vs. need to be flexible as the job market requires and also to achieve excellence. Related to this, Mr. Montgomery asked about the idea of students setting goals for themselves and how to get them to set their own agenda/goals. Mr. Sottile believes that mastery is looked at as a journey, not as a means, which learning is a tool on that journey and is what we would want our students to understand. By students having the mindset that learning is a journey, not the destination, it can allow space for them to set goals for their own learning. At the elementary level, Ms. Benedetto and Ms. Mariniello spoke about teaching students to reflect on their work will provide them with opportunities to look at where they are and where they might go in a particular lesson, to understand what the challenges were of a particular assignment and to recognize that what they struggled with at first, that ultimately with persistence they did succeed. These processes also allows students to discover their passions. Ms. Mariniello believes that grit is fostered through many experiences which allows students to grow their love of learning. Ms. Chapnick spoke to Project Lead The Way and how the 4th graders were taking the lead and identifying how they want to pursue their interests. MSS provides different experiences for students, such as through Destination Imagination, various clubs, etc. and teachers continually look for ways to engage students in many ways. There are many layers to providing experiences for students to grow and to identify their strengths and teachers are always reflecting on what works.

Administrators and the Board also discussed how structures are measured to determine if we are successful, how to give the A students the grit to move beyond their achievements. As way of example, Mr. Cohen spoke to the new graphic novel course where students are demonstrating grit and persistence as the course is proving more challenging then they might have originally thought. Dr. Kor spoke of the conflict between mastery and rigor and the challenge to design instruction to allow for rigor, not necessarily mastery and that is what needs to be measured. Mr. Sottile spoke of mastery as an illusionary goal and denotes finalization. He spoke of defining a "successful school experience" as one that provides a wide array of experiences for students and to teach that more is learned from struggles then from successes and to ensure that we provide the tools to students so that they work through their frustrations and challenges.

Mr. McCormack spoke to grit related to physical education and athletics and how students push their limits to try different things. Team building allows them to learn to work with others and builds perseverance that will carry through other areas of their school life. Students who take many sports gain enjoyment just to participate vs. a student who wants to master only one sport. Discussions are underway as to how to increase rigor in this area and to acknowledge the benefits of PE, designing instruction to support students' rigor and grit, and making connections across all classes.

A second question for discussion was "How do we measure individual and school-wide or systemic success? Dr. Kor believes that most of the time is spent on looking at individual students, not necessarily school-wide test scores and the other data points. Examples of current measures were presented. It is important to look at programs to ensure that all students find something that motivates them. She spoke of how to look at the student who seems disenfranchised to be sure courses provide opportunities for all students. Mr. Whitney asked if it is possible to develop our own way to assess our students’ success, with each student getting a rating, possibly against a goal. Dr. Kor spoke to the Tri-State assessments that evaluates critical thinking as part of the curriculum, expectations and student performance. As a District, we would need to decide what makes the most sense and develop matrix. She believes it would be valuable to develop a matrix along the lines of grit. She spoke to the idea of providing capstone projects which are designed to measure students' success as students demonstrate what they have learned. Mr. Corbett also spoke to the importance of grit being modeled and not necessarily measurable.

Dr. Harrison believes that there might be value in beginning a discussion to look at the capstone project concept or developing a grit matrix. Dr. Kor cautioned that it is important to be clear on what is being measured, but possibly better to name what is valued and how to measure that.

Mr. Whitney noted that these types of discussions are valuable and through the strategic planning process, other opportunities will be identified.

Discussion: 5.2 Discussion on Rate of Compensation for Substitute Teachers

Dr. Kor reported that there is a shortage of substitute teachers in the District, but also a state-wide shortage, making for a competitive market. Irvington's current rate of $90 per day is not the lowest but it is not competitive. Another issue is the increased cost when teachers must cover the classes due to lack of substitutes versus the cost of paying a substitute.  Administration is asking the Board to consider increasing the rate of pay from $90.00 per day to $100 in an attempt to increase the substitute pool. Mr. Montgomery asked if increasing the rate of pay would enable us to hire better qualified subs. Mrs. Palmieri doesn't believe that increasing the salary would not necessarily lead to better quality.

Board members agreed to the $10 per day increase but Mr. Montgomery would also like to consider if the whole substitute system could be improved by raising the bar. The Board discussed the increased cost of $100 or 110 per day. Ms. Miller reported that it would cost approximately $16,000 more to raise to $100. Administration isn't sure if more money would necessarily alleviate the substitute pool shortage.

The Board will act on a motion to increase the rate of pay to $100 per day at the next meeting.

Discussion: 5.3 Discussion of Conducting a Transportation Study

As a follow-up to the previous Board discussion on the value of having a transportation study completed, Mr. Whitney stated that he is in favor of doing a study to provide the Board with information on current bus routes, etc. Ms. Palmieri and Mr. Hanna would like to expand the study from its original scope---determine the impact of expanding transportation to private roads and looking at current utilization.

The Board discussed many aspects of the transportation program such as the length of routes, would any changes impact ridership negatively or positively and what might they be, i.e. changing the start of the school day especially at the high school, can shuttle buses be provided to transport MS/HS students who stay late at school, etc. Of note, 58% of the cost of transportation is spent on transporting private school students/special education students.

Mrs. Palmieri feels that bussing is a large cost to the district bit it may not be effective in its current form. Mr. Montgomery agrees that an improved system might be beneficial and lead to increase ridership. Another data point might be to also study the start time of schools, especially at the high school. Also, in a previous presentation on transportation it was noted that by increasing the distances, substantial savings might be realized. Mr. Whitney stated that the point of a study would be to determine if we could change the start time at the high school, but ultimately we are part of the consortium and must be mindful of the current configuration.

Ms. Miller will work with the consultant to determine the cost of an expanded study. Dr. Harrison stressed that changes to start times will have broader implications and cannot happen in the immediate.
