	4. Presentations/Discussions

	Discussion: 4.1 2015-16 Budget Discussion and Revisions (Presentation)

	Dr. Harrison reviewed the budget process that began in February and the changes to the recommended budget as discussed at previous meetings as well as the Board's "charge" to present a budget that fell at most a 3.5% tax rate increase. He also noted that added to the budget is the addition of a literacy coach which the Board requested further information on, a review of replacing a security greeter with a part time clerical but there are limitations. Other new reductions have been realized in the textbook budget line, the proposed painting budget, reduction of the proposed clerical at the high school to a 10 month position as well as the purchase of iPads through a PTSA grant and Project Lead The Way supplies to further reduce the budget by another $67,080 and an additional $101,717 for the literacy coach (including s actual fringe benefits). with a total budget of $57,664,000, a budget to budget increase of 2.43%, a tax rate increase of 3.54% and $384,655 under Irvington's tax cap.

	Dr. Kor addressed the job description of the proposed Literacy Coach and noted that it would greatly benefit the Grades 6-12 program and support literacy across the curriculum to ensure that our  graduating students have strong reading and comprehension skills.

	Mr. Grados commented that he is happy with the proposed budget. Mrs. Kerner noted that at an earlier curriculum meeting today, and noted that it was discussed that this position would also help to differentiate instruction in the classroom and meet the needs of all learners. Mrs. Kerner also noted that there might be some flexibility to shift to fifth grade if the need arose to do so. She also stated that she is passionate about this position as it will benefit the students. Mrs. Kerner also agreed that it is a good budget, good for students and the District and it answers the charge of the Board, and adds back items that had been previously eliminated. Mr. Montgomery commented on the reduction to textbook budget line and feels there must be other areas to reduce as we do over budget on other items each year. Mr. Montgomery stated that he might be more fiscally conservative but is happy to go along with fellow members as it is a good budget. With regard to the Literacy Coach, he feels that the ELA program has backed away from the higher level courses and now with the coach provide further differentiation given the different courses provided, i.e. honors, AP, etc. and asks that the Board be kept informed as the position is further developed.

	Mrs. Kashkin thanked the administrators for coming in very close to the Board's requests and that it is a very smart budget and great for the kids.

	Dr. Harrison thanked the administrators for helping craft the budget as well as thanked the PTSA for their assistance in providing needed items beyond the budget.

	Discussion: 4.2 Discussion on Field Testing

	Dr. Harrison noted that the District has been notified that we are to administer the Grades 3 and 6 Mathematics as well as Earth Science Regents. Dr. Harrison has asked SED for legal citations requiring district participation as well as explanation of the consequences that a district would face if they do not administer and has spoken with an SED individual who would need to get back with answers but hopes that we administer the tests, but if not, the tests should be returned to SED. As of today, there was no further information available to us on the questions. Dr. Harrison believes that, with possible consultation with our legal counsel, will need to delve deeper to determine any legal citations, etc. and the Board must assess what their legal responsibilities are with regard to this matter. He added that districts who did not administer the field tests last year commented that they have not seen any consequences.

	Mr. Grados noted that his children chose to take the tests and that it is a personal decision among family members. The other issue is that the Board ultimately has to do what is in the best interest of the District, and believes that any legal comments will be ambiguous at best, but would support the District opting out of the field tests if there is sufficient backup from counsel that  there would be no loss of funding or other consequences..

	Mrs. Kerner also agreed with a community member who spoke that it is time to make a stand so that Albany will listen to communities and that opt out will make voices heard about the concern and with regards to field testing, based on information at hand now, it does not seem that there are financial strings attached to opting out, and believes that District should take a stand. She would also would like the Board as a whole to have a voice and wants to put on the table for the Board to have a conversation as to how, as a Board, they feel about all of issues over testing and if comfortable with scripting a letter to Albany voicing our concerns. Mr. Montgomery also feels that there doesn't seem to be any reason to administer the field test given the sentiment in the community and the negative experience for some children, therefore, doesn't seem a reason to administer them to our students. However, he did stated that he hasn’t followed the overall opt out issue closely and doesn't have a clear sense of all the issues but believes that assessments serve two purposes: student performance as well as provide scores for teachers and administrators' effectiveness, but need to ask how we use the assessments but also need to look at what other ways to assess our teachers/district. Mrs. Kashkin also agrees that field tests are of no value to the students or district and takes away time from the classroom and doesn't feel that they are worth it, both personally and as a Board member. She would like to see our legal counsel's opinion on the District and barring any regulations also feels we should opt out.  As per Mrs. Kerner's request to have a conversation and possibly contact Albany, she is in agreement and will discuss with Phil Whitney, perhaps joining with other District's to have a larger voice.

	5. Comments from the Community

	With regard to the field tests, a parent noted that it seems that other communities have taken a stand on the issue and that the number of districts will continue to dwindle who will administer the tests. He feels that it is time to stand up for students, not only parents, but also in the District's and the opt out numbers might indicate that the message to Albany is that enough is enough. With regard to the opt out, he doesn't believe the District shouldn't be reminding parents of the percentages as they are well aware of what is being said from Albany.

	The Board and administration was thanked for the correspondence to parents regarding the assessments. With regard to field testing, also feels that there is no value or useable data to the District of these tests and commented that is in favor of the Common Core but of issue is that the assessments do not test the children appropriately with what they have learned.

	A parent noted that her children in 4th and 6th grade opted out and that she is also a substitute saying that it is heartbreaking to see children who are not capable of taking the assessments struggle greatly and feels that the field tests are detrimental to struggling students.
