3. Discussion/Presentation

Presentation:  3.1 Introduction of the Irvington UFSD Program Assessment Model

Dr. Kor spoke about The Irvington UFSD Program Assessment Model…How Will We Know?

She said the conversation starting at the retreat in August and was developed with the thought it will become part of the Irvington culture.  Dr. Harrison and Dr. Kor used many resources to come up with the model that will be used. This model with be used both backwards and forwards.

Goals:

- Implement practices to elevate student thinking.

- Develop balanced assessments.

- Use data to inform instruction.

Dr. Kor said to get into the practice of looking at data and how data can inform them, Instructional Leaders took on a data project. The goal was to get everyone more comfortable wokring with and using data.

One way of assessing progress is the use standardized assessments such as NYS 3-8, NYS Regents and AP Exams are used to measure student progress.

Each year the District looks at standardized assessments and compares them from one year to the next and also compares to other like schools.

The Irvington UFSD model of Program Assessment will use the following framework and become a component of annual work beginning in 2018-19.

- Approach: What are the Questions?

- Implementation: What are the elements of an effective assessment plan?

- Results: What do we know now?

There was a discussion about Regents Exams and AP Exams and offerings.  There will be a further discussion regarding this topic in the Fall.

Presentation: 3.2 Update on Capital Projects

Carol Stein gave an update on the Capital Project Planning.  A Capital Project Planning Committee has been formed. The Capital Project is needed to address the needs of the District that cannot be met in the General Fund budget. She stated that in 2022-23 the District’s debt payments drop by $1.2 million, therefore, this Capital Project could be tax neutral.  Ms. Stein outlined the Project timelines one leading up to the community vote and another timeline after a successful community vote.

The Capital Project Planning Committee was formed from a cross section of stakeholders  (including B & G Committee members and representatives from each school) and the District’s strategic advisors; architects and engineers (H2M), construction management company (Triton Construction) and security consultant (Altaris Emergency Management Group) and technology (Edutek). The CPP Committee worked in 3 smaller groups and identified potential scope of work in the following areas: Safety and Security, Academic/Student/Staff Needs, Storage & Protection Resources Needs, Facility Maintenance/Infrastructure Needs, Energy Efficiency/HVAC Needs and Paving/Sidewalks Needs.  She said some of the smaller/standalone items identified by the CPCC may be taken care of in the regular budget.

She explained the new safety measures that are included in the 2018-19 budget will be done this Summer include a Visitor Management System, window numbering visible from the outside, safety  procedure flip charts in every room and radios for all safety team members and possibly add additional cameras and updating Safety/Go Bags. Still exploring the proximity door readers.  Ms. Stein thanked all the members of the Capital Planning Project Committee for their time and the effort put into developing this plan.
