P URPOSE OF THIS RTI P LAN


This Response to Intervention (RTI) document has been created as a framework for meeting the
needs of all students K-5 in the Irvington Union Free School District.
New York State Regulations define RTI as a school district's process to determine if a student
responds to scientific, research-based instruction (NYSEDRTI, 2006).

Response to Intervention (RTI) is the practice of providing high-quality instruction and
intervention closely matched to student needs (NASDSE, 2006). RTI uses scientific, research-
based curriculum to meet students’ needs. RTI ensures ALL children receive research-based
instruction and intervention when needed. RTI seeks to close achievement gaps for students at
risk, students with disabilities and English language learners by preventing smaller learning
problems from becoming significant learning gaps (NYSEDRTI, 2006).

General Education, Academic Support and Special Education teachers must monitor and
measure students’ responses to instruction and interventions. Only after direct and explicit
instruction, as well as opportunities for differentiated instruction and practice with research-
based curricula, may students be considered for special education evaluations.


   “To ensure that underachievement in a child suspected of having a specific learning
   disability is not due to lack of appropriate instruction in reading or math, the group must
   consider…data that demonstrate that prior to, or as a part of, the referral process, the
   child was provided appropriate instruction in regular education settings, delivered by
   qualified personnel; and data-based documentation of repeated assessments of
   achievement at reasonable intervals” (34 C.F.R. §300.309(b)).


General and special education teachers must monitor and measure students’ responses to
instruction and interventions. Only after direct and explicit instruction, as well as opportunities
for differentiated instruction and practice with research-based curricula, may students be
considered for referral to special education.

This RTI plan describes a three tiered process of meeting ALL students’ needs. This plan
outlines the staff, methods and materials that will be used in instruction and progress monitoring
at all stages of the framework. This RTI plan is a three tiered framework for providing students
with early interventions and support. It also provides for assured documentation of interventions
and progress for all students. This plan provides guidelines for research-based curriculum and
instructional practices to improve student achievement.

RTI IN I RVINGTON


All students will receive instruction on the Tier I level using a core instructional program of
scientifically-based curriculum. Students who receive Academic Intervention Services (AIS)
reading, math, occupational therapy, physical therapy, counseling and/or speech and language
therapy receive Tier II or Tier III support and instruction based on their needs. Students who
receive special education services are considered Tier III. Although, not all Tier III students are
classified under the Committee on Special Education (CSE).

A SSESSMENT


Data are used to identify academic and behavioral needs of individual students and design
instruction to meet student needs. Progress monitoring tools are used to evaluate the
effectiveness of instruction at all levels.

UNIVERSAL SCREENING

     Universal screenings are measures of important skills known to be strong indicators of
      student achievement. They are administered within the first two (2) weeks of school to
      all students as an initial baseline and help to identify students who do not meet grade
      level expectations. Screenings are administered by classroom teachers.
     Results are used as a starting point for instruction or as an indication for further
      evaluation.
     Any students entering the district after the Initial Screening Period (ISP) are administered
      the screening assessments no later than two weeks from entry date.
     In reading and math, data are collected at least four (4) times a year in grades K-5.
      Curriculum-Based Measures are used including the Fountas & Pinnell Benchmark
      Assessment System and Mad Minute Assessments.

PROGRESS MONITORING

     Progress monitoring assessments are used regularly throughout the school-year to
      evaluate student achievement towards grade level benchmarks.
     Data are collected, evaluated, and used to provide information on the effectiveness of
      instruction and to determine appropriate intervention(s).
     Assessment measures are administered by classroom teachers or members of the RTI
      Team which includes; Literacy and Math Specialists, Special Education Teachers and/or
      related service providers to measure student progress in designated areas.
     Progress monitoring results guide classroom teachers as well as the RTI Team in the
      determination of level of intervention (i.e., frequency, intensity and duration).
     In reading and math, progress monitoring data are collected on a regular basis. The level
      of intervention dictates the frequency of progress monitoring. Tools include Fountas &
      Pinnell, Wilson, Fuchs’ Word Identification, Mad Minute and Unit Assessments.

DIAGNOSTIC ASSESSMENTS

     Diagnostic Assessments are administered as part of a more targeted evaluation to
      problem identification and differentiation of instruction in specific areas of need.
     Results provide information about learning styles and/or behavioral needs to design
      appropriate interventions.
     These assessments are administered to students in Tier I, Tier II, or Tier III based on
      functioning. They are administered by specialized members of the RTI Team.
     In reading and math, the diagnostic assessment tools may include select subtests
      standardized instruments based on student needs.




                                                                                                 4

MANDATED ASSESSMENTS

      State Wide Assessments are group-administered tests used for school and/or state
       reporting purposes that provide feedback about student learning and the effectiveness of
       instructional programs. They are administered each year in grades 3, 4 and 5.
      Mandated Assessments are required components when a student is referred and/or
       classified under CSE.

PARENT NOTIFICATION

      Information about the RTI process is provided to all parents.
      When a student requires supplemental intervention, notification to parents includes all
       required information and is provided in a language the parent understands.
      Parents of students receiving any intervention beyond that of a general education class
       are informed of the right to request an evaluation for special education services.
      The nature and frequency of communication is defined clearly and ensures that parents
       are regularly informed of their child’s progress.

RTI Process

Tier I- Core Instruction

      Students are screened at least four (4) times per year (September, December, March,
       June) with the Fountas & Pinnell Benchmark Assessment System.
      Classroom interventions are provided by the general education teacher to those students
       who require additional support during core instruction.
      For students not achieving benchmarks, individual teachers consult with academic
       support teacher(s) in area(s) of need.
      Teachers can bring student data to regularly scheduled grade level meetings for
       discussion with their instructional colleagues.
      Benchmarks for instructional reading levels are based on local norms and criteria for each
       grade level.
      Assessment results are the primary data used to recommend consider additional
       intervention(s). Teacher input and student work samples are also considered.

Tier II- Supplemental Intervention

      Students are assessed at least four (4) times per year (September, December, March, and
       June) with the F& P BAS subtests.
      Interventions are provided outside of core instruction by classroom teachers, Academic
       support teachers and other staff.
      Students are assessed with CBMs and results are evaluated and used in an ongoing
       manner (at least every other week). CBM testing results are the primary data to move
       students between tiers.
      After a period of 4-6 weeks of Tier II intervention, progress is reviewed by the academic
       support and general education teachers who determine if a referral to the RTI Team is
       warranted at this time.
      Consideration of ongoing Tier II intervention is decided based on level of progress.

Tier III- Intensive Intervention

      Students are considered for Tier III intervention when there is evidence of insufficient
       progress. Benchmarks are used to determine need for continued support, close
       monitoring of progress, additional support and/or diagnostic evaluations.

      An integral component of the movement to Tier III is the RTI Team process. RTI Team
       convenes regarding individual students who have not responded to lesser levels of
       intervention. The RTI Team is multidisciplinary, comprised of teachers, support staff
       and administration.

                                  RTI Problem Solving Process



Problem Identification

      1. Define the primary and secondary area(s) of concern and describe them in measurable,
      observable terms.

      2. Describe the difference between the referred student and typical peers.

      3. Identify areas of strength that may contribute to future success and compensatory
      strategies.

Problem Analysis

      1. Generate hypotheses and possible contributions to the student’s difficulty including (a)
      the learner, (b) the environment, and (c) the curriculum.

      2. Discuss alternate hypotheses that are supported by Tier I universal screening and Tier
      II/III progress monitoring data.

      3. Collect additional data, if necessary, to confirm or reject hypotheses.

Intervention Plan Development

      1. Write measurable goal(s).

      2. Brainstorm possible ways to intervene to achieve goal(s).

      3. Select interventions (i.e., strategies, materials, and/or services) that are most likely to
      address the causes of the student’s problem, which are supported by research and the
      classroom teacher.

      4. Write an intervention and progress monitoring plan.

Plan Evaluation

      1. Review progress monitoring data.

      2. Determine success of intervention(s).

      3. Identify status of goal achievement.

      4. Make necessary revisions, if appropriate.

      5. Set next review date.

                              RTI Team Meeting Protocol



 Step 1: Summarize Referral Concerns – The referring teacher provides relevant
  background information, summarizes current events leading to the referral and the
  student’s response to interventions already implemented, as reflected by progress
  monitoring data.

 Step 2: Inventory Student Strengths – The referring teacher identifies the student’s
  strengths, talents, and interests.

 Step 3: Review Baseline Data – Information that has been gathered prior to the meeting
  is presented to assist the Team in understanding the referral concerns. Examples of data
  to review include universal screening results, curriculum based measures (i.e., progress
  monitoring results), Fountas & Pinnell reading levels, report cards, information from the
  student’s cumulative folder, qualitative observations, etc.

 Step 4: Identify Areas of Concern – No more than one or two areas of concern are stated
  in measurable and observable terms to have a focused discussion and increase the
  likelihood of a successful intervention plan.

 Step 5: Define Goals and Methods for Progress Monitoring – Specific goals are
  operationally defined that are expected to be achieved in 6 to 8 weeks when the RTI
  Team reconvenes as a follow-up meeting. Methods for monitoring the student’s response
  to intervention are selected. The Team decides who will be responsible for which
  intervention and how to collect data for the follow-up RTI Team meeting.

 Step 6: Develop an Intervention Plan – The RTI Team brainstorms strategies and
  interventions to address the referral concerns. Each RTI Team member has an
  opportunity to contribute an idea. The referring teacher selects the most viable
  suggestions. Any staff members supporting the student are listed along with relevant
  intervention.

 Step 7: Plan for Follow Up – The RTI Team Leader identifies a follow-up date to review
  the Intervention Plan (within ~6 to 8 weeks). The referring teacher, along with relevant
  staff, agrees to inform the parent(s)/guardian(s) about the RTI Intervention Plan. A copy
  of the Plan may be shared at the teacher’s discretion.
