6. Discussions/Presentations

Information: 6.1 Grades 6-12 End of Year Update

Dr. Harrison spoke of the efforts to align a K-12 continuum, the professional learning taking place as well as program growth and expansion taking place in the District and that all work is aligned with the strategic objectives that  Dr. Kor addressed the overarching goals, the work being done to accomplishment the goals, looking at the level of accountability. The goal to impart to students the tools for a well-developed brain where they are engaged in deep thinking and metacognition. She spoke to the development of assessments which is a multi-year goal, to ensure that the assessments will provide an accurate look at students' learning, skills and thinking. Tonight's presentation will assess the approach, implementation and results to the high school and middle school's building goals. David Sottile, Principal of IMS, and Allyson Daley, Asst. principal, spoke of the work taking place at IMS. Mr. Sottile spoke of the sustained commitment to the goals and how learning is enhanced by that continuity with the focus on getting students to be deeper, thinking students. Student work was shared that iillustrates that students are engaged in critical thought, inquiry and practices that meet that goal. Essential Questions in math, ELA, science were shared to convey the classroom discussions to reach the goal of higher/deeper thinking.

Mr. Sottile spoke to QFT (Questioning Formulation Technique) to promote thinking which has been a topic of discussion at faculty meetings. QFT is learning task that has been introduced to staff across all content, and idea is to start generating questions and as a group, refine the questions, what new questions are generated, and is a momentum building process to engage students and have them become invested in their learning. The practice results in a priority question, which then allows each student to explore a topic of their choice, making the course more meaningful. Other learning practices were shared in ELA, such as dialectical loop writing.

David Sottile provided an overview of Goal 2 - Develop balanced assessment - with a variety of assessments for the purpose of generating different types of information. Ms. Daley shared types of assessments currently used at IMS, including pre-assessments to identify skill and knowledge that students already possess-content knowledge- and helps teachers to plan their lessons more accurately; on-demand writing to assess students' writing level to better identify and teach to the writing goal in the different types of writing, i.e argumentation, persuasive, etc. Students are also able to identify writing goals to improve their writing. It was noted that there is power in these types of assessments to serve as benchmarks. Other examples of assessments were shared, including those that utilize Google Forms with instanetous student performance, use of exit tickets, etc. The summative assessment tasks reflect the assessment of higher level thinking, the big ideas of a particular area of learning, such as in social studies, enduring issues, etc. It requires students to apply all their prior learning and requires a higher level of thinking. This will be the first year that this assessment will be utilized. Assessments in world language are ongoing and used to identify a student's level of proficiency with the expectation that students be at a novice high level in speaking by the end of 7th grade. Mr. Sottile noted that students in 93% of students in grades 7 and 8th have meet or exceeded expectations with 78% exceeding expectations. He then shared several teachers' reflections on the work that is being undertaken and are saying that the reflection of teaching and assessments, they are seeing results and are excited the work being undertaken.

Following the IMS presentation, members of the Board had the opportunity to ask questions and commented on the presentation and were curious as to what processes are used to determine the rigor of the curriculum, and when will the Board see further linkage between curriculum and assessment such as being done in World Language. David and Allyson were thanked for an excellent presentation.

A community member asked about the content of the World Language program at the middle school, such as using Google Classroom to connect with students in other countries and to expose them to different cultures. Also commented on the use of water bottles by the Board. Google Voice is used as well as connections with students who are living at Abbott House who are Spanish-speaking students.

Dave Cohen and Matt Samuelson spoke of the great work taking place at the high school this year. The focus was on the curriculum at the high school vis a vis the goals and instructional practices, essential questions, closing out questions, Google Classroom, etc. The many opportunities that are created to allow students to be engaged in their learning and the results are in higher level of thinking and develop their level of understanding over time were highlighted. Mr. Cohen spoke of the building blocks of learning through the K-12 curriculum. Samples of practices used at the high school were provided including Essential Questions and open ended group work as well as the Interactive Science Notebook. Examples of learning strategies were shared such as open ended problem solving to explore students' conceptual understanding of trigonometry, using prediction strategies to make a plan on how to solve a particular math problem through an open ended questions. The use of Google Classroom is used to create a digital meeting place for sharing assignments, work, ideas, collaboration and organization which results in greater student engagement, teacher support and peer feedback and improved executive functioning through Google Calendar. Examples were share on students working towards meeting their Daily Learning Targets in their World Language class. Matt Samuelson spoke to balanced assessments at the high school to assess for the knowledge and skills especially as they take the Regents with the goal to create engaging assessments and shared an example of a summative assessment in Global History, use of discovery to construct knowledge and demonstrate their knowledge. Examples of formative performances-based assessment were also provided in the area of world language and in an English class which can provide immediate feedback to the students and demonstration of their knowledge of the content.

Matt Samuelson addressed the work at the high school with respect to the RTI referral process and to make linkage to need and appropriate interventions to best support the student's needs. The needs are aligned with research-based strategies that can be implemented, tracked, revised and reviewed over time.

The Board had the opportunity to ask clarifying questions, suggestions and commented on elements of the presentation.
