	3. Superintendent's Report

		Dr. Harrison reported:

-
			Irvington Girls' Basketball Coach, Gina Maher, has been selected as 2013 Russell Athletic National Women's Basketball Coach of the Year and will be an assistant coach at the WBCA HS All-America Game in New Orleans on April 6th.

-
			The Girls' Basketball team is playing in the Regional Semi-finals this evening against Coleman Catholic.

-
			Dows Lane and MSS students will be participating in Worlds Math Day on March 6th. Students play math fact games against other children in their age group from around the world. Several DL and MSS students appeared in the top 100 with second grader Miles Demarest reaching number 1 in his age group at a point in the competition.

-
			Westchester Magazine ran their annual feature of local high school profiles and unfortunately, Irvington was not included as a result of not receiving the original request. Contact has been made with the publisher to have Irvington's profile included in the online version of the magazine.

-
			With regard to the HS principal search, a representative committee interviewed six candidates on Friday, March 1st and identified three candidates to participate in the next round which will include another personal interview, teacher observations and a clinical exercise next week. Process is still on track to make recommendation to the Board in April. (Mrs. Kerner thanked the high school students, Toby Schwed and Madde DeRose, for participating on the committee and noted that their contributions were invaluable).

-
			Upcoming events include the High School spring musical, Damn Yankee, which opens on Friday the 8th with shows on the 9th, 15th and 16th at 7:30 pm and a 2:00 pm show on March 10th.

-
			On Friday, March 15th, the IEF will sponsor MSS Brain Challenge at 7:00 pm.

-
			Dr. Harrison reported that the space utilization study being conducted by Dr. Paul Seversky is nearly complete and will likely include considerations that focus on  excess building capacity and projected declining enrollment which might lead to discussions on how to use district building assets going forward. A possible consideration might include the possibility of closing Main Street School. Given that there must be a community discussion regarding space utilization, the MSS principal search will be suspended until such time that there can been in depth conversations within the community. Also, there is an ethical obligation in hiring and the pool of candidates might also be diminished if there is a possibility of the school closing.

		Discussion:  5.1 Workers' Compensation Third Party Administration

		Following the recommendation for the District to become self-insured for Workers' Compensation, John and Tina Penuci of Benetech, provided an overview for the Board regarding workers' compensation self-insurance. Benetech currently represents 62 schools in claims administration with over 23 years of experience. Mr. Penuci noted that 90% school districts do self-insure.

		and provided an overview of what self insurance workers compensation entrails. Costs are predicated on claims. Two components to look at: actual expenses? and 2) What is exposure? There are risks and exposures but school districts generally have low exposure when compared to other businesses.  Also highlighted, is that district is always in control of funds, including investing of funds. The level of reserves for Irvington will be determined by an acctuarial accounting, approximately $350,000. Any transfers from a reserve fund would require approval by the Board and also reviewed annually by the external auditors.

		Resolution: WHEREAS, the Board of Education of the Irvington Union Free School District administers the Irvington Union Free School District; and WHEREAS, Dory Baldwin, hereinafter referred to as the “Donor” desires to make a gift to the Board of Education in order to create a scholarship fund, to be known as the “Jessie Ian Scholarship Fund”; and WHEREAS Education Law §§ 1709(12-a) and 2503(1) authorizes the Board of Education: [t]o take and hold in trust for the purpose of awarding scholarships in [the] schools any real estate transferred to it by gift, grant, bequest or devise, or any gift, legacy or annuity, of whatever kind, given or bequeathed to [the] board and apply the same, or the interest and proceeds thereof, according to the instructions of the donor or testator. NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED, that the Board of Education of the Irvington Union Free School District hereby creates the above-named Scholarship Fund, as set forth below, based upon the understanding between the parties: The Jessie Ian Scholarship Fund 1. The Donor shall deliver to the Board, for deposit in the trust and agency account in the bank designated by the Board of Education the sum of one thousand dollars ($1,000) each year for a period of three (3) years, i.e. three thousand dollars ($3,000) in total, which the Donor hereby gives to the Board forever and unconditionally, except as hereinafter set forth. 2. The scholarship shall be named the Jesse Ian Scholarship Fund. 3. The scholarship shall be awarded annually in the amount of one thousand dollars ($1,000.00) to a graduating senior with a demonstrated career interest in nursing. In the event there are no graduating seniors meeting these criteria in a given year, the scholarship shall be carried over until such time as a qualified candidate exists. Notwithstanding the foregoing, in no event shall the annual scholarship exceed one thousand dollars ($1,000.00). 4. The funds allocated to said scholarship shall come from such income or interest as is accumulated in a particular year plus such invasion of the principal as is necessary to issue said scholarship. 5. The details of the organization and administration of the Scholarship Fund will be left to the discretion of the Board of Education.

	11. Presentations/Discussions

		Presentation:  11.1 Superintendent's 2013-14 Proposed Budget (Presentation)

		Dr. Harrison began the presentation by noting that there have been five previous presentations to provide fiscal transparency. As a district, must think about where we stand, what we want for the future, and want to serve students to best of ability and at end of day each student can achieve their personal best. The proposed budget does not recommend the elimination of any current programs or current staff members, invests in facilities, supports growth of staff, expands technological opportunities and balances fiscal responsibility and educational quality.

		Ms. Miller reviewed the projected 2013-14 revenues, noting that majority of revenue comes through property tax. Total expenditures proposed would results in a 5.13% increase, mostly derived from salaries and other. 73% of salaries are directly tied to classrooms. Irvington's reserve funds included a tax certiorari reserve in the beginning of the year was $1,936,742 and the balance as of January 31st was $128,833. The unreserved and undesignated is $2,046,240 for unintended expenses and is at the maximum allowed by the State.

		The Superintendent's proposed budget is $53,781,582 or a 5.13% increase over the current budget and does include the financing of bonds to pay settled tax certioraries. There have been some efficiencies and reductions to reduce the budget over tax cap by $1,149,039.

		The costs beyond the Board's control were again highlighted, i.e. pension contributions, health insurance, tax certs and special education services. Also reviewed was the tax cap calculation formula---Irvington's allowable tax levy increase within the tax cap is 2.62,and could fluctuate with any changes in staffing. Enrollment growth does not get factored into the calculation.

		In response to the Board'srequest to present what the budget would look like to have the budget come in at 3.95%, Dr. Harrison presented Level 2 Reductions and their consequences. Some reductions included JV field hockey and JV lacrosse, eliminate additional sections of .2 FTE and .2 FTE social studies, totaling a savings of $606,164 but still $534,067 over the tax cap with a tax rate increase of 3.0%. Many of the reductions could impact class size, modified sports, etc.

		Additionally, if it would be the Board's goal to reach cap, Level three reductions would be an additional $560,033 and would bring the budget to $1,506.00under tax cap or a 1.82% tax rate increase. To achieve this, reductions include, but are not limited to, the elimination of JV sports, the HS architectural program, elimination of one librarian, a .50 teacher leaders at Dows Lane, loss of co-curricular opportunities, increased class size at MSS, loss of personalized learning experiences.

		Ms. Miller provide scenarios and examples of what tax rates would be for specific home assessed values. Ms. Miller provided a five year budget trend analysis, showing that the five year average in tax increase of 2.45%. Also the taxable assessments have been on the decline, however there has been a very small increase this year, which helps contain the tax rate.

		In summation, the proposed budget is a commitment to fiscal prudence, addresses unfunded mandates and preserves Irvington's commitment to a high-quality education for every student. Dr Harrison thanked the Board for their consideration of the proposed budget.

		Mr. Whitney asked if possible to rank what the 29% Other is comprised of - $352,000 - to see what the top 5 or ten areas that could be eliminated. With regard to the maintenance position, would there be savings made in other areas and show as an offset to savings o elsewhere. Ms. Miller noted that it would vary from year to year and difficult to specify what the offset expenses would be.

		Maria Kashkin asked about one of the two requested new special education position that is still in the proposed.

		Robyn Kerner asked what the tax rate would be if you looked at the actual budget for the 2012-13 school year. Ms. Miller noted that the actual figures will be under last year's budget amount but cannot calculate until the end of the school year.

		With regard to being self insured, Ms. Miller reduced the budget by $200,000.
