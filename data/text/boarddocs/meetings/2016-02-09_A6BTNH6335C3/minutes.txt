	5. Presentations/Discussions (Attachment)

	Information, Presentation: 5.1 2016-17 Push Ahead Budget/Reality of the Budget Landscape

	Mr. Whitney reported that the 2016-17 budget development will be very challenging due to the low inflation rate in the country and despite the District having exercised fiscal restraint over the past couple of years, it will be a difficult budget year.

	Dr. Harrison reported that the District is in a difficult position with budgetary decisions made made over the next several months. The District is losing local control as Albany imposes mandates and constraints that are impacting the District’s ability to fulfill obligations to all students while maintaining fiscal constraint. Irvington has lost approximately $2.5 in state aid, with GEA being restored over two years, so that only $68,000 will be realized from the $2M owed. The Tax Cap is also hindering the ability to meet the commitment to all students. Dr. Harrison reviewed previous reductions to the budget and noted that in 2013-14, the Board and community overrode the tax cap but that the budget has come under the cap the last two years which shows fiscal restraint. He stressed that the tax cap is not 2% but is directly linked to the CPI, which is only .12% this year. Another negative factor is that tax base growth, is 0% this year, meaning no new properties have come online to help grow the base which would help to increase the tax cap rate.

	Ms. Miller provided the tax cap calculation, again noting that the CPI is .12%, which equates to a $58,503 allowable growth for the budget. Also due to changes in PILOTS and capital debt, the tax cap will be $37,080 less than last year. If the tax cap was a flat 2%, we would realize approximate $1.055 increase. Dr. Harrison also noted that if the budget were to fail twice, the budget would actually be increased as it reverts to the previous year's tax levy, as mandated by SED Law.

	Also noted as that a push ahead budget does not include any new initiatives for next year, but maintains what is in place currently and is calculated to be $1,056,213 over tax cap and does not reflect contract settlements for the three bargaining units that will be negotiated as they are expiring. The numbers are estimates and in flux and based on known data at this time.

	An overview of next year's expenditures was reviewed and included factors such as:

-
		negotiated contracts (only one at this time) and step increases;

-
		fringe benefits;

-
		instructional programs; and

-
		Transportation, BOCES admin, buildings and grounds.

	Potential considerations to stay within the cap, for illustrative purposes only, include:

-
		Personnel:

-
		bus monitors;

-
		K-1 aides;

-
		interscholastic athletics (all sports)

-
		painting and repairs and

-
		other non-staff items such as technology, professional development and supplies; and

-
		increase use of fund balance

	Use of the fund balance would also be necessary to reach the reduction of $1,056,213, which the state allows 4% to be retained and is the difference between expenses and revenue at the end of the year and can be used to cover unbudgeted emergency necessities, cash flow between July 1 and October to pay expenses until the tax revenue is received. However, a low fund balance could result in a lower bond rating, and could lead to a "Fiscal Stress" designation by NYS. Ms. Miller illustrated the results should the fund balance is used this year over the next three years at which time the fund balance would be at $0 leaving no cushion for unexpected expenses and would require staff layoffs to meet our obligations or taking on debt. Dr. Harrison stressed that use of the fund balance needs to be carefully considered due to the ramifications in future years. The Board discussed the pros and cons of cutting into the fund balance and the possible future impacts to the District. The current situation does not allow for needs and goals of the district to grow programs and enhance opportunities for students and to maintain the infastructure of the District. So the question is "How can we manage this in the current tax cap climate?” Many conversations and decisions will need to be made, such as presenting a budget under the cap, or if going over the cap, how much would be acceptable. The next steps will include further component budget reviews at the next two Board meetings, identify savings, and encourage community engagement.

	Mr. Whitney reiterated that community input is needed in the process as the Board considers difficult options, either to spend more than Albany allows under the tax cap formula and will require 60% voter approval, cut expenses, or a combination of cutting expenses and using the fund balance, which translates to borrowing on the future. Making any decisions tonight would be premature at this time. Asked if it is known what the situation is for surrounding districts, Ms. Miller noted that she is aware that Hastings will be using their fund balance to stay under the cap, but that probably would need to exceed the cap the following year. However, Dr. Harrison noted that each district has different issues, perhaps more layers to reduce staff but he couldn't say at this time. Mr. Whitney noted that we've used $422,000 from the fund balance previously and wondered if there is another figure that would be comfortable, but Ms. Miller is hesitant to increase beyond that next year.

	Board members reacted to the information presented and noted that significant tax certs, especially along the Route 119 corridor, affect Irvington. It was noted that the tax cert account will have a deficit of $600,000 after negotiated settlements are paid. Mr. Hanna believes that there is a commitment to recover from the previous cuts and he would not like to lose the progress the District has made in restoring staff and programs, but also don't want to add a fiscal burden to residents. Mr. Montgomery would like a history (five years) of the year end surplus used to fund the tax cert account and/or the fund balance and the amount of claims that have yet to be settled and how they might be paid for to ensure that proper provisions are being made to meet these obligations. Ms. Miller will contact the district's attorney who handles the tax certioraris for that information. Mr. Montgomery agrees with Mr. Hanna to not move backwards but also need to be mindful of costs, due to real expenses in personnel and might need to take a few hundred thousand cut equaling several positions as well as go over tax cap by a little bit.

	Mr. Grados believes this is the same discussion over the past several years and when the Board went to community to override the tax cap, it was explained that in order for the schools to progress, it was necessary to exceed the cap but that once a certain level was reached, we would stay under the cap and for the last two years stayed under the cap, but stressed that if the tax cap was indeed a 2% cap, the budget could be kept under the cap and would continue to follow through on commitment made to community. He believes that the majority of community wants fiscal prudence and maintain level of educational quality. Being faced with the almost 0% tax cap will necessitate going over the cap as the community does not want to lose bus monitors, aids, or reductions in athletics. He also believes it must be a cooperative effort between the Board and administration to find savings but also ask the employees to assist as well. He would like to use the same amount of the fund balance as in the past and suggests that the message from the Board to administration is to continue to find cuts, address areas in line items that goes unspent to draw savings and whittle down the budget, but present the budget that it feels is best for the District, at which time it is the Board's decision whether it makes sense to start conversations with employee units and the community if there is a desire to go over the cap. He also knows that families moving in are moving in for the schools and again, asks the question, “what do we want to look like (as a district)?” Mr. Montgomery noted that by tightening up on the budget, the result will be less to be put in the fund balance in future years.

	Mrs. Palmieri is in favor of going over the tax cap, by listening to staff and students in the strategic planning process as to their aspirations, she believes the community wants more from their schools. With the IEF holding fundraisers to provide opportunities this tells her that the community wants more. As the district has begun to be set on a path to success and is on the right trajectory, should not look at cuts, as that would be going in the wrong direction, and will work actively to rally support for a tax cap override. As for using the fund balance, it is her opinion to use fund balance but agreed that a closer look is needed.

	Mr. Whitney believes there is consensus amongst the Board for the administration to show some fiscal prudence, look for savings, but balance prudent cuts with prudent use of fund balance, with an eye to not staying under the tax cap. He believes that the fund balance issue should be addressed sooner rather than later. Mr. Grados believes that after looking at the history of the fund balance, the Board might be able to identify a number that they are comfortable with to help cushion the impact on the bottom line of the budget. Mr. Montgomery noted that there has never been a conversation as to what amount the fund balance should be.
