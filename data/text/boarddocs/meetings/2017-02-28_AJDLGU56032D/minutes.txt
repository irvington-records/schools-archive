4. Administrators' Update

Dr. Harrison read the following statement

In public education, our business seems to be more and more linked with politics with each passing day.  We are accustomed to political policy influencing school funding and curriculum, but now we have entered a new and more complicated era.

​As we watch the evening news and read new​s websites, we are watching a political debate regarding how schools should meet the needs of our transgender youth or how immigrants should be treated.  In the Irvington schools, we are not going to engage in politics or the debate.  We are going to support our students.  With this said,​ we ​will continue to support our students who have gone through a gender identity shift or are in the midst of the process.

If we have students enrolled or those who may enroll in our schools that are from another country, we will not question citizenship or immigration status.  In fact, we will follow New York State guidance that requires us to not ask questions on this topic.

In Irvington we are about our students, not about where they were born or what their gender identity might be.  Regardless of politics, this is the Irvington school family.  We will continue to welcome and support all our students and their families.  We will focus on education and social-emotional develop​ment​ and leave the politics to Washington, DC and Albany.

Dr. Harrison also highlighted that MSS physical education teacher, Laura Shaw, was appointed to the Executive Council of the NY State Association for Health, Physical Education, Recreation and Dance, Inc. IMS teacher, Caroline Knudsen, IMS teacher, passed her Level 1 Google for Education Certified Educator Exam.

Dr. Kor was featured in a video produced by Rubicon Atlas highlighting the work that she has led in Irvington which recognizes the outstanding work in Irvington and the great curriculum leadership. The video can be viewed on the District’s Facebook page and website.

Asst. Supt. for Business, Carol Stein, reported the following:

The Ford F350 truck that was ordered in the fall, was finally received today.

Field maintenance will begin in a few weeks to continue with our program of maintaining the fields with proper fertilization and seeding.

6. Presentations/Discussions

Discussion: 6.1 Website Design Update

Dr. Harrison provided an overview of the process to develop a new website for the district as well as to create an identity for the district. Phil Whitney, a member of the Communications Committee, spoke of the district’s history of the many images, most notably the bulldog as the icon to identify Irvington, noting that not everyone is happy with the fierce bulldog which is often used as well as the letter "I" and is not easy to use as an identifier. Both the functionality as well as the design became the focus of the Communications Committee. Mr. Hanna, also a member of the Communications Committee, noted that the District's Facebook page is successful in getting stories out to the stakeholders about student and staff successes but that the website is not user friendly. The goal of a new website is to be sure that people going to the website have a good experience. Drew Coburn and Chuck Rothier have been consulting with the committee to help design the website as well as how best to illustrate the "Irvingtonness" of the district which is about the community. Mr. Coburn shared the feedback from the five focus groups that were conducted nothing that it was clear that there is an emotional aspect to the closeness of the community and the connectedness of the community and the district. Mr. Rothier spoke to the key to branding an identity and with the goal to bring that connectedness of district/community into a design. He provided two options that visually illustrate the District's identify, one a chevron-type design and the other a crest, provides white space that denotes "possibility". This option would be able to house different marks for athletics, arts, etc. which would also provide flexibility over time.

Dave Graeber favored the second option which denoted possibility, the flexibility, the blue. Ms. Palmieri noted that the first option reminds her of a tie and agreed that option 2 was preferable, especially with another color being introduced. She commented on the font and suggested that the use of rounder fonts is warmer.

Presentation: 6.2 2017-18 Budget Presentation: District Operations: Business Office B&G, Transportation and Debt Service (Presentation)

Dr. Harrison provided an overview of the objectives in developing a budget which include ensuring the fiscal health of the district, to provide students with a high quality learning environment, as well as being mindful of the impact of budgetary decisions on the entire community. Carol Stein provided an overview of the budget components in the area of the Board of Education, transportation, District and Business Offices, facilities and operations, public communications, administrative technology, debt service and insurance. The presentation illustrated the line item amounts in the 2016-17 budget along with those proposed for 2017-18 budget, showing the variances between the two and provided explanations for variances. She noted that a major shift being made to budgeting is that health waivers have been moved to the benefits code to ensure that the comparison in lines is apples to apples. She provided an overview of each category. With respect to the facilities operations and maintenance budget, Ms. Stein reported a 3.9% increase, in part due to the waiver re-coding, the addition of a security guard to circulate around the campus, increase in electric costs and a consideration to purchase a program to help with scheduling of district facilities. There are no proposed increases to building and capital repair budget lines, however, a list of priorities will continue to b reviewed. She called to the Board's attention a list totaling $448,435 in projects that are not included in the budget as they have fallen into a lower priority and $4,695,300 in capital repairs but the needs will need to be addressed at some time. The cost of providing new infill for Meszaros Field is only an estimate at $5,000. Ms. Stein provided items identified in the area of equipment, noting that each year certain equipment needs to be replaced. Dr. Harrison pointed out that he believes that it will be necessary following the budget vote for the Board to consider holding a referendum to a bond to address the many projects needed to ensure our facilities are maintained appropriately.

Special items were reviewed which include insurance, sewer tax, BOCES admin fee and BOCES capital fee, representing a .4% increase. There is a 4.5% increase in the proposed transportation budget with an increase in transportation to private/special education, coordination and in-district transportation. She reviewed what the cost of providing four new buses ($318,200) to accommodate late pickups, later morning pick up times for HS and improve afternoon start times at the HS/MS as well as reduce overcrowding on MSS runs. Dr. Harrison provided survey data on start times of schools in the region, which shows Irvington’s start times are consistent across the region. Other options as well as implications were discussed including having HS/MS start at the same time, or proposing reducing the mileage limitations.

The total change in the budget areas discussed in the presentation amounts to $255,660 or a 1.90% budget to budget variance. Dr. Harrison reminds the Board that this is part of the larger lens and will need to look at the budget in its entirety once all components are addressed and to determine the tax cap levy.

The Board asked questions on the information presented, including the rational for having an additional security guard to which Dr. Harrison addressed what t he additional personnel would provide. There was further discussion on the transportation issue, especially pertaining to the early pick up times for high school students. There seemed to be consensus among the Board members that there must be a solution. Mrs. Palmieri believes that a committee would be a good option as she feels that the product from last year's consultant was disappointing. She would also like to look into late busing options, such as running a shuttle. Dr. Harrison feels that a new set of eyes on the situation would be helpful but believes that the cost of $15,000 could be very conservative. Mr. Hanna wondered if there would be value of talking to other school officials about moving start times.Mrs. Palmieri suggested the installation of Nest cameras around the Disrict as a security measure.
