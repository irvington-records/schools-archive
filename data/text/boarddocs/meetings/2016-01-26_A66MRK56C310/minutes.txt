	4. Presentations/Discussions

	Information: 4.1 Roundtable Discussion: School-Based Needs

	Prior to the discussion, Dr. Harrison reported that last year there was opportunity to have healthy growth within the tax cap, however, this year’s situation is quite different due to the tax cap. Given a CPI of .12% and a tax base growth factor of 0% this year, it translates into a very difficult budget year. The discussion tonight will have a different tone then last year as there will be constraints during the budget discussions, with possible reductions as well as programmatic changes to meet the challenges. The meeting on February 9th might outline the challenges the District is facing and begins the budget development process. He also stated that the District is becoming solidified and noted that no school in the District operates in isolation, with a consistent focus on building a solid foundation for all learners. The administrators joined the Board and discussed programs at each building, beginning with Fundations at Dows Lane. Ms. Mariniello and Ms. Benedetto explained that the program has been expanded to Grade 2, which provides cohesive language components, decoding, attacking multisyllabic words, with increasing word skills that follow them through the grades. Ms. Marieniello noted that the program sets students on the path to attacking complex texts. The goal is also to spark the students’ enthusiasm for learning, with incorporating educational technology to work in a 21st Century world. Also at Dows Lane, PTLW has been launched in 3rd grade and hope to keep expanding the program to show students what it means to take their learning to the next level. They spoke to the emphasis on building a school community through character building activities, accountable talk, Habits of Mind.  As these programs involved the psychologists, Mr. Whitney asked if there is capacity of psychologists to build this program and what is needed to build the technology program. Ms. Mariniello did admit that staff is stretched but the staff does want to do this work and hope to build capacity. With respect to technology capacity, the teacher and the way students access technology has changed to ensure that all students are exposed to lab time. Dr. Kor did note that without additional technology staff, PTLW could not be expanded to other grades at the elementary level. Mr. Montgomery would like a more informed discussion about what exactly the program would look like as there might be other areas that might want to be expanded, i.e. world language program especially given budgetary constraints. There was discussion regarding the STEM program and how it is delivered in grades K-12 through sequences. Budgetary requests would be to increase a half-time psychologist, which would allow each to be more present in their own building to allow for more efficiency. Dr. Harrison also noted that there is need for increase in emotional support in all schools to address students' needs that seem to be growing, not only in Irvington but countrywide.

	Ms. Chapnick, Principal of Main Street School, shared some of the work that stems from the Board's budget support last year, noting that there are commonalities with Dow Lane, beginning with Project Lead The Way which helps students to learn to problem solve, work collaboratively with peers, and expands on many aspects what is being learned in the classroom, allowing students to put the lessons into action. Work will need to be done to investigate what the next generation of science standards and how to incorporate them into the program and identify what should be taught in the classroom vs in technology classes. Response to Intervention (RtI) was also expanded through the budget this year, and learning has been provided to teachers to provide level one intervention, providing greater flexibility in delivering support as need. Ms. Chapnick spoke to how character building is at the heart of all that takes place at MSS with Habits of Mind being taught explicitly, in all classes, cafeteria, etc. Global citizenry is also folded into character building with projects connecting students via technology, such as using Google Hangout to connect beyond Irvington as outlined in Goal 7 of the Technology Plan. The social worker that was supported by this year's budget, has been a tremendous asset to the District, connecting with families and making for great experiences for students. MSS will look to expand club monies, such as expanding a mindful garden and Destination Imagination. Further expansion of support of the psychologist and technology teacher is also a need at MSS. Mr. Montgomery asked about any enrichments being provided to students. Ms. Chapnick explained that at MSS, teachers differentiate instruction for struggling students and those who would benefit from enrichment vis a vis the modules and by other means, such as PLTW.

	Mr. Sottile, MS principal, again spoke to a focus on the whole child across all dimensions, emotional, social, and academic. Mr. Sottile noted that the involvement of library media center  has opened eyes as to what the library can be to learning and the importance of literacy across all disciplines. This is an area that is hoped to be grown and seems to be understaffed to allow for exciting opportunities. Ms. Daley spoke to the impact of the new library media specialist deeper involvement, with working teachers and students to match resources to needs, such as providing an array of appropriate texts would be appropriate to a student's level on a particular subject. The expansion of exposing students to current events has been a focus with the library media specialist  as well by providing instruction to students on how go about finding resources and is working on building relationships with students.. They believe that another library media specialist would allow for further push-in of her expertise into classrooms while also covering the library. Dr. Kor noted that when the middle school was built, it was the intention to have a second specialist to serve middle school students. Another request that potentially has a campus wide benefit would be in the area of a study skills program which would fall under a library media specialist. The additional staff member would also free the technology teacher who currently teaches study skills. Another budget request is to provide a late bus run to allow for students to stay after school to take advantage of clubs or new opportunities that might be offered, noting that there had been a late bus run but was eliminated from the budget six years ago. The model was to have a bus to serve the southern end of the district and another to serve the northern end of the district. This would also be shared with high school students.

	Mr. Cohen, HS principal, spoke to the journey the students take in Irvington, K-12, with conversation on how to maximize the high school experience. Last year, the asks were around infrastructure such as additional clerical staff, noting the addition has had a profound impact on stabilizing the office to allow to administrators to spend more time with teachers, more time to communicate with families, allow for more meetings, and a calm stability at the high school. Steve Corbett, Asst. Principal, spoke to the addition of the .5 psychologist,  provides crises intervention, testing, outreach to parents, but more importantly, creating partnerships in the school community, helping make the teachers and counselors more attuned to needs of students, leading to greater access and capacity to serve students when most needed. Focus has been on collaboration and using common language. Courses have been added to the PLTW program to create sequences and are asking to add a .2 teaching position which will give students more choice and flexibility in scheduling. Flexibility also allows for maintaining class sizes. Another ask is to add Chromebooks, 10 tubs of 5 Chromebooks each, which make them more accessible, to allow for more collaboration and access to expand utilization of programs such as Google Apps, Google Classroom, etc.

	Mr. Montgomery noted that it will be important to differentiate requests of large budget costs, staffing, from smaller costs, such as Chromebooks. Mr. Whitney spoke to the previous years' conversations on expanding world language into the lower grades, even given budgetary constraints, and asked if possible to put together a proposal that might get the community to rally around supporting a budget that goes over the tax cap. Dr. Kor spoke to the implications of a world language program at MSS along with PLTW, noting that there isn't enough time in a day but they are looking at options to present to the Board but it is a very complex issue.

	Mr. Montgomery noted that through last year’s budget, staff was not added to core academics and he is not seeing resources devoted to core academics again, especially at the high school, such as offering physics, expanded world language, more honors level courses and wonders about an unbalance in academics. Mr. Palermo spoke to how support staff might reduce barriers to students thereby allowing them to access academics more successfully. Mr. Hanna also expressed his concerns of having ample time to complete the work of curriculum design, curriculum mapping, etc. Dr. Kor believes there is overlap but that also different teachers are being tapped for the work, but acknowledged that teachers have a lot on their plates, but that seems to be the way of the world, but it is an area that is monitored to be comprehensive and thoughtful as to what is being done. Dr. Harrison concluded that over the past three or four years much work has been accomplished and programs added and want to continue to offer more opportunities, but must also stop and look at the good things that have been done over a short time and should be proud of what has been accomplished. He did note that capacity issues are a concern but need to look to ensure the quality of the instruction being provided. He again reiterated that it will be a difficult budget season, but the challenges will be outlined at the February 9th meeting as to what might need to be done.

	5. Comments from the Community

	A community member believes that the Board needs to take up the issue of getting rid of football, referring to articles written by doctors regarding brain injuries from football. He noted that new awareness over injuries, concussions and the possibility of the school district being sued. Mr. Whitney acknowledged the discussions taking place and that it makes sense to engage the community in order to hear from all sides. Mr. Montgomery would like fact finding on the risks and whether football is a little or a lot more risky relative to other sports. Dr. Harrison and administrators will need to determine how to go about gathering the facts before discussing it further. Mr. Montgomery believes the Board would be remiss not to look further into it.
