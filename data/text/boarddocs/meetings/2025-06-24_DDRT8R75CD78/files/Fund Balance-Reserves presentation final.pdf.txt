Categories of Fund Balance
Fund Balance

• Unassigned Fund Balance
  o   Recommended and maximum amount is 4% of next year’s budget

• Appropriated Fund Balance
  o Funds designated for revenue for next year’s budget
  o Approved in budget process


• Reserve for Encumbrances
  o Incomplete orders at year-end that carry over to next year’s budget
  o Amounts fluctuate from year-to-year


• Reserve Funds
  o Restricted for specific purposes
  o Funds assist with long range financial planning, managing
    liabilities and future budgetary “surprises”

Why Have Unassigned Fund Balance?

• Office of State Comptroller/SED allows up to 4% of the next year’s
  budget as unassigned funds and recommends 4% as a measure of
  fiscal health to avoid fiscal stress

• Previously larger levels were allowed – municipalities are not
  restricted to 4%

• Cash flow – avoid borrowing for Tax Anticipation Notes (T.A.N.) and
  resulting interest costs to fund operations until tax funds arrive in
  October and February

• Strong financial health leads to a better credit rating typically
  resulting in a lower borrowing rate for future capital projects

• Ability to weather any potential storms and manage fluctuations in
  key drivers such as pension match rates or insurance premium
  increases

Why Have Reserves?

• Used for long range planning – known costs
  o   Worker’s Comp tail claim payments
  o   Tax Certiorari refund payments
  o   Legal settlements
  o   Contractual payments to retirees

• Used for long range planning – future costs
  o   Long range facilities plan


• Used to manage economic fluctuations and reduce
  budgetary swings
  o   TRS/ERS rates increasing can result in severe budgetary pressure
  o Inflation effects - example utilities, health insurance
  o Costs that may be uninsured or within deductible limits

Example of Reserve Use: Tax Certiorari Reserve

• Began year with $4,690,922

• Money is added and subtracted each year

• Transferred $485,516 to General Fund to pay out on settlements for
  current fiscal year

• Total exposure is ~$38,150,800 based on actual filings but realistic
  exposure is closer to $6.4-$10.8 million through settlements and court
  proceedings

• In 2012-13, the District borrowed $3,575,000 to augment reserve. Final
  payment for this bond occurred last year and the goal is not to have to
  borrow again as interest payments create a budget burden.

• Recommend maintaining current level by adding to tax cert reserve
  with at least $400,000 allocation of surplus funds available to avoid
  future borrowing

Example: Long Range Facility Planning Aided by Capital Reserve

• The District maintains a long range facilities plan. This includes work
  that cannot be covered in an annual budget resulting from our building
  conditions survey and from improvement ideas. Some projects
  contained in our long range facility plan include:
       o   Roofing and ceilings
       o   HVAC and plumbing fixtures
       o   Electrical upgrades
       o   Windows and door replacements
       o   Turf field replacement and field improvements
       o   Paving and concrete work
       o   Repurposing of space requiring renovations for academic needs

• When identified areas of concern become more urgently needed or
  desired, District seeks to initiate a capital project with voters.

• Funding is typically through a voter approved bond but amount may be
  offset with funds in a capital reserve. Use of the reserve reduces annual
  borrowing costs that must be included with the general fund budget

• The community approved a spending proposition in May to fund various
  projects, including the track and turf replacement.

Do We Gain Interest on Our Reserves?

YES!
• Our reserve funds are invested throughout the year
  o   Investments must follow NY General Municipal Law, Article 5-G and
      3-A, which requires safety and liquidity
  o   District primarily invests through a Local Government Investment
      Pool managed by NYLAF, NY Liquid Asset Fund; https://nylaf.org/


• Some years, interest rates are low and thus cannot be expected
  to add much to our reserves

• Current year, we expect approximately $518,000 in interest
  revenue with rates approximately 4.2-4.4% much of this year
  with a high of 5.2%! While interest rates were lower than our
  experience in 2023-24, our increased balances kept interest
  revenue strong.

What are the Key Variances Creating Surplus This Year?


Revenue:
  • Interest revenue
  • Tuition of non-resident students (Special Ed and General Ed)
  • Sales tax revenue
  • State aid from prior year expenditures (BOCES, Excess Cost)

Expenditures:
  • Special Ed tuitions
  • Health Insurance (higher rates but better mix with waiver program)
  • Utilities
  • Facility projects coming in under estimates
  • Non-Contract salary such as reduced overtime
  • BOCES expenses
  • Reduced Worker’s Comp expenses
  • Offset with deficit in Transportation expense

       Reserve Funding Recommendations
       With an expected surplus of ~$2,300,000:
           • Fund to 4% limit for Unassigned Fund Balance
           • Add $0 to Worker’s Comp Reserve
           • Add $400,000 to Employee Benefits Reserve
             o Plan for larger concentration of staff eligible to retire in the next few years

           • Add $400,000 to Tax Certiorari Reserve
           • Add $600,000 to Liability Reserve
           • Add $400,000 to Insurance Reserve
           • Add $500,000 to TRS/ERS Reserve
           • Add $400,000 to Capital Reserve
           • Add any remaining surplus funds to the Tax Certiorari Reserve

These estimates are based on current financials and accrual estimates. Final numbers/funding
recommendation is available end of July/August as audit is completed and will be provided to
the Board prior to finalizing reserve funding.
