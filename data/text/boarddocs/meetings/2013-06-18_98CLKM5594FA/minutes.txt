		Discussion: 7.7 Upcoming Board of Education Retreat/2013-14 Board of Education Goals

		Mr. Grados stated that the purpose of a retreat is to teach board how to be a better board and is meant to be a workshop for board as to how the Board will lead and become better board members. Personally, Mr. Grados has found retreats to be very good and helps to begin fostering a working relationship with Board members. It was noted that an outside consultant facilitates these workshops which makes better use of the time and can also act as an authority to be sure that the Board does not violate the Open Meetings Law so as not to discuss what should be discussed in a public meeting. Good governance is encouraged by having retreats.

		Mr. Montgomery wanted to clarify where the boundary is with the Open Meeting Law in terms of goal setting and planning  and to that the Board exercises good governance.

		Mr. Grados stressed that in the past, the Board has ensured that the retreat does focus only on what can be discussed just amongst the Board, i.e. Board/Superintendent relations, Board relations and good governance. Dr. Harrison stated that any formulation of goals would be discussed in the public forum.

		Discussion: 7.8 Long-Range Planning Initiative

		The Board discussed the pros and cons of hiring a consultant to help inform the process but that ultimately it is a process that would be done in collaboration with the Administration with the leading the process. The majority of the Board agreed that a consultant would focus on a direction and help in create a fairly high level plan that the administration can carry out. Mrs. Kerner noted that she is not against hiring a consultant but that she didn't see any that any of the proposals would bring us to where the Board wants to go and is not certain if using a school consultant is such a good idea but suggested that perhaps a management consultant be considered which might be more focused on action and outcome to move the District forward. It was mentioned that the cost could be prohibitive.

		Mr. Montgomery noted that Dr. Harrison had solicited proposals from consultants for long-term planning and that after looking at the proposals, he has concerns that the final product might be a template and lead to middle of the road solutions and not the high end goals that Irvington requires; he also is not clear as to how much of the process the Board could develop along with administration without the use of a consultant. Mr. Whitney, who has experience in leading efforts on strategic planning, noted that it is extremely difficult to lead such a process and that in his opinion the Board cannot do this alone as no member of the Board has experience in a school district's strategic plan. He also noted that templates are helpful as the can focus on a direction and ultimately create a fairly high level plan that administration would then carry out. Mr. Grados agreed,stating that he doesn't feel that the Board has the know-how or experience to put together a bona fide strategic pan, stressing that the Board will lead the process and feels strongly that a structure is necessary and a consultant would be money well spent initially and then possibly every five or seven years.

		The issue of time is also a factor in utilizing a consultant to help focus the process, noted Mr. Whitney, who believes that the process should be completed by December so as not to run into the budget process which will begin in January.

		Dr. Harrison stated that the Board needs to ask for what they want and has asked that the Board share their visions, noting that this discussion is an opportunity for individuals to identify concerns and it might be that this pool of consultants cannot meet the District's needs. He also noted that there is value on having consultant with educational experience as there is an analysis that must also take place. He suggested that the Board appoint a point person who can do initial phone conversations with consultants.

		In conclusion, the Board will look to retain a consultant and that concerns should be sent to the Superintendent as soon as possible. Two Board members can then meet with the consultants and conduct initial conversations along with Dr. Harrison. Mr. Grados also offered the members to investigate other possible consultants that they think are might be "outside the box" and not offer a cookie cutter plan, as he doesn't feel it is the best use of administrations  time.

	11. Presentations/Discussions

		Information: 11.1 School Facility Report Card

		Beverly Miller reported it is a requirement by the SED for Districts to present the report card to the BOE annually. Ms. Miller provided a sample of one of the buildings report cards to illustrate the type of information that is on the report card. Information includes general building information, 5 year building conditions, etc. and noted that all district buildings were rated as satisfactory, The report card also lists health and safety improvements that were indicated in the five year plan. It also ensures that the District has a health and safety committee and that the Board is aware of the conditions of the building. It verifies that the District has looked into various testing, i.e. lead, asbestos, etc.

		The process allows for a good overview of the work that needs to be done in each building. However, many of the items would require a capital fund project which would need voter approval.

		Discussion: 11.2 Space Utilization Study (Presentation)

		Dr. Harrison presented a synopsis of the discussion that took place at the June 4th Board of Education meeting with consultant Paul Seversky regarding the findings of Space Utilization Study that he conducted. Dr. Harrison noted that there was confusion regarding the enrollment data that was presented at that time but that it is possible to begin conversations around several scenarios that the Board would be comfortable pursuing and address what the next steps might be, including costing out the options. He also asked that the Board identify a member to work closely with administration on the project.

		Mr. Grados stated that the Board had determined that it was a good idea to conduct a proper space utilization study as one had not been done in many years but it was never the intention to have an expert provide exactly what the District's enrollment would be in five years, at best to provide a forecast. Based on facts of the current real estate market and with new building, it doesn't appear that the District will be facing a drastic decrease in enrollment nor can it be assumed that there will be a drastic increase but perhaps remain stable over the next several years.

		The Board agreed that it was necessary to narrow down the options and then to look at all the implications,not only costs but other ramifications of a particular scenario, such as the impact on the cafeteria if 5th grade were to be moved to the campus, the impact on the Village if students were no longer housed at Main Street School, etc. The Board rejected the option to move both 4th and 5th grades to Dows Lane, and directed the Administration to focus on analyzing moving 4th grade to Dows Lane and 5th Grade to the Campus, including associated costs and logistical implications associated with such a move. The Board also discussed possible option as to where the Administrative offices might be moved to. It was noted that the savings should be significant if the option to close Main Street School to students is to be considered and to keep in mind that many new building regulations would need to be satisfied in the event that students are moved from Main Street but that at some future time, it became necessary to reopen MSS to students, it would be costly to bring the building up to current code.
