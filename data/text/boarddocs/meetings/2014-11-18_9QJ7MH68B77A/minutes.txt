	Information: 3.2 Bond Refunding (Refinancing)

	Ms. Miller reported that the District is pursuing a second bond refinancing, mainly for the debt incurred for the construction of the new campus. The District is being advised to refinance this debt in order to gain a lower interest rate but does not extend the length of the debt. The fiscal advisor estimates that we could save over $5 million over the life of the debt and approximately $280,000 in the 2015-16 school year. She is also hoping that this can be used as an efficiency towards the property tax freeze credit that must be met in order for homeowners to be eligible to receive the credit.

	I response to Mr. Montgomery inquiry if a process is in place to evaluate our fiscal advisors’ performance, specifically having to do with their last bond rate, in that the underwriters’ were not committing to that particular low rate, Ms. Miller reported that the financials did come out to the exact amount of the bid.

	Discussion: 3.3 BAN (Bond Anticipation Note) for Capital Projects

	Ms. Miller noted that the borrowings for the capital projects will be done through a BAN at the maximum amount that the voters approved in October, however, the District will only bond the actual amount of the projects’ costs. She is anticipating that the bonded debt will be much lower than previously expected as the first payment will be built into the 2015-16 budget thereby reducing the amount of the BAN. The District’s fiscal advisors are recommending that the resolution be on the December 15th agenda so as to have the resolution in place, with borrowing taking place several months later, but before June 30th. Ms. Miller noted that BAN interest rates are slightly more fluid than bond interest rates, therefore, the rate could be lower since the length of time is shorter than if borrowing through a bond.

	Mr. Montgomery asked about the economics of going for a bond vs. a BAN to fund the capital projects and the pros and cons of both were discussed.

	Information: 3.4 Overview of the Property Tax Freeze Credit

	Ms. Miller reported that school districts had to stay below the tax cap  in order for home owners to receive the property tax refund this year. For the 2015-16 school year, districts must stay below the cap and show a 1% savings over this year’s tax levy ($498,000) and prove that the savings will hold over three years. Ms. Miller has contacted SED to get the criteria but it hasn't yet been provided. She believes that the bond refunding can be used towards the 1%, as well as last year's bond refunding. She is also looking at other ways to reach the 1% savings for the 2015-16 school year. Other efficiencies being looked into is the transportation consolidation done over the past few years, but until standards are established, it is difficult to move to find additional efficiencies.

	4. Presentations/Discussions

	Discussion: 4.1 Report on Tri State World Language Review (Presentation)

	Dr. Kor stated that last year’s World Language Tri State Review was the District's fourth review. Members of the Tri State Review collect evidence and create a report, not to comment on what a district should do in a particular subject but a report of their findings of our current program. The report includes commendations as well as recommendations. Dr. Kor provided an overview of what a review entails, including but not limited to the eight indicators, performance based assessments, student performance data, parent and community feedback, however, noting there is no standardized testing in the area of world language. Dr. Kor reviewed the commendations and recommendations of the eight indicators, noting that the commendations seem to reflect good instruction that was witnessed in a classroom and recommendations are more systemic in nature. Commendations included, but were not limited to: the department’s solid foundation that provides students with opportunities to integrate higher order thinking skills that enhances learning, a varied curriculum materials, professional learning for teachers, a shared vision statement that outlines the shared goals and that the world language students demonstrate confidence, enthusiasm and dedication to their learning of world languages, throughout middle/high school and beyond.

	Recommendations included, but not limited to: a review and refinement of the mission statement and district goals, find ways to bolster authentic opportunities for students, consideration of revamping unit plans with differentiated activities, tasks, and or assessments to align to local, state or national benchmarks, further partnerships with quad villages to expand opportunities for teachers and administrators, further integration of technology, and development of a formal system for generating and monitoring student data,

	The next steps for the World Language Department include curriculum mapping, working on technology, differentiation and performance assessments/authentic assessments.

	Comments from the Board

	Mr. Grados noted that a Tri State Review is just one data point and what administration would need from the Board in order to address some of the recommendations to improve the World Language program but that the ultimate goal of the program must be defined, i.e. what is the expected proficiency of a student when moving onto college, is it reading, writing and/or speaking proficiency. Board members discussed at length the importance of creating a vision and mission of the program and the necessity to define expectations in order to know how the program needs to be designed and it would impact instruction in the immediate. Dr. Kor believes that the world language department would benefit from outside expertise to help focus on defining the vision and goals of the department and to move the program forward by ensuring that there is horizontal/vertical outcomes as a child moves along in the program as well as working with staff on curriculum development. (Mr. Montgomery noted that he would like to see a board meeting that addresses what the vision is for all programs.) By defining the vision and goals, expectations of the program would be addressed as to what we want the students to achieve, i.e. proficiency in writing, reading or speaking. It was noted that fiscal implications that must be part of the conversations when looking to improve the program, and it was noted that over the years, world language has often been addressed, i.e. elimination at grades 4/5 during times of fiscal constraints. Mr. Grados pointed out that the expectations of the program will drive the budget conversations.

	Mrs. Kerner asked about the recommendation that concerned adopting a systematic process of collecting, sharing and comparing data across the languages.  Mr. Montgomery asked about how the District goes beyond the essential questions that are included in the report as well as what is meant by “authentic” experiences, how that is important to the experiences of the students, what are the benefits/usefulness of authentic experiences and why it matters, and stated that he hasn’t heard enough to convince him that this concern is well placed. Dr. Kor provided examples of authentic experience currently taking place, such as a French class at the high school is connecting with a class in Switzerland and engaging in communications with native speaking students, and help to engage students to apply their lessons and knowledge in real-world situations and/or problems.  Dr. Harrison noted that providing these experiences is one way to enrich a student's learning and perhaps increasing their enthusiasm and motivation for the language.

	Mrs. Kashkin asked for clarification on what professional development is expected to achieve, i.e. help teachers with the first essential question (To what extent is the World Language 6-12 curriculum effectively sequenced and articulated within and across languages.), develop of curriculum, or to provide more rigor within the curriculum, Mrs. Kashkin believes that the first essential question on sequencing of the world language program has been asked over the years and she noted her frustration that it has yet to be addressed.

Mrs. Kerner acknowledged Jill Rembar for her contributions to the world language program over the years.

	11. Board of Education Discussion

	With regard to the information the Board receives on student discipline, Mr. Montgomery asked for a more systemic information sharing on student suspensions, i.e. nature of infraction, length of suspension, grade and believes that it will be useful to have some oversight into that process to ensure that the approach is reasonable, balanced and fair and it is difficult to know without having that information. In light of the conversations happening, especially in NYC, as to the efficacy of suspension, Mr. Montgomery believes it is a fair question as the Board is responsible for overseeing policy and how policies are administered. He would like to hear how other members of the Board feel about the subject. Mr. Grados’ view is that this type of information might be valid if the Board had any indication that there was a problem, but doesn’t feel that there is anything there for the Board to find. Mrs. Kerner would see value in looking for trends in the types of suspension. Mr. Whitney would support receiving the data going forward. Dr. Harrison asked for clarification of what Mr. Montgomery means by providing “oversight”. Mr. Montgomery believes that these are serious events and would best understand the information if it is laid out in one document. Dr. Harrison stressed the complexity of the dynamics surrounding disciplinary decisions, i.e. if discipline of a child with disabilities might look different than another student, the progressiveness of disciplinary issues, etc. He also expressed concern that the data could be misinterpreted. Dr. Harrison will look into what it will take to compile the information on students referred to Superintendent for a hearing which will include grade, nature of infraction, consequence/length of suspension and will communicate with the Board.
