	3. Superintendent's Report

		Information:  3.1 District's Update, News and Events

-
			Dr. Harrison reported that two critical areas are currently being undertaken in the District: technology and curriculum mapping initiatives.  Administration is exploring ways to share staff development with the Quad villages to support each other's technology efforts, with an initial focus on teacher learning through a series of workshops. The other area being addressed is curriculum mapping, with the launch this week of administrators and the Instructional Leadership Team who will work together to develop the systems and organization to begin to articulate a written curriculum for all subjects and grades. A web-based curriculum software, Rubicon Atlas, will be utilized to house the curriculum. The Instructional Team Leaders will be trained in the program and then will train their colleagues.

-
			Dr. Harrison also reported that he has had conversations with SED officials regarding the requirement to launch a Parent Data Portal as part of the Race to the Top (RTTT) Funding that the District had previously received. He noted that the District does have a current platform that would satisfy the requirements to provide the student data but the State is requiring that district's select one of three State selected vendors that they have contracted with. For the first two years, there would be no cost to the district, however, in the years beyond the two years the cost could be somewhere between $35,000-$50,000 to maintain the "dashboards". This would be difficult to account for. Irvington received approximately $2,980 per year, or approximately $11,000, in RTTT funds over a three year period, but the State is mandating districts who took advantage of the funding to sign with one of their vendors. Other districts are considering waiving participation this initiative and would give back the initial money as we could provide comparable services at a much lower costs. Dr. Harrison met with Ken Slentz of SED and expressed his concerns about incurring this unfunded mandate which would impact all student's. There is a deadline of October 31st to choose one of the platforms. He is continuing to press the subject with the State for either latitude to use our existing system or be given assurances that the cost will not be prohibitive to the school system.

		          Mrs. Kerner noted that initially the School Board at the time did not vote to participate in RTTT for the very reasons of not knowing what the costs the District would be mandated to

		          incur down the line. However, with pressure from the State, the Board did move forward and participated in RTTT.

-
			Safe Routes to School grant work is nearly completed with the lighting on campus hill to be installed within a weeks time.

-
			Dr. Harrison noted that there is concern and interest in a proposed policy pertaining to non-school sponsored student expression. He provided an overview of the process associated with introducing policies or changes in mandates or case law which necessitate updating current policies. Two members of the Board of Education sit on the Policy Committee  that works with administration and school counsel to review policies prior to Board review. Dr. Harrison noted that the policy in question was presented to the Board in August and has been available for review prior to a second reading at which time the Board could continue discussion prior to adoption or tabled by the Board. There were concerns expressed by some Board Members regarding the policy and it was tabled and sent back to the committee where some changes were made. Dr. Harrison noted that it appears likely that this policy will be tabled due to concerns and further discussion amongst the Board as well as students and parents. Dr. Harrison stressed that student self-expression is encouraged and the policy's intent is not to stifle that right but to balance that against the rights of other students as well. It is a difficult balancing act. Dr. Harrison stated that he believes that the leaders of the District are committed to making your (students) voices heard but that the Board and administration is charged and obligated to protect the rights of all students. Dr. Harrison will provide an opportunity for students to share their thinking in a forum that is convenient for students to attend.

		Discussion:  6.1 Procedures for Community Comment on Items that Appear on Agenda

		Prior to hearing comments from the community regarding the proposed policies regarding student expression, it was reiterated that a meeting would be scheduled to continue discussion with the students and it was suggested by Mrs. Kerner that it be an open meeting with Board members, students and community members. A meeting date would be identified in the next week or so and it will be communicated broadly.

		A student petition, signed by approximately 200 students, addressed their issues with elements of the draft policy, Non-School Sponsored Student Expression. The issues addressed in the petition, as well as comments made by approximately 25 students and parents in attendance at the Board meeting included:

-
			that the requirement of submission of material to the Principal or designee 10 business days prior to possible distribution is "restrictive to the dissemination of student reported news or time sensitive material..." and could render the information irrelevant;

-
			the definition of "obscene" is "far too vague and open-ended"

-
			the "rule stating that the principal or designee has the power to "regulate (the) time, place, and manner of distribution...is overly restrictive because the Principal or designee could effectively prevent a student from distributing even an approved form of expression."

-
			raises questions about privacy and expression rights of students to regulate "Personal Expression Off School Grounds"

-
			policy is unacceptable to students who do not want to let their right to free speech and expression be repressed and asks that the opinions of students be heard and discussed prior to vote on the policy.

-
			Students and parents asserted that the student's first amendment rights would be violated with the implementation of this policy and was, therefore, unconstitutional. An opinion on the proposed policy was provided by an attorney from the Student Press Law Center which stated that the policy was unconstitutional and violates students rights. They offered their services to the Board in reviewing the policy.

		Additionally, questions were raised as to why the Board felt it necessary to implement such a policy and asked if there are problems within the district; what investigation did the District undertake when drafting the policy, i.e sample policy, ACLU, etc.; the insufficient amount of time the Board provided for students to comment on such a policy; the district did not have the right to dictate student behavior off campus, that that it is the role of parents and seen as disenfranchising parents. A student commented that such a policy would prohibit students from deciding to run for an office or club at the last minute as the policy would require at least 10 days. A parent noted that cases have been decided which are on point, such as Tinker vs. Des Moines School District (see comments below) and that such a policy would instill fear in students expressing themselves and asks students to check their constitutional rights at the door. In Addition, comments were made that the district would be curtailing student creativity and that the discussion should be about what is the optimal pedagogical situation leading to "teachable" moments. Comments were also made that colleges are looking for students who are willing to take chances and risks, even if it means making mistakes. It was also commented  that the Board did intend to adopt the policy as it was on the Consent Agenda for approval.

		Policy - Adoption:  8.1 Policy #5634 - Naming of Facilities

		Policy #7360 and 7370 were tabled to allow for further discussion. A petition, signed by 210 students, was submitted regarding Policy #7370 - Non-Sponsored Student Personal Expression, related to their concerns with the contents of the policy.

		Policy - Adoption: (TABLED) 8.2 Policy #7360 - School Sponsored Student Expression

		 Policy - Adoption: (TABLED) 8.3 Policy #7370 - Non-School Sponsored Student Personal Expression

		Dr. Harrison stated that the Non-School Sponsored Student Personal Expression draft policy is the genesis from recommendations by Counsel and that many other districts in this area have this type of policy. It is also a recommended policy by the New York State School Boards Association. Carolyn Grimaldi, District's legal counsel, noted that it grew out of the need to balance rights of all students and to ensure that the rights of other students are not impinged upon.

		Following the comments made by students and parents, Ms. Grimaldi noted that the draft policy is formatted from NYSSBA's recommended policy, however, the timeline can be at the discretion of a district. Ms. Grimaldi also noted that the Tinker (in response to a comment made by several audience members) ruling is not directly on point as it dealt with students wearing arm bands. There are two cases, Eisner and Hazelwood, that spoke to this evenings' discussion. Mrs. Kerner noted that seven states overturned many of the points in Hazelwood and gave school District's more choice to determine the level of restrictiveness, and she feels that it is not question of what is right or wrong but a decision as to what the District is comfortable with. Mr. Montgomery also noted that he has similar concerns as those expressed and feels that the Board shouldn't be guided as to how restricted the law wants us to be but be guided as to what kind of district we want to be.  He's also not clear if another policy needs to be implemented to ensure that there no harassing speech, etc. and should identify where the problems are and find a less restrictive way to deal with them.

		Mr. Whitney stated that he feels that there is a process issue here and that the impression was that the policy was being "rammed through" and it should be more obvious that there  should be more discussion. Mr. Whitney also stated that he feels that the policy is overly restrictive for the District and need to focus in on what is of concern, i.e distribution of material around the school, and process to review materials but that is a narrow issue and it is important to be clear with the community of  the issues that need to be discussed.

		Maria Kashkin expressed that she is conflicted and thought the reason for such a policy was to create a middle ground and feels there is a need for some type of mechanism to provide for  checks and balances,  i.e. when/where materials can be distributed. By way of example, she noted that there are procedures for holding bake sales and that procedures are necessary to maintain some type of control within a building.

		Mrs. Kerner was in agreement with Mr. Montgomery and Mr. Whitney, expressing her desire to see a district that celebrates students who take risks, who are willing to fail and who might make a huge difference as they go forward. She Commended the students for coming out to express their concerns and hopes that the administration and staff will celebrate their taking a stand. She believes that District has a good group of kids and feels that having no policy is the way to go at this time.

		Mr. Grados noted that the Board didn't craft policy to be against First Amendment rights, but that without a policy, no one knows where the lines are drawn and could lead to decisions being made on the spot, leading to inconsistency of decisions. Due to confidentiality, the Board cannot talk about the issues that led up to discussing the need for such a policy. Mr. Grados also stated that not to have a policy makes enforcement subjective when issues occur and therefore, is not in favor of not having a policy in place.

		Dr. Harrison again stated that there is no intent to stifle speech and thought that the petition was a powerful voice and that the District needs students to be creative and risk-takers. Mr. Whitney identified the need for communication strategies but that  some boundaries are needed and looks forward to further dialogue on the subject, stating that this is an opportunity for a teachable moment, acknowledging that students did take risks.

	12. Presentations/Discussions

		Presentation:  12.1 District/Board Goals and Theory of Action

		Dr. Harrison stated that education is changing very rapidly, with the role and responsibility of schools changing, with greater fiscal constraints and increased accountability. It is necessary to think of how we will educate our children to be successful in college and to meet the needs in the job market. Dr. Harrison highlighted the focus of the district thus far in the school year, and highlighted the utilization of a set of three theories to advance opportunities for students known as Theories of Action. Theories of Action are a set of assumptions on how to move the District to where we want to go as a District, to improve and advance student opportunities and achievement.

		Dr. Harrison reviewed the District's goals from 2012-13: Curriculum and Instruction, Facilities, and a long-range planning goal. He requested that the Board consider last year's goals and to identify any that should be carried over, as they also look to prioritize needs and provide for motivation and direction for district employees. He reviewed the questions to keep in mind  to determine what the needs of the district are, including but not limited to curricula, facilities, and technology. The Board discussed their thoughts about the goals, a set of objectives for the District's work and developing specific goals that would guide the work of the BOE, leadership and faculty.

		Mr. Grados noted that conversations always look to what our schools should look like, such as expanding languages, introducing younger students to technology courses, such as programming or teaching Mandarin, but that decisions need to be made and especially with the tax cap question that asks "what do we want to add and where do you want to take from." He stressed that any additions can only be presented with what could be eliminated.

		For Mrs. Kerner the first, broader question is "Where do we want to be?" - This is the ideal, and how are we going to get there? She feels that ideas shouldn't be limited due to money and that the conversation dovetails along with long-term planning, as it is part of the vision as to where we want to be. Mr Whitney noted that we are now focused on the Common Core and how does the district define standards that go beyond the CCLS. He also believes that the District's ELA program needs to be addressed to identify needs.
