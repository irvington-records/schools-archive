2. Presentation/Discussion

Presentation:  2.1 Strategic Plan Update

Dr.Harrison provided an overview of the district's Strategic Vision whose purpose was to develop a framework to create a common path for the District's future, but also to name community aspirations for the district, establish objectives to enhance learning experiences, identify indicators of success, ability to make informed decisions in the areas of curriculum, extra and co-curricular, facilities and budgert.  As part of the process and to ensure careful planning, six overarching objectives were created through which all planning will be done through the frame of the vision, all decisions, including curriculum, budget, etc. He also noted that a Strategic Plan document will be published as a community resourceas to the direction the school community is heading. He addressed limitations that can be imposed on the district that are beyond control, such as funding resrtictions vis a vis the tax cap, new state and/or federal mandates, environmental factors as well as shifts in student needs.

Dr. Kor addressed the work that has been undertaken and that had been shared through the building's action plans which realize the objectives of the strategic plan. Dr Kor shared examples of recent initiatives vis a vis the objectives. These include the introduction of Project Lead the Way, PLTW, in Grade 5 and middle school's green architecture elective, interdisciplinary writing across subject areas at the high school, and an expansion of math stations at the elementary level, with the main goal as to add fun back into the math curriculum and to increase self-reliance. Moving forward, looking to expand the STEM program K-12, with implementation of the next generation science standards and believe need to round out this priority that was laid out several years ago. Also looking to further develop technology instruction, however, it will require increased staffing and also to expand access to World Language through use of clubs, etc. with limitations at this time but further conversations are required to define the vision with realistic expectations.

Dr. Kor provided examples of evidence of how the goals are being carried out in the classroom, including using Google Exploration, conversations in target language, increases in student experiences. She touched upon the Innovation Fund, which will allow students to create grant applications, the redesign of classroom furniture with flexible seating for students to find how they are comfortable in a learning environment. Many of these opportunities are funded through the IEF. There will be an update to the Technology Plan and more reflecive of new initiative as well as where we will be moving forward.

Dr. Harrison reflected on Objective #4: Foster the creation of a culture where students are active participants in society and shared the many examples of how our students are reaching out to others in need, such as Be a Friend Project at MSS and midnight run at the middle school. The goal is to find ways to expand the students' voice, not only in Irvington, but globally through goals and build opportunities for students through clubs and more foral structures to build leadership skills. This objective will be reviewed to possibly restructure the objective in order to better reflect practices. Objective #5 speaks to strengthen local connections to and ownership of the schools through communications, both school-based and district-wide, launching the Bulldog Booster Club, creating a district identity through the logo, etc. A new website will be launched Spring 2018 and hope to make it a tool for the community.

Carol Stein addressed Objective #6 which is to ensure the fiscal health of the district and to provide a high quality learning environment. She noted that this year the facilities were a focus but there is much more to do, including preventative maintenance which also increases a student's learning environment. Risk management and long term planning are also areas that require continual assessment. It is imporftant to also understand certain factors to help predict the future when the budget process begins. Looking for fiscal efficiencies is a daily process, looking at vendors to ensure getting the most out of spending.

Student assessments were again discussed and reference made to previous discussion on assessments to know how well the district is doing, and will help measure effectiveness of curriculum. Community-wide surveys will be administered to gain feedback to inform initiatives, etc. Upcoming meetings will include conversations with administrators as to their initiatives, what there needs will be to set the stage for budget discussions as well as planning for the 2018-19 district goals. Dr. Harrisopn concluded by stressing that the goals are fluid and that the social/emotional learning must be further expanded.

Following the presentation, board members had the opportunity to ask questions regarding the vision for the district in five years, the direction of the technology program for the future, further clarity of the Capstone projects that will be another opportunity for students to explore their passions. Dr. Kor wanted to acknowledge that there is already in play many of the ideas that have been supported by the Board over the past years through budget, etc. and will continue to provide opportunities to build self-reliance, opening the doors to broaden experiences of students in the Irvington schools, both individually and collectively, etc. Catherine Palmieri noted that it is critical that there is alignment with the goals and the budget process and hopes to have that part of the budget discussions. Deb Hargraves asked about the fluidity of the plan that Dr. Harrison spoke of and how it will change over five years. Dr. Harrison acknowledged that there will be supplements to the plan over time. Dave Graeber asked for further information on the K-12 STEM program, through PLTW. Dr. Kor addressed this question through the new generation of science standards, and cautioned to be realistic about what can be achieved in one school year. He also asked for an update on the assessment-development. A reasonal expectation would be by 2020 to have assessments in each unit of study, adding that New York State will not be updating their assessments in subjects even though they are updating the learning standards which adds conflict for teachers as they must change what they are teaching but the assessments will not address those changes. Dr. Harrison did agree that there is work to be done, however, there are limitations as to how fast change can be realized, both in funding and human resources. Mr. Graeber would like to better understand where we were, where we are going especially in conjunction with budget discussions, acknowledging that it is a process. Brian Friedman was curicous as to how the Board will be able to determine what resources are needed to continue the process and to be sure that the resources allocated are meeting expectations.

Dr. Kor addressed the questions of implementing world language opportunities at the elementary level and noted that there is energy on the administrators part to add clubs, experiences for students for cultural experiences and exposure. She noted that it is a very complex, thoughtful process and to have a successful program must include having the teachers on board and must be collaborative in order to be successful. There are conversations as to how to begin the dialogue but that the reality is that there must be a balance of desires, and what can be fit into a school day.
