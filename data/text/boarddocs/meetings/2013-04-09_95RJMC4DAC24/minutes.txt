	10. Presentations/Discussions

		Discussion:  10.1 2013-14 Budget Discussion (Presentation)

		Dr. Harrison provided a brief review of the budget development process thus far this year, beginning with line by line presentations of each budget area. Throughout the budget development process, a number of reductions were recommended and potential savings were identified. Dr. Harrison stressed that in the past there was a great desire to contain spending which led to many reductions (staff/programs/extra curricular, etc.). Due to increases in pensions, health care, obligation to pay tax certs and possible obligations to special education, deep reductions to current programs would be necessary in order to propose a budget within the tax levy cap. The Board and parents have communicated that in order to preserve the quality of education in Irvington and restore some of the lost programs and staff, it is necessary to consider a budget that exceeds the tax cap levy for the 2013-14 budget.

		Ms. Miller, Asst. Supt. for Business, identified approximately $500,000 in reductions which included the elimination of bus monitors, and has become a point of discussion. Other opportunities to find savings included renegotiated copier leases to save $100,000, $90,000 in savings by becoming self-funded for workman's compensation, and savings in energy conservation initiatives. Ms. Miller also wrote a grant to provide for the inventoring of documents and digitizing all records, which will reduce need for physical space. Centralized postage services and increasing purchasing through BOCES, which are then aidable have also realized savings as well as an increase in shared services with the quad districts such as staff development.

		Further areas for the Board to consider for the 2013-14 budget includes:

-
			an additional  maintenance position, its costs  would be offset from savings from not having to outsource;

-
			reclaim a .5 FTE guidance position, an increase to proposed budget of $59,880;

-
			increase the number of electives as 19 electives have been eliminated over the past few years. Areas have been identified to reintroduce courses in Science, Math, fine arts. Dr. Kor looked at available staff, high course requests and the above were the clearest. Other areas would be more complicated from staffing requests and student interest.

-
			restore clerical staff that had been reduced to 10 months back to 12 months in order to be sure that all schools and the athletic office have support especially during the summer with a cost increase of $25,762.

		There was further discussion regarding the elimination of bus monitors, as recommended by the Budget Task Force, but conversations and concerns have been expressed primarily by parents of   elementary-aged children. Administration feels that safe transportation could be delivered without the monitors but parents have expressed the feeling that they are important to the safety of the children.. Administration has considered reinstating all monitors or adding back half the monitors.

		In summary, the administration is committed to fiscal prudence and containing costs, noting that there has only been a budget growth of 1.31% over five years; the proposed budget meets challenges of unfunded mandated but is committed to introducing opportunities to enhance student learning experiencing and preserving the high-quality education that is expected in Irvington. Dr. Harrison noted that the recommended budget does not eliminate any staff, invests in the infrastructure and facilities of the District, expands technological opportunities, reinstates a full time assistant principal at Dows Lane but also balances fiscal responsibilities.

		Challenge for tonight's meeting is to identify areas that should be included in the budget which will be adopted at the April 23rd meeting and discussion on the above noted items for consideration. The proposed budget represents a $2,741,420 or a 5.36% budget to budget increase and includes funding for tax certioraris while responding to community members' call for the Board and Administration  to be ambitious in providing opportunities instead of the contractions that the District has had over the years.

			The Board considered each of the above-noted items separately, and consensus was reached to include all recommended items to add back to the budget as well as maintain the bus monitors but exclude the the maintenance position at this time, noting that all were difficult decisions and choices. Members expressed concern that the budget will require a 60% majority. Mrs. Kerner noted that

			a 4.85% might be easier to market rather than 4.87% budget increase,  but that would require reducing the budget by $10,000; the Board asked Dr. Harrison to recommend where a $10,000 reduction could be made without impacting any areas. Dr. Harrison again reminded the Board that the budget must be adopted at the April 23rd meeting.
