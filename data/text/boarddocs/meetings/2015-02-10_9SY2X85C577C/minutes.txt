	5. Presentations/Discussions (Presentation)

	Discussion: 5.2 2015-16 Budget Presentation: Curriculum, Technology, Special Education/PPS and Athletics (presentation)

	Tonight is the first of a series of public presentation on the proposed budget, with this meeting’s focused on classroom/instruction, technology, pupil personnel services, special education and athletics. Dr. Harrison reviewed the budget process, budget-development objectives of the budget, i.e. recommending a fiscally responsible budget that is mindful of the entire community as well as opportunities to enhance the students’ experiences and to continue to provide required and valued services and programs. To meet the objectives, there are challenges given the limitations of the tax cap, uncertainty of state aid as well as pension, employee benefits, etc.

	The presentations provides the budget category variance, the percentage increase due to proposed initiatives, and the actual budget-to-budget increase without new initiatives, but with required increases such as contractural salary obligations. The proposed instruction-related budgets has a budget variance of 4.63% with 1.69% is the result of new initiatives, and 2.94% is the cost to maintain the current program. Dr. Kor reviewed eight considerations including, but not limited to, restoration of world language at 5th grade (.40 FTE), increase of AIS math at the elementary level, expansion of programs such as Project Lead the Way and Fundations, a 1.0 FTE increase in clerical staffing at HS,  as well as providing occupational education for high school students and an increase in ESL staff to fulfill the new mandates.

	Following the presentation, the Board then had the opportunity to ask questions on the instructional component. Mr. Whitney had questions related to salaries, professional development that is included in the cost of the additional Fundations program, (only for the first year of the program). Cost of benefits for the additional clerical was questioned as it is approximately 75%. Ms. Miller provided a breakdown of the benefits which include health, ERS, FICA, etc. Mr. Montgomery thought it might helpful to also indicate what the aid would be on a BOCES expense such as Occupational Ed to understand the true cost of the program. Mrs. Kashkin asked if the Board would see a plan of the revamped world language program at MSS. Dr. Kor said that perhaps a framework could be shared but a curriculum couldn't be developed until it is included in the proposed budget.

	Mrs. Kashkin expressed concern that the world language program will be worth the investment as it is a great ask and would be supportive of an improved program. Mr. Whitney agreed. It was also noted that the cost of developing the curriculum is not included in the cost presented. Mrs. Kerner asked about the model that will be used in the AIS program. The goal K-5 is to be more responsive at an earlier age and the additional staff would provide for more flexibility in scheduling. Mrs. Kerner asked about assessing the level of interventions (8:51). Mrs. Kerner also commented again about addressing the needs of the higher level learners to ensure that enrichment is being provided. Ms. Chapnick and Mr. Lichtenwalner responded to this concern and noted that the Math coach, Christine Rosner, does provide support to teachers in the approach to teaching math which supports all levels of learners. Mrs. Kerner also asked about other electives that will be offered and would be interested to know what might be offered to gain a sense of increases to electives.

	Mrs. Kerner asked about the expense of iPads connected with adding PLTW at the elementary level. She would also like to see the data of the effects of the Fundations program at K-1. She also would like to see if there is other ways to provide the support at the high school without incurring the costs. She would also like to see what causes the significant differentials in budget lines between push ahead and proposed budget, such as Other Expense - Instructional. She also would like to see actual budgets at this point as opposed to approved budget, which is much more relevant as it could be higher or lower than what was budgeted. Mr. Montgomery had questions regarding the requirements of the PLTW to offer additional courses. In response to Mr. Montgomery's question on professional development costs in Fundations, it would be a break even as there wouldn't be the cost of professional development at the K-1 level. Mr. Montgomery asked about the number of students who are required to receive ENL services and would like the data provided.

	Co-curricular budget points included a 2.36% budget variance with 4.55% in new initiatives and the actual budget-to-budget is a 2.2% reduction. Of note, is an increase in club amounts to reflect the request at MSS and the MS to increase the number of clubs offered.

	Regarding curriculum improvement budget points, the budget variance is 1.72% with 1.18% increase in new initiatives and a 0.54% increase in budget to budget. An increase to the budget is a purchase of a program called Ames Web which will monitor RTI progress.

	Also addressed were Library expenses, which has a budget variance of 4.93%, instructional technology has a budget variance of 13.06%, central data processing shows a significant increase of 34.98% due to reclassification of BOCES services. As well as the purchase of self-managing service for people to change their own passwords, at a cost of $9,600, a one-time cost. Mr. Montgomery requested that when expenses are reclassified, that a footnote or some other alert be provided to make it easier to understand. The Guidance budget includes an increase for the expansion to Grades 6-8 of the Naviance program.

	Dr. Kor reviewed the special education budget considerations including a 2.0 FTE increase in staff to support the ICT model, and the addition of a 6-12 chairperson. The budget variance for this category is 11.26% with 2.02% in new initiatives. PPS budget points include a 12.78% budget category variance as well as a 10.52% increase due to new initiatives which include an additional .50 FTE psychologist at the high school as well as restoration of a full time social worker in lieu of a part time community aide.

	The athletic considerations call for a 7.21% budget variance with a 3.76% increase in new initiatives with an actual 3.45% in actual budget-to-budget increases. New considerations include the purchase of necessary athletic field equipment as well as coaching apparel.

	In summation, the total of new considerations presented total $712,986 which expands programs as well as will enhance experiences for all the students.

	Mr. Montgomery commented that these requests are in addition to current budget, so it is possible to look at what is currently in the budget that could be traded with the new initiatives.
