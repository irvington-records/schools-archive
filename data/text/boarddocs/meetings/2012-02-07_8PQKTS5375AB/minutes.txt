		Discussion, Information:  3.3 2012-13 Budget Discussion: Superintendent's Proposed 2012-13 School Year Budget. (Presentation)(2012-13 Line Budget)

		Mr. Dawson reported that this will be the first look at budget and that there will be several more meetings to discuss the budget before it is finalized. Mr. Dawson also noted that there are many more constraints imposed on school districts by New York State's Tax Cap legislation.

		Dr. Roelle presented a brief overview of the new tax cap legislation that is being imposed on school districts beginning with the 2012-13 budget. and noted that when it was first introduced it was referred to as a 2% tax cap which is inaccurate. Irvington's tax cap is slightly above, as the rate is formula driven. Dr. Roelle presented budgetary changes reflected in the proposed budget. To address tax cap, critical choices needed to be faced in order to be under the tax cap. He noted that staff needed to be reduced and that there were painful conversations with administrations when discussing the proposed budget. Firstly, the goal was to position the school district as best it could for the future as next year it won't be easier. Driving forces of some of the necessary budgeting decisions are the Common Core Standards and APPR which are linked together.  Dr. Roelle extended great respect to all administrators as they worked through the difficult process of developing the proposed budget.

		Dr. Roelle reiterated that the proposed budget being presented is not absolute, but a work in progress and will be tweaked as the weeks go on.  Dr. Roelle stated that the process to develop the budget was to look at each category in order to minimize the negative reaction in one area and he feels that that is the most fairest way to approach developing a budget.

		Highlights of the Proposed Budget:

		Expenditures/Revenues -  Major sources of revenues, i.e. State Aid, sales tax, property taxes, Fund Balance (Irvington has maintained the maximum of 4% fund balance) etc. Irvington is expecting to receive $24,000 in increased State Aid for the 2012-13 school year.

		The TAX CAP in Irvington is 2.15% equaling an allowable tax levy of $45,906,398. The District's goals with regard to the budget were reviewed, i.e. integrity of programs, maintain class size as close to 2011-12 levels and present a recommended budget that is below tax cap.

		Current Budget: $50,324,892

		Proposed Budget: $51,075,000

		Budget to Budget increase 1.49%

		Proposed Budget is $34,845 under TAX CAP.

		Major factors that increased the budget:

-
			Instructional, Teachers and Employees' retirement costs

-
			Special Education tuitions to private schools

		Reductions:

		Elementary - 1.2 FTE teaching positions and five teacher aides, one security aide and one teaching assistant at Dows Lane

		Middle School - Grade 6 programmatic reductions: exploratory and world language programs = 4.0 FTE

		High School  - Guidance services - 1.0

		District Office - One Clerical position

		Additions:

		Dows Lane - Special Ed teacher and teaching assistant

		K-12 increase to ELL .5 FTE

		Restore art chair K-12 .2 FTE (settled agreement)

		One custodial position

		The administration has recommended that the Board consider including the position of Assistant Superintendent for Curriculum and Personnel. Dr. Roelle strongly supports this position to move the district forward. The need for professional development for teachers and administrators  is expected to double/triple over the next couple of years. The budgeted salary is $180,000.

		Overall staffing changes: 295.22 vs 284.62, a reduction of 10.60 FTE

		Dr. Raina Kor provided an overview of the curriculum and instruction budget:

		Major initiatives include: Professional development to meet new rigorous standards of common core standards and APPR.

		New costs total $71,090 to meet new mandates of APPR, including $22,000 in new student assessments and $17,000 in test scoring since scoring can not longer be done in house.

		Presented how the budget is distributed: employee benefits, special education and instruction represent most of the "guts" of the program. Next year is very similar to this year, but comparing the instruction, special education and benefits line you don't want it to fall much below 75% (Irvington is 74.66%) In the future, you want to focus on increasing instruction.

		Revenues

		The District is losing $258,555 in a Federal jobs bill that will be expiring. The District allocated those funds to pay for staffing which must be maintained and therefore accounted for in the 2012-13 budget.

		It is recommended to use $400,000 from the Fund Balance to help reduce the tax levy.

		Ms. Beverly Miller presented the line by line budget for instruction, including the addition of $180,000 for the administrator. The proposed budget represents an increase of $341,643 over the 2011-12 school year. It was noted that the budget also includes transfer of salary to Dows Lane for a part-time administrator (The Athletic Director will be .5 FTE at Dows Lane).

		Ms. Miller presented the line by line budget for the Pupil Personnel Office, noting that the estimated 2012-13 budget is $1,625,950, or a reduction of $76,248 from the 2011-12 school year estimated expenditures, the larges reduction being the reduction of one guidance counselor.

		The Co-curricular Activities proposed budget is $108,350, which is $770 over the current budget of $105,850. To offset the increase in advisors salaries, the chaperones/food concessions budget line was reduced by $1,200 over the current year's budget.

		Employee Benefits - An increase of 2.32%, or $233,180 over the current budget of $9,760,442, is expected for 2012-13, representing an increase of $61,230 in Employees' Retirement System and the teachers' retirement funding is expected to increase by $149,795. It is anticipated that there will be an increase of $53,850 in health insurance benefits - going up 8.5%.

		Inter-fund Transfers - There is significant increase of 6.64% in the Inter-fund Transfer budget line. Ms. Miller noted that it had been under budgeted in the past and the proposed budget now more accurately reflects the actual.

		In summary, all budget lines combined represent an increase of 1.49% in the overall proposed budget.

		Revenues

		In the 2011-12 school year, revenues totaled $5,007,267 and it is anticipated that revenues will be reduced to $5,203,456 which means that the reduction must be absorbed within the tax cap.

		Board Member's Comments/Questions:

		Board members noted that from the standpoint of budget presentations, the proposed budget was presented with organization, clarity and a level of detail.

		Question: How would you respond to remarks made that he (Dr. Roelle) has "no skin the in game" since he is an interim?

		Response: Dr. Roelle responded that he takes the work very seriously, is founding member of National Public Schools of Tomorrow and believes in public schools, has come to enjoy working in Irvington, and takes it to heart. Never enjoyed developing budgets that call for staff reductions, etc. but put aside as it is life's work.

		Question: Why the need for additional special education teachers?

		Answer: Must  meet state requirements based on number of students identified as they move to next grade.

		Question: Parents have expressed concern with status of Guidance Department and how to increase quality. How will district do with one fewer counselor?

		Answer: This was a difficult recommendation to make; looked at what is furthest away from classroom; talked about restructuring department but does mean increase in case load. Have not yet finalized restructuring department. Spoke about part-time reduction but concerns on how that would work.

		Question: Will teacher reductions impact study hall time, and who would be monitoring study halls?

		Answer: Reduction primarily at Middle School --- 6th grade World Language would be replaced with study hall, taken over by a supervisory teacher which is part of their day.. Exploratory program elimination would result in study hall and would be a supervisor responsibility. In certain cases in 8th grade this would result in a second study hall

		Question: Transportation: District has suffered with quality of transportation especially at beginning of year, but feels funny to be paying more in transportation when contemplating reduction in teachers. Is there anything we can do to reduce costs.

		Answer: This is a priority of the budget task force and will formulate recommendations which the budget task force will consider. (John Montgomery). Bev: might even increase more based on needing to find a new contract. Also been discussed to increase the mileage.

		District is still required to provide transportation to private/parochial schools. Could change central pickup points. Any recommendation to change limits

		must be approved by voter proposition.  Important to remember that what can be controlled is a very small piece, bigger portion of budget is mandated, i.e. special education transportation.

		Question: It appears staffing costs are roughly flat, where is cost increase coming from?

		Answer: Cost increases are due, in part, to increases in benefit costs.

		Question: What is Arts in Education?

		Answer: Every grant through PTSA/IEF goes through Arts in Education. Arts in Education offsets IEF/PTSA costs and the money is returned to the District following year.

		Question: Looks like everyone is sharing pain, but ask to reconsider or look at guidance position. In years past, department hs been lacking, increased positions but now reducing 30% in department, is being challenged more then other departments. Is it possible to present another one or two options on how to remedy?

		Question: Members of Community posed that moving administrative offices from trailer to Dows Lane could cost as high as $600,000. How can we consider this move at this time?

		Answer: The cost is no where near $600,000 and cost has not been quoted.The money is not coming from general fund but from the capital budget line that is available since projects in prior years were under budget or determined not to be needed and allows us to do this. Trailers are rapidly deteriorating.

		Question: When will people hear about their personal taxes?

		Answer: A guess-timate is that taxes will be under 3.5%.

		Question: Regarding the $17,000 budgeted for test scoring. Is it possible to save money by working with the other quad village districts to score tests?

		Answer: There are advantages to sending out. We have scoring days while other districts pay teachers and pay substitutes? It is possible to do a cost analysis. Can  look at cost would be. Some district's pay retired teachers, still cost associated. Hopefully see reduction in time if tests are taken on line.
