                                 BUDGET DEVELOPMENT

Beginning early in the school year, the Board of Education and the Administration
began discussions relating to the school budget for the 2012-13 school year. The Board
and the administrators have been mindful of the ongoing fiscal crisis in the nation and
the State, and the recently adopted New York legislation to “CAP” property taxes.

On October 11, 2011, the Board of Education adopted four goals for the District. One of
the four goals adopted was:

        To address the New York State Tax Cap Legislation in the Development of the
        2012-13 School Year Budget.

Soon thereafter, the Assistant Superintendent for Business provided a detailed
presentation to the Board of Education on October 24, 2011 regarding the tax cap
legislation. Such presentation included issues that the District would need to face in
developing the 2012-13 school year budget.

At the November 15, 2011 Board of Education meeting, the Superintendent provided
an overview of budget issues that the administration would be addressing in
developing the next budget. Such presentation noted the need to address New York
State’s implementation of the Common Core Standards, student assessments and
teacher evaluations. It also reviewed instructional matters relating to administrative
staffing, class size and teacher staffing, student transportation and buildings and
grounds’ needs. The Superintendent noted that the goal of the administration in
developing the budget would be to develop a fiscally responsible budget that
maintains the integrity of the instructional programs as much as possible.

In meetings held on December 19, 2011 and January 10, 2012, the Assistant
Superintendent for Business began budget line item reviews for various sections of the
budget. Guided by the Board’s goal relating to the tax cap and the ongoing fiscal

Middle School Proposed Reductions
There exist a number of partial positions recommended for reduction at the Middle
School. Most of the reductions are a result of low enrollment and the reductions will not
reflect any substantial impact on class size. Additional section reductions are achieved
by modifying the team structure in grades 7 and 8 to one team in each grade.

There are two program areas recommended for elimination – the exploratory program
and 6th Grade World Language.

The exploratory program enables students to have a “sampling” of experiences in art,
music and technology. The administration does not believe that the elimination of this
program will significantly diminish the core instructional program for students. Therefore,
it is recommended that these programs be eliminated.

Currently, students in sixth grade take a course in either French or Spanish that meets
every other day. This course, when combined with courses in 7th and 8th grades, leads
to the award of one high school credit in the language of choice. Due to the fact that
students can still meet the requirements for the same level of high school credit without
a course in grade six, the administration recommends that the 6th grade program be
eliminated.

                                 REVENUES and TAX CAP

The previous sections of this document addressed the projected budget changes and
allocations that are included in the proposed expenditures budget. With the new tax
cap legislation in effect, the revenues budget warrants a careful review. The District will
need to provide the State with its tax cap calculation by March 1, 2012.

The projected revenues for the forthcoming school year are reflected below.
         State Aid
         Currently, the District expects to receive $2,499,549 in State aid. On
         January 12, 2012, Governor Cuomo announced his proposed budget for New
         York State for the new fiscal year beginning on April 1, 2012. If Governor Cuomo’s
         budget is adopted by the Legislature, our projected State aid for the 2012-13
         school year will be $2,523,764; a $24,215 increase.

         Federal Jobs Fund Program
         Currently, the District receives $158,555 from the Federal Jobs Fund Program.
         When the funds were allocated, the government advised school districts that the
         funds would “dry up” after June 1, 2012. In Irvington, such funds have been used
         to pay salary and related costs for two special education teachers. While the
         special education teachers are still needed and included in the proposed
         budget, the Federal funds to pay for the positions will not be forthcoming. The
         total loss of this revenue source is $158,555

         Fund Balance
         Currently, the District maintains a fund balance of 4% --- which is the maximum
         amount that is allowed by State law. Only three years ago the maximum fund
         balance was 2%.

         The administration believes that the Fund Balance should be used as the “rainy
         day” emergency fund. Given the current fiscal climate and our obligation to
         meet the new tax cap legislation, the administration recommends that the Board
         of Education utilize $400,000 of undesignated, unreserved fund balance as a
         means of reducing the tax levy. The administration notes that the District must
         use restraint in using the fund balance as a revenue source as these funds
         represent one-time revenue that is supporting recurring expenses.

         Other Revenue Issues
         There exist a few revenue sources that will reflect decreased revenue for 2012-13.
         These sources include:

                                       SUMMARY
The administration believes that the proposed Irvington School District budget is a
responsible budget. The budget maintains the integrity of the core instructional
programs in the District.

Certainly, the current economic climate and new rules governing tax levies presented
significant challenges to the administration as it developed this budget. A budget with
a 1.49% increase over the current budget could not be achieved without staffing
reductions. While such reductions were necessary, they were achieved in a thoughtful
manner with a goal toward maintaining the overall integrity of the core instructional
programs.

The administration looks forward to working with the Board of Education to review the
proposed budget and to arrive at a budget that the Board can present to the
community in May 2012.
