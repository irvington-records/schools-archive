Presentation: 6.1 iASK Presentation

Dr. Harrison introduced co-chairs Allyson Felix and Tanya Hunt as well as Coalition Coordinator, Lisa Tomeny, who provided an overview of what iASK (Irvington About Safe Kids) is and the programs that it sponsors to the Irvington community to promote substance prevention and coordination of resources.  iASK is part of CAB (Community Advisory Board). Ms. Felix noted that iASK was formed in 2014 through a five-year Federal grant which provides funding for community-based coalitions to prevent youth substance abuse. $625,000 was the total award and is renewable for an additional five years. Representatives from the community, including but not limited to school district administrators and students, local government, parents, law enforcement, business, healthcare providers, etc. serve on the coalition.  Programs that have been funded include SafeRides run by students, a pre-prom event and a bus to the prom for all students which promotes a sober and safe celebration, the Positive Impact Club where middle school students made baby blankets to donate to families with parents in recovery, a sticker campaign by students to place stickers on pizza boxes during Superbowl week with prevention messages and a back-to-school positive post-it campaign and post-card campaign mailer to all Irvington residents regarding the hazards of marijuana use. A key focus of iASK is to work with the community to build support and the partnership with the village and the school district is most important. Ms. Felix noted that the organization is grateful for Dr. Harrison, David Cohen and David Sottile's participation. iASK has also sponsored many workshops for parents and students. They also sponsor training of staff to further their reach into the schools, etc.The monthly meetings focus on topics that are mainly generated by students and as a means to keep in touch with concerns of our students. Surveys are conducted every other year as another means of collecting data from our students which informs the strategic plan for the following year. The grant requires that improvement/impact is demonstrated in order to maintain the grant funding. iASK maintains a website, Twitter page, Facebook, etc.

Following the presentation, Mrs. Hargraves asked where most of the funding is spent and what would be needed to sustain the program beyond the grant funding period. Much of the funding has been initially spent on building the website and infrastructure of the program and it was noted that monies must be spent towards drug and alcohol prevention. It was suggested that perhaps funding towards D.A.R.E. might be allowable. This will be looked into.

6.2 - 2016-17 APPR (Presentation)

Dr. Kor provided an overview of the recent changes at the state level to the teacher evaluations, moving to 3012-D. She also noted that the District has had a APPR Committee since 1999.

Changes to the structure of the evaluation process include that each teacher will receive a score in only two categories: student performance category --- 50% State assessment or Student Learning Objective and 50% observation school visit category. Previously there had been three components which a teacher's evaluation score was based on. Since 2001, the district utilizes the Danielson's Framework for Teaching. The framework has four domains --- planning and preparation; classroom environment; instruction; and professional responsibilities but the District is not allowed to score other than on a single observation or as in the teacher’s planning, which is limiting. Previously, a binder or portfolio would be evidence of their work in Domain 1 and 4, but now the state only wants evaluations to be based on what the students are doing within the classroom. Pre and post observations are also conducted.

She provided the distribution of observation values for non-tenured teacher:

- a full period announced observation (30%) ,

- a 20 minute unannounced walkthrough (30%);

- a 10-15 minute announced independent evaluator (not an administrator in the building); walkthrough (10%); and

- an unannounced observation (30%).

The distribution for tenured teachers are:

- an announced observation (45%);

- 20 minute extended walkthrough (45%);

- and a 10-15 minute walk through (10%).

Dr. Kor also noted that over the past five years administrators have conducted walkthroughs in every building during administrative counsel meetings. Goals are developed for walkthroughs (what should administrators look for) and debriefs of evidence is then conducted following the walkthroughs. The administrators’ focus is on what are they seeing the students doing during the walkthrough, looking for self-reliance or being engaged in learning, trying hard not to focus on what the teachers are saying, as it is more about the system of teaching and learning. Dr. Kor stressed that this is what the work that administrators should be doing even though it is a great stress when coupled with other mandates from the State, etc.

With respect to the student performance category, the difference between individual SLOs and school-wide SLOs were provided and she noted that NYS put a hold on using the assessment scores and cannot be used as part of the calculation of teacher scores --- they are advisory only. The district now must determine what to use instead, such as administering pre-assessments at the beginning of the year and then post assessments, thus moving to school-wide assessments which reflect collective contributions of all teachers to the student's learning over the previous years with every teacher getting 50% of their score in the student performance category from 4th grade assessments. Middle School will use the 8th grade assessments for their school-wide SLO and high school teachers will be evaluated on the ELA Regent scores, again with the philosophy that all teachers contribute to the students learning. This will not apply if a course ends in a Regents exam as it is still required to have a SLO, but only required if 50% or more of their caseload is comprised of students taking the Regents, and if not, they may use the school-wide SLO.

Action (Consent): 12.3 High School French Class Field Trip to France

Mrs. Hargraves expressed her concerns about the trip given the experience from last year when the same trip was cancelled due to the terrorist attacks in Paris. Parents had voted on whether to go forward with trip or cancel and it became clear that the trip should be cancelled.  As it was less than 90 days from trip when the tour company was informed the school was cancelling the trip, parents were able to recover on 50% of the cost, however, the tour company renegotiated for a $500 cancellation tour. Mrs. Hargraves noted that some families had paid in full --- $2,600 and is co concerned that the same language is in this year's paperwork and cancellation policy is not clear. Requests that we ask legal counsel to look at insurance program and that families are given clearer guidance on policy. She is also concerned that the Board will feel the repercussions should the families be faced with same decisions as last year.

Dr. Harrison noted that the resolution approves only the trip, not the tour company and once trip is approved, will then ask legal counsel to provide guidance. He also noted that other tour companies were looked into and that the increase is cost is due to the enhanced insurance. Mrs. Hargraves would also like to know if there would be the option for going to another destination should there be issues again with going to France.
