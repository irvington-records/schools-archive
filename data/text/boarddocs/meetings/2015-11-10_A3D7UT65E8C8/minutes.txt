11. Presentations/Discussions

Presentation: 11.1 2014-15 Student Performance Report (Attachment)

Dr. Kor reported that presentation covers Regents scores, AP scores and NYS assessment scores, noting that typically, five years of data is provided to see how students are doing year to year. Dr. Kor noted that our SAT scores are significantly above the national average and noted that our athletic teams also do very well academically, very often achieving scholar athletic status.

Also highlighted:

- 95% of students graduated with a Regents Diploma in 2015;

- three national merit semi-finalists

- 18 AP courses were offered which 530 students participated and 526 taking the exams and 405 passed;

Mrs. Palmieri also asked the difference in Irvington "B" and College "A" is reconciled and what their exam score is vs their course score. Dr. Kor noted that that would be an important data point.

The NYS Assessments for Grade 3 - 8 in Mathematics and ELA were reviewed, noting that the tests are very long and grueling for third graders and scores do not take into account the number of classified students sitting for the assessments. Comparisons with 12  Westchester districts that we compare our results were also reviewed. In looking at the data, Dr. Kor noted the data is analyzed for trends and can help inform what might be needed within a particular cohort. She reviewed areas of strength by grade level as well as opportunities for growth in both Mathematics and ELA. She emphasized that Social Studies and Science also plays a role in teaching ELA. She highlighted the Algebra scores which include scores of the 8th graders who took the Algebra Regents. She noted that it is important to analysis the data of our struggling students to ensure that their needs are being met, with intervention provided to students who fall below a certain score.

Mr. Montgomery asked what benchmark we should be aiming for as a District in Regents as a measurement to be able to assess if we are successful or not relative to the resources we provide our students. Mr. Cohen again noted that often it is the cohort, the number of students with needs and looking at trends. Mr. Montgomery had previously asked what measures the administration thinks would provide the information as to how we can determine if we are doing a good job educating our children, using quantitative measures. Dr. Kor noted that there are only five mandated Regents exams: English, Integrated Algebra, Global History, US History and Living Environment and believes Irvington should only give those exams and develop more creative assessments.

There was a conversation regarding students who receive outside tutoring and if the number of students is known. Mrs. Palmieri believes that that information would be worthwhile to have as there is has been discussion in the community. Dr. Kor noted that topic has been discussed over the years and she doesn't feel that the data gathered would be accurate and therefore not be valid or useful data.

11.2 Discussion on the Transportation of Students Residing in Private Community

Dr. Harrison referenced the previous comments made by residents of the Downingwood Condo complex, a private complex, requesting an additional stop within the complex to reduce the walking distances to the current bus stop. He also noted that this issue was raised at the end of last school year. Ms. Miller reported that an internal analysis of the potential impact of adding one and two stops revealed that because of the three tiered bus system, adding additional stops at Downingwood (Birch Lane and Downingridge Road) would necessitate the other schools’ (MSS, MS and HS) bus runs to start earlier, for example, the high school pickup would go from 6:24am to 6:19am. 16 students at Dows Lane and 54 students at the high school would be impacted. Another issue would be that Dows Lane students would be dropped off before staff arrives to provide supervision which would require the hiring an aide. The afternoon runs would be the reverse, with return times later in the afternoon.

Transportation Coordinator, Joe Urbanowitz, noted other issues to consider are that other private developments might want the same consideration which might require an additional one or two buses and the time impact on the high school runs might cause more students to be driven to school. Ms. Palmieri noted that she believes that the high school buses are underutilized.

The administration is asking for the Board's consent to conduct a study to determine the private communities within the District and possible impact on our transportation system as well as the broader scale impact when including other private roads across the district. The cost of this scope would be $5,000. The district has also asked for the cost if it were to include a bus utilization study as well. It was noted that the transportation company does conduct two ridership counts per year, one towards the end of September/early October and the second would be done in January. The data will be provided to the district.

Before considering any changes to current transportation program, Mr. Montgomery would like to better understand the distinction between private and public roads, if there are there any legal distinctions, the number of private streets/communities in the district and would like a list of them, and is there a policy on this and if not, should there be and should private areas be treated any differently than public areas. He would like to better understand the bigger picture before considering increasing runs and making any decisions, and feels that this should not be just a Downingwood issue but that any decision made should be applied consistently over public and/or private roads.

Another factors to consider in any decision is the maintenance of private roads, the overall safety of the private roads and the ability to do 3-point turns, will utilization be increased or decreased with any time adjustments. Mr. Hanna asked how routes are developed year to year due to shifting enrollments. Assumptions are made in June using current enrollments with some adjustments made as it gets closer to the start of school.

Dr. Harrison noted that the purpose of such a study would address many of the questions raised in the conversation to gain as much information on impacts before making any decisions. In conclusion, the administration will look to do an analysis on the impact of existing tiers if we were to enter into other private roads/communities and which would be safe. Ms. Palmieri would like to get a clearer picture to see if and how bus utilization would be impacted by any changes to current routes. In conclusion, Ms. Palmieri stated that the issue will be looked at comprehensively appreciate their time and share their concerns.
