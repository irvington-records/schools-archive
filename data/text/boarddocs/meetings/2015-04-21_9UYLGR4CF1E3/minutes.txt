	4. Review 2015-16 Budget and Adoption

	Discussion: 4.1 2015-16 Proposed Budget Overview (Presentation)

	Dr. Harrison noted that tonight is a culmination of work that has taken place over the past several months and results in a budget that is both fiscally responsible but also maintains and enhances students' learning opportunities, adds programs, staff, professional development and facilities maintenance. The budget is approximately $384,655 below the cap that includes savings in bond refinancing. Additionally to plan for future needs of the facilities a Capital Reserve Fund would be established which voters must approve such a proposition on May 19th. Other highlights are the budget considerations contained in the proposed budget, including but not limited to: increased Mathematics AIS at Dows and MSS, expansion of Project Lead the Way at the high school and introducing it at the elementary and middle school levels, increase to special education staff, restoring a grades 6-12 literacy coach as well as the full time social worker, etc.

	Dr. Harrison reviewed the revenue side of the budget, and noted that the total budget amount of $57,664,000 with a tax levy increase of 2.23%, noting that shifts in tax assessments has had an impact on the taxes, resulting in a 3.54% tax rate increase, and that the averaged assessed value of a home will see an increase of $585 in their school taxes.

	Mrs. Kerner reiterated for community members that might not have been involved in the budget meetings or discussions, that the development of the proposed budget has been a process and that the Board has had input into the entire process with tonight being the final step in the budget development process.

	Action: 4.2 Adoption of 2015-16 District Budget

	Prior to a motion to adopt the budget and a roll call vote, Board members all agreed that it was a good budget. Mr. Montgomery stated that he felt that there had been good discussions over the past several months with respect to building the budget. Mrs. Kerner agreed with Mr. Montgomery and that ideally it would have been good to come in a little lower, but that the budget does meet the needs of the students and district. She also asked if literacy coach is the proper term as it is hard to describe what the position will be. Mrs. Kashkin believes that it is a smart budget, with a nice balance that addresses everyone's needs. Mr. Whitney agreed that it is a prudent budget and that it was wise not to spend up to the tax cap which balances needs of the district but does not put too much stress on the community

	Resolved, that the Irvington Union Free School District's 2015-16 proposed budget be set at $57,664,000 and that this proposed budget be voted on by the community at the Annual Meeting on May 19, 2015 between the hours of 7:00 am and 9:00 pm at the Irvington Main Street School.

	5. Presentations/Discussions

	Discussion: 5.1 Dows Lane Presentation (Presentation)

	Dr. Kor provided an overview of the Theories of Action and Goals stating that they are the foundation of all the work being done in the Irvington schools. The work being done is based on three theories: 21st Century Learners, Professional Development, Practices & Observation. Theory 1 is all about students, with the goal to prepare all learners to thrive in a global society. Action 2 is all about professional development which is the foundation of student success.  Action 3 is determining success with visits to all classrooms and assuring there is a common understanding among all staff members.

	Randy Lichtenwalner, Principal of Dows Lane and Meg Benedetto, Assistant Principal, provided a snapshot of the population of Dows Lane, reporting that there are currently 537 students enrolled, with 48 teachers and aides. The focus of the presentation was on mathematics instruction, instructional design, Fundations, Habits of Mind and Technology. Mr. Lichtenwalner used pictures of the activities in the school to illustrate the activities.

	Ms. Benedetto spoke to the Common Core in the area of Mathematics and noting that with the assistance of Christine Rosner, staff has engaged in Math Module Mondays to discuss how the modules build on each other and how they are aligned with the Theories of Actions. There is also reflection on what the data says about the instruction.

	Mr. Lichtenwalner highlighted the professional development taking place at Dows Lane, especially in the area of instructional design, noting that student success in the classroom starts outside of the classroom with teachers’ design of instruction and development using Understanding by Design (UbD) along with utilizing the Rubicon Atlas software. Faculty meetings have also focused on how assessment can inform curriculum design. He highlighted the Fundations program in the kindergarten and 1st grade classes Results of the Fundations program are promising and teachers are astounded at progress that students are making with the program.

	Ms. Benedetto spoke to Habits of Mind which incorporate 16 habits for students to call upon to communicate with a peer, a group, and community to foster success. Currently students are taught discretely as to how it looks in one’s life and in the classroom such as thinking flexibly or managing impulsivity.

	In the area of technology, Mr. Lichtenwalner noted that 3rd graders participated in the Hour of Code this year and teachers have been looking at where they can use technology in content area units and have been given training on using technology-aided informative assessments. Further training in Rubicon Atlas has been taking place as well as Google PD teams to turnkey training to other staff members.

	The Board had questions and asked for greater detail with regards to professional development at Dows Lane, such a how Ms. Rosner's time is spent, the format of the after school math professional development, and how the teachers are adapting to the new approaches to teaching along with the amount of professional development that has taken place, to which Mr. Lichtenwalner responded that teachers view it is an investment of their time and planning out well articulated content lessons and assessments, which can be overwhelming at times, but believes that most teachers see it as a positive. Mr. Whitney also asked how the assessment data is being utilized by teachers.

	In response to Mrs. Kashkin’s question if the math modules are designed by teachers or from Engage NY, Ms. Benedetto noted that they originally were provided by NYS but over the last two years, have been edited by teachers. With respect to the Google PD teams, the focus at Dows Lane is to look at how apps can be used to enhance the teachers' work and perhaps eventually find its way into the classroom. Mr. Lichtenwalner provided an example of how technology might be used in kindergarten in the area of mathematics.

	Mrs. Kerner wondered if parents are asked for feedback on assessments to gain qualitative data. Mr. Lichtenwalner believes that teachers are in communication with parents and such communication often happens informally. He also noted that at the lower grades, very often it is done through speaking with the students. With respect to integrating technology into the classroom, Mr. Lichtenwalner said that there was alignment across a particular grade level but individual teachers have the ability to use some creativity in their class. She also asked that a deeper conversation with the Board regarding the Habits of Mind would be helpful in exploring how they are integrated across all grade levels and how they meet the goals of the District as they seem to drive much of the work being done in the District. Dr. Harrison agreed that there would be value in having a deeper conversation on how they meld into practice. Mr. Montgomery would also like to have a conversation on the Habits of Mind as it would make sense to align particular language with the language used in developing district goals.

	Mr. Montgomery asked how all this work is used to evaluate success and if the outputs are correct. Mr. Lichtenwalner pointed out that there are many ways to assess the outcomes of the work, using data, observation of teachers, student work and assessments. Student work says a lot of how the instruction is being absorbed by the students and using the language of Habits of Mind, etc. Dr. Kor said that the benchmarks have not yet been set as we haven't been able to look at work across the grades and what we can expect by looking at student work, noting that by looking at student work success can be seen using the Fundations. Mr. Montgomery also asked about differentiation and how it is approached, either for challenging students or working with students who need support. Mr. Lichtenwalner noted that assessments are used to look at each child's growth and teachers then use that information to engage and meet the needs of all students wherever they might be on the spectrum.

	The point was raised about other content areas, such as art, music, PE, whereas Dr. Kor reported that work is being done across all content areas and it was suggested that there be further conversation as to what is being done in these other areas.

	David Rubin asked about the how a teacher might respond to a student’s answer on a math question. Ms. Benedetto noted that there will be different ways to arrive at the correct answer and that there might be other strategies to get to the answer and a teacher might point out the more efficient way to reach the answer. Mr. Rubin also stated his concern over the 3rd grade math test, noting that all the information that might be on the test hasn't been presented to all students, and asked that it would create an uneven base to evaluate and assess the results and how would that be reconciled in that situation. Mr. Lichtenwalner responded that the results of the state assessments are more useful on a macro level to evaluate the program, but on an individual basis, there won't be a major impact on a particular student with no repercussions on the student as it often has to do with the pacing of a particular class.  Dr. Harrison related that it is also a flaw of the assessments to the overall intent and design of the assessments.
