Introduction
The Irvington Union Free School District is committed to meeting the New York State
Standards for Professional Learning. These Standards, along with the Irvington Portrait of
a Graduate and District goals, serve as a framework to guide the district's decisions
regarding professional learning.

Professional Learning Plan Goals
“ Create a culture of professional learning, where the expectations are professional
growth and the acquisition of deep pedagogical knowledge.
“ Improve pedagogical practices, instructional methodologies, curricular understanding
and engagement strategies for all staff, resulting in increased levels of student
achievement.

Irvington UFSD Strategic Plan Goals
The 2026-2029 Irvington UFSD Strategic Plan was adopted in the winter of 2026. Four
strategic priorities were identified including: Student Success, Learning Environment,
Stewardship of Resources and Community Engagement. The focus of the Professional
Learning Plan will be on two elements of the Strategic Plan: Student Success and
Learning Environment.

Student Success

* Provide all students with a variety of pathways to maximize their academic growth,
attain essential skills, and explore their passions.

“ Ensure a consistent and balanced assessment system that provides clear, timely, and
meaningful feedback on student progress.

“ Engage in a cycle of program evaluation to ensure that the curriculum is grounded in
research, evidence, and data.

Learning Environment

Foster an affirming, welcoming and inclusive culture for students and staff that
promotes risk taking, resilience, and engagement and flexibility.

Attract and retain high quality educators.

Provide relevant professional learning, which targets the needs of faculty.

Create an environment of support for educators to empower and inspire student
engagement.

New York State Ten Professional Learning Standards

Designing Professional Development: Professional development design is based on
data; is derived from the experience, expertise and needs of the recipients; reflects best
practices in sustained job-embedded learning; and incorporates knowledge of how
adults learn.

Content Knowledge and Quality Teaching: Professional development expands
educators’ content knowledge and the knowledge and skills necessary to provide
developmentally appropriate instructional strategies and assess student progress.
Research-based Professional Learning: Professional development is

research-based and provides educators with opportunities to analyze, apply and
engage in research.

Collaboration: Professional development ensures that educators have the
knowledge, skill and opportunity to collaborate in a respectful and trusting
environment.

Diverse Learning: Professional development ensures that educators have

the knowledge and skill to meet the diverse learning needs of all students.

Student Learning Environments: Professional development ensures that educators
are able to create safe, secure, supportive, and equitable learning environments for all
students.

Parent, Family and Community Engagement: Professional development ensures that
educators have the knowledge, skill, and opportunity to engage and collaborate with
parents, families, and other community members as active partners in children’s
education.

Data-driven Professional Practice: Professional development uses disaggregated
student data and other evidence of student learning to determine professional
development learning needs and priorities, to monitor student progress, and help
sustain continuous professional growth.

Technology: Professional development promotes technological literacy and
facilitates the effective use of all appropriate technology.

Evaluation: Professional development is evaluated using multiple sources of
information to assess its effectiveness in improving professional practice and student
learning.

The Irvington UFSD Plan and Process
This section of the plan outlines the structures and framework for the development and
continued monitoring of the Irvington Professional Learning Plan. The planning team, as
required by New York State, will have a set of responsibilities and a structure and process
for setting and evaluating the plan. Each year the Irvington Professional Learning Plan will
be reviewed for relevance and changes as needed by shifting goals, practices,
expectations and standards.

Irvington Professional Learning Planning Team Timeline:

¢ June-August: Buildings and Departments identify goals for the coming year.
Development of Plans for Superintendent's Conference Day

¢ April: Develop and distribute Needs Survey to all staff (every three years)

* May/June: Review survey results and make recommendations for the following year

Irvington Professional Learning Planning Team will:

“ meet at least once per year to review data, professional learning plan, strategic plan;

“ provide leadership for professional learning by considering resources and systems and
their effectiveness in meeting district goals and expectations;

“ use feedback from professional learning experiences;

collaborate to align professional learning experiences to meet the needs of all staff
with District-wide initiatives and expectations;

“ consider yearly professional learning goals that will reflect current District initiatives;

“ consider opportunities for teachers to enhance their performance in the classroom as
primary learners and classroom leaders;

consider opportunities for in-District CTLE hours.

Areas of Focus for Professional Learning

Assessment

All classroom teachers are expected to create and implement a balanced assessment
system. All classrooms should reflect an appropriate combination of summative and
formative assessment.

Data Analysis

Increased and regular use of data has been established as a goal for the IUFSD. The
District Data Plan should serve as a resource to identify valuable professional learning
necessary to implement the plan.

Compliance Training

Each school year, compliance trainings must be identified and required deadline dates
must be set. The following compliance trainings are required: Child Abuse: Mandatory
Reporting, Discrimination and Harassment, Irvington UFSD Employee Handbook, New
York Education Law 2-d, School Violence: Identifying & Addressing, Student Mental
Health: Awareness, Intervention and Referral, The Dignity for All Students Act, Workplace
Violence and Prevention.

Instructional Design

A well-developed unit of study considers the alignment of each unit component using
assessment, the social-emotional needs of all students, the creation of welcoming and
affirming spaces, alignment with the Portrait of a Graduate, and the effective use of
technology.

Instructional Practices

Instructional practices should take into consideration a range of strategies, approaches
and differentiation. Instructional practices are selected by individual teachers, but also
reflect and align with District/building goals and expectations.

Instructional Technology Integration
As technology continues to expand, professional development is needed to provide
teachers with access and experience with new technology, to support student access.

Portrait of a Graduate
The District adopted a Portrait of a Graduate which provides a framework for curriculum
development, assessments, and student experiences The four tenets - Courageous

What “counts” toward CTLE Hours?

Acceptable CTLE must be conducted through activities designed to improve the teacher
or leader's pedagogical and/or leadership skills, targeted at improving student
performance, including but not limited to formal CTLE activities. Such activities shall
promote the professionalization of teaching and educational leadership, as applicable,
and be closely aligned to District goals for student performance.

How long do I need to maintain the documentation of my CTLE hours?
Educators must maintain CTLE records and documentation for at least three years from
the end of the registration period in which they completed the CTLE.

Do I need to submit my CTLE documentation to NYSED?

Educators will not send documentation of their CTLE clock hours to the Department each
year or for the re-registration process. However, CTLE documentation must be available
upon request by the Department.

For all CTLE activities, educators should be able to provide a Completion of Approved
CTLE Hour(s) Certificate form/approved CTLE certificate, or be able to generate a CTLE
certificate with the information requested on the CTLE completion form.

Summaries of activities listed in professional learning management systems (i.e.

Frontline) will not be accepted as documentation of CTLE clock hours at this time if the
Department requests documentation.

What is the CTLE Language Acquisition Requirement?

The continuing teacher and leader education (CTLE) language acquisition requirement
varies by certificate holder.

ESOL and Bilinqual Education Teachers

Educators who are subject to CTLE, and hold a Professional English to Speakers of Other
Languages (ESOL) certificate or Bilingual Education extension, must complete a
minimum of 50 percent of their required CTLE clock hours in language acquisition
aligned with the core content area of instruction taught, including a focus on best
practices for co-teaching strategies, and integrating language and content instruction for
ELLs.

Other Teachers, School Building Leaders, and School District Leaders

All other Professional teachers, School Building Leader, and School District Leader
certificate holders must complete a minimum of 15 percent of the required CTLE clock
hours dedicated to language acquisition addressing the needs of ELLs, including a focus
on best practices for co-teaching strategies and integrating language and content
instruction for ELLs.

School District Business Leaders

School district business leaders (SDBLs) who hold the Professional SDBL certificate would
complete a minimum of 15 percent of the required CTLE clock hours dedicated to the
needs of ELLs and Federal, State, and local mandates for ELLs.

Teaching Assistants - Level III

Teaching Assistant Level Ill certificate holders must complete a minimum of 15 percent of
the required CTLE clock hours dedicated to language acquisition, addressing the needs of
ELLs and integrating language and content instruction for such ELLs.
