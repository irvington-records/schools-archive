1300 EMPLOYEE ACTIVITIES (formerly 6430)

Political Activities

The Board of Education recognizes the right of its employees, as citizens, to engage in
political activities and to exercise their constitutionally-protected rights to address
matters of public concern.

However, a District employee's constitutional rights to address matters of public
concern are limited when the speech or action occurs on school grounds and/or during
school activities, or when the employee’s speech adversely impacts, or is reasonably
expected to adversely impact, the efficient operation of school business or the
employee’s ability to effectively perform his or her assigned duties. Without limiting the
foregoing, constitutional protection to freedom of expression will not extend to
expressions including, but not limited to, libelous, slanderous, vulgar, lewd, indecent or
obscene words or images or to words or images which by their very use incite others to
damage property or physically injure persons.When speech does not address a matter
of public concern, when such speech occurs on school grounds or during school
activities, or when such speech is reasonably expected to adversely impact school
operations, the District may impose reasonable restrictions on the time, place, manner
and content of such speech, and may impose discipline when appropriate.

Teachers may not use their classrooms or school surroundings as a means to promote
their personal political views and beliefs. However, teachers are encouraged to address
issues of current events for their instructional and informational value to students, to
invite public and/or political figures to visit the classroom as a community resource, and
to motivate students to participate in the political process.

Solicitations by Staff Personnel

Staff members shall not engage in advertising or commercial solicitations on school time
and shall not engage in advertising or commercial activity involving district students at
any time, except as authorized by the Superintendent and/or designee.
