		4. Business of the Board I

		Discussion: 4.1 Discussion on Long Range Planning Proposal

		With regard to the RFP issued for long range planning consultation services, Mr. Montgomery noted that the Board generally supported the idea of bringing in a consultant to facilitate and provide various services in developing a strategic plan, and added that only one response was received to the RFP from a firm that the Board had been in contact with and was interested in, ECRA. It was noted that the process needs to include a cross section of all Irvington community stakeholders but that such surveys for the broader community would be an additional cost. The Long Range Planning Committee Board discussed possible ways to reach the broader community, including conducting an anonymous survey vis a vis Survey Monkey or another such provider to reach students and community members. Additionally, the proposed set of focus groups of various stakeholders, i.e. faculty, students, parents and community members would constitute a cross section of the population. Surveys would available to administrators, faculty, the process will produce a set of district goals and objectives for the period that the strategy plan would cover. The timing will be tight and the process will begin this month with conversations with the Board and be completed by March/April. In order to achieve this timeframe, the majority of the  Board believes that having a consultant run the process is the way to achieve that objective.

		Mrs. Kerner noted that she didn't realize that the community and students would not be part of the survey process and that ultimately it is the community that drives the district. Mr. Grados noted that it is a question as to how much the Board want to spend on the process, reiterating that the community will be included in the focus groups and the Board will figure out how to include them in the surveys.

		In conclusion, Mr. Montgomery still isn't sure that a consultant is the way to go, but that of those firms that were contacted, ERCA would deliver the most in the process. Mrs. Kerner noted that it might be helpful to provide the previous information gathered from the Community Conversation that took place about six to seven years ago.

		11. Presentations/Discussions

		11.1 – Irvington High School Counseling Program (Presentation)

		High School Principal, Dave Cohen, Assistant High School Principal, Deb Mariniello and Guidance Department Chair, Michael DiGennaro, presented an overview of the current guidance program, including its long and short term goals, the plan for each of the four years that a student is at the high school, as well as the range of counseling services that are provided to students by the department. It was noted that there have been several personnel changes within the Department over the past five years and that the program continues to be a work in process as well as a learning experience that will keep cycling back and build upon strong start. Also in attendance were Claudia Rodriguez and Andrew Lund, high school guidance counselors. Heather Attenello was unable to attend.

		The presentation highlighted the framework for the vision of the guidance program which consists of the three pillars which are interwoven and dependent upon each other: college and career path, social-emotional development and academic achievement. Ms. Mariniello provided an overview of the process to advance the guidance program: develop, implement, evaluate and reflect which is done continually. Mr.  Cohen identified the four long term goals that have been developed, such as fostering a strong partnership between student, family and school community, developing confidence, consistently analyzing and assessing the curriculum, and identify and implement systems which support students and will prepare students beyond high school. The short term goals are drilled down and include an analysis of the college process and look for improvements, improving scheduling process, establish proactive communication, streamline the data reporting systems, i.e. eSchool Plus, and foster stronger collaboration with staff, and provide staff development to the guidance counselors to better guide students.

		Mr. DiGennaro provided an overview of the comprehensive four year plan that has been developed, noting that a student's first steps into the career and college process actually begin in sixth grade. In freshman year, students’ begin to identify their learning styles and preferences, to fine-tuning the college process in their senior year. Over the four years, informational meetings are held for students as well as parents regarding the college process.

		The guidance department also provides students with personal, social and academic services on a daily basis, with a minimum of 12 formal meetings with each student.

		Mr. DiGennaro reported that an analysis of the past five years shows that Irvington students submitted 1,435 applications of the top schools which resulted in 363 acceptance, with Irvington at or above the national acceptance rate at many colleges, i.e. Brown University, Columbia, etc. however, Irvington was found to be below in some acceptance rates at select colleges such as Harvard, Princeton, etc.

		The presentation also outlined what is currently underway to advance the Guidance Department, including review of the scheduling process to ensure that the master schedule meets the needs of the 21st century skill sets needed, as well as to identify and correct conflicts in scheduling into ensure that students can take courses that they would like to experience, to look at class sizes in certain content areas, and identify opportunities to strength the "backbone" of the master schedule. Ms. Mariniello concluded that this year has been about finding infrastructure weaknesses and to identify how best to strengthen the program. Mr. Cohen reiterated that the goal is to develop a comprehensive college process and that the administration is committed to building a comprehensive department, with this year’s focus on assessment and evaluation of the program.

		Along with the review process, new programs have been introduced this year which include, but are not limited to, 9th grade parents’ meet and greets to provide a comprehensive overview of the four years of high school, utilization of the Naviance program to support the college application process to a greater degree, improved email communications, as well as weekly guidance meetings to foster growth and consistent practices within the department.

		Mr. DiGennaro also noted that it is top priority for the department to ensure that students’ data be  100% accurate and that a personal goal of his is to see that college acceptance rates be at or above the national average. Board members were interested in gaining a better understanding as to how to best to analyze the student acceptance information and what would be important to look at to be sure that the District is meeting the needs of students and that they are accepted to selective schools. Mr. Montgomery was interested in what the District’s current practice is reaching out to colleges.Maria Kashkin noted that a large amount of time had been spent this year to ensure the accuracy of the report cards and if that amount of time would be ongoing. Mr. Cohen stated that the current leadership team is looking at the issues and hopes that better processes will eliminate such a labor intensive process in the future.

		Mr. Whitney asked what the single greatest strength and greatest weakness of the department. Mr. DeGennaro noted that the are many resources used along with a strong team but must now to show these strengths to the public and the Board; with weaknesses found in the processes and systems, and again noted the turnover in leadership. Mr. Whitney asked what the short term priority is for the Department as there is much on your plate but need to be clear and  focused on what is of utmost importance.

		Mrs. Kerner would like to see a master calendar for parents so they are aware of what is expected at certain times of the year. Mr. DeGennaro said that a "road map"  will be provided on the website for each grade level of what is needed. Mrs. Kerner asked about the technology aspect of what is needed for students and then look at needs from a budget perspective to determine if "the tail is wagging the dog". Mrs. Kerner stressed that it is important that the Board hear from staff as to what might be important to provide, be it technology or other resources, to achieve what has been outlined, especially as the Board approaches the budget as well as the strategic plan.
