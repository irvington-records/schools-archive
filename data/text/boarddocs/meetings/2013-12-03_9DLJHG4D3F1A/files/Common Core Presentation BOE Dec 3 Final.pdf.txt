What Makes Complex Text Complex?
 Subtle and/or frequent transitions
 Multiple and/or subtle themes and purposes
 Density of information
 Unfamiliar settings, topics or events
 Lack of repetition, overlap or similarity in words and sentences
 Complex sentences
 Uncommon vocabulary
 Lack of words, sentences or paragraphs that review or pull things together
  for the student
 Longer paragraphs
 Any text structure which is less narrative and/or mixes structures

Shift 5: Writing from Sources


What
 Students support their ideas in writing, citing specific
    evidence to defend their stance

Shift 6: Academic Vocabulary
What?
   Students will be exposed to and use both Tier 2 and Tier 3 words with
    an expectation of application as learners
     Tier 2 – abstract, general academic (across content areas, encountered in
      written language, high utility
         vary, relative, surface, layer
    Jose avoided playing the ukulele – which word would the teacher pre-teach?
     Tier 3 – Domain-Specific – highly specialized, subject specific, low
      occurrences in text, lacking generalization
         Lava, legislature, circumference
Why?
   General academic vocabulary is critical to comprehension and is
    directly related to a student’s ability to build his/her background
    knowledge increasing their opportunity for higher level learning

Mathematical Practices

①   Make sense of problems and persevere in solving them

②   Reason abstractly and quantitatively

③   Construct viable arguments and critique the reasoning of
    others

④   Model with mathematics

⑤   Use appropriate tools strategically

⑥   Attend to precision

⑦   Look for and make use of structure

⑧   Look for and express regularity in repeated reasoning

A Teacher’s Perspective on CCLS for Math


    “I am so proud of the students and the way they have risen to the
    challenge of the changes to the math program this year. The start was a
    little rough because there was so much newness. New language, new
    procedures, new concepts, new ways to deliver instruction and new higher
    expectations. Now that we have worked through the "newness" the kids
    are able to see that they can work to meet the higher
    expectations. Overall my students are feeling new confidence in
    themselves and their ability. They are doing a much better job than
    students of past years in many areas. They are becoming more thoughtful
    when reading a word problem. They are able to explain the thinking they
    are doing behind the math. They are learning to show their thinking with
    numbers, pictures and words.

    We still have a long road ahead of us but it can only be easier and our
    students will be better mathematicians and thinkers.” 4th Grade Teacher
