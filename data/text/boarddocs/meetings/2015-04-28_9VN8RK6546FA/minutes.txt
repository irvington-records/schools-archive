	3. Discussions

	Discussion: 3.1 Main Street School Presentation

	Joyce Chapnick, Main Street School principal, highlighted that the idea of being a global citizen is steeped in everyday life at MSS. Currently enrollment at MSS is a total of 163 students, 123 students in Grade 4 and 140 in Grade 5. The ELA curriculum at MSS utilizes Reader's and Writer's Workshops, which are Common Core aligned. ELA curriculum also integrates texts with people and places around the world. In math, the curriculum is Common Core aligned. Math Specialist, Christine Rosner, has been working with teachers to unpack the math modules and has been helping teachers to fully understand what it means to be a teacher of math. Ms. Chapnick highlighted the social studies and science curriculum.

	Integration of technology at MSS is across all content areas and examples were provided by month of what students will study using technology, with integrated projects as well as technology software, such as Carrell software. Special areas of opportunities for students in music, PE, Music and Library were reviewed and Ms. Chapnick noted that MSS offers experiences. MSS hosts events that include parents to participate in their child's experiences, such as Parents as Reading Partners.

	Ms. Chapnick stated that character building is the cornerstone at MSS, vis a vis, Habits of Mind, Dispositions, Monthly assemblies, morning meetings, etc. and are embedded in all areas at MSS. Students connect with their school, but also the wider community and are asked to think beyond themselves. Good choices are celebrated through assemblies where students are recognized for their actions. She again reiterated that global citizenship is sprinkled throughout the entire curriculum. Students generate ideas, create posters, and conduct fundraising such as the recent Hike for Hunger which they identified to donate their funds.

	Ms. Chapnick reviewed the support that is provided to teachers to build collective capacity   and the development of a focus around the dispositions, using faculty meetings, study groups, math coach, etc. Ms. Chapnick shared a video of a math lesson, demonstrating how students are working together to solve a math problem.

	Related to Theory 3 in the Theory of Action, Ms. Chapnick identified the cycle of observation, formal and informal observation with the teacher and the Framework for Teaching which is the evaluation tool for teachers, to reflect on what worked and the evidence in a particular lesson and to provide feedback for the teacher in the post-observation.

	In response to Mr. Whitney’s question as to how much time is provided for spent with Social Studies, Ms, Chapnick noted that although here is flex time built into the schedule , social studies is routine. Ms. Kashkin thanked Ms. Chapnick for her very informative presentation and especially the video of student's learning. She asked how teachers know that students are getting the curriculum. Ms. Chapnick noted that there are mid-session assessments, exit tickets, etc. Teachers are getting qualitative and quantitative data on a daily basis. Mrs. Kerner asked about the time devoted to specials on a weekly basis and if ICT classes were larger by design. She also asked if there are too many initiatives for teachers and how they juggle all the commitments. Ms. Chapnick noted that there are structures and commitments are staggered but not teachers are committed to all initiatives.

	In response to Mr. Montgomery’s inquiry for clarification of what is Fountas & Pinnel, Ms. Chapnick explained that it is a one to one assessment tool used in ELA, where the teacher can monitor a student's fluency level. Mr. Whitney asked if there were any technology components  that might not be working or might be revised. Ms. Chapnick noted that the technology curriculum has been developed overtime and Dr. Kor noted that many of the projects will be replaced with the introduction of Project Lead the Way next year and incorporated into the classroom. Mr. Montgomery asked how the theories (of action) were arrived at and if they are a means to another end, or a goal onto themselves. Dr. Harrison provided that the Theories were introduced several years ago and it was a way to develop a common language throughout all schools, with administrators brainstorming what they wanted to see in the classrooms and to prepare students for the future. Dr. Kor further stated that they are global, flexible and generic and it is a means to communicate a consistent message across the entire district. Mr. Montgomery would like to go deeper as to how success is defined by the Board.

	Discussion: 3.2 World Language Plan

	Dr.Kor reported that consultant Greg Duncan is extremely knowledgeable and shares a similar goal with District’s world language program which is to ensure that speaking is a strong component of our program. Mr. Duncan has visited all MS and HS language classes and recommends targeting our French and Spanish program this year and will refine the action plan to set benchmarks for each course so that teachers are clear as to expectations to ensure more rigor in student experiences. The assessments are will be reviewed to assured they are aligned with the benchmarks and to incorporate technology into the world language program to provide experiences with the language to move forward with the goals of the program. Board members asked how the Board will be involved in setting the mission/vision of the program Mr. Whitney asked about how the Board will be involved in setting/creating the mission of the program. and suggested that there should be discussions between administration and the Board to develop the mission, which would outline the goal of the program and ultimately what it should look like i.e., literacy fluency, speaking fluency. Integration of parent/student feedback regarding the program was suggested as well and need to explore ways to bring voices to the conversation. There was considerable dialogue between the Board as to what their role should be in setting the direction/mission/goals of the world language program and if having a K-12 program is a desirable outcome which is more of a philosophical conversation. Mr. Whitney stated that it should be the faculty’s responsibility to construct a mission and relay it to the Board as they are the experts and it is the Board’s responsibility is to provide oversight as the Board is not knowledgeable enough to get the faculty in a direction that can be followed. Ms. Kashkin stated that Board members are not educators and do not set goals for any other department. The Board also discussed the benefits of introducing at lower grades which would impact other subject areas. Mrs. Kashkin noted that it is her understanding is that the current work is to how to strengthen our current 6-12 program, not necessarily looking at bringing language to lower grades. Dr. Kor confirmed that work currently being undertaken is to ensure that established benchmarks are being met and student are achieving those benchmarks and not a conversation of expanding the program. Dr. Harrison noted that such a conversation would relate to long range planning that would focus on defining what an experience or success looks like in Irvington and would enable objectives to be established to focus on what the programs would look like. Mr. Montgomery would like to see the Board take ownership of the big picture issues and it should be a process of gathering information, and getting input from the community, and tapping into experts in the community as well as educators. Mr. Whitney asked the Board where they would like to begin the process, i.e. strengthening the 6-12 program or start with a blank slate. It was agreed that the work that has begun should be continued but having community conversation as a starting point would be valuable. Dr. Harrison suggested that the Board begin to think about the questions that are trying to be answered. Mr. Montgomery would like the administration to give a run down on what the value and educational benefits of a world language program are and what can be learned from research. The Board discussed how best to go about the process in order for this to be moved forward by developing a timeline with specific targets to meet. Mrs. Kerner referenced previous community conversations about this topic and noted that it would be interesting to see what was desirable at that time.  She also stated that language has been important to parents.

	Dr. Kor agreed that proficiency levels would be established and what students are expected to know as they move along in the program. Dr. Kor also stated that the resources used are also part of the work being done. It was also noted that the assurance should be that students are learning the same material consistently, teacher notwithstanding.

	Mr. Montgomery asked what the plan is for Greek and if it would be expanded to three years. Dr. Kor stated that the challenge of staffing, but that Irvington is lucky to have a staff member who brings some expertise but only a handful of students are interested and the question becomes what is the threshold that a program can be sustained. Mr. Montgomery noted that it is a unique facet of Irvington and it would be desirable to find a way to expand the program. Mrs. Kerner would challenge the administration to be innovative to provide the students that which is desirable in an education in Irvington and would encourage them to seek ways to support their learning and meet their passion. Dr. Kor noted that Dave Cohen is working on sequencing of courses which has not occurred in the past.

	Mr. Montgomery feels that there should be standard, well -articulated curriculum in world language to use instead of creating a curriculum in Rubicon Atlas. Dr. Kor believes that it is necessary to use Rubicon Atlas as it is with other subjects. Dr. Harrison stressed that it is important for teachers to take ownership through the creation of a particular curriculum. Dr. Kor also stressed that the goal of all curriculum is to provide our students with authentic experiences, and in world language it is to provide opportunities for students to speak with native speakers. Mr. Montgomery doesn’t believe that it is the authentic experiences should be the goal but a means to the goal of the program.
