In preparation for Tuesday’s Board Meeting we are providing an overview of work that
reflects changes in curriculum and instruction, professional learning and expectations of

   ● Shared responsibility for students’ literacy development
           ○ ELA-specific standards include fiction, poetry, and drama but also literary
               nonfiction (e.g., speeches, essays, and historical documents)
           ○ Literacy standards specific to history/social studies and science, and

               deeper knowledge, skill and thinking
   ● Interest in developing a strong, well aligned writing curriculum
   ● Interest in expanding learning opportunities by providing a range of courses, both
       selectives and electives

Last year was the first year for this course. As a result of feedback and experience, the
following changes occurred:

       Strategies for Timed Writing
          ➔ Based on student feedback, a unit called “Strategies for Timed Writing” was
             added early in the year
                 ◆ Students develop and practice strategies for breaking down a complex
                     task, preparing for timed writing when the prompt is given in
                     advance, outlining in a way that is responsive to the task, collecting
                     and choosing the best evidence, and planning analytical thinking.
                 ◆ Students learn how to address a variety of tasks including text-based
                     analysis, text-based argument, thematic essays, and DBQs.
                 ◆ The unit is scaffolded so students practice tasks from several
                     disciplines (reading, writing, social studies, science, etc.) first with the
                     prompt given in advance.
                 ◆ Over the course of the unit, students how to condense their processes
                     to outline when the prompt isn’t given in advance. In essence, the unit
                     teaches students to write a more successful response by addressing
                     all parts of a task, adapting to the intended audience, and preparing
                     critical thinking in a short time period.

       Interdisciplinary Unit Design
          ➔ Expanded dialogue with our social studies and science colleagues in order to
              build interdisciplinary connections for students and to support literacy in the
              content areas.
                 ◆ The ninth grade team has an ongoing dialogue with social studies and
                      science about ways to improve critical thinking in students’ writing.
                      As a result of being in the second year of implementation, teachers
                      have a better idea of students’ needs and processes and are
                      supporting a broader variety of tasks from other disciplines.

Current Observations based on Student Feedback and Practice
   ➔ Students saw value in the personal narrative unit as preparation for writing
      the college essay. Likewise, they also see value in the Strategies for Timed
      Writing unit, since it applies to so many other content areas. Although many
      students would like to have more space in their schedules, they are cognizant
      of the role of the course in teaching them foundational writing skills for high
      school and college expectations.
   ➔ Students are learning to value planning (outlining) and revising as critical
      pieces of the writing process. We are seeing less “on the fly” preparation and
      more critical thinking as they apply the strategies, processes, and habits
      being taught.
   ➔ After reading the student reflections after the personal statement unit, it
      seems that the students were surprised how much consistent drafting and
      rethinking of their work can benefit the final product. This seemed to be the
      biggest take-away, which is precisely what this course is trying to reinforce.
   ➔ Students really had a great learning experience when it came to writing a
      personal statement with word count limits. I think, in the past, they were
      given a lot of freedom with the length of the assignment, and as a result, their
      writing was not always effective - lots of fluff and unnecessary details. The
      importance of editing was also a huge take-away.

New Course - Comic Books and Graphic Novels as Literature
  ➔ Course Description: This course examines graphic novels and comics as a literary
     art form, analyzing works in a variety of genres. From the re-imagining of Joseph
     Campbell’s “hero’s journey” in popular comic book superheroes past and present to
     the socio-cultural impact of graphic novels such as Persepolis and Maus, this course
     seeks to strengthen students’ ability to decipher the signs and symbols of text, to
     build a knowledge of different modes of literary criticism and to apply various
     critical lenses to the engaging world of sequential art. In terms of the District Theory
     of Action, it is designed to challenge students with rigorous texts in a collaborative,
     constructivist classroom through activities and features such as our online
     discussion forum.
  ➔ Class web site: www.tinyurl.com/ihscomics
  ➔ Student feedback: While most students feel the material to be highly engaging,
     many have expressed that the course is much more challenging than they expected.
     Students have completed two essays so far this semester on two full-length graphic
     novels, as well as weekly reading and writing assignments.




New Course - Existential Literature: Heroes & Victims
  ➔ Course Description: From Shakespeare’s Hamlet to television’s Walter White,
     existential characters struggle between living meaningful, authentic lives in a
     disordered, chaotic world or surrendering to the numbing absurdity of modern life.
     In this course, students examine how existential thinkers and writers confront this
     dilemma by creating both existential heroes and victims. Students will analyze
     representative texts from a variety of authors, genres and time periods, including
     Fyodor Dostoyevsky, Rainer Maria Rilke, T.S. Eliot, Philip K. Dick, Kurt Vonnegut,
     David Foster Wallace, Charles Bukowski, and others. In addition to works of
     literature, this course will analyze examples of existentialism in film and television
     as well. In terms of the District Theory of Action, it is designed to challenge students
     with rigorous texts in a collaborative, constructivist classroom through activities
     and features such as our online discussion forum.
  ➔ Class web site: www.tinyurl.com/irvexistlit
  ➔ Student feedback: The level of discourse around extremely dense and complex
     existential concepts and themes has exceeded my expectations and students have
     also felt the need to “up their game.” Students have completed two essays so far this
     semester on two full-length graphic novels, as well as weekly reading and writing
     assignments.

New Course - Presentational Speaking
  ➔ Course Description: In this course, students learn skills and strategies for
     communicating effectively in a variety of settings and scenarios. Formal speaking
     and listening tasks provide opportunities for students to develop evidence, word
     choice, tone, and rhetoric in order to present information and/or convey
     perspective. Students also learn to adapt organization, development, substance, and
     style for different audiences, purposes, and tasks. They integrate diverse sources of
     information, including the strategic use of digital media, to support findings and
     enhance interest when presenting. Students prepare and research in advance of a
     range of collaborative discussions (including one-on-one, in groups, and teacher-
     led) designed to promote the thoughtful, well-reasoned exchange of ideas. Students
     work with peers to promote civil, democratic discussions and decision-making
     based on the exploration of a full range of perspectives on a given issue. Students
     perform the following types of speeches: a speech of introduction, an informative
     speech, a persuasive speech and an impromptu speech.

➔ Accountable Talk
  A greater focus has been placed on communication this year at IMS both as writers
  and in discussion. Through “Accountable Talk” common expectations and practices
  can be seen across classrooms.
   (Accountable Talk refers to talk that is meaningful, respectful, and mutually beneficial to both
   speaker and listener. Accountable talk uses evidence appropriate to the discipline (e.g., proofs
   in mathematics, data from investigations in science, textual details in literature, documentary
   sources in history). Accountable talk stimulates higher-order thinking—helping students to
   learn, reflect on their learning, and communicate their knowledge and understanding.)

   Much of this work is based on practices that have been built over the last several
   years:
      ➔ Instructional strategies such as the Fishbowl, Socratic Seminar and book
          talks
      ➔ Questioning and discussion as a focal point amongst teachers during
          professional learning sessions
      ➔ Structures that have become part of student experiences across disciplines
          and are used to engage students in deeper thinking about content and the
          connections that exist across subject areas

Exploring a Partnership with Bard University
As a district, we have had several conversations about writing and developing consistency.
Our Assured Writing Experiences (AWEs) were implemented with the goal of addressing standards
and genres cohesively, but at this point we need a well articulated writing program that reflects
identified outcomes for grade levels with clearly defined expectations. This will include:
a common approach about teaching writing as a district: what and how we assess, what rigor is, and
how much writing we expect students to engage in during the course of a year.
    ➔ Bard University offers a program aligned to all of our district work, including UbD work.
    ➔ Bard’s focus is about thinking.
    ➔ There are basic PD courses we would provide for middle school and high school teachers
        including special education teachers
    ➔ We will start in spring (May) and develop a plan in collaboration with Bard
    ➔ We could build in coaching model and additional workshops next year, including one-on-
        one work and group work
    ➔ Bard is a well-established and well-respected program designed to support secondary and
        college level teaching.
