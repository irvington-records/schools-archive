    Overview
    Positive school climate is an essential component of successful and effective schools. It is defined as shared beliefs, values, and attitudes that
    shape interactions between students, teachers, and administrators, while setting the parameters of acceptable behavior and norms for the
    school.

    The Irvington Union Free School District (UFSD) School Quality and Climate Surveys asked parents, community members, staff members, and
    students in grades 6-12 for feedback on school climate, district leaders, and district initiatives. Results will be used to inform decisions at the
    campus and district levels.

    K12 Insight partnered with district team members to develop the surveys, which addressed the following topics:

               Academic support
               Student support
               School leadership
               Family involvement
               Safety and behavior
               Communication
               Board of Education
               District Leadership Team
               Capital project
               Strategic objectives

    Details of the Study and Understanding the Results
    The surveys were open April 9-29.

    Parents, community members, staff members, and students in grades 6-12 could participate via a public link on the district’s website. The
    survey was translated into Spanish. Paper surveys were available on request.

    Pre-survey communications included emails, a phone script, a district website description, and social media posts.

    This report summarizes survey results and and breaks them down by participant group. Results do not reflect random sampling; therefore,
    they should not be generalized to all Irvington UFSD parents, community members, staff members, and students in grades 6-12. Rather, results
    reflect only the perceptions and opinions of survey participants. School-level reports are also included in this project.

    Findings for each item in the report exclude participants who did not answer. In charts and graphs, data labels less than 5 percent are not
    shown. Percentages may not total 100 due to rounding.

     Irvington – Parents and Community Members
     Do you have any specific recommendations for improving the quality of the educational experience at Irvington? (N=283)
     Parents said they want improvements in the instruction of English Language Arts, particularly when it comes to writing instruction.
     Parents said they want more technology education and better technology available for their children within the schools.
     Parents said they would like the schools to focus on 21st century learning skills such as project-based learning,
     collaboration, and problem-solving.

     Parents said they would like more languages available and for language instruction to start earlier in their students'
     educational career.

     Irvington – Students
     Do you have any specific recommendations for improving the quality of the educational experience at Irvington? (N=328)
     Students said they want better technology available and more reliable WIFI.
     Students said that teachers should be more consistent in how they treat students and ensure students are mastering content.
     Students said they were concerned about building security.
     Students said they would like less homework assigned to them.

        •   93% of participating parents, 85% of participating staff members, and 79% of participating students rated their school as excellent or good. It
            may be beneficial to explore why such a higher proportion of parents rated their school as excellent or good compared with staff members.

        •   87% of staff members agreed or strongly agreed that students receive timely feedback on their work compared with 56% of students. Sharing
            this discrepancy with teachers may help them understand their feedback is not as timely as they think it is.

        •   81% of staff members agreed or strongly agreed that teachers successfully show how lessons relate to life outside of school compared with
            36% of students. Developing ways to show how lessons relate to life outside of school can help increase curriculum relevance and student
            engagement.

        •   50% of students agreed or strongly agreed that school administrators make decisions that are in the best interests of students and that
            school administrators make themselves available when they have a concern.

        •   40% of parents indicated they do not know if staff members are aware of safety and security procedures and 31% indicated they do not know
            if students are aware of safety and security procedures. Sharing information about how staff members and students are taught and practice
            the safety and security procedures can help increase the perception of school safety.

        •   Parent, community member, staff member, and student participants selected safety and security as their first choice for a capital project.

        •   “Providing students with a rigorous, comprehensive, enriched, and diversified curricula; expanding STEM education and integrating
            technology” was the strategic objective with the highest weighted score from parents, community members, and students and the second
            highest weighted score from staff members. “Fostering the social and emotional growth of all students and promoting a culture where
            students are active participants in society including civic and global engagement and learning” was the strategic objective with the highest
            weighted score from participating staff members.
