5. Presentations/Discussions

Presentation: 5.1 Dows Lane Presentation

Deb Mariniello, Principal, and Meg Benedetto, Asst. Principal, shared some of the work that is taking place at Dows Lane, with the aim of raising the bar and building a comprehensive program, looking at it as a whole school. Ms. Mariniello noted that initiatives are collaborative, and includes members of the Site and PAC committee. Ms. Benedetto spoke to the Fundations program which was extended to 2nd grade this year, which is a systematic and sequential program, providing a phonological basis for reading and writing. Project Lead The Way is also a team effort with help from the PTSA, and provides students the experience of digging into STEM learning, allowing for collaboration among students. In the area of mathematics, the goal has been to increase the accountable talk, a deeper level of understanding in mathematics and allows for flexible grouping. In the area of UBD - Teaching for Understanding, Ms. Mariniello noted that 27 teachers participated in a three day workshop to extend teachers' thinking, with all speaking the same curriculum language. Ms. Benedetto noted that early release days are valuable for this type of teacher collaboration. She spoke of the Response to Intervention --- intervention tiers that are in place at Dows Lane to provide the appropriate level of support for students. Character Building activities were highlighted, including Habits of Mind, monthly themes, and incorporating what it is to be present and truly aware of one’s environment. Ms. Mariniello spoke to the school-wide expectations that began this year, beginning with the morning message that is said each day, teaching expected behaviors while in common areas, such as at recess. A video that was made with staff to show students what is unacceptable and acceptable behavior was shared.

Through the use of technology, Dows Lane is building capacity with Google Tools, Chromebook Buckets and iPad apps. The staff has explored what the potentials are for their grade and the work of the Technology Committee is shared with all staff. The work is comprehensive, thoughtful and purposeful in order to build a plan that is sustainable. Ms. Mariniello thanked the Board and parents for their support.

6. 2016-17 Proposed Budget Overview and Adoption (attachment)

Information: 6.1 2016-17 Proposed Budget Overview

Dr. Harrison began with the budget process used in creating the recommended budget, with the objective of continuing to provide an excellent educational program while maintaining a responsible tax rate while keeping the budget below cap. Inclusions into the recommended budget include field maintenance by an outside vendor and increase World Language by .02 FTE which will provide greater flexibility in scheduling at the high school level. Some considerations that were not included in the budget included purchase of 40 air conditioners, increase of technology staff, library media specialist, etc. Noteworthy initiatives include increased STEM/PLTW staffing at IHS, professional development, purchase of a technology switch, creation of modified volleyball team, and enhanced maintenance of athletic fields, etc.

The Tax Cap Calculation was renewed and Dr. Harrison again noted that Irvington had a tax cap of-.07% or $37,000 less than the 2015-16 levy, which created hurdles but through creative budgeting and efficiencies, the proposed budget remains under the cap, with the help of additional state aid. The proposed budget of $58,330,000 is 1.15% budget to budget increase. Tax rate doesn't directly align with the budget to budget tax rate, but with changes in assessments, the estimated actual tax rate increase is 1.25%, with the average assessed home of $803,075 realizing a tax rate increase of $214 per household. Dr. Harrison reported that the PTSA has offered to support athletic equipment needs and will fund approximately $15,000 and thanked them for their support in helping to with the budget process two years in a row to help get to a target budget.

Action (Consent): 10.1 SEQRA Resolution - Dows Lane Elementary School and Main Street School - Smart Schools Projects

Resolution: WHEREAS, the Board of Education of the Irvington Union Free School District (“District”) has proposed to perform various capital improvement projects at Dows Lane Elementary School and Main Street School and

WHEREAS, the Board wishes to fully comply with its obligations under the State Environmental Quality Review Act (“SEQRA”) and the regulations thereunder with respect to the proposed action, in its capacity as Lead Agency; and

WHEREAS, the Board has carefully considered the nature and scope of the proposed action; and

WHEREAS, upon review of the foregoing, the Board makes the following determinations: 1. The proposed actions involve performing various capital improvement projects at Dows Lane Elementary School and Main Street School. 2. The proposed project represents the replacement, rehabilitation or reconstruction of a structure or facility, in kind, on the same site within the meaning of 6 NYCRR § 617 (5) (c) (2); and/or alternatively represents a routine activity of an educational institution within the meaning of 6 NYCRR § 617.5 (c) (8); and/or alternatively represents maintenance or repair involving no substantial changes in the existing structure or facility within the meaning of 6 NYCRR § 617 (5) (c) (1). 3. The proposed action will in no case have a significant adverse impact based on the environment based on the criteria contained in 6 NYCRR § 617.7 (c) and are not otherwise a Type I action as defined by 6 NYCRR § 617.4. 4. The proposed action is a Type II action within the meaning of 6 NYCRR § 617.5 and are therefore not subject to review under SEQRA and the regulations thereunder.

NOW THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED, that the Board finds and concludes that the proposed action is a Type II action within the meaning of 6 NYCRR § 617.5 and therefore are not subject to review under SEQRA and the regulations thereunder.
