                 BOCES 2016-17 OVERVIEW & ANNUAL BUDGET REPORT

WHAT IS BOCES
BOCES, for those who aren’t familiar with it, stands for Board of Educational Cooperative
Services. New York State has created a number of regional BOCES. We, along with several
other dozen area districts, are served by Southern Western BOCES. It’s mission is to
collaborate with school districts and communities to meet educational challenges by providing
regional leadership, and cost-effective, high-quality services.

COST SAVINGS AND SHARE SERVICES FOR DISTRICTS
Community members sometimes ask what are ways our district can save on costs?
Participation in SW BOCES is one method. When the district uses certain services or makes
certain purchases, the cost is reduced through the regional shared services or the increased
purchasing power a regional BOCES offers. For example, through SW BOCES, component
district students can receive special education and vocational-technical instruction that
individual districts would be hard pressed to offer.

At the annual budget meeting I attended on April 6th, I sat with a group of administrators who
were proud of the new video production center that was recently completed. Students studying
at the center learn about production and have opportunities to visit HBO to learn more about
careers in the field. At the meeting we were served a delicious meal by BOCES culinary
students from Pleasantville, New Rochelle, and White Plains who will continue to study culinary
arts after graduation. As we look to improve our districts web services, we are using the
BOCES' Lower Hudson Regional Information Center (LHRIC) to help provide guidance.

Additionally for Irvington and a handful of other districts, BOCES is a source of revenue, as they
rent space from our district; the 2016-17 budget includes $300,537 in revenue to IUFSD.
Additionally, State Aid on BOCES services is budgeted at $405,663. BOCES provides many
more services that can be found on their website http://swboces.org/

COST-DRIVERS FOR BOCES
Our BOE members were wondering what the BOCES cost-drivers are when we reviewed our
own budget and noted increased line items for BOCES. For IUFSD, all BOCES codes in the
IUFSD 2016-17 budget add up to $2,298,129.

In the BOCES administrative budget presentation, it was clear that many components are
contained or have been lowered; for example, administrative costs are down nearly 9%. The
one area where there budget has grown significantly, is their retirement healthcare costs, this
year it increased 12.3% or $671,000. I believe we should follow-up to understand how their
growth in health care costs may continue balloon over the next decade and what that may mean
for our district. As a component district, our board will be voting on their budget and new board
members at our next meeting on April 19th. The annual cost to participate in SW BOCES is
fairly low, under $9,000. What was not made clear at the budget meeting, but as a new member
I would like to follow up on is why other costs for services are also increasing. Perhaps we can
invite BOCES to provide a presentation to our BOE in the future.
