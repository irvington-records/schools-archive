 Understanding Increased Special Education Expenses
                                     22

Over the past 10 years, special education services and requirements have
changed dramatically
 As a result of these shifts in the landscape of education and student
  needs, the budget has increased significantly:
   2005-06 Special Ed Budget            $4,156,392
   2010-11    Special Ed Budget         $6,574,022
   2015-16    Special Ed Budget         $8,381,916
                Increase over 10 years: $4,225,524
 Excess Cost Aid (includes public, private & high cost) decreased while
  expenses have increased:
   2005-06 Aid                           $697,763
   2010-11    Aid                        $667,623
   2015-16    Aid                        $661,494
                Decrease over 10 years:   $ 36,269

    Understanding Increased Special Education Expenses
                                                            23

●    Shifts in Student Needs/Disabilities
      Number of students with IEPs 10 year history
         о Low: 171
         о Mean: 184
         о High/Current: 207
      Out of District Placements:
         о   Tuition/transportation/related services costs typically exceed $100,000/student
         о   Low: 21
         о   Median: 25
         о   High: 28
         о   Current : 24
      Although our numbers have not fluctuated greatly over the past ten years the types of disabilities
       represented in our population has shifted


    The complexity of the needs of the students has increased
      The Other Health Impaired Classification which notably includes ADHD increased from 7.5% or our
       disabled population to 25.6% over the time span
      Autism has risen from 11.5 to 16.9% over the time span. Many autistic students have significant needs
       which tend to be very costly to address
      Speech Language Impaired dropped from 25% to 15%. This category tends to be relatively inexpensive to
       serve. Many are now served by preventative services outside of special education
      Learning Disabilities have dropped from 33% to 28%. This also tends to be a relatively less expensive
       group to serve

Understanding Increased Special Education Expenses
                                     26

There have been numerous shifts in the financial landscape related
to special education:
 Out of district tuitions rise, on average, 8% per year
 Despite increases in tuition obligations, there’s been a $33,000 decline
  in federal aide
 State aid for “excess cost” for special educations services has declined.
 State supported schools and Children’s Residential Project (CRP)
  placements are no longer paid directly by the state and were shifted to
  district budgets
 John Cardinal O’Connor School did not exist before the 09-10 school
  year for which we provide CSE oversight and staffing
 Legal fees were moved from the general legal fees line to a budget line
  in the PPS budget

           New Budgetary Considerations
                                        37

● Consideration #1
IMS/IHS 1.0  FTE Library Media Specialist                      $106,000
We currently have 1 LMS for over a 1,000 students. This position has been
developed to support the needs of the library as well as a ripple effect in
technology and study skills. This position would allow for the increase of PLTW
and technology instruction at the MS and HS. Additionally, we would increase the
librarian staff at the Campus by .06 FTE. This person would also assume
responsibility for teaching a revamped study skills curriculum at the MS that will
focus on research skills, study strategies, and organization.

● Consideration #2
IHS     0.2  FTE PLTW Principles of Engineering                $21,200
This position allows for expansion and will permit us to add another required
class to the PLTW engineering sequence. The additional engineering class could
be taught within the science department.

           New Budgetary Considerations
                                        38

● Consideration #3
MSS/DL 1.0   FTE Technology Teacher                             $106,000
This will permit the growth of PLTW to grades 2 and 5 while creating the
opportunity for increased technology instruction in all elementary grades. The
latter has always been a challenge as we only have one technology teacher for
grades K-5.

● Consideration #4
MSS/DL 0.5   FTE School Psychologist                            $53,000
We currently have a psychologist who is part-time (.8 at MSS and .2 MS/HS).
Therefore, 1 day a week Main Street School does not have the necessary support
for students. In addition, the increase as well as ongoing mental health needs that
continue to be present at all levels, has increased the need for psychologist
support. With the additional FTE, we could increase levels of support, provide
more proactive programming to support students in need, and continue to
decrease the overwhelming caseloads of our psychologists.
