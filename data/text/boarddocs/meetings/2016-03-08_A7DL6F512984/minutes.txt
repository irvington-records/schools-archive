	4. Presentations/Discussions

	Presentation: 4.1 Budget Presentation: Curriculum, Athletics, Technology and Special Education/Pupil Personnel Services and Employee Benefits (Attachment)

	Dr. Harrison reviewed the previous meetings where the budget and its challenges this year were discussed  with respect to the tax levy cap and reiterated the fiscal and educational objectives in preparing the proposed budget.

	Some of the budget points highlighted in the presentation include but not limited to:

-
		Instruction: budget category variance = 2.46% ($420,373) which includes but not limited to contractural salary increases and increase in textbook requests.

		Co-curricular: budget category variance = 1.63% ($2,011) for contactural salary increases

-
		Curriculum Improvement/Administration = 4.24% (73,827 which accounts for recoding of one clerical salary and recoding of Rubicon.

-
		Instructional technology: budget category variance = 11.56% ($120,214) which incorporates a change in purchasing practices with no leasing of equipment as well as salary increases, increased cost of outside contracts as well as supplies for PLTW.

-
		Guidance: budget category variance =3.22% connected with contractural salary increases and costs of administering PSAT exams.

-
		Special Education-Scott Palermo and Gail Krieger spoke of the challenges and changes to special education program and services in the district, noting the increases since 2005-06 school year as well as the excess cost aid that the District has received which has decreased $36,269 over the past 10 years. Mr. Palermo reviewed the shifts in the disabilities among our student population, with the current high of 207 classified students with increased referrals to CSE, and noted that the number of students placed out of district hasn't fluctuated greatly with this year 24 students out of district but the cost of such placements has increased as has the complexity of needs of our students that require  more expensive services and programs. It was noted that when comparing costs per student, Irvington seems higher than in other districts and to determine why, it is important to identify what costs are being factored into the costs to understand the difference in cost. The Board requested such comparison data. Residential placement costs were reviewed as were staffing increases over the past 10 years. It was noted that the PPS office has been greatly impacted by having the John Cardinal O'Connor School in the district since 2009 with respect to administrators' and clerical time, with services billed back to the student's home district. Currently there are 40 students attending JCOS each requiring at least an annual meeting, with the coordination being done by the clerical for each meeting. Overall, there is a budget variance of 2.30% ($192,482) which comprises contractural salary increases as well as increased out of district tuitions. Mr. Montgomery noted that he was unclear as to why the costs in special education has grown approximately 7% yearly. The Board would like 10 years reconciliation of special education tuition to better understand the costs escalations. It was noted that the trend in numbers in Irvington as well as statewide, are growing, with no reduction anytime soon.

-
		The PPS budget includes contractural salary increases as well as increases in health services for Irvington students placed out of district, driving the cost increase of 6.97% ($79,373).

-
		With respect to the athletic budget, there is a decrease of 0.73% (-$6,313) due to a decrease in equipment needs.

-
		Employee benefits has an increase for 2016-17 of 1.78% ($219,806) due to ERS blended rate of 17.72% and TRS rate of approximately 12%, down from 19.78% and 13.26% respectively. Health Insurance rates are expected to increase 4.05% for active employees.

	For the 2016-17 school year, the proposed revenue is expected to increase by $51,534 over

	this year, which includes an increase in fund balance appropriation to $500,000 from $420,000. The total proposed budget is $58,880,510 which will require a tax levy or cuts to balance the budget of $1,095,976. A review of the budget summary shows a 2.13% budget increase. Dr. Harrison noted if the tax cap levy was a true 2%, the proposed budget would be very close to the cap.

	Several new budget considerations were presented:

-
		1.0 FTE Library Media specialist,

-
		.02 FTE increase to expand PLTW

-
		1.0 FTE technology teacher and

-
		.05 FTE school psychologist

	These budgetary considerations total $286,200, a budget to budget increase 2.62% or a total budget of $59,175,710. Administrators provided the Board with the rationale for these considerations and how each would enhance programs and support students. In order to support the additional psychologist, having the caseload of the 5.5 psychologists would be helpful.

	Dr. Harrison spoke to the ramifications of staying within the tax cap and provided examples of reductions that might be considered should the Board decide to stay under the cap, with reductions in transportation and building repairs/maintenance, elimination of proposed new website, two teaching positions and one clerical, miscellaneous reductions in technology, professional development, supplies, etc. He also spoke to the implications of increasing the amount allocated from the fund balance.

	The Superintendent will present his proposed budget on March 22nd with several meetings to discuss the proposed budget prior to the April 19th meeting for budget adoption.

	The Board responded to the presentation with specific questions for greater clarity on the information presented regarding secondary salaries being higher than elementary level salaries, increases in technology equipment, with the request to have what the specific needs are in this area, increases in BOCES codes and asked for further information on the cost drivers in this area. It was suggested that districts should look closer at BOCES budgetary operations.

	With respect to the new considerations as well as possible reductions, the Board considered each of the new considerations as well as reductions but thought it best to discuss in context of going over the tax cap. The Board discussed the tax cap ramifications and consensus was the following:

	Mr. Hanna would be hesitant to increase use of fund balance as it is not a sustainable strategy and poses risk to the District down the road but might need to consider increase; he also believes in a long term solution rat her than short term fixes to the budget. Mr. Montgomery believes that there should be cuts along with going over the tax cap, but that cuts should come from staffing. He sees the fund balance as an asset and once it is used, it is gone and doesn't believe that District is currently in an emergency situation and would not be an adequate use of fund balance at this time, and believes it is fair to ask tax payers to share the burden by shouldering the $500,000 allocation through exceeding the tax cap. He also noted that he is not necessarily in favor of reducing maintenance line. Mr. Whitney will provide greater detail of the maintenance budget for the Board to determine if there are discretionary reductions. Mrs. Palmieri feels a closer look at what maintenance will be required on the new turf fields, and noted that she is loath to borrow on the future by using the fund balance, agreeing with Mr. Montgomery that not currently an emergency situation. She also believes that employees will need to shoulder some of the burden.

	Members commented on the four considerations:

	Mr. Hanna believes if decision is to override the tax cap, he would be in favor of adding library media specialist as well as the increase of .5 psychologist;

	Mrs. Palmieri wants the budget to fall in line with a strategic plan but concurs that many hires were ancillary to core curriculum and would support three technology positions as that is where district will be heading but again noted that important to know how much over the cap would be required to add positions and there might need to cuts.

	In summation: The Board directed the Superintendent to create a budget that goes over the tax cap around 2%, thus not contemplating drastic cuts, with teaching positions on side and considerations as potential adds, but also to include the .02 science immediately into the budget. Mr. Montgomery continues to not see the value of the engineering programs. and would like to see 1% over the tax cap with two positions being eliminated, but Mr. Whitney doesn't believe that cutting positions and going over the tax cap is a wise decision. Mr. Hanna also believes that if making cuts then the budget should come in under the cap, and not do a half override. He would like to add positions in order to keep the district moving forward. Catherine Palmieri is open to considering a tax cap override but needs greater details. Mr. Montgomery noted that he is not comfortable in supporting the considerations as he would like to see the focus to be adding to core academic programs, not peripheral positions, also stating again that costs are not aligned with where they should be and need to be shared sacrifices.

	7. Business of the Board

	Action: 7.1 Adoption of 2016-17 School Calendar

	Prior to the Board’s adoption of the 2016-17 calendarDr. Harrison spoke of the time that is need for the teachers at the elementary level to close up their classrooms at the end of the school year as there is camp, etc. in the building and to that end, the Irvington Teachers' Union asked that consideration be given to an early release on Friday, June 16th for the teachers to have the extra time. He also noted that in the past, the last week of school had early dismissal for several days and the students came in for one hour on the last day of school. He also noted that secondary teachers have a very flexible schedule due to exams which provides time for them to close out.

	The Board considered this request and decided to amend the calendar to have June 16th as an early release day.

	Mr. Montgomery and Mr. Hanna noted that they continue to believe that movement of half days to Mondays and Fridays would be more desirable to parents as opposed to mid-week releases/delays. The pros and cons were discussed with respect to changing half days, including scheduling difficulties, and it would be a hardship, staffing issues and have curricular impact as well as impact on providing special education services if scheduling A/B days were changed. Dr. Kor also noted that feedback from some parents in the past has been that Mondays and Fridays can be difficult to schedule child care for various reasons.

	Resolved, that the Irvington Board of Education adopt the 2016-17 School Calendar with the addition of an early release on June 16, 2017.

	Motion by John Montgomery, second by Catherine Palmieri.

	Final Resolution: Motion Carries

	Yes: Phil Whitney, Catherine Palmieri, John Montgomery, Michael Hanna
