		Presentation:  3.4 Executive Sessions

		Dr.Roelle, in response to community comments regarding the Board's use of Executive Session, provided an overview of how the Irvington Board of Education uses Executive Sessions. The various forms of meetings that the Board is engaged in includes annual reorganization meeting,

		Regular meetings need to take place to conduct any type of business. In order for the Board to take action on any matter, it must be done so in a Regular or Special Meeting.

		Dr. Roelle reviewed the Board's policy  on Executive Session (#1730) which outlines the types of topics acceptable to discuss in Executive Session.

		Only one provision in Education Law allows a Board to take action in an Executive Session and that is for 3020a action, disciplinary charges against a certificated teacher. If any action must be taken following a discussion in an Executive Session, other than disciplinary charges, the Board must do so in the pubic meeting.

		Dr. Roelle gave a sampling of topics that have been discussed since mid-August in  Executive Session:

-
			recruitment of next Superintendent;

-
			collective bargaining (four uncompleted contracts); and

-
			litigation: tax certioraris, teacher grievances, potential arbitration, complaints against the District, proposed settlements in personnel matters.

		Dr. Roelle noted that the above topics are very appropriate to discuss in Executive Session. In addition, there is a wide range of conversations that the Board might have, i.e review the appointment for an administrator, i.e., stipend for curriculum and instruction, tenure recommendations, interview consultants, i.e. Superintendent search, review confidential auditors' report, which the Board has responsibility to meet before it is presented at a public meeting, student matters involving possible litigation regarding student placements (Special Education) as well as student discipline appeals.

		Dr. Roelle also noted that it is unacceptable for the Board to conduct business via email. In addition, opinion letters from counsel are considered confidential material between attorney/client. Legal Counsel is often present at Executive Sessions.

		Dr. Roelle also stated that he is proud to share that the Board very often stops itself before having an inappropriate conversation during Executive Sessions and that they take their role very seriously. Additionally, that he, Dr. Roelle, takes responsibility for guiding executive session topics very seriously as he values his own credibility.
