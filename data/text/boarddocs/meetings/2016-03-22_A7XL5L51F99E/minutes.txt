	6. Presentations/Discussions

	Presentation: 6.1 Superintendent's Proposed 2016-17 Budget (Attachment)

	Dr. Harrison provided an overview of the previous meetings presentations and discussed focused on the budget, Dr. Harrison spoke of presenting a responsible budget that provides exceptional programs for students but also being mindful of the taxes.

	The budgetary adjustments that have been made since the previous budget presentations include reductions to the buildings & grounds budget such as the painting/upgrades to of various facilities, deletion of outside contractors to perform some maintenance programs, and purchases of some items, reduction in transportation contracts, etc. were reviewed. However, increases to the budget include an increase in the technology equipment budget line, installation of an irrigation system at Lower Dows Field and replacing the existing fence in front of MSS. With the decreases totaling $215,985 and increases at $14,800, the total budget reductions equal $201,185.00, with a new budget total of $58,688,325 or a budget to budget increase of 1.78%.

	With respect to state aid, an additional $250,000 has been identified in the area of special education (100% reimbursement of 2013-14 and 2014-15 aid to students placed residentially). With reductions/increases/additional aid, the budget is now approximately $500,000 over the tax cap. It is hoped that increases in state aid, such as restoration of GEA and foundation aid will further reduce the amount over the tax cap. The estimated tax rate increase is 2.35% and in part due to lower assessed values with the average home value in Irvington of $803,075 having an estimated $402 per year increase in taxes. He reviewed the trends of the past six years and noted that this budget represents the lowest tax increase in that time, which also highlights the flaws in the tax cap.

	Additional considerations which had been previously presented for the Board’s consideration and total $564,800 include additional staffing requests: a 1.0 FTE Library Media Specialist, 1.0 Technology Teacher, a .2 FTE PLTW-Engineering and a .5 FTE School Psychologist as well as the purchase and electrical upgrade for 40 air conditions, grounds foreman or outside contractor to oversee the maintenance of fields as well as a replacement of a network switch.

	Following the presentation, members of the Board had clarifying questions to further understand the certain items in the budget, possible consequences of not including certain items, such as the switch on the server. Mr. Hanna reiterated that the tax cap is connected to the rate of inflation which is currently low. Because the previous year's tax levy is the bases for the following year's tax cap, the District's lower tax cap is partially due to the District's budget for this year being below the tax cap. Other items affecting the tax cap levy for next year could be the elimination of the PILOT which could have a negative impact on the 2017-18 budget. He also noted the previous cuts to the programs have impacted the District and it takes time to recover. He would like to see information as to what has been added against what had been cut. It was also noted that the assessed value has decreased due to tax certioraris, a large property being sold to a not for profit organization and one that is exempt based on religious property. The Board asked for a more concrete figure for the hiring of an outside contractor for grounds maintenance to help with the decision. Mrs. Palmieri noted that there are strains put on the band/orchestra teacher, Deb Tuzman, with a caseload of approximately 400 students and would like to see some elevation on that. She would also like to see commitment to world language program to increase a part time employee in order not to lose a fine teacher.

	Mr. Whitney commented that his view is that it had looked grim to stay under the tax cap, but now that it is reduced to approximately $550,000 over, he would like to try to get under the tax cap as it’s the Board's fiduciary duty to the community, noting that the previous tax cap override passed only by 15 votes, approximately. He also noted the recent reevaluation by the Town of Greenburgh, as some will see big increases, some none and some lower. He hopes that increased state aid will also further the amount over cap. The other Board members’ concurred with Mr. Whitney and Ms. Palmieri’s level of confidence to get the 60% over cap has dropped, and should dig a little deeper to find the savings, noting that great progress has been made in the District and would not want to put a pause on that, but agrees that community members are feeling pain from the reassessment. Mr. Grados reiterated the trends in tax increases in Irvington since 2011-12, but going back to 2013-14 when it was decided to override the tax cap, in order to try to restore the deep cuts previously made to programs, the Board promised the community at the time that the Board was setting the budget at a level that they felt there wouldn't be need to go over tax cap repeatedly and the trends shows that that promise was followed through. However, what was not considered at the time was that the District would have a negative tax cap. In addition, he believes the nervousness in the community, even though the budget increase is low, the assessments will confuse the issue and voters may not vote for a fiscally reasonable and believes the budget should be under tax cap this year but might have to go over next year. Mr. Hanna reiterated that he wants to see progress in our schools and doesn't want to create shock and thinks that part of the process is to look to find further savings to stay under the cap and find a balance for all. In summary, the Board asked the administration to try to present a budget under the tax cap, however, at a subsequent meeting, it might be felt that staying under would be too high a cost to the District. Comment was made that one-time expenses, such as the network switch, should be considered differently than considering adding staff which carries into the future.

	The administration will look to other means to stay under the cap without going too deep into the fund balance. To clarify, they need to look to further cut the budget by $635,000.

	Information: 6.2 Athletic Participation in Modified and High School Sports (Attachment)

	Mr. Hanna spoke of the value that athletics bring to students' experiences. He noted that the first opportunity to participate in athletics is in modified sports and that students were cut from the team this year due to large interest. Along with Artie McCormack, the historical participation information was reviewed in terms of how many students made the team, how many were cut, and a survey was conducted to 6-11th grade students to indicate what sports they would like to participate in next year, with over 400 responses received. However, there are limitations to field and gym space when considering increasing opportunities. The findings of the data showed that potentially expanding a second girls’ volleyball team in the fall and share the gym, with 35+ who tried out with only 15 making the team. There is potential to expanding girls and boys basketball but would need to redo Dows Lane gym floor and would cost approximately $40,000. The High School data showed that varsity and JV teams have had no cuts with several sports not having cuts, such as field and track. These could be added as budget considerations. To add a volleyball team is only $6,500 annually. Mr. McCormick noted that each team would play a full schedule. Mr. Grados asked if the situation with students being cut is an anomaly this year or an ongoing situation. Mr. McCormack noted that the number of students are cyclical for certain teams. Mr. Grados noted that certain sports could be phased out with increases in other sports, such as football and girls field hockey.
