	1. Public Hearing - 2013-14 Budget

		Action, Procedural: 1.1 Open Public Hearing

		Public Hearings are mandated by SED to provide a final opportunity for community members to speak on the proposed budget prior to the vote. Dr. Harrison provided a brief overview of the proposed budget. He noted that over the last five years, Irvington has had an average  increase of1.37%, the lowest in Westchester County, but as a result, lost approximately 39 positions and 19 high school electives. The ability to maintain and grow programs has been increasingly difficult. Of significant impact this year includes pension costs, health insurance and tax certioraris exceeding $3.7 million in exposure.

		Dr. Harrison highlighted efficiencies that have been identified, totaling nearly $500,000. The proposed budget provides for growth in learning experiences, expands technological opportunities and invests in infrastructure. The budget represents a budget to budget increase of 5.85%. Assessed value has increased to $75,271,675 and results in a 4.85% tax levy, less than the budget to budget increase. The average home value in the district would be approximately $739. The budget does represent fiscal responsibility but continues to provide for educational opportunity for all students.

	12. Presentations/Discussions

		Presentation: 12.1 Overview of Professional Development and Learning for Teachers (Presentation)

		Dr. Kor highlighted specific activities in Irvington with regard to professional development and described the three key components: curriculum, assessment and instruction. She stressed that it is important that staff come to school as learners and model learning for students and are part of a system in developing a culture of learning. Dr.Kor highlighted ways professional learning is delivered, i.e. out of district workshops, in-district learning provided by consultant and long term provided with coaches, peer observation, committee work and mentoring. She also highlighted examples of professional learning that is taking place this year in Irvington, such as integration of deeper thinking in the classroom through Habits of Mind, study groups at MSS in Math, and through out all content areas is the revision of the common core learning standards. Dr. Kor also provided the ways in which the district measures the success of the professional learning by looking at internal measures such as consistency in classrooms, teacher observations and in-district student achievement. External measures such as state assessments and Tri-state review are also used in assessing programs. Professional development must be deep and comprehensive and way to assess the effectiveness and to differentiate the development amongst staff as well.

		Dr. Kor also mentioned how the US compares with other countries with respect to professional development and the findings indicate that that the US lags behind in the levels of professional development provided, however, instructional time is greater in the US than in other countries, about 80% compared with 60% in classroom.

		Members of the Board asked about how administrators assure consistency within the classroom since different people doing observations and a concern were raised regarding the amount of lost teacher time when the Tri-State review begins.

		Comments from Community

		Dan Knowler  asked what  the result of the extended professional learning is in the other countries? We depend on other systems to see how we are doing since we are small in comparison and it takes a while to see trends and has the professional learning paid off for our students?

		Several community members asked how the district assesses if the amount allocated to professional development is the right amount and if there has been an audit of the learning to be sure that the money is being maximized.

		A concern was raised about the special education teachers being trained in the common core and specifically those in the co-teaching class.
