	10. Presentations/Discussions

	Presentation: 10.1 2014-15 Budget Presentation - Curriculum, Instruction, Technology and Employee Benefits (Presentation)

	Dr. Harrison noted that this will be last in the series of budget presentations pertaining to the 2014-15 budget. He spoke of the budgeting process being fluid with adjustments being made along the way. During the process, guesstimates were made regarding the District’s tax cap but felt that the proposed budget would fall within the cap. However, it was also known that there would be challenges to continue to provide excellent educational opportunities for students and to remain under the tax cap. However, updated information from the Comptroller shows that the tax cap for Irvington is significantly higher than originally thought. With the unanticipated spike in the tax cap, all needs could be comfortably included within the tax cap. However, it is recognized that any potential budget isn’t just about the tax cap and, therefore, that there will be a different dialogue from the previous two years. The recommendations that are being made by administration will require a conversation about what is a financially responsible budget and what are the needs of the children. The administration will ask the Board for guidance as to their thoughts on a proposed budget.

	An overview of the curriculum, instruction, technology was provided by Dr. Kor and included the central data processing code which covers costs of the infrastructure software, i.e. Microsoft, Finance Manager, etc. and is expected to decrease by $6,100 due to a software cost reduction. There is a request for an increase in curriculum administration and improvement in the area of professional development for principals and assistant principals. (Mrs. Kerner asked if it is possible to have the total staff development budgeted amount in one spreadsheet that includes all staff development costs as there are several different budget lines and makes it difficult to see the entire expense both the current year and the budget request for 2014-15 year).

	In the Instruction code, 2110, Dr. Kor highlighted the areas where there are new requests that are built into the budget. 2.24% is for new initiatives. There are ten considerations in the area of instruction and include a request to increase music staffing by .40 FTE to a total of 6 FTE to address the increase in music participation grades 4-12. Mr. Whitney asked if the .40 is enough to address the current needs. Dr. Kor would like to begin conversations on developing a vision for the program in the long term and with this increase is to get back to a manageable level for the current staff as the HS/MS band teacher has a case load of 300 students. Other considerations include a 1.0 FTE middle school teacher for the co-teach model in science, which is now necessary with the new demands in literacy in the CCLS to better support students with special education needs. Dr. Kor noted that there is less support given to students in the higher grades and that administration is aware of the students greater needs that must be addressed. The co-teach model allows for all the students in the class to meet the needs of all students, both the general ed and special education students. Consideration 3 is a request for a .80 FTE ELA teacher at the high school to provide writing workshops for freshman students that will focus on developing proficiency in the various modes of writing. This will provide a rigorous writing program that will improve students’ writing skills in various genres of writing and valuable for all students. Also included in this consideration is a .20 FTE increase in ELA academic intervention and a .20 FTE to provide a technology course to address increased interest in technology courses and applications.

	Other considerations include increases in staffing at the high school, elementary math AIS, a Department Chair in the area of Special Ed, as well as monitors at the elementary level. Other considerations include cafeteria and classroom furniture, monitor training, and an early literacy program materials and staff development for Kindergarten and 1st grade. This will provide consistency across all classrooms and the goal is to be able to provide an early intervention approach using Fundations, designed to be used in conjunction with another approach or program such as our balanced literacy approach. Currently, the reading specialists and some special ed teachers are utilizing the program but classroom teachers have not received staff development in the program.

	Other areas covered in the presentation include Library and Instructional Technology, and co-curricular activities. Dr. Kor noted that the budget for co-curricular expenses has been decreased over the last several years and therefore the MSS play has not been able to be covered within the budget but covered by IEF or the PTSA.

	With regards to the employee benefits component of the budget, there is a budget increase of $929,905 or 7.92%, in the areas of employee benefits, mainly due to an increase in NYS Teachers Retirement and NYS Employees Retirement rates as well as an increase in health insurance rate and a large increase in Medicare reimbursements.

	The budget considerations presented thus far in the process, results in a 5.18% over last year’s budget. Ms. Miller provided an overview of the tax cap and noted that it is multi-faceted and many calculations are involved in calculating a District’s tax cap. Due to the tax base growth factor for Irvington which is based upon the income that is reported to the State on residents income tax,supplied by the Commissioner of Tax and Finance is 4.88%, which is the largest in Westchester County and the second in New York State, which results in an increase to the District’s tax cap to $51,534,987 or 6.64%. The 2014-15 revenue estimates provides for a budget of $791,085 under the tax cap and signifies a 4.82% tax rate increase. Following this information, Mrs. Kerner noted that the tax cap discussion is irrelevant at this point but still necessary to discuss what budget increase the community would be comfortable with. Dr. Harrison stated that he will continue to meet with administrators to see where there might be more opportunities to find efficiencies and asks the Board to provide that threshold. The Board asked for a list of non-negotiables that cannot be considered in any conversations to reduce the budget amount. Mr. Grados thought that it would be strange to ask the community for a tax increase in the amount greater than was asked for last year, so it is best come up with a percentage but to also see what the non-negotiable percentage increase is. Ms. Miller noted that a very rough estimate is approximately a 4% tax rate increase without any considerations included. Mr. Whitney suggests that the conversation be continued next week.

	A community member referred to the 2012-13 Audited Report in the area of health insurance and asked how the amount in the proposed budget is lower than the amount stated in the report. Ms. Miller invited him contact her to further explain the differences in the budget amount and the amount in the report.
