Summary - Fishtank
    ●   Engagement
        ○ Key questions led to strong conversations
        ○ Parents reported that students were talking about topics at home
        ○ Adding in work from professional learning (Rhonda Graff, Katie Cunningham,
          Boces workshops) helped with engagement
        ○ The number of key questions was overwhelming at times

    ●   Texts
        ○ Culturally responsive, diverse, and engaging
        ○ Students enjoyed the texts
        ○ Higher level of engagement when students had copies
        ○ Some texts were too dense and not accessible for all students
        ○ Concern over grade-level appropriateness of texts
        ○ Presented multiple perspectives on a topic, especially marginalized groups

Summary - Fishtank
●   Instructional Components
    ○ Texts, key questions, and target tasks supported vocabulary development

    ○ Concerns with the amount of explicit writing instruction, especially about the writing
      process

    ○ Teachers expressed concerns over lack of explicit fluency and morphology instruction

    ○ Vocabulary words were appropriate for K but the definitions were not accessible for students

    ○ The children's level of conversation increased with use of the vocabulary from the units.

    ○ The unit spent time on sentence structure where students learned about coordinate and
      subordinate conjunctions and how to expand their sentences by using them. This was a
      great way to support sentence/paragraph level work.

Summary - Fishtank

   ● Usability/Materials Organization
      ○ While materials are mostly easy to access (no student consumables), they must be
        printed, lesson plan pages are not numbered, and all books must be sourced, which
        makes it challenging to implement.

      ○ The lesson plan is written in a user friendly manner but there are a lot of suggestions
        and different optional prompts to go through and the layout of it impacts teacher
        readability.

      ○ Slides were basic and needed a lot of work to support instruction

Summary - Arts & Letters
   ● Use a knowledge building approach to develop critical thinking and
     comprehension skills

   ● Incorporate a variety of diverse, complex texts with students reading the same
     texts

   ● Vocabulary that connects to topics/themes

   ● Integrate reading and writing - learning to write/writing to learn

   ● Include foundational skills instruction - fluency, some phonics/morphology, and
     sentence/paragraph-level writing

   ● Integrate science and social studies

   ● Students engage in daily academic discourse

Summary - Arts & Letters
    ● Focuses more on direct instruction and guided practice, providing clear models for
      students to follow.

    ● Typically follows a more linear progression, with a structured sequence of lessons that
      build on each other.

    ● Four modules

    ● Prologue lessons (additional pre-teach lessons) to support ELLs and SWDs

    ● Homework included

    ● User friendly (adequate slides, student consumables)

    ● Has data repository, with the same collection as Eureka2, our math curriculum

Meeting with Katie Cunningham on 5/14
   While both programs had strengths, there was a strong preference for Arts and Letters.
   Key reasons included:
   ● Sustainable Routines: Teachers found the structure and routines in Arts and Letters to
     be manageable and easy to embed into daily instruction.

   ● Depth of Thinking: The program encourages deeper student thinking and more
     meaningful engagement.

   ● Listening Comprehension in Primary Grades: Teachers appreciated this foundational
     component in the early grades, which supports a broad range of learners and gave
     teachers important data they never had before.

   ● Consistency Across Classrooms: The program structure supports consistent
     instructional practices across classrooms and grade levels to a higher degree than
     Fishtank where teachers felt they had to make more decisions.

Meeting with Katie Cunningham on 5/14
 While both programs had strengths, there was a strong preference for Arts and Letters.
 Key reasons included:

  ● Student Engagement and Accessibility: Teachers found the texts more kid-friendly and
    noted that the arts integration was highly engaging. The materials also better supported
    students below grade level.

  ● Instructional Support: The explicit, in-depth instruction provided strong guidance for
    teachers and better met the needs of diverse learners.

  ● Assessment Usefulness: While Fishtank’s daily writing tasks were seen as rigorous,
    teachers found the Arts and Letters assessments-both formative and summative-to be
    more actionable, better aligned, and more instructionally useful.
