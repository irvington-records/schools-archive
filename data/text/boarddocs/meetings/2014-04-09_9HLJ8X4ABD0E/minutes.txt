	3. Presentations/Discussions

	Discussion: 3.1 Fields and Facilities Discussion (Presentation)

	Facilities projects which total $1,824,500 with another $40,000 in a construction manager salary.  With respect to a bond, it will be cost neutral as there is a bond retiring in 2015-16.

	Dr. Harrison reviewed what remains to be decided by the Board: Type of surface for Meszaros field and surface for Oley Track. AT a previous meeting, the Board had agreed to install a new natural turf field and needed drainage and irrigation on East Field.

	A tentative timeline was presented and Dr. Harrison noted that much lead time is necessary to prepare the specs to be submitted to SED, and there is approximately 26-28 weeks wait time to gain approval from SED. A possible bond date for September or October might be too soon depending on SED’s approval timeline. The Board would approve the final project scope at a meeting in June.

	Mr. Grados opened the discussion to the community prior to the Board’s discussion and noted that all questions will be answered tonight and throughout process.

	Several community members expressed their desire for artificial turf to be considered due to the needs of the district and community and that the weather doesn’t impact playability. A comment was made that 35 out of 46 fields in the county are artificially turfed and that environmental concerns are addressed by using organic infill.

	Linda Pierpont and Sean Brady, members of the Field Project committee also expressed their opinion that artificial turf was necessary given weather factors and need. Esther Samra, who was a member of the fields committee, expressed her appreciation to serve on the committee and noted that much was agreed upon by the members of the community. Differences of opinion did exist, specifically whether to recommend artificial or natural turf, and that some members question the raw data as to hours of use of Meszaros Field that had been presented. Other issues that are disputed regarding the use of natural turf, such as the time needed to rest natural fields, cost of natural fields, and effects of weather on playing conditions. She suggests that resources be spent on building four natural turf fields and engage Jared Minik

	Mr. Grados thanked the community for the emails and phone calls and will ask to put forth a motion to approve artificial turf with organic infill and to resurface Oley Track but offered the Board time to comment. Mr. Montgomery commented on the information that has been circulated and not sure if he has a clear picture but that he believes that it does make sense to have an artificial turf with organic infill to meet the needs of the District and community and will provide the flexibility that is necessary. Maria Kashkin noted that it is telling that the majority of districts and/or communities in Westchester County have turf fields and believes that the District cannot maintain all grass fields and would be able to better maintain the three other fields if Meszaros Field was turfed and also agrees with using artificial turf with an organic infill. Mr. Whitney is in favor of using an organic infill and also agreed that it would be difficult to maintain grass fields. Mrs. Kerner also agreed with the use of organic infill and noted that all the fields must be looked as a system and that it is important to look at what needs to be done to Dows Lane’s fields as well.

	With respect to renovating the fields on Dows Lane, LAN has done an initial analysis, and believe that there is healthy soil which would benefit from additional aeration and better maintenance. Upper Dows could benefit from drainage and irrigation at lower Dows and that overall, the fields are manageable and maintainable, but more money would need to be spent on maintenance. The drainage and irrigation could be included in a proposed bond. Mrs. Kerner also thinks it makes sense to add $90,000 to the referendum to provide the opportunity to renovate the Dows Lane fields. Mrs. Kashkin feels that a commitment must be made to installing some sort of irrigation system to lower Dows to maintain the field and should be funded out of the 2014-15 budget.

	With regard to the facilities projects, Mrs. Kerner stated that she is comfortable with all projects being recommended but does not support spending $140,000 to fix the middle school’s dust bowl, as there are other items that would be a priority. Mrs. Kashkin noted that the recommended improvement is not for purely aesthetic reasons but that the dust is affecting the HVAC systems. Mr. Whitney noted that recommending asphalt was a cheaper solution to the problem and will provide a playing surface for the students that are also needed. Mr. Montgomery questioned why the HS gym lights were not removed from the list. Mr. Whitney noted that it was debated and it was decided that the lighting is subpar, dim and inadequate for the main gym and stated that it was a failure of the original decisions with wrong fixtures and wiring being installed during construction. Mr. Montgomery asked for clarification as to how the manager’s job would differ from LAN’s responsibilities to the District. Mr. Grados responded that architect’s job is to be sure that work is being done in conformance with the plans, but a manager will be on site and ensure that proper materials are being used, work being done properly, etc. Mrs. Kerner also referred to the prior construction of the campus and the problems that resulted due to the lack of a construction manager on site and feels that it is a necessary expense to ensure that the project is done as expected. From an operational point of view, Mrs. Kerner suggested that perhaps some of these decisions be reserved until after the budget vote.

	Discussion: 3.2 2014-15 Budget Discussion (Presentation)

	Dr. Harrison introduced some of the changes to the proposed budget following the Board’s discussion at the April 1st Board meeting. The restored items total $177,000. Deleted from the budget is $18,000 for communications services.

	The proposed budget is $56,294,000, a budget to budget increase of 4.11% and estimated tax increase of 3.36% and under the tax cap by $817. The estimated increase in taxes for the average assed home would be $538.00 and the lowest in four years.

	Comments from the Board included:

	Mrs. Kerner noted that it has been an interesting year for budget planning due to changing numbers for the tax cap and then higher state aid and again stressed the need for long range planning for budgeting. She and Mr. Montgomery had questions and concerns regarding the proposed increase in the athletic trainer’s time ($40,000).  Mrs. Kerner asked if it is the best use of resources and might a nurse might perform some of the testing. Mr. Montgomery asked for a breakdown of duties of the athletic trainer, i.e. time for concussion management as well as time spent at games. Mrs. Kashkin noted that there is a management component where all athletes should have a baseline but currently many do not and this added cost would be cover those exams. Mr. McCormack further expanded on the responsibilities that the athletic trainer would have such as testing a student to return to a sport following an injury and much more involved in the District's athletic programs. She asks for some backup to see how the 11,000 hours of services were arrived at.

	Another question came up was with regard with the behavioral TA recommendation and what type of specific training this person would receive. Ms. Gail Krieger, CPSE chair, noted that the plan is still in development, but BCBA training is being. Mr. Montgomery stated that he doesn’t  believe that there has been a consensus of the Board on the full Budget and that the proposal for the 9th grade writing might be something that he couldn't support if it is included in the budget before there is further discussion on how it should approached. Mrs. Kerner asked if the Board could be provided with a "plan of attack" to address the deficiencies in the current program that this recommended proposal would improve. Mr. Grados also would like to have a plan presented and how it fits in with the entire curriculum. Mrs. Kashkin believes that there is more rigor needed at the high school level for writing.
