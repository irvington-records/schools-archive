	Curriculum/Instruction News

	Dr. Kor reported that there will be changes to elementary report cards to reflect the Common Core Standards. A committee convened last year to review and revamp the report card to be better aligned with what is being taught and how the students’ work reflects which of the Common Core Standards are being met. There will be a parent guide sent home with each child to explain the changes.

	Mr. Montgomery stated that it appears that Irvington is setting standards based on the Common Core and questioned whether Irvington should move beyond the common core in its instruction. Dr. Kor responded that instruction does not stop at the Common Core which is a rigorous set of standards. She also pointed out that the CCLS helps teachers to differentiate instruction as they can look ahead at what will be taught in higher grades. The other difference in instruction is the critical thinking skills that come from the Habits of Mind that the district is utilizing which teach children to think deeply.

	Dr. Kor reported on a situation that has occurred at the high school where a computer glitch affected the calculation of grades of current seniors during their 9th grade which affected their GPA. The report cards were accurate but that data was pulled last year from an incomplete data set, i.e. less than the four quarters, therefore changing the grades on their transcripts. This was identified during an audit of the data. The corrections will be made to those current seniors' prior to requesting transcripts to ensure accuracy in their transcripts that will be sent to colleges. It was noted that no prior year students' data was affected as the reports were run at the end of the 2013-14 school year.

	Mr. Montgomery asked why students/parents hadn't been notified earlier than today of this situation. Dr. Kor noted that the issue had to be researched to identify where the problem was, to understand the problem and its source which had taken most of the last week. Sufficient information was not available until now and that it was the administration's responsibility to ensure that there was resolution and the administration felt that at no time was a student compromised. Mr. Montgomery stated that he did not agree with the District’s delay in reporting the issue to students/parents.

	6. Presentation

	Discussion: 6.1 2013-14 Student Performance Report (Presentation)

	Dr. Kor provided an update on how Irvington students performed on state assessments, i.e. Grades 3-8 Mathematics and ELA, Grades 4 and 8 Science, Regents exams. Also included is student performance on the AP exams.

	Of note,

-
		95% of students graduated with a Regents Diploma;

-
		there is one National Merit Semi-Finalist; the number of finalists has not yet been determined and it was noted that students need to apply to be considered finalists;

-
		559 students were enrolled in 17 AP course; and

-
		2014 SAT scores rose in critical reading, math and writing, i.e. 606, 624 and 617 respectively with the national averages being 496, 514, and 488

-
		Dr. Kor presented comparative state assessment data with the 12 comparative districts. She noted that 2013 was the year that the mathematics assessments in grades 3 and 4 were focused on common core standards. She noted that data analysis is challenging since the state no longer releases the entire test questions to allow the District to determine students’ answers on individual questions but they are now group results by standards; and

-
		21 out of 27 varsity teams were recognized as NYS Scholar Athlete teams (90 or higher GPA) and three of the teams achieved the highest average GPA in NYS for their sport.

	Dr. Kor reviewed the current 9th grade cohort's scores going back to their 3rd grade as well looking at this year’s 4th graders' scores and comparing them to their 3rd grade scores, noted that they performed at a higher level in 4th grade, possibly suggesting that learning is cumulative over the course of their education. She also stressed that there are many variables that go into looking at the testing data and that assessments are only one measure of a student’s learning.

	In closing she noted that the data is used along with formative and summative classroom data to plan for instruction and the data will be analyzed to continue to meet the expectations as well as look at our students longitudinally to ensure that students' needs are being addressed.

	12. Discussion

	Discussion: 12.1 Discussion on 2014-15 District Goals

	Dr. Harrison noted that the goals developed by Ms. Kerner and Mr. Montgomery were also discussed at the September 23rd Board meeting and as discussed at that meeting, actionable goals might be best suited to be incorporated within the Superintendent's goals and undertaken by his administrative team.

	The Board discussed the six  individual goals and addressed some outstanding questions that remain in order to provide clarity to the meaning of a goal #5 (Uphold high standards for District staff while ensuring that all staff who meet those standards have meaningful career opportunities, professional development and a satisfying work environment). and #2 (Provide top-quality instruction for all students) and how that might intersect with # 5. Mr. Montgomery noted that in his view goal 2 deals with all staff, not just instructional, whereas goal 5 speaks to instructional staff. Mrs. Kerner felt that 2 focuses on students where #5 focuses on teachers. The Board agreed that #2 was broadly written where #5 focuses on instructional staff.

	Once again, the language will be tweaked in order to provide clarity as to the intent of the goals which will then be distributed to the Board to ensure that it captures their thinking.
