                  Understanding the Value of PLRDs
 Examples of critical professional experiences that occurred through PLRDs:

    Teachers worked in grade level & grade-level groups to review, understand and revise unit planners
     to align to the NextGen Standards
    Teachers collaborated in departmental & grade-level teams to create tasks and assessments related
     to social studies professional learning
    Teachers developed, reviewed and revised unit planners to reflect a deeper understanding of
     teaching through the Understanding by Design model
    Tech Committee members designed and presented workshops to teachers on topics of interest
     related to using technology in the classroom. Teachers had choice in which session to attend and
     then had time at the next PLRD to work to implement the learning
    Specials teachers revised Unit planners

    Collaborative sessions with Math and Literacy Coaches based on new standards, NYS test results,
     data from modules and writing assessments (K-5)
    Vertical articulation to align learner outcomes and promote program continuity

    K-12 special education staff collaborated to identify possible expansion to the continuum of services

                  Understanding the Value of PLRDs
 How did the PLRD model benefit the District?
    Examples of how PLRDs advanced instructional priorities:
       Continuity - Regular PLRD meetings provided for greater continuity of focus and increased
        momentum
       Extension of Professional Learning - Teachers used time to plan and implement learning from PD
        with consultants
       Coaching - Instructional coaches regularly met with teachers to support curriculum writing,
        program implementation, data review, etc.
       Increased Technology Integration - Technology professional development promoted the
        integration of technology
       Applying learning from faculty meetings related to District goals for the creation of and/or
        revision to classroom tasks such as formative assessment

    There were a number of instances, whereby, PLRD opportunities reduced teacher pull out from
     their classrooms. Examples include:
       NYSSLS Implementation/Amplify planning, professional learning K-8
       Special Education Professional Learning on IEP writing, 6-12 transition goals, etc.
       Professional learning for developing assessments and teaching into the block
       Reduced pull out for collaborative curriculum development

            Understanding the Value of PLRDs

 What we learned - challenges and areas for consideration:

   Seeking an enhanced approach for K-12 meeting opportunities to allow
    teachers from all schools to participate.

   Reviewing scheduling of school events & programs, such as parent-teacher
    conferences, to minimize impacts on families and support the continuity of
    instruction.

        Elementary End-of-Year Early Release Days
 What occurs on end-of-year early release days?

    The middle school and high school faculty operate on an exam schedule that provides
     for time for end-of-year needs
     Elementary
    Preparation and facilitation of end-of-year programs, events and experiences, i.e.
     moving up ceremonies, school-wide assemblies
    Grade-level articulation with staff within and between school buildings, team meetings
     for case management
    Kindergarten preparation based upon screening and registration process

    Data collection, organization and transfer within and across buildings
       Aimsweb, Math Modules, Assured Writing Experiences
       Review and transition of health files

    Packing of rooms to prepare for summer camp and summer cleaning/maintenance
