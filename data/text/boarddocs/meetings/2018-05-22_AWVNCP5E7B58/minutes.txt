2. Discussion/Presentation

Information:  2.1 Grades K-5 End of Year Update

Dr. Harrison thanked the community for their support of the 2018-19 budget and congratulated Michael Hanna and Beth Propper who were voted in as Board Trustees and thanked Erin Bernstein for her candidacy for the Board of Education. Dr. Harrison also noted that the Board will vote tonight on the IFA contract as well as tenure candidates.

By way of introducing the Elementary administrators, Deb Marieniello, Principal of Dows Lane, Joyce Chapnick, Principal of Main Street School and Andrea Kantor, Asst. Principal, Dows Lane, Dr. Harrison spoke of the District's K-12 focus that includes the Theories of Action and the six strategic objectives that drive most of the work that takes places within the District. Dr. Kor reviewed the 2017-18 overarching goals, noting that tonight's presentation will provide samples of what is going on in the classrooms each day, so by sharing one student's work, it is an example of what is going on in a particular grade or across the school. She stressed that the foundation of the District's belief is grounded in the Theories of Action, which include professional learning to build collective capacity with goal that all educators are able to help students gain a well-developed brain. The four learning goals are knowledge, skill, making meaning and transfer. Another goal this year was to devlelop a balanced assessment system and increasie the use of data to inform instruction and planning.

The Dows Lane administrators shared examples of the work that has taken place this year at Dows Lane that meet the goals, sharied the outcomes and the evidence of the work undertaken. Ms. Mariniello spoke to the approach of balanced literacy and it's implemtation through the Workshop Model.She then shared examples of work at all grade levels, from Kindergarten's Reading Playdate whose purpose is to help students think more deeply about what they are reading, or, at the third grade level, how multiple texts, such as persuasive reading, are used so that students form opinions, can support their opinions, and to take it further, be able to have an authentic response to another student or teacher, noting that in order to have an authentic, meaningful discussion, students must have a strong opinion to engage in authentic discussion. This learning tool also requires that students learn to listen without speaking, which is often difficult to do.

Ms. Kantor then shared the work of the social/emotional learning/character learning that is taking place at Dows Lane this year, integrating the Habits of Mind and use of consistent language, vis a vis morning meeting, character education assemblies. It was noted that there has been incredible growth in the discussions among the students. Also highlighted was the integration of technology through the implementation of Genius Hour, Makerspace, STEM challenges, PLTW and use of tools such as Flipgrid, Padlet and Google Classroom. Teachers were then used as facilitators for other teachers which increased experiences for the students. The use of these tools does require hire level of thinking and enables the students to self-reflect on their persistence, etc. She shared examples of the various types of experiences that provide opportunities. Ms. Mariniello expressed her joy at observing the learning and student engagement that takes place at Dows Lane on a daily basis, often through the power of inquiry which the students are engaged in.  Ms. Mariniello and Ms. Kantor spoke to approaches used at Dows Lane to assess students learning, such as balanced assessment and' how do they know' if students are learning. Students reflect on their learning by self-assessing their work and how they might get better at the particular task, which again requires deeper thinking and metacognition skills. The use of data that drives instruction was addressed with the use of Aimsweb data for assessment of student learning, but also designing benchmarking criteria that will help teachers create target learning. The work that has been done to analyze how students are doing is also part of the UbD with shifts in instructional practices, opportunities to raise student thinking, making connections through authentic instruction.

The Board asked follow up questions on the Dows Lane presentation, particularly with the use of Aimsweb and other benchmark criteria, how the data is used for individual students as well as in particular classrooms. David Graeber thanked them for the substance of the presentation but also asked about correlating the state assessment data with the Aimsweb data, also wondering what percentage of students will be reading at the highest level, Q, by the end of the year. Deb Hargraves still believes that the student performance would be helpful to incorporate into the presentation. Michael Hanna also expressed his thanks and his appreciation for the thoughtfulness and thoroughness of the presentation.Maura Gedid also asked about privacy through Google Classroom.

Joyce Chapnick, Main Street Principal, presented the work that has been taking place at Main Street School this year, noting that the foundation has been set by Dows Lane's presentation, and noting that it is evident is that each school is part of a system that builds each year. Examples were provided that are representative of the work of a particular grade or class to get a sense of the work taking place. She highlighted where the most progress has been made on the district goals and again reiterated that all students do have a common language by using Habits of Mind, etc. and always with the goal to foster higher-level of thinking, creativity and consistency. The use of essential questions is used throughout units of study to engage with their peers and to ultimately end unit of study to name what they learned from the unit. Independent work stations allow students to work through problems and debrief their learning with peers. Exit tickets are used to assess students' understanding. Ms. Chapnick spoke to developing balanced assessment and progress has been made in increased level of thinking measured through student work and systems and practices that reflect the use of data to support student learning and growth and needs. She shared samples of work that illustrate students work and their understanding of a subject through their writing and which serve as an assessment of the learning that has taken place, as well as ensure that there is deeper level of understanding. She too provided examples of progress of a student vis a vis Aimsweb, noting that it is only one data point and it is only the second year in use at MSS. She shared that student attendance is also now being looked at and an important factor related to student learning and social emotional growth and the connectivity with their peers and is part of the intervention plan, beginning with Tier 1 intervention. Dr. Harrison noted that the presentation tonight was a window into the depth of assessment that are now in place and has so much pride in the work that the teachers are doing and the progress made.

Board members had the opportunity to ask questions. Maria Kashkin thanked the administrators since for board members, the question has been "how do we know" and over the years many interventions and money has been spent on struggling students and the presentation shows that there are systems in place and that students are learning. Deb Hargraves noted that there were more examples of math and would like to see more examples in the area of writing/reading as well as the assessment data to illustrate the growth. Dave Graeber commented on the fact that attendance is being taken into account as it is so important to learning, feels that it was a breakthrough presentation as it shows the substance of what the students are doing. Michael Hanna agrees and spoke to the continuity of the adminisitrators and staying with the work that has been undertaken over the years. Board would like to see the correlation of the systems in use to further their understanding of the work being undertaken.
