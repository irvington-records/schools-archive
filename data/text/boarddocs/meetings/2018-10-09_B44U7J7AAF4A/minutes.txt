4. Administrators' Update/Board Committee & Liaison Reports

Report: 4.1 District Updates, News, Events

Dr. Harrison asked that all keep informed of the many events and developments that are underway throughout the district by visiting the Irvington UFSD Facebook page, the district website and school mailings. Dr. Harrison gave an overview of several highlights to date, including:

- Homecoming -- Dr. Harrison spoke about how exciting it was to see the increased school spirit and attendance among all ages in support of our schools. He also gave a nod of thanks to the Bulldog Booster Club for all of their efforts.

- Late Bus -- There are now 2 late buses from the Middle School and High School to support students who want to stay late for assistance or after school activities.

- Recycling Initiative -- a pilot program at the Main Street School was very successful last year. Based on that success, Dr. Harrison noted, the program has expanded across the district to other schools. In the latest Dow's Lane newsletter, it was reported that, since the beginning of the school year, Dow's Lane cafeteria trash has been reduced by 50%. Dr. Harrison pointed out that while staff is doing their part, most importantly, the students are taking ownership which reflects on the remarkable success.

- PSAT/Pre ACT -- Dr. Harrison noted that we offer the PSAT and the Pre-ACT to sophomores which allows the students an increased level of confidence, enabling them to be prepared for the actual SAT and ACT.

- Security -- Dr. Harrison discussed how the District continues to make progress on enhanced security practices and is grateful to the generous grant of nearly $30,000 secured by the Irvington PTSA enabling the purchase of video surveillance cameras that are being installed in each school. He also spoke about BERT (Building Emergency Response Teams) that act as first level responders for each building in the event of an emergency. Each team is made up of school administrators, custodians, teachers, psychologists and guidance counselors and have received more in-depth training this year. Since the first day of school, the District has rolled out more extensive emergency drills in our schools that have been supported both by Altaris and the Irvington Police Department.

- Staff Acknowledgements -- Dr. Harrison gave a note of recognition to Jennifer DeLisi-Hall, English Teacher at the High School, as she is being honored by the New York State English Council with an "Educator of Excellence" award -- one of just 23 teachers state-wide to earn this prestigious title. Jennifer will be recognized at the upcoming New York State Education Council in Albany later this month. Dr. Harrison also noted that three other teachers from our District, Karen Bodnar, Amy Panitz and Sara Rust will be presenting at the Science Teacher's Association of New York State at the annual conference in Rochester, November 2-5. Dr. Kor noted that Helen Pashley has been a tremendous asset to our District and is very involved at the state level. Working with Helen enabled our Middle School Teachers to present at the upcoming New York State Education Council in Albany. The Irvington School District is very excited and proud!

Curriculum/HR Updates:

PLRD (Professional Learning Release Day) -- Dr. Kor said that this new dedicated professional learning time began today (October 9). It enabled Dow's Teachers to work on RTI Data Review and Unit Planners, K-1 to unpack and set up science kits, Main Street teachers worked on ELA, Unit Planners and Informational, Middle School teachers focused on Transfer Goals and Student Thinking Section of Unit Planners and High School teachers worked on knowledge skill and inventory. Dr. Kor pointed out that it was only the first day and the program will be assessed going forward as to how to use the time most effectively.

Action (Consent): 7.5 Charitable Trust Resolution

WHEREAS, in response to the Federal Tax Cuts and Jobs Act (TCJA), the State of New York has enacted legislation as part of its FY2019 Budget that authorizes counties, local governments, and school districts to establish a charitable gifts reserve fund that may be used to defray some of the costs related to public education, healthcare, or other public service provided by that entity; and

WHEREAS, the State of New York has also enacted legislation which allows counties, local governments, and public school districts the option to authorize by means of resolution a real property tax credit of up to 95% of the amount of a property owner’s monetary contribution(s) to an established charitable reserve fund during the “associated credit year”; and

WHEREAS, individual taxpayers may be incentivized to contribute generously to these charitable funds out of the expectation that the pairing of an increased charitable deduction at the federal level combined with a real property tax credit at the local level may mitigate the effects of the new $10,000 cap on state and local tax deductions imposed by TCJA; and

WHEREAS, the Internal Revenue Service (IRS) issued on August 23, 2018 proposed regulations providing rules on the availability of federal charitable contribution deductions when the taxpayer receives or expects to receive a corresponding state or local tax credit; and

WHEREAS, the proposed regulations rules that a taxpayer who makes a contribution to a charitable reserve fund and subsequently receives a state or local tax credit that exceeds 15 percent of the contribution must reduce his or her charitable deduction by the amount of any tax credit he or she received at the state or local level; and

WHEREAS, state, county, and local entities have expressed confusion, doubt, and skepticism on the draft regulations and have raised questions as to whether they constitute an arbitrary and capricious interpretation of federal tax law, particularly in the context of the treatment of existing state tax credit programs that pre-date the TCJA and the stated preservation of the deductibility of contributions made by businesses; and

WHEREAS, the IRS has invited the public to submit comments on the proposed regulations no later than October 11, 2018;

NOW, THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED, that the Board of Education of the Irvington Union Free School District believes the proposed regulations issued by the IRS on August 23, 2018, dramatically impair the incentives for individual taxpayers to make contributions to these charitable reserve funds as established by law in the State of New York and, in doing so, impair the mission of these funds, namely providing a high-quality public education to all children residing within the Irvington Union Free School District; and

BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED, that the Board of Education of the Irvington Union Free School District believes the proposed regulations issued by the IRS on August 23, 2018, provide insufficient clarity on many topics as well as arbitrary and unfair distinctions between deductions for individuals compared to deductions for businesses, and contributions to support public education compared to contributions to support private education; and

BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED, that the Board of Education of the Irvington Union Free School District finds it necessary and appropriate to propose changes to the proposed regulations issued by the IRS to provide additional clarity for the taxpayers of the Irvington Union Free School District and to explain and remove any arbitrary distinctions that would effectively and unfairly disadvantage the taxpayers of the Irvington Union Free School District;

BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED, that the Board of Education of the Irvington Union Free School District shall submit public comments on or before October 11, 2018 that shall reflect the concerns outlined above; and

BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED, that the Board of Education of the Irvington Union Free School District shall seek to collaborate with other like-minded state, county, municipality, or school district agencies or organizations to express these concerns publicly and to advocate for fair regulations and equitable and principled interpretation of federal tax law that recognizes and rewards the value of contributions in support of public education, no matter whether the contributor is an individual or a business, or whether the charitable entity is public or private.

WHEREAS; the Irvington Union Free School District pursuant to the authority granted in General Municipal Law, Article 5A (Public Contracts), Section 103 desires to become a member of Sourcewell (Formerly NJPA), a purchasing cooperative.

WHEREAS; Section 103 of the General Municipal Law permits Irvington Union Free School District to make purchases of apparatus, materials, equipment or supplies, or to contract for services related to the installation, maintenance or repair of apparatus, materials, equipment, and supplies, may make such purchases, or may contract for such services related to the installation, maintenance or repair of apparatus, materials, equipment, and supplies, as may be required by Irvington Union Free School District therein through the use of a contract let by the United States of America or any agency thereof, any state or any other county or political subdivision or district therein if such contract was let to the lowest responsible bidder or on the basis of best value in a manner consistent with this section and made available for use by other governmental entities;

WHEREAS; the Irvington Union Free School District desires to participate for the purpose of fulfilling and executing its respective public governmental purposes, goals, objectives, programs and functions;

WHEREAS; the Irvington Union Free School District has reviewed the benefits of participating in this program and based on this review has concluded the program will provide the best value to taxpayers of this Irvington Union Free School District through the anticipated savings to be realized;

NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED, by the Board of Education of the Irvington Union Free School District is authorized to become a member of Sourcewell and that the Irvington Board of Education or designee is authorized to register for the Sourcewell program on behalf of Irvington Union Free School District.
