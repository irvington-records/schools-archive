	5. Presentations/Discussions

	Presentation: 5.1 School-Based Needs and Assessing Student Learning

	Dr. Harrison noted that the format tonight will be a less formal approach with dialogue from school-based leaders speaking to their initiatives of this year and also what they see as their needs for next year which will then lead into budget discussions at future board meetings.

	Dows Lane Principal Randy Lichtenwalner and Assistant Principal Meg Benedetto reported that it has been a productive, exciting year at Dows Lane. To sum it up, the theme is supporting students, those that struggle a little more as well as those who are ready for more challenged.

	Of note:

-
		The Fundations program, a phonics and handwriting program, in kindergarten and 1st grade. Thus far, it has been successful for students and provides a set of data that will then show students who are in need of extra support;

-
		Consultants working with teachers, such as Shelly Harwaine, who provides an inquiry-based learning model to help students pose questions that they are interested in, allowing them to then to research their individual questions. Students are very invested in the process, taking learning to a deeper level.

-
		Curriculum mapping, using Rubicon Atlas, has continued at Dows Lane. The use of data has been extended in order to look at how students are doing, both in their reading and math, looking at assessments across grade-levels to look at the program as a whole.

-
		Utilizing both outside consultants and in-house staff development has been taking place in many areas, including the Teaching Topic Thursdays and Dows U, giving teachers a voice and share areas of expertise.

-
		An increase in student support by the math interventionist for kindergardners as areas of need have become clear. Ms. Benedetto highlighted the supports that are being put in place to help staff and student in areas of student behavior.

	Looking forward, the desire is to expand Fundations in second grade as well as to continue to build on student support services such as RTI, as well as inquiry-based learning, which connects also to technology, including STEM opportunities utilizing Project Lead the Way. The hope is to build and expand tiered intervention to fill in gaps of learning in second and third grade, to prevent longer-term support. Social/emotion support also has been identified as an area of increased need to help those students who might be going through Other areas that they would like to expand upon are character ed programs, such as Dows Days, etc., as well as continued curriculum mapping and alignment across the content areas.

	Mr. Grados noted that the earlier intervention is provided to students, the better for the students and it is important to provide funding for support. Mr. Whitney asked about the financial impacts on the budget for the additional support services and if it would require additional staff. Additional and/or supplemental personnel would be needed for additional math support and social/emotional support. Mrs. Kerner asked about ways to improve our current math programs before we move forward with more resources being spent on PLTW, which is important to develop engineers, etc. but also important is the development of strong foundational. Mrs. Kashkin asked for examples of what the students are getting from Fundations that they were not getting in the past. Consistency from classroom to classroom is one of the benefits and it is seen that students are beginning to read texts earlier and to know words in a deeper way.

	At the Main Street School, Principal Joyce Chapnick noted:

-
		The math coach has been an amazing resource to both teachers and students, especially helping them work through the math modules Additional ASI math support has enabled to provide more consistency and greater support to students in the classroom as well as pull them out for greater reinforcement.

-
		Training of aides has also been helpful this year to ensure that the language and consistency surrounding student behavior and was an increase to the budget.

	Looking forward to next year, the focus will be to find ways to expand opportunities for students, to look more globally by increasing club opportunities that will foster students' interest and passions as well. Funding is provided by PTSA and IEF, but Ms. Chapnick believes it is a responsibility of the District to help mold leadership skills. She would also like to see increased AIS support in order to provide more flexibility in providing services, as there is a disparity in ELA and Math AIS services, or to help students who might need support in a targeted area. Ms. Chapnick is also proposing returning the world language program in fifth grade, which would tie in to increasing global awareness, giving students a lens into other cultures.

	Mrs. Kerner noted that world language was cut in previous budgets was partly due to the program's curriculum and that an outline of how the program would be delivered differently would be very helpful for the Board to have in making decisions. Mr. Grados agreed that it was the fracture of the program at the time that it was eliminated and that public sentiment would be in favor of reinstituting world language. Mrs. Kerner would like to see the State scores for a few years back to help support decisions with regards to increasing AIS support.

	At the Middle School – Asst. Principal, Allyson Daley shared the opportunities that came from last year's budget including as the increase support of students’ academic as well as their social/emotional growth with the structures and systems that are in place to allow children to be exposed to extra-curricular activities, such as the intramural program that was restored through last year's budget as well as clubs that came directly from students' interest, such as the geography club, the Spanish club which helps students develop leadership skills. Next year, they would like support for a debate club which students are very interested in having. Last year’s increase in music staffing, specifically in band, has provided students more frequent, targeted instruction with positive results in the students' success. Mr. Sottile noted the increase in a wide-range of experiences for students both in and out of classroom and he would want to increase the range of opportunities, which requires integration into staff's learning and growing to achieve this for students. One area is continued curriculum mapping and alignment and integration of the common core learning standards in all disciplines. The theme of adult learning at the middle school is the integration of thinking skills by using grade-level learning to share feedback and the results of this is common approaches to learning experiences in all classes. Google Apps is a tool that staff is using that provides feedback to students as well as sharing professional learning and skills that results in broader learning experiences for students. The 6th grade technology program was enhanced this year by having a teacher who can work with each student and part of the 6th grade team and provides a more coherent program for the students and how technology relates to their learning, using software, etc. The use of Chromebooks and Google Apps for Education has excited teachers to find ways to collaborate and help students integrate their skills. Additional Chromebooks will be requested in order to increase the collaboration. Mr. Sottile also spoke about the benefits of introducing PTLW's Gateway program to the middle school, which helps students to become deeper thinkers and enriches learning experiences. He also spoke about the club programs and would like to support students' interests to provide an expansion of clubs.

	With regard to the co-teaching model at the Middle School, Mr. Sottile would request an addition 1.0 FTE to support the model that would support all learners, whether classified or not, by providing differentiation when needed for student learning in all subject areas, and currently teachers are providing the extra support. Mrs. Kerner recollects that the ICT model isn't as efficient as it might be and if improvements could be identified to current program and services delivered differently before additional funds are budgeted. Mrs. Kerner would also like to see instances of how technology has improved instruction, i.e. the net effect on students and would like to see data on the positive influence of using data.

	At the High School, Principal Mr. Cohen reviewed major topics in the areas of curriculum, instruction, with a focus on STEM (Science, Technology, Engineering, Math) and support services, noting that ways to build sequence opportunities have been a topic of discussion at the high school, including expansion of PLTW and looking at technology as a tool to student learning. The 9th grade writing program that was included in the budget last year seems to be having a positive effect on students' writing with a focus on narrative and essay writing, and providing greater experiences as well as inter-disciplinary writing experiences and focusing on writing as a process not an outcome.

	Mr. Cohen addressed the Innovation Fund initiative and the exciting opportunities that it will open to students. Ms. Mariniello highlighted the Bridges program which was begun this year to hopefully inspire students who might struggle, not only academically but finding their own way.  Ms. Mariniello thanked the PTSA for supporting the Bridges program and ways to increase the rigor of the program and expand it through the senior class. World language has also been a focus this year, using the Tri-States visit's recommendations from last year. Addressing the vision of the guidance program that was discussed last year, it was necessary to look outside and within the current program to name it and then build it. This has been done through the use of a consultant as well as the counselors spending time with guidance colleagues in the area to see what might be useful in Irvington but also increasing college visits to create relationships and to see what is happening at the college level.

	Mr. Cohen addressed the needs of the high school for next year, beginning with expanding the PTLW program, and offering additional engineering courses, computer programming courses, perhaps occupational education needs to be considered. Instruction needs to prepare students to grow and learn so that jobs that don't even exist yet, might be of interest to them in the future. Mr. Cohen also stated that infrastructural needs are also important at the high school, especially in the area of clerical support and the struggle has been how to best support the structure, noting that most neighboring schools have 5-7 clerical support staff vs. three in Irvington. There have been reporting issues at the high school, but Mr. Cohen's concerns are great in this area of support and would allow for the administrative staff to use their time more appropriately. This will be a budget request to increase clerical staff for next year. Also being requested for next year will a Dept. Chair for Special Education in order to participate in weekly meetings where instructional rounds are discussed and will provide a link in different content areas. The lack of a special ed chair doesn't allow for sharing of information that is brought back to all staff. Gaps have also been identified at the high school for ICT support, etc. Therefore, utilizing resource room teachers is the model that should be utilized which would enhance student learning. additional mental health supports is needed as students exhibit stress and are struggling. The student assistance counselor has become an important part of the network but the caseload of the school psychologist is maxed out. Ms. Mariniello passionately spoke to the needs of the high school students and to support them in all ways, as they are still children. Mrs. Kerner asked if there is one goal for the guidance department and Mr. Cohen said the conversation is to be able to identify what we want the students to leave the high school with.

	Mr. Whitney noted that his take away from tonight's conversation is both trepidation and respect and wonders if our teachers' are highly stressed and what is going on with them. He noted that to hear from administration tonight has been extremely useful to the Board.

	Dr. Harrison proposed that another Work Session be held to hold the conversation regarding student assessment and UBD, which both tie together.

	Mr. Grados left prior to the conclusion of the discussion.
