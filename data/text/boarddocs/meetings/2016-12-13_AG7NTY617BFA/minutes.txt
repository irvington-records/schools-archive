4. Administrators' Update/Board Committee & Liaison Reports

Dr. Harrison reported on the following;

- The development of the new website is underway. Jesse Lubinsky helped facilitate focus groups consisting of staff, students and parents to gain feedback and thanked everyone who contributed to the focus groups as well as Mr. Lubinsky for facilitating the process.. The information will be shared with the Board at a later date.

- The budget process is underway with meetings being held with administrators, to begin the process of building the budget. Last year, Irvington saw a negative increase with the tax cap, however, there is a little more optimism for next year, noting that the state is looking to make adjustments to Foundation Aid, which Irvington is underfunded by approximately $1 million. Concern is growing in general over seeing little in state aid increases as the state will be paying the increased foundation aide on the backs of the wealthier school districts. There will be an advocacy movement to address the needs of these districts.

- The IHS/IMS Band will perform this week.

Information: 4.2 Curriculum/Instruction and Business Office Update

Dr. Kor again spoke to about the Science Fair scheduled for March 25, noting that Lisa Armogan, a parent and one of the organizers, will be visiting science classes and promoting high school students as mentors to the younger students. This year the arts will be added to the program.

- The Wellness Committee met and many of the curriculum activities taking place in District were presented and acknowledged that the District has come a long way in this area.

6. Presentations/Discussions

Presentation: 6.1 2015-16 Student Performance Report

Dr. Harrison reported that this year's report is in a different format, and is a layover as we transition to another presentation model that the Board has requested. Dr. Kor thanked Dr. Harrison for creating the graphs in the report. Dr. Kor addressed how the data is used and the difficulties of reporting the data are due to many variabilities, especially now in light of students opting out of standardized testing, looking at different cohorts, etc. which can skew the data. Summary of information presented includes, but not limited to:

- Irvington continues to perform at a very high level, i.e. SAT scores and ACT scores higher than national average;

- Many athletic teams recognized as scholars;

- ELA scores rank among top six among measured cohorts of schools;

- shifts in ensuring test-type questions in planning instruction;

- using data to identify which standards posed challenges for individual students which can now do since the state releases the questions

- Charts illustrating students’ performance in Level 1-4 in ELA, Math, Science as well as the Regents were presented, and it was noted that the State now scores ELA Common Core in 5 levels. Data was shared with comparative districts, including: Ardsley, Blind Brook, Briarcliff Manor, Bronxville, Byram Hills, Chappaqua, Dobbs Ferry, Edgemont, Hastings, Pleasantville, Rye Neck and Scarsdale. Of note, selection is based on similar population, socio-economics as well as similar number of students, however, again due to the opting out, numbers are skewed.

Summarization of information includes:

- evidence of continued growth in Level 3-4 and decreases in Level 1-2;

	and the mean score average of MS ELA is the highest in our regional cohort.

- with respect to the Regents courses, there is consideration of the value/need to continue as few colleges consider the Regents diploma outside of NYS;

- review of the Advanced Placement courses, with Dr. Kor noting that Irvington has open enrollment in all AP courses which increases access to all students and is a point of pride for the District. She reviewed the number of AP courses offered over a 13 year period, with this year’s 20 courses being the highest numbered offered in recent history. The participation and passing rates were illustrated over the last 13 years as well, along with scores graphically presented. The Board and Principal Cohen discussed the grading of AP exams as well as final course grades as there are discrepancies and disparities in the exam grade and final grade in a course noting that individual teachers grade AP course work differently, with Ms. Palmieri thinking that one of the challenges is to be sure there is equitable treatment of AP course grading. Deb Hargraves is curious as to how scores either inform teaching or design of course(s), what is gleaned from scores or is open enrollment of AP creating the disparity in grading. It was agreed that this is a subject that further discussion would be helpful.

- Specific questions were asked regarding cohort trends in test scores over the years in certain areas.Also noted was that out of the 569 students enrolled in AP courses, 558 did sit for the exams.

In summary, examining testing data is only one window into how our students perform and that through the variables, one must be careful in what conclusions are drawn as they are not necessarily representative of how students are doing overall. Mr. Graeber wants the testing data to matter as much time is spent on testing, and it should be used.

Information: 6.2 Strategic Plan Update

Dr. Harrison updated the Board as to where the District is with the Strategic Plan and provided a very brief overview of the process that took place last year with community groups, creating goals to focus on, along with a revised Mission Statement. Following, an admin sub-committee, including David Sottile, Raina Kor, David Cohen and Joyce Chapnick worked to align what is going on in the District with the objectives.

A special meeting will be scheduled in January to present the work of the committee, where we are today, the vision of where the District is going, and to work through writing the final strategic plan. Work sessions will be scheduled to allow for community input as to future direction of the District. The objective is to adopt the Strategic Plan in March 2017. The Board agreed with the timeline. Ms. Palmieri asked if the conversation will also include 9:56.

Discussion: 6.3 Discussion on Food Services

Carol Stein reported that Food and Wellness meeting Dave Alperts and area representative , attended and addressed the changes made as a result of the survey comments and how they've been implemented in the cafeteria. Changes/enhancements include:

- smoothie machine has been purchased for smoothie station;

- sampling pizza in the spring that the students seemed to like, even though it is whole wheat which is required through the National School Lunch standards;

- working with IT department to improve the website, communicate with parents as to what is happening with the food program; and

- gluten-free meals will be noted on menu by the use of an *

- the long lines have been addressed

She noted that many of the comments from the survey are out of the District's ability to make changes as they are mandated by the Federal School Lunch program.

Deb Hargraves asked if the notion of a kiosk has been discussed to help ease the lines and noted that a large number of students leave campus or have food delivered. Dr. Harrison noted that the District will go out to bid for food service next year and can include items that might be appealing to students, such as a premium meal offering.

Mrs. Stein reiterated that the preparation of food is limited and it is difficult to compete against local stores, such as Geordanes. She also noted that the food service makes money on selling snacks and would be difficult to curtail the sale and also noted that they must meet the standards so that for the most part, they are healthy snacks. Deb Hargraves noted that many parents are not in favor of students leaving the campus as it is expensive and would appreciate alternatives being introduced to our program.

It was suggested that changes and improvements be highlighted on Facebook.

Discussion: 6.4 Discussion on Child Care Program

Catherine Palmieri, Michael Hanna and Dr. Harrison discussed looking forward and think about providing child care for our teachers and wanted to hear the other members' opinions. Dr. Harrison noted that space would be an issue, would need to get licensed by the State, with stringent regulations by the State, curriculum would need to be developed, etc. Research would need to be done to see if it would even viable, with space being an issue, with only available space at Dows Lane would be where Easter Seals has a classroom and would need to give them a one year notice to vacate. It was asked if leasing space would be possible.

Deb Hargraves agreed that it is a great idea, but thinks about the challenges, legal and otherwise. If not too time consuming  to investigate, she would be open to hearing more about but believes given the constraints might be overwhelming. Board asked if there are other Districts in the area who provides child care. Ms. Palmieri thought it would be a positive to help teachers. Mr. Hanna believes that it is important to prioritize these discussions, as this is a worthy discussion but that there are many other items that are in front of the Board.
