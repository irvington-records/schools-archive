	13. Presentations/Discussions

	Presentation: 13.1 Instructional Design using the Understanding by Design (UBD) Framework (Attachment)

	Dr. Kor began by stating that the to teach for understanding is the goal of teachers, to teach how to think and to provide  deeper learning that will take students through their school years and beyond. Dr. Kor played a video that spoke to how to educate students for the unknown but know that it is necessary to teach skills that enables the student to connect acquired knowledge with different concepts which requires understanding of how concepts are relevant to other areas and across disciplines. The work being done in Irvington is to look at how we teach and using Understanding by Design as a framework on how instruction is thought about. Dr. Kor noted that this work has been taking place over the past 14 years in various pockets within the District. She shared the essential questions that teachers ask as they look at particular instruction to ensure that it will lead to understanding and be transferable, therefore enabling students to think in a broader way or rather the "big ideas". Dr. Kor shared that a range of assessments are used to assess students' understanding including tasks and projects, informal checks for understanding, conversations along with small group instruction, and if students are able to explain, connect and predict outcomes in different contents that would show that students are learning to think at deeper levels and are applying their skill and knowledge from one year to the next.

	She reviewed the link between learning for understanding (using Understanding by Design) and curriculum mapping, using a software called Rubicon Atlas which houses the curriculum being written by our teachers. It provides a place for teachers to place their work or to access resources with the advantage of ensuring ensures that all aspects of a unit are addressed and for teachers not to lose track of what is being taught..

	Mr. Whitney asked how, as a District, identify teachers who may not agree with this methodology of teaching and how do we know if it is a problem or not. If it proves to be problematic, how is it dealt with. Dr. Kor acknowledges that this work being undertaken is difficult and the District is putting into place support for teachers to ensure that they are successful as well as to build their capacity. Dr. Kor also believes that all the new curriculum will be based on deeper level of thinking, inquiry. In response to a question from the Board if  these learning experiences will be required of new hires? It would not be essential to have had this experience but would look to the person to be teachable.
