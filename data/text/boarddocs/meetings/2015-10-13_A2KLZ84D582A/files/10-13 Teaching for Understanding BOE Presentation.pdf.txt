   What is Understanding?
    Effective use of knowledge and skill in varied,
     important, realistic, and novel situations
    Insight into key ideas, their rationale, and their
     connections, as reflected in wise expression –
     knowing & showing “why?” and “so what?”

   Key Questions
      Is it possible to have a great deal of knowledge but
       limited understanding?
      What is the relationship between understanding,
       knowledge and skill?
      What counts as evidence of student understanding?

    Understanding by Design
     Helps us focus on big ideas and complex
      performance
     Moves us beyond micro-managing instruction via
      overly fragmented and isolated lessons and activities
     Forces us to constantly consider the alignment of
      elements
     Guides us to think about whether we are on course,
      and make adjustments in light of evidence

    Designing with Reflection in Mind
         “Children are rarely provided work in redefining,
         reshaping, and reordering what has been
         encountered. The cultivation of reflectiveness is
         one of the great problems one faces . . .
                                                in devising curricula”


                      Jerome Bruner, “Growth of the Mind” 1966




Grant Wiggins and Jay McTighe – Understanding by Design

 Implications of Bruner Quote
 • Understanding constantly requires students to
   rethink what they likely thought was settled
 • Developing understanding (or not) is more a function
   of “spiral” curricular design than teacher style or
   method
 • Conventional linear (text-book driven) scope and
   sequence is a major impediment to developing
   understanding




Grant Wiggins and Jay McTighe – Understanding by Design

    Understanding involves “Big Ideas”
     • Is it a Big Idea?
         Does it . . .
          have lasting value/transfer to other inquiries?
          lie at the “heart” of the discipline?
          require “uncoverage” (abstract or often-
          misunderstood ideas)?
         serve as a key concept/strategy for making
          knowledge, skills, and acts more coherent,
          meaningful, and, useful?


Grant Wiggins and Jay McTighe – Understanding by Design

    Framing Understandings as
    Essential Questions
     What questions –
         are at the heart of the subject?
         are arguable - and important to argue about?
         often recur - and should recur - in professional work,
          as well as in classroom inquiry?
         can effectively provoke and sustain engaged inquiry
          and focus final performance(s)?
         often raise important conceptual or philosophical
          issues?
         Can provide purpose for learning?

Grant Wiggins and Jay McTighe – Understanding by Design

   Indicators of Understanding
   You really understand when you can . . .
      Explain, connect, systematize, predict it
      Show its importance, meaning
      Apply or adapt it to novel situations
      See it as a plausible perspective, question its
       assumptions
      See it as its author saw it
      Avoid and point out common misconceptions, biases,
       or simplistic views
