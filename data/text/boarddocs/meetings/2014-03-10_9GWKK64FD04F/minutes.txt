	3. Work Session of the Board of Education – Field Project Committee Report (Presentation) (Report)

	On behalf of the Board and administration, Robyn Kerner thanked the members of the Field Project Committee for all their incredible work and that she hopes that it will help the community, Board and administration to make a decision on our fields. Mrs. Kerner also noted that facilities projects that need to be taken care of and could be included in a referendum, will be addressed on Thursday night.

	Dr. Harrison reported that the committee was charged with a large task and one they took very seriously. The baseline that was established by the Board was to restore the playing surface at East Field and Meszaros Field to its historic grass playing surface and the committee was charged with investigating potential enhancements to the established baseline. Additionally, Dr. Harrison noted that the committee was comprised of 11 individuals who brought a variety of experiences, both professionally and civically. The committee met for over 20 hours in a three week period. Members of the committee included: Ann Acheson, Sean Brady, Michael Gyrory, Michael Hanna, Deb Hargraves, Michael Havraniak (who had to resign due to work conflict.)Rick Rasulo and Deb Hargraves co-chaired and Michael Hanna recorded the minutes of each meeting.

	Rick Rasulo provided a synopsis of the committee’s report via a PowerPoint presentation which addressed the five major questions that the committee was charged with along with their responses. The five questions were:

	Question 1: Should artificial turf be considered for East Field? The committee’s response was a unanimous vote that it should not.

	Question 2: Does artificial or natural turf better meet the needs of school programs and the community? The committee voted it 7-3 that it should be turfed.

	Question 3: Should the addition of lights be considered for any of our fields? The Committee’s response was no (9-1aginst lights at this time but recommend the installation of conduit for possible future use).

	Question 4: Should replacement of Oley Track mirror the existing or should we consideration alterations? The committee voted unanimously to replace it on the existing footprint.

	Question 5: Are other athletic facility improvements that should be considered?

	The committee also analyzed the District’s athletic programs, including the type of sports that are offered, number of athletes in each sport, how often the fields are used, seasons, when are fields rested, etc., the inventory of the District’s fields as well as the Village’s sport facilities, the size, type and condition of the fields as well as the use of the fields (including physical education classes) and field maintenance, which is a critical consideration. The conclusion of the committee is that all the fields are interrelated and must be looked at as system and not in isolation.

	Deb Hargraves compiled a field usage chart for both the spring and fall playing seasons for each of the District’s fields as well as the Village’s fields, four district and two Village fields. The chart broke out weekday use and weekend use for practice, use and community usage, including the number of players. Ms. Hargraves noted that each of the fields is used every day. She noted that over 200 children are using lower Dows for AYSO soccer. She also highlighted that the participation of the younger children follows through to their playing at the school level.

	The conclusion to use natural grass on East Field was due to several factors, including protecting natural green space, it is used primarily as a practice field and considerations for drainage and the neighbors at Fieldpoint were taken into account and found to be in the best interest of students, the community and the neighbors.  However, the DEC/DOH has concluded that 1 foot cap is sufficient on East Field, thus saving approximately $100,000-200,000.

	With respect to Meszaros Field, the committee recommends artificial turf with organic infill. Oley Track has reached the end of its service and the asphalt surface is in need of repair, which the committee recommends installation of a rubberized surface above the repaired asphalt.

	Other improvements included, but not limited to, a widened delineated playing field striping on Meszaros to accommodate regulation size men’s lacrosse/soccer field, some sports field equipment, such as lacrosse nets, additional home bleachers, security enhancements to protect the investment of the field and proper used of the tower should be explored for storage of maintenance equipment. Also it was recommended that outsourcing of the maintenance of the artificial turf field be considered and a schedule be developed for East Field.

	Dr. Harrison reviewed the financial implications of the Committee’s recommended scope of the project. He also noted that when meeting with DEC/DOH several weeks ago, and they determined that there is already 1 foot of clean fill and that it isn’t necessary to bring in more than 1 ft. soil cap and that further excavation is not necessary, thereby reducing the cost and not having to remove trees and reducing the number of construction trucks necessary.

	The anticipated cost of the remediation of East Field is $1,216,000 to install a seeded field, a drainage and irrigation system. The cost for Meszaros and Oley Track are anticipated to be $1,797,525 to install a hybrid turf, drainage with additional 6” stone base (total 12” base) and to replace Oley Track. If organic turf is considered, $60,000-80,000 would be added to the cost. Dr. Harrison reinforced that the costs are for illustrative and stressed that the projected costs are conservative and might be lower but wanted to be sure that everyone had a complete picture of the expenses involved in such projects.

	Following the presentation, there was further discussion and questions among the Board and Committee members regarding the information presented including pros and cons of natural vs. artificial turf, field usage within the community, safety of artificial turf, the recommendation to use organic or hybrid organic fill instead of crumb rubber, drainage, permeable track surfaces vs. impervious surfaces, lighting and ongoing maintenance costs of renovations of fields.

	 Anne Acheson, a member of the committee and one of the minority in favor of using natural fields, noted that DEC will mandate that East Field have a maintenance plan and that the field would not be able to fall into disrepair to ensure that the soil cap remains in tack.

	Other questions from the Board included if there was possible revenue to be gained by having an artificial turf, such as camps, etc. and the cost of properly maintaining upper and lower Dows Fields so that it doesn’t deteriorate. It was noted that Upper Dows field has deteriorated due to the East Field being out of commission but that it had been acceptably maintained. Ms. Acheson noted that it is possible to research the optimal type of seed to use for this area that factors in soil type and maintenance. The District should have a clear picture of what needs to be done to get them back in good order and what the ongoing maintenance costs are. Dr. Harrison noted that irrigation is an issue to maintaining healthy fields on lower Dows. Mrs. Kerner also asked if the proposed budget needs to be looked at to be sure that there is sufficient monies to support the need for properly maintaining the fields.
