	5. Superintendent's Report

	Dr. Harrison reported on the following:

-
		Transportation update - Interest was expressed at previous board meetings to conduct a study to assess the viability of extending current bus routes into private communities and to that end, a consultant has been retained to conduct the study. At the request of the Board, the scope of the study has been expanded to look deeper into other aspects of our transportation program, and will include the length of bus rides, ridership, efficiency of routes, etc. The study will take approximately six weeks and findings will be discussed at a future meeting. Also, Montauk Transportation has advised the District that they are not interested in renewing the contract for next year. Administration is looking into that and will report back to the Board.

-
		Strategic Plan - Community feedback is important in creating a strategic plan. The committee is meeting tomorrow and will review the feedback received from the recent survey. Additional students and alumni and a parent will join the group.

-
		Bond Update –Currently planning on bids for remaining bond projects as several had to go back out to bid. Negotiations are still ongoing with homeowners at Fieldpoint regarding easements. It will be a very tight timeline to break ground in June.

-
		Budget - conversations will begin at the next meeting and there are reasons to be concerned regarding state funding. In addition to the tax cap formula being damaged, there is no inflation and no growth to the tax base which will keep the percentage of our tax cap very low. Districts will be looking for restoration of GEA and adjustments to Foundation Aid when the Governor proposes his budget tomorrow. Thank you to the PTSA for advocating for the district with respect to funding.

-
		The 2016-17 district calendar was to be discussed tonight, but SED has not announced the testing dates and hope to be able to present a draft in several weeks.

-
		Dr. Harrison and Artie McCormack, Athletic Director, attended a press conference where County Executive Astorino announced the county’s Safer Sports Initiative related to managing concussion treatment. He noted that Irvington has one of the most comprehensive concussion management program. Dr. Marc Herzog, a district resident and Westchester County Commissioner for Mental Health was important to the development of the plan.

	6. Presentations/Discussions

	Discussion: 6.1 Technology and Project Lead the Way Progress (Presentation)

	Dr. Kor and Jesse Lubinsky, Director of Technology, provided an update on the technology program, and took the opportunity to give a window into the thinking and concerns regarding the upcoming budget discussions. Dr. Kor began with the Theories of Action and how technology is  linked to them across all content areas. Examples were provided of the work being done for each of the seven technology goals. Mr. Lubinsky noted that with the expansion of mobile networking, the bandwidth will need to be increased. Mr. Lubinsky spoke about the use of Google Classroom which provides teachers with course management solutions such as collecting assignments or distributes materials to students. Privacy is constantly being monitored. Apps that teachers might choose to use are matched to the curriculum, not outside it, and used as additional materials and resources. In response to a question as to how parents are informed as to what is being done in the classroom through technology, Dr. Kor reminded that this discussion is focused on how technology is being used in the classroom through tools to improve instruction which is different than a discussion as to the teaching of technology, such as coding, etc. Goal #6 does deal with best practices in technology integration and innovation within the classroom as well as innovative programs, such as in PLTW and the mobile apps class at the MS as well as the new class at 8th grade for computer programming being offered as a "0" period course. Dr. Kor spoke to the overlap of goals which means that they are aligned and connected, such as Goal 7: Global Education and Citizenship which uses technology to connect with students in the States and internationally, providing students with exposure to different cultures, viś a viś Google Hangouts.

	With respect to professional development, teachers who were excited expanded their capacity and are now be used as a resource to peers. A data collection program, Clarity, has been purchased, which will allow teachers to survey students and parents and measure data over time to see what areas have improved and, where improvement can be made. The goal is to collect data in the fall and spring from teachers, students and parents and Mr. Lubinsky noted that responses were on the high end. Dr. Kor also noted that due to the complexity of the program, use of this program is in its infancy  and it is still yet to be determined how the data collected will be utilized.

	A brief review of the PLTW was provided and the current course offerings and units that are also required as part of PLTW. Dr. Kor noted that training for PLTW is significant for teachers. The District received a Bemis grant at the MS which is a two year grant as well as the PTSA's financial support of PLTW which allowed for much of this year's accomplishments in the program. Dr. Kor noted that it is also important to communicate with parents as to what the students are doing in the classroom with respect to PLTW. To this end an Instagram feed has been created that allows parents, etc. to view pictures of the students in action in the classroom.

	With respect to budgetary considerations, Dr. Kor did note that classrooms need to be redesigned to allow for more flexible learning spaces and that with STEM work, the traditional classroom setup is not conducive. She also reviewed possible course sequence options viś a viś PLTW, such as computer science, biomedical science, etc. as well as expansion of the program at the other elementary grade levels. However, staffing is a huge issue, especially at the elementary level and will be the biggest obstacle to growing the program. With respect to any changes and/or needs, the District is waiting for the State to provide the new Science Standards before those discussions are initiated.

	7. Comments from the Community

	A question was asked about programs for 6th grade. Dr. Kor said that there are discussions about moving some programs into 6th grade which would allow for 8th graders to take more specialized units, but again stated that there are limitations due to staffing, with currently only one teacher trained in PLTW.

	A parent of two daughters in 7th and 9th grade commented that the cell phone policy requires students to keep their phones in their lockers, but that her children have experienced that some teachers use them as part of instruction (example: utilization of emojies in the class assignment) so that students without iPhones seem to be at a disadvantage, making them feel left out. The Board discussed the policy (#3161-Students and Personal Electronic Devices--Bring Your Own Device (BYOD)) which states that no child should feel disadvantaged due to lack of technology and staff must be aware of this policy. Mr. Montgomery would like the policy to be made clear from the top down that technology should not be required if not all students have access to the technology. Dr. Harrison will have this on the next administrative meeting for discussion and to make sure that the policy and the school's handbook on the matter is in alignment.
