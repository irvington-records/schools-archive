5. Presentations/Discussions

Discussion: 5.1 Administrators Roundtable Discussion

Dr. Harrison noted that this is the 3rd annual roundtable conversation for the purpose of having the Board and administration team focus on the work going on in our schools, update the Board on each school's goal work as well as possible needs that will be addressed in the upcoming budget conversations. Goals developed this year are:

- focus on instruction

- development of model assessments

- development a new website.

Dr. Kor addressed the work that is taking place in the schools and based on the Theories of Action, embedding thinking curriculum and using Understanding By Design framework. Shenoted that the last cohort of teachers are being trained this week at which point all teachers will be trained in UBD. Professional development is closely linked to the goals and has been aligned to ensure that it is moving toward realization in all classrooms.

Dows Lane Principal, Deb Marinello, and Asst. Principal, Jackie Salcito, shared an update on what is taking place at Dows Lane this year:

- data-driven instruction with teachers sharing through learning sessions in math, looking at student work with the District's math consultant to inform thinking, looking at what follows in the curriculum for the students and what the students need to be thinking to complete the problems

- benchmarking student work viś a viś data storage program, AIMSweb

- balanced literacy and using guided reading, identifying needs.

- Very proud of work to commitment to social/emotional learning with Character Committee and Technology Committee. Once monthly, there is a schoolwide meeting to talk about theme of the month and getting entire school involved in displaying school spirit.

- Assessing what technology and technology integration into the curriculum should look like for the youngest students in Irvington.

Dr. Kor noted that the introduction of Fundations in literacy has played an important role in building the early learners' literacy skills as it utilizes consistent language through the early grades, building their fluency as well as their understanding and being able to infer texts.

Joyce Chapnick, Principal of Main Street School, shared some of the work at MSS this year and noted that more than ever that Irvington is a K-12 system and the purposeful way of planning of everything that is taking place in the schools for each year, that nothing is done in isolation and is built upon year after year. She spoke to:

- Math learning sessions at MSS look similar to Dows Lane and noting that there are point teachers taking the lead and go through the unit planners which are shared with all teachers.

- Essential Questions are also utilized to see how students' thinking can be moved forward along with utilizing Theories of Action to ensure that students' are developing the ability to acquire deeper thinking in all areas.

- Math coach, Christine Rosner, works to support staff in moving the math program along. Dr Kor noted the goal is to develop strong teachers of mathematics so that whatever program is used, the teachers are comfortable with the content, in their knowledge and strategies to instruct their students.

- Tiered interventions, including RTI, for either academics or behavioral, including interventions strategies for a particular student and teachers monitoring and assess their progress or lack of progress and what next steps might be. This also creates a paper trail for each child in order thereby creating historical data.

- AIMSweb is utilized and is a valuable tool in the area of intervention.

- Addressed the Project Lead The Way curriculum

- Activities around character education, global citizenship, etc.

Allyson Daley, Asst. principal at the Irvington Middle School, spoke of the professional development at the middle school which also utilizes the Essential Questions, a valuable starting point for staff, to develop curriculum to elevate and deepen student thinking.  Other examples of staff development with the goal to deepen students’ thinking and learning include:

- A series of faculty meetings were designed this year with staff to share examples of strategies used in their classroom and receiving feedback from their peers, i.e. using accountable talk, utilizing entrance or exit tickets for students to respond to a particular unit, etc. in class lessons. The work that Sara Rust, literacy coach at the secondary level, is undertaking with middle school teachers.

- BARD, which is intense and hands on to enable teachers to develop lessons to again deepen student thinking and writing, DR. Kor spoke in depth about the BARD program and the many elements it offers to our staff.

- Metamorphisis which supports teachers who teach mathematics, again to help students engage in true problem solving, etc.

David Sottile, Principal of the Middle School, spoke to the next level of work at the middle school, which will be looking at assessment tools utilized to ensure they measure what we want students to learn. He spoke of the connection with the sixth grade teachers’ visit with the fourth and fifth grade teachers, and Christine Rosner to best build instruction that will take place in sixth grade, using the same language and to build the bridge from elementary to secondary learning which enables better understanding as to where best to start when they are designing their instruction that meets students' needs in Math.  At the middle school, technology integration is a multi-pronged program, with hardware as well as the use of Google Suite, especially Google Classroom, noting that a  number of teachers are on board and many teachers taking advantage of training to understand the tool and its abilities, and how students can use devices on hand in the classrooms.  With respect to Project Lead the Way at the middle school, Mr. Sottile noted that teachers are thinking about ways to integrate technology into the curriculum. Two foundation courses are offered, and has been a great experience in program writing, with the physical learning space reconfigured for flexibility in learning. Teachers are becoming more comfortable with the course content and are able to modify the modulars accordingly, especially as students are entering the middle school with several years of PLTW curriculum and exposure from elementary, requiring courses to go deeper into the subject matter. Assessments in these courses are more of a "show me" what you know rather than a paper test asking "what do you know'.

David Cohen, Irvington High School Principal and Matthew Samuelson, Assistant Principal,  highlighted the initiatives taking place at the high school, with a focus on building learning communities and the professional development, around the implementation phase, focusing on closing activities to assess what the students know at the end of the lesson. Mr. Cohen highlighted powerful work of professional development in the areas of Math, ELA, World Language etc. with the end results of all professional learning to push students learning forward, and increase proficiency as well as engage students,

As an aside, it was noted the State has not acted upon our Smart Schools grant which provide funds for devices and wi-fi as the state has not met to review grants, and Irvington is in the next cohort to be reviewed. The District is prepared to move as soon as the State approves the plan.

He to spoke of the impact that Sara Rust has had in the high school and teachers are utilizing her as a resource. Matt Samuelson spoke of activities in faculty meetings, UBD cohort training and department meetings, which the coaching that the Department Chairs are overseeing, but it is observable that many of the techniques being offered in the professional development are being implemented in the classroom and are very much teacher driven. Mr. Cohen spoke to the use of technology in the teaching process, and teachers wanting to get deeper into using Google Classroom or other Google components. There is increased direction by teachers for students to utilize the Chromebooks at the high school, especially in the 9th grade writing class getting feedback on an individual basis. They are witnessing greater dependency on technology and will continue to work with teachers in options and ways for implementation.

Of impact at the high school is the development of the master schedule, which is the foundation and structure of the high school experiences, and also provides better understanding to how it supports professional learning, not just class scheduling and extra-curricular opportunities, clubs, etc. There is a scheduling committee representative of every department which is looking at what is and what is not working in our scheduling, with review of different models available well as looking at other districts, and seeking input from all staff to weigh in on the different models. The remainder of the school year will be devoted to collecting data on different models to find a model that most aligns with the attributes that are most desirable for the high school. Mr. Cohen believes this is transformational work taking place, is exciting work, and is building culture.

With respect to the upcoming budget development, Dr. Harrison spoke of the increase in the dependency on technology, evident in the increase in help desk requests, but with the same amount of staff, the need for additional staffing at the high school as there are currently limitations in class options, a desire to restore cuts that took place in the area of arts, and the desire for ELA coaches at the elementary level as it is evident of the positive impact that the coach at the MS/HS level is having. What will be important to Irvington with respect to the budget will be the aid amount the District receives, if Foundation Aid is restored, which will allow for meeting these needs.

Discussion: 5.2 School Security Discussion

Catherine Palmieri referred to questions and concerns raised regarding a fairly open campus at the secondary campus, what security is in place currently, and what opportunities there might be to address any issues. Dr. Harrison said that there is a public piece to the conversation as well as a private conversation to be had. At the elementary level, the campus' are secured along with security greeters, and all doors are locked. At the secondary campus, there is a security greeter at the entrance of the Maher Gym and the Middle School lobby. The challenge is that there is an open campus which is very much a part of Irvington, but there are some potential short/long term goals, however, limited due to security staff placed at the desk. He noted that there might be value in having a roaming security guard, in and out of the buildings, further enforcement of parking concerns, and possibly going to ID cards for students and staff. Staff now wear ID badges and custodians now have shirts that identify them. The bigger picture would be to look to enclose the campus but is very involved and would be extremely costly, but there might be more affordable ways of enclosing the breezeways, however, funding would be via a bond referendum, not the general budget. There is also opportunity to extend the camera system throughout the district, and ultimately having a swipe card system. Dr. Harrison also believes that is a multi-year endeavor, with long and short term objective of adding an additional security person at the campus, student ID and long term upgrades would need to be financed through a bond. Deb Hargraves agreed and asked about the desk orientation at the Maher Gym to which Dr. Harrison agreed it was not ideal but noted that there are limited options. Dr. Kor noted that one of the options would be to move the main entrance to the atrium. Ms. Hargraves also thought perhaps the use of mirrors for observation in the hallways might be helpful and questioned using walkie-talkies instead of cell phones. She also noted that the Maher Gym needs to be marked if it is the main entrance. Dr. Harrison said that signage is being looked into.
