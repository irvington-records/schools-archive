           Special Education Financial Trends
      New Students
         2016-17: 7 students newly registered with IEPs; one needs 1:1 aide, 2 are in ICT
          classroom and one will likely need an out of district placement in near future
         2015-16: 6 students registered; 2 had previous out of district placements and one
          became a settlement
         2014-15: 8 students registered; 1 went to our Special class and has a 1:1 aide
      New 8:1:1 Class added in 2016-17 for 5th grade Special class and New ICT for
       Kindergarten
         Two additional teachers and aides
         Prevents more costly Out of District placement
         Serves goal to keep students in home district
      Budgetary Implications
         Must plan for new students and have a small contingency tuition line
         Easier to plan for internal referrals and manage costs/programs

           Special Education Financial Trends
     Comparison to Other Districts can be flawed

      Comparing cost per student or overall costs among districts is tricky
      Mix of special education students; how many high need/high cost
       students exist in total number?
      Number of non-resident students district educates including parent
       placed at private schools and tuition students affects expenditures
       (offsetting revenue not shown in report card)
      Expense coding differences
        Legal expenses vary in placement
        Health Waivers are benefits in most districts, not in salary line
        Speech, OT and PT often coded partially to Education Support codes and
         not A2250
        PPS Admin in A2250 but oversees Health Services, ENL

     Conclusions
      Special Ed spending has increased yet spending per pupil has leveled from investments in
         special classes and ICT program
           Changes in Student needs
           Budgetary concern for today and future
           Aid ratio declining from state
        Spending per pupil viewed on the percent change basis, while showing variability, has
         declined and sometimes is lower than Regular Ed
           Reflects program strategies to keep students in district where possible
           Reflects vulnerability of new high cost students moving in to district
        Majority of spending is tuition and salaries
           Continue to maximize special classes and student ratios
           Find appropriate placements to avoid stipulation agreements where advantageous
           Utilize consortium where possible
           Maintain strong tracking of student costs for management of expenses and forecasting
           Review programs for effectiveness relative to costs
        Ensure proper coding of expenses for better benchmark comparisons
        Maximize state aid and obtain all revenue due
