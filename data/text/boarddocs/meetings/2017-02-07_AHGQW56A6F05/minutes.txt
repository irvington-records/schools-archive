5. Presentations/Discussions

Information: 5.1 Draft of 2017-18 School Calendar

Dr. Harrison noted that in constructing the school calendar, there is value in trying to align it with surrounding districts, especially for transportation purposes as well as the athletics program.  Of note, 184 days are mandatory and are inclusive of four superintendents days, addresses contractural obligations, and federal and religious holidays. Dr. Harrison noted that next year's calendar is straight forward with students’ first day being September 5th and last student day is June 21, 2018. He highlighted the breaks, early release as well as delayed opening days for professional development, rating days as well as the superintendent conference days. The calendar will be published on the web and the Board will be asked to adopt the calendar at its March 7th meeting.

Additionally, Dr. Harrison addressed a brief discussion with the Board regarding a regular early release on an ongoing basis to support professional development and the Board charged the administration to further discuss and possibly put a proposal in front of the Board for their consideration. However, it was the feeling of administrators that it is premature at this point to bring a proposal to the Board as discussions with faculty, etc. are necessary as well as considerations for child care, etc.

Mrs. Palmieri noted that she has received comments from parents that teachers seem to be out of the classrooms for professional development more so this year. Deb Hargraves asked why Christmas Day is not noted on the calendar as other holidays are noted, and should be noted to be consistent. Dave Graeber suggested that it might be a benefit to communicate to parents the benefits to the students of professional development time, including PD during school hours. Mrs. Palmieri noted that the concerns expressed were more about the teachers being pulled out of the class for training, not necessary conference days or early releases or delayed openings. Dr. Harrison believes that much progress has been made in the area of professional development and that perhaps a plateau has been reached resulting in less professional development needed, at least in the short term.

Information: 5.2 Special Education Budget Review

Dr. Harrison reported that over the past several years, the District has worked to build critical programs and services for the students with special needs, as well fulfilling mandates of Federal and State regulations. Carol Stein presented an analysis of the components of the special education budget which has been designed to provide a deeper understanding and learning experience of the District's programs. The 2017-18 budgetary needs will be discussed on March 7th.

Mrs. Stein noted that the analysis of special education expenses was a good exercise for her to become familiar with the District's spending in this area. She reviewed the special education student count, with 204 students currently identified as special education or 11.6% of total student population, but stressed that the numbers do fluctuate, with an upward trend in students being identified with Autism and Other Health Impaired, which includes ADHD. She reviewed the expenditures, by category and per pupil since 2006-07, which was $36,148 vs. $10,871 for regular students in 2015-16, and that costs are expected to increase next year due to students with higher needs. Comparison to other spending categories, i.e. B&G, regular ed, extra-curricular was reviewed, and of note, difficult to predict spending in special ed due to variability of enrollment of students with special needs. Details of special education spending was reviewed, including salaries, tuitions, PT/OT services, supplies, other contractural areas as well for John Cardinal O’Connor School (JCOS), where the district is required to provide related services to students from other Districts are placed by their parents and whose district of residence is billed back for expenditures, with salaries and tuitions the bulk of the expenditures. She also noted that the District's speech teachers are coded to special education whereas many district's code to general education as well as they do service non-special education students. Mrs. Stein also reviewed the number of teacher aides and teaching assistants, out of district tuitions and BOCES costs, including a student’s related services. This year there are currently nine students in public school placements, nine in private school placements and 10 in BOCES program.

Also reviewed were new students to the District, legal costs, homebound instruction costs which also has variability year to year. Revenue was reviewed, with the district receiving tuition for eight Non-resident students this year who are enrolled in special class programs as well as students accepted through a Consortium program. The health waiver buy-back is coded to the special ed salary line but belong in a benefits and will be adjusted accurately going forward. Graphs were provided for net special education expenditures, excess cost aid and spending per pupil from 2008 to current year.

Prior to reviewing comparison to other districts spending in the area to special education, Ms. Stein cautioned that often expenses are coded differently, population of special needs, etc. which will skew the comparisons. Using Forecast 5, a program that provides cost drivers for districts, showed that Irvington's spending among comparison districts is only slightly higher, with staffing ratios appearing low, but again, coding could be different among the comparison districts. The data does provide some information to perhaps take a closer look at budgetary spending in the area of special ed, but again, stressed the variability of the population. Ms. Stein also wants to look closer at the aid received to be sure every dollar due is accounted for and received. Coding for psychologists, nurses, etc. are coded under PPS budget but that non-special education students are served as well.

Mr. Graeber asked if it is possible to do an analysis of the actual cost of serving children in a specific area, a program to program analysis to determine if an area is going to continue to climb, where possible savings might be, or if it is financially beneficial to place students in district program vs outside placements. Deb Hargraves thanked Ms. Stein for the presentation as it helps the Board to better understand the subject of special education. Mr. Whitney noted that there is value in looking at staffing as there has been an increase over the last couple of years, and to look at why those decisions were made. The Board also had questions regarding the consortium program, what if any adjustments are made to programs year-to-year, homebound instruction, inclusion of special ed programs into the strategic plan, costs of BOCES programs relative to consortium or in district programs and non-resident tuition paying students. Mrs. Palmieri wanted to note on the record that expenses are through 2015/16 but looking at 2016-17 costs, spending is up, with an average of $40,000 per student. She also noted that NYS spending on regular ed students is $11,256 vs $10,906 in Irvington and that the average NYS spend on special ed students is $29,911  --- based on 2012 numbers---pointing to an increase in spending for special education in Irvington over the last several years. Carol Stein would need to do an analysis of the data used for the state to arrive at these figures as might not be comparing “apples to apples”.

The Board accepted comments from the members of the audience and included but not limited to:

- Board members referred to students as "autistic" but should be referred to as students with autism.

- Where do revenues received from non-resident tuitions get accounted for in the budget?

- Billing for Medicaid might be a revenue stream to be looked at as well as getting funds from the IDEA.

- What programs might be developed for children with autistic who should be educated in-district and that district must be proactive in this area with the trends going upward?.

- Has the district analyzed at what grade level students are being placed out of district and at what grade level they are if they come back to district?

- Request for a more detailed line item budget, as line items have been moved to accurately highlight either an increase or decrease in spending.

- Need to look at what services/needs are necessary for each child rather than to classify them.

- Thanked the District for investing in a program that has kept her child in district and asks for continued creative thinking. Would love to see added to strategic plan as to how to make students with needs a valued part of the Irvington school district and community.

- Would be helpful to see how the classifications of students match up the national trends and will give insight to think for the future and how to address the needs.

- Thanked Mrs. Stein for a tremendous fete in analysis and thanked the Board for support of her children, two in residential placement. She noted that her twins look exactly the same on paper but their outcomes are totally different, so you cannot generalize about programs and children’s specific needs.. She would recommend identifying students’ needs when they are little so as to help students stay in the district. Strongly recommend additional support in the PPS office as it is, in her opinion, understaffed.

- There has been an inverse relationship between the amount being spent and the number of students served over the past 10 years, with spending up, student count down.

Dr. Harrison noted that during his tenure, special education programs have added and not cut and that any adjustments +/- have been made accordingly to needs, i.e. aides reduced as per demand or vis a versa. Dr. Harrison concluded the conversation stating that there is no current discussion to shift money but always looking to be sure getting the most for the money spent and that each students’ needs are addressed and served.
