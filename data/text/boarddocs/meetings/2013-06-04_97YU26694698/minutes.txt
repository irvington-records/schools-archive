	       TAX ANTICIPATION NOTE RESOLUTION OF IRVINGTON UNION FREE SCHOOL DISTRICT, NEW YORK, ADOPTED JUNE 4, 2013, AUTHORIZING THE ISSUANCE OF NOT TO EXCEED $3,000,000 TAX ANTICIPATION NOTES IN ANTICIPATION OF THE RECEIPT OF TAXES TO BE LEVIED FOR THE FISCAL YEAR ENDING JUNE 30, 2014

	RESOLVED BY THE BOARD OF EDUCATION OF IRVINGTON UNION FREE SCHOOL DISTRICT, IN THE COUNTY OF WESTCHESTER, NEW YORK, AS FOLLOWS:

	Section 1. Tax Anticipation Notes (herein called “Notes”) of Irvington Union Free School District, in the County of Westchester, New York (herein called “District”), in the principal amount of not to exceed $3,000,000, and any notes in renewal thereof, are hereby authorized to be issued pursuant to the provisions of Sections 24.00 and 39.00 of the Local Finance Law, constituting Chapter 33-a of the Consolidated Laws of the State of New York (herein called “Law”).

	Section 2. The following additional matters are hereby determined and declared:

	(a) The Notes shall be issued in anticipation of the collection of real estate taxes to be levied for school purposes for the fiscal year commencing July 1, 2013 and ending June 30, 2014, and the proceeds of the Notes shall be used only for the purposes for which said taxes are levied.

	(b) The Notes shall mature within the period of one year from the date of their issuance.

	(c) The Notes are not issued in renewal of other notes.

	(d) The total amount of such taxes remains uncollected at the date of adoption of this resolution.

	Section 3. The Notes hereby authorized shall contain the recital of validity prescribed by Section 52.00 of the Law and shall be general obligations of the District, and the faith and credit of the District are hereby pledged to the punctual payment of the principal of and interest on the Notes and unless the Notes are otherwise paid or payment provided for, an amount sufficient for such payment shall be inserted in the budget of the District and a tax sufficient to provide for the payment thereof shall be levied and collected.

	Section 4. Subject to the provisions of this resolution and the Law, and pursuant to Sections 50.00, 56.00, 60.00 and 61.00 of the Law, the power to sell and issue the Notes authorized pursuant hereto, or any renewals thereof, and to determine the terms, form and contents, including the manner of execution, of such Notes, and to execute arbitrage certifications relative thereto, is hereby delegated to the President of the Board of Education, the chief fiscal officer of the District.

	Section 5. The Notes shall be executed in the name of the District by the manual signature of the President of the Board of Education, the Vice President of the Board of Education, the District Treasurer, the District Clerk, or such other officer of the District as shall be designated by the chief fiscal officer of the District, and shall have the corporate seal of the District impressed or imprinted thereon which corporate seal may be attested by the manual signature of the District Clerk.

	Section 6. This resolution shall take effect immediately.

	12. Presentations/Discussions

		Discussion, Presentation:  12.1 Space Utilization Study (Study)

		Dr. Harrison began by stated that the space utilization discussion began many years ago regarding the District's space utilization and use of physical space. An internal audit was conducted several years ago but no action was taken. Dr. Paul Seversky, of the consulting firm, SES Study Team, conducted an analysis of how the District utilizes space, distributes and utilizes staff and to begin conversations as to what possibilities might be before us. The study has taken most of the year to put together the information and further time to analyze the housing market.

		Dr. Seversky noted that transparency is important to this discussion. He stated that there is a new normal in the economy and that there is a dilemma facing communities and Boards of Education across the state and outlined the four variables that are currently facing districts:

-
			state aid to support school districts may stay flat for the foreseeable future;

-
			the capacity for taxpayers of a district to shoulder more of the financial responsibility through property taxes may or may not be possible;

-
			higher achievement is expected for all students; and

-
			school districts, the State and Federal perspective are requiring the delivery of education programs to all students to enable them to be productive citizens in the workforce, to be competitive in the global economy as well as possess the basic skills to pursue post-high school specialized education opportunities.

		The question posed for the purposes of the study was 'Are there options to the current practice that might provide more efficient ways or patterns to organize how the kindergarten though grade twelve program is implemented/delivered over the next  three years?" and noted that the Board and administration had no preconceived notions as to what the findings would or should be. The report provides for five options that the Board and communicate can discuss with tonight's goal to present major findings and time for clarifying questions but, Dr. Seversky stated, it would not appropriate for advising what should be done. He stressed also that what the community values does count in these discussions.

		Highlights of the report included:

		The enrollment benchmark for the purposes of the study was October 1, 2012 with an enrollment of 1,844. Pupil capacity districtwide, based on District class size goals, is 2,158 amounting to an under available operating capacity of 314 students or 14.6%.

		The enrollment projections analysis gives a low, mid and high. In Irvington, the capacity is remaining fairly stable. Statistical analysis used is set forth by the commissioner's regulations. The study provided for eight possible scenarios along with rationales for each and Dr. Seversky noted that all but one scenario calls for utilizing the Main Street School in some way, with one option to sell or rent the building but noted that it is viewed as a community asset.

		Irvington is faced at this time with both opportunity and challenges with regard to  He also reiterated that Irvington has utilized the Princeton model which is single schools housing same grade levels and which is economical to the District as there is no duplication of staff.

		Dr. Seversky feels Irvington is at the point where we might do nothing at this time and see if the increase in enrollment this year is a blip, noting that the analysis indicates that there will be a significant portion of unused pupil capacity in the current 6-12 buildings, if the space is deployed in the same way as now exists. Looking at the projected enrollments through 2017-18, enrollment is projected to be approximately 1,516 - 1,693. Dr. Seversky does not suggest that the District take over any student space for adult usage (such as utilizing for district office staff) since if the enrollment does increase, the space would need to be used for students.

		The study did not take a look at the quality of the structures, especially the MSS, which might need work nor did it provide cost analysis of the options provided in the report.

		Following Dr.Seversky's overview of the study, there was discussion amongst the Board regarding how the data was compiled and its findings regarding enrollment projections and questioned how the unanticipated increase enrollment for Kindergarten this past year may have impacted the findings that were made regarding the projected enrollment.

		Mrs. Kerner ntoed that a suggested timeframe for moving forward with the discussion was provided to the Board which provides for community engagement.
