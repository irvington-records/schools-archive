		Administrators learned last night of rumors that the Latin program might be cut or that there are difficulties in students enrolling for the class in 9th grade.  There is a misunderstanding as the District values and fully supports the Latin program. Dr. Kor and Mr. Cohen will provide clarify to students and community.

-

-
		Reminder to vote on May 19th from 7:00 am – 9:00 pm in the MSS.

	Dr. Kor addressed the rumors and misunderstanding among students that the Administration and Board of Education is not in support of the Latin program which surprised her as there has always been support of the entire program. The perception is that because of the freshman writing and the new recommendation that health be taken in 9th grade, students will not be able fit Latin into their schedule. She stressed that this in no way precludes students from taking Latin, but that it is accurate to say that there are limitations to the number of courses that students can take in a day but administration continues to support and encourage those students who would want a four year sequences if that is a student’s goal. Mr. Cohen provided the rationale for requiring health be taken in freshman year and also expressed his surprise at the misunderstanding. Over the course of conversations about the desire to expand course offerings to students,challenges were expected when the freshman writing course was introduced last year and in anticipation of those challenges, Latin 1 was offered as a 10th period class.  Additionally, the enrollment for Latin 1 is consistent with previous years and in response to Mr. Montgomery requesting the data on enrollment, Dr. Kor related that for the 2014-15 school year that  50 students were enrolled in Latin 1, 64 in 09-10, 68 in 10-11 and 50 in 11-12 students.Administration also believes that taking health in freshman year fits nicely with the writing course as both are .5 courses and can be taken on A/B days and would then make available the opportunity for other rich electives that are offered in the upper classes to be taken without needing to fit in health which is mandated to graduate.

	Mrs. Kerner asked about the approval process when changing requirements for graduation and noted a similar situation arose earlier in the year when it was discussed that a public speaking course was being considered as a requirement to graduate. Dr. Harrison noted that the situations differ as health is a State requirement and the administration was attempting to accommodate opportunities for other courses to be taken as upper classman as well as to prevent students coming up on graduation and not being able to take the necessary course which has sometimes been the case. Mr. Montgomery stated that the Board needs to be kept more in the loop and Mrs. Kerner also believes that students must be part of these types of conversations. She noted that communication has been less than perfect on matters. Dr. Harrison replied that he prefers not to have dialogue that is evaluative in nature in a public discussion.

	Before opening up the meeting for public comment, Mr. Whitney noted that from his perspective, the challenge is “was there proper due diligence done on the impact/effect  of making health a requirement in 9th grade and is there limited flexibility for students to take courses they want to take?

	6. Comments From Members of the Community

	There were students and parents in attendance to address the rumors that the Latin program might be cut or to address the difficulties in 9th graders scheduling this class due to the requirement to take Health in 9th grade as well as the new freshman writing class.

	Latin teacher, Michele Cella, apologized to the Board and Dr. Harrison and Dr. Kor for the situation at hand and that when the art teacher expressed her concerns to her that they would be “fighting” over the same students next year, she had a conversation with Mr. Cohen and everyone is committed to the Latin program. She fully understands that everyone is committed to making the Latin program the best it can be and when she expressed her concerns to her higher level students about the enrollment decline she did not expect students to post chats on Facebook that there was an expression that 1st year Latin was going to be dropped but had hoped that several would join her to express their passion for the program. She noted that the students’ passion is reaffirming, even if misguided in their argument and knows that the program has the support of the administration. Mr. Whitney reiterated that the Board is committed to the Latin program.

	Following the comments from students and parents, members of the Board had the opportunity to ask questions and respond:

	Mrs. Kerner commended the students for coming and making their points known but had been told that there has been an overt effort to stop students coming tonight and that communication is everything and wants to encourage participation, especially students, and is very concerned about the situation and will be further discussed in another setting. Mr. Montgomery concurred with Mrs. Kerner. Mr. Montgomery believes that the decision to require health in 9th grade should be overturned unless there is a good reason to do so and in his view, this matter should have come to the Board  and he wants direct administration to change that. Also, would like to have documentation that the 9th grade writing program be evaluated and if the trade-offs are worth it.

	Mrs. Kerner believes that it is the Board's decision as to what type of district we want to be and needs to be more process in place to ensure that communications take place and that parents/students are informed of the day to day. She also believes that the Board did request a report on how the writing program is going and if it is something we want to continue. Mr. Montgomery would also like a mechanism to hear from students on their experiences. Mrs. Kerner would also like to discuss having a student liaison on the Board to get their point of view.

	Dr. Harrison noted that the emotion brought to the table by all is appreciated but would caution not to respond in emotion but to take the opportunity to reflect and process what has been said. He also supports students having vehicle to voice themselves. He also rejects the notion that there have been communication problems and that with one situation he acknowledged that the administration took ownership and feels that there has been broader communication throughout the district and would like to have specifics from the Board in which he can respond to. Rash decisions might have greater impact and must be mindful of that.

	7. Presentations/Discussions

	Discussion, Presentation: 7.1 Irvington Middle School Presentation (Presentation)

	Mr. Sottile and Ms. Daley presented enrollment and staffing at the Middle School year, noting that there are currently 129 students in 6th grade, 142 in 7th and 145 in 9th grade.. He highlighted that there are 50 teachers (13 of whom are shared) and the challenges of scheduling. In reviewing the Theories of Action, he acknowledged that no Theory of Action exists in isolation and reviewed how Theory 2, professional development, is realized at the middle school. Ms. Daley provided examples of the types of the building-based structures are in place at IMS that foster staff development such as common planning blocks, grade level learning sessions and faculty meetings. Mr. Sottile and Ms. Daley provided an overview of the academic programs at IMS and the tools that students are learning to deepen their learning and understanding on subject matters. An overview of the integration of technology at the middle school and the tools used to enhance the students' learning experiences, such as using Google Apps for Education, iDO, bring your own device as well as STEM education which includes coding, engineering/design process and robotics education were provided.

	Ms. Daley highlighted the character building programs such as the character building facilitators (CBF) which are high school students and middle school students looking at specific challenges and problems and looking for ways to handle situations. She also thanked the IEF/PTSA for supporting the CBF. She referred to the extra-curricular activities offered at the IMS and noted that they are based on student interest in academics, athletics, as well as social interests. Mr., Sottile also referred to the opportunities that are provided through the IEF and the PTSA that would not be possible built provide rich, authentic experiences linked to what they are learning and expressed his appreciation to both organizations.

	Mr. Sottile provided examples of Theory 3 - Leadership, Reflection and Feedback -  in action at IMS, such as the multiple layers of classroom observation with informal, instructional rounds and building walkthroughs as well as the evaluation process which focuses on growth through feedback and refection.

	The Board had follow up questions which included what is meant by rigorous, authentic learning experience. Mr. Sottile believes that it is more student-focused as they are engaged in the learning and are being asked to apply their knowledge and give back more than they are given. Mr. Montgomery asked if thought is given to the possible distraction when students are using digital technology and how is it determined if it is net positive. Mr. Sottile believes it is analyzed by teachers through the lens of what the students are creating/learning.

	Mrs. Kerner asked if and when the curriculum for some of the unified arts classes would be reviewed. Mr. Sottile believes that review is ongoing to look at what students should walk away with or what is the big idea of a particular class. It was noted that the current coding and programming will be deepened next year with the introduction of Project Lead The Way. With respect to clubs, Mrs. Kerner asked if the funding is at the desirable level. Mr. Sottile noted that the budget had been cut but that the additional funding has helped to offer clubs that students are interested in.

	Mr. Whitney asked how a 7th graders experience will differ in 8th grade and how the Common Core impacts their learning experiences. Ms. Daley noted that students will be faced with deeper level of critical analysis as well as volume of writing assignments.

	Information: 7.2 Field Testing

	Dr. Harrison noted that since the last public discussion, a memo from Steve Katz from SED, reiterates the state’s position that schools have a responsibility to administer field tests but also mistakenly states that the District had decided not to administer the earth science exams at the high school. It also identifies areas of the law which was reviewed by the District's legal counsel, and it could be construed as a directive from the Commissioner of Education and not conducting the field testing could carry consequences on the District. With this information, he believes that the District's position should be examined. He also noted that other districts have not received such a letter, including those that have decided not to administer field tests.

	The Board discussed that, as a Board they would not look to opt out of the field testing, especially now in light of the memo, but Mr. Whitney noted that each family has the option to opt out their children out, not encouraging or discouraging everyone to make their own decisions.
