	10. Presentations/Discussions

	Presentation: 10.1 Superintendent's Recommended 2014-15 Budget (Presentation)

	Dr. Harrison thanked all the administrators for their contributions in creating the proposed budget, noting that the budget aligns with the expectations for district as well as for the students. Dr. Harrison outlined how the District Goals informed the proposed budget, both in instruction and curriculum and maintenance of the District's facilities. The proposal also acknowledges the need to balance educational opportunities with the challenges of required costs for pension and health insurance benefits, special education services but also looks to contain tax increases.

	Dr. Harrison further explained that the proposed budget expands academic programs, such as increased math ASI in the elementary school and English at the secondary level, introduces a writing program in 9th grade a well as technology courses. The proposed budget also includes an increase of 0.4 FTE in music and reinstates two clubs at the Middle School as well as funding of the MSS play. In the area of athletics, it is recommended to increase the athletic trainer services, reinstate a varsity winter coach and restores intramurals at the Middle School that had been cut from the budget several years ago. Support services are also recommended with the addition of two monitors for recess at DL, supplemental training to the monitors at DL and MSS, creation of an Asst. Director position at PPS and additional leadership in technology by separating the duties of the technology coordinator from the CIO duties which will help to rollout future technology plans.

	The recommended budget also includes a proposal to enhance communications within the community, a review and update the Board of Education policy manual, replacement of classroom and cafeteria furniture which hasn't been replaced in 10 years, and to perform necessary maintenance on school facilities. Dr. Harrison noted that some items were removed from the proposed budget in order to maintain the balance between opportunities and tax increases. Items that were removed from the proposed budget that had been part of the individual budget component preliminary budgets include, but are not limited to: reductions in salaries due to clerical retirements, reduction in arts and education, and the elimination of bus monitors except for special education students, a .5 FTE increase in the community aide as well as the elimination of the proposed crises TA.

	The proposed budget totals $56,445,400, which represents a 4.39% budget to budget increase and a tax rate increase of 3.92%, and $1,218,603 under the tax cap. Dr. Harrison reviewed the projected revenues which is 4.42% over this year's expected revenues. Dr. Harrison noted that salary and benefits account for 78% of the proposed budget. Dr. Harrison reviewed the calculation formula to arrive at the tax cap rate and noted that Irvington has seen an increase in adjusted growth of 1.0488%. The proposed budget represents a tax increase of $632 for a home with an averaged assessed value. It was also noted, that over the past six years, the average percentage tax increase is 2.84% and 1.88% budget to budget increase.

	Dr. Harrison believes that the budget is both academically and fiscally responsible. The next step is for the Board to communicate their priorities for the proposed budget and to hear comments and questions from the community prior to the adoption of the budget on April 24th.

	Mrs. Kerner wanted to highlight that the tax cap discussion is no longer relevant for Irvington but will be more typical of the type of budget conversations in the past as to what is the appropriate tax rate that is cost efficient to the community but will also maintain the districts programs.

	Following comments and questions from the community, the Board discussed the proposed budget in order to give the administration further direction.

	Maria Kashkin stated that she finds the proposed budget to be an enriching but  need some choices will need to be made and she’d rather see the academic enrichments rather than the other enrichments kept in the budget, such as maintaining the special education crises TA position. Robyn Kerner and Phil Whitney also agreed that it is a necessary position and in the long run might be a better use of funds that could ultimately impact the entire class.  Mrs. Kerner would also like to look to keeping academic enrichment as opposed to increasing the athletic trainer. Always important to look at the number of students who would be impacted by decisions.

	Phil Whitney stated that he feels that a 3.94% increase is in the general area of where the proposed budget should be and that there are many costs that the Board and administration has no control over. He did express concern about adding employees as it is increases the cost base and asked if the headcount is where we were prior to the budget reductions that began in 2008. He also feels that there will be further conversations regarding the elimination of bus monitors.

	For John Montgomery, he stated that when looking at a spending item the question to ask if it leads to a substantial improvement in whatever area it is concerned with and if there are areas where we could cut and not have much impact on children's learning. Additionally, he asked that because The district is benefiting from an increase in the tax cap rate this year, must ask what is going to happen next year and could face a tight cap and should be cautious in what is added this year.  Also asked if we could consider maintenance projects paid through the budget and not funded through a referendum. He would also like to be more transparent as to how we pay for tax certs. He expressed concerns about the concept of the 9th grade writing program and would like to take a broader view of performing arts, including the drama department and be sure we are allocating monies appropriately. With regard to the math AIS, need to look also at enrichment and if money is allocated correctly.

	Robyn Kerner ask if there was a way to touch base next year on how the new hires are doing in order to evaluate the additions and have a more formalized process to report on new initiatives---student success as a result of initiative. She noted that there are three more meetings prior to the adoption of the proposed budget and the Board wants to hear from the community on any thoughts and/or comments on the budget and get involved. She added that t is difficult to hear from  the community on the night that the budget must be adopted and it is then too late to consider

	Dr. Harrison asked again what tax levy target might be acceptable for the proposed budget or  if 3.94% is the range the Board is comfortable with. Phil Whitney feels that under 4% is desirable but he would want to subtract something if something is added. John Montgomery feels that the case should be strong for adding items since over the past few years the budget has increased and important to justify the increases and show that money is being spent wisely,  but for him, he isn't there yet.
