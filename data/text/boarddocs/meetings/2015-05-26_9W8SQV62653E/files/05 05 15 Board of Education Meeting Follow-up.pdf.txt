Why have students take health in 9th grade?

As students transition to High School they are faced with new adolescent experiences, peer pressures, freedoms and
decisions. Developmentally, a 9th grade Health course supports these transitions and provides early
interventions. Recent discussions with the Community Advisory Board (CAB) & iASK CAB leadership have identified the
need to provide more comprehensive, targeted programming to our students in the dangers of drug and alcohol use,
mental health needs, and other risk factors impacting our teenagers. With the support of iASK CAB, we will be able to
deliver instruction that can be supplemented with expert resources/speakers to address these needs in a
developmentally appropriate way while having an impact early in the high school experience.

Additionally, by taking Health in 9th grade, we optimize the 1/2 credit courses and provide a meaningful experience for
free periods. In 9th grade, Health offsets Physical Education, Science Lab or Writing to create 2 full periods. This
minimizes "holes" in a schedule and therefore opens opportunities for other elective courses in 9th grade and more
specifically, in future years when students have far more elective options and choices.

Is taking health in 9th a requirement?

It is strongly recommended, however we reserve the ability to be responsive to specific student needs. Transition
meetings are scheduled with current 8th graders and their families. In these meetings the High School Guidance
Counselors will discuss course selections and any questions/desires around electives. Effort will be made to
accommodate requests to maximize opportunities within the student's’ academic programs.

What is the relationship between health and other electives?

Given that the 1/2 credit course pairings in Physical Education, Science Lab, Writing and Health combine to create two
full periods, 9th graders have room for at least one elective during the standard period 1-9 schedule. They can also add
Music during period 10. In anticipation of 9th graders looking to enroll in both Latin 1 and Art, we had added a 10th
period Latin 1 class for the 2014-15 school year. This scheduling decision provided the flexibility to successfully
maximize 9th grade enrollment in Latin 1 and Art. For 9th graders looking to enroll in Latin 1, Art and Music, there is the
option for a student to drop their lunch period.

Note: If a student received 8th grade credit for Studio Art, they can progress to a subsequent art course in 9th grade.

Assessing the 9th grade writing course.

It is challenging to evaluate the effectiveness of any program in its early stages. Although we can identify indicators of
success, these can be subjective in the area of writing. Valid and reliable data come from multiple years of
implementation allowing for course refinement and cohort variation.

As discussed in prior Board of Education meetings, as the 2014-15 school year comes to an end, short term evidence will
include feedback from our students who are currently enrolled in the course as well as our content area teachers,
especially Social Studies knowing the English and SS teachers have been working together to examine student writing.
Additionally, we employ a portfolio approach as one means to assess process writing. This includes our AWE Folders,
the feedback loop from this year's course, and student self-assessments. Student performance on ELA Regents Exam
will provide another lens by which to review student progress. However, with the rollout of the new Regents exam, we
note that the exam only targets limited forms of writing; thus, it may not be a strong benchmark.

Over the course of the year informal feedback from students has been used to inform teacher practice and
understanding of student perspective. Here are some examples collected during the year:

    ●   “Even though I don’t like writing, this class gives me the chance to improve without the pressure of my work
        being graded.”
    ●   “The best thing about lab was learning the steps of the research process for a topic I chose myself.” (2 more
        responses like this)
    ●   “It helps me think about and improve my writing, even though I would rather have a free period.”
    ●   “I would like the writing to be more connected to what we do in English. (2 more responses like this) But I like
        that the class size is so small.”
    ●   “Helps with learning new techniques in our writing. At times, it’s hard to manage writing for both classes.”
    ●   “I think we get a lot of individual time with the teacher, but I think we should spend more time just writing
        without all of the research.”
    ●   “The small size of the class helps (5 more responses like this), but it’s still a lot of writing.”
    ●   “I think it’s a great educational tool.”
    ●   “It allows the teacher to give me more attention.” (3 more responses like this)
    ●   “I would rather the class be connected to English—I don’t know why they have to be separate classes.”
