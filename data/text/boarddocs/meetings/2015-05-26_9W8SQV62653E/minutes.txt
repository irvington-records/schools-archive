	4. Presentations/Discussions

	Discussion 4.1 – Follow up on May 5th Board of Ed Meeting’s Discussion regarding Latin and Health

	Dr. Harrison began by noting that students taking health in 9th grade is not a new requirement but looked at as a partner with the 9th grade writing course.  Health is a required course and administration saw the mating both the health and writing course taken in 9th grade might be more age appropriate and relevant. Enrollment trends in Latin were reviewed and it was noted that 76 students have requested the course for next year, and recommending health in 9th grade does not appear to impact students' ability to take Latin as well. It was again reiterated that taking health in 9th grade is only being strongly recommended but is not required. Scheduling is underway and efforts will be made to accommodate every student's requests.

	Mrs. Kerner noted that the required writing class was also discussed with respect to evaluating the course and that perhaps look at making it optional. Dr. Kor noted that the course hasn't run a year and time is needed to effectively evaluate the progress of the program and there should be follow through with the commitment made to the curse. Dave Cohen concurred and added that they are open to assessing its efficacy but it would be premature to change course.  Mr. Whitney noted that this discussion wasn't part of the agenda and asked Board Members if there were other comments about the health issue.  Mr. Montgomery asked when the issue of the writing course would be discussed. With regards to taking Health in 9th grade, he asked under what specific instances would a student not be required to take it. Mr. Cohen replied that it would be looked at if the issue was brought to administration, whatever the reason for not taking it, such as sequencing, other desired courses, such as art, etc. The goal is to provide students with their desired opportunities.

	In preparing the agenda, Mr. Whitney noted that he was attempting to separate the health conversation with the evaluation of the new writing course. Mr. Grados asked what exactly the issue is if health is only strongly suggested and that the writing course is a separate conversation. Mrs. Kerner asked if there were systems in place in Guidance to flag students if they have required subjects outstanding in order to graduate. Mr. Cohen noted that there are processes to ensure that all required courses are met.  Mrs. Kashkin noted that there are not many electives offered to 9th graders and

	Mr. Montgomery stated that he believes that the issue is broader and noted that he had been opposed to putting the writing program in 9th grade schedule and having conversations with some parents that feedback from parents and students is necessary and should be done in a systematic way as his concerns have been confirmed in his conversations.  He would like to see that, as a Board, they should receive information from students who have gone through the program.  When asked for how he envisions the model for obtaining student feedback, he believes that it should be confidential, from students, and to keep in mind people do have axes to grind but that it couldn't hurt to solicit information.  He also believes another aspect of this, which is how the existence of writing course taking up .5 9th grade credit and its effect on college transcripts, is it a good trade off to having a credit in another subject area.  Mr. Whitney's point of view is how to get useful information and that it is up to the Board to direct the administration as to what to provide, i.e. raw data, quantitative data. Mrs. Kerner would like more flexible scheduling rather than restrictive for students as there are many options for students and that with the writing and health is forcing students into a mold and that the totality of both is limiting students. Dr. Kor also agreed that there should be an evaluation but evaluating a writing course is more complex.

	Mrs. Kashkin raised the question if consideration could be made that if student takes Latin in freshman year that they could opt out of the writing class as writing is a component of Latin. Mr. Cohen doesn't see that they target the same goal. Mrs. Kashkin asked if the curriculum could be provided to help give the Board a better picture of the course. There was discussion as to what the performance metric might look like for the course. One measure would be content teachers seeing a deeper level of writing of their students in that particular area and would take several years to see if students feel that their writing is better as they move along their high school years and become a better writer.

	Mrs. Kerner concluded after all the community comments believes that extending Latin to the lower grades should play a role in any discussion about bringing language down to lower grades.

	7. Business of the Board

	Action (Consent): 7.1 2015 APPR Plan Resolution

	Resolution: Whereas the Board of Education of the Irvington School District has long supported high standards and accountability for our students and staff; Whereas our district benefits from local control, opposes legislative remedies that assume that one size fits all, and has serious concerns about the new APPR plan; Whereas the newly enacted State budget requires the Commissioner of Education to adopt regulations by June 30th, 2015, thereby creating a truncated timeline, and assuring ongoing, piecemeal changes to APPR without adequate public consideration; Whereas the State budget legislation threatens that state aid will be withheld unless local contract negotiations conclude by September 1 of this and each subsequent year – another virtually impossible deadline given the obligations of collective bargaining; Whereas the parent opt-out movement has demonstrated that parents do not favor the proposed nexus of high stakes assessment and teacher evaluation and the labeling of teachers or children based on these assessments, and has further exposed issues related to the reliability, transparency and length of the tests; Whereas it is in the best interest of the students, staff and public education in the state and in this school district that we develop and implement an appropriate APPR evaluation system, rather than the recently enacted system which relies on an untested, opaque value-added model (VAM), focuses on only three snapshots in time (assessment and 2 evaluations) out of an entire school year, uses a basic scoring grid rather than a matrix based on multiple measures, and relies on a SLO model for the majority of teachers; and Whereas the current efforts in Congress to reauthorize the Elementary and Secondary Education Act (ESEA) recognize the error of mandating that test scores be linked to teacher and administrator evaluations; now, therefore, be it Resolved, that the Board of Education of the Irvington School District: 1. requests that the Board of Regents convene a task force of qualified practitioners and independent experts to review the reliability, transparency, developmental appropriateness, and length of the state tests and to assure the validity of linking the tests to the proposed evaluation system, 2. requests that the Board of Regents, Commissioner of Education and State legislators allow that draft regulations be filed by the Commissioner on June 30, 2015, followed by a 90 day comment period and that the deadline for school district submission of modified APPR plans be moved to September 1, 2016, and 3. requests that the Board of Regents, Commissioner of Education and State legislators perform a detailed review of the evaluation proposal, gather input from qualified practitioners and independent experts, and reject the elements of the Cuomo Education Reform Agenda which place undue reliance on state tests and constitute inappropriate reforms to APPR. Our communities, teachers and students deserve nothing less.
