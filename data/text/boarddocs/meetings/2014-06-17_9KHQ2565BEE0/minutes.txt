	3. Follow-up to June 3rd Technology Presentation

	The Board had requested that they be given an opportunity to have a follow-up conversation to the June 3rd Technology Presentation by Raina Kor and Jesse Lubinsky in order to address any questions or comments they might have.

	The Board was interested in further understanding how the District assesses the success of its technology instruction, students’ abilities, what is working or not working well,  the flipped classroom model, etc. Mr. Montgomery asked how the goals of the district are being met by the current technology program. Dr. Kor responded that the work of this year will continue and that due to the absence of standards there is a gap but that that will be undertaken in the next school year with a full time Director of Technology, Mr. Lubinsky as well as the creation of a technology committee.

	With regards to the technology course offerings at the high school, Dr. Kor noted that the programs offered are somewhat driven by student interest and trends, along with ensuring  that the needs of students are being met, i.e. sequences, especially at the higher level technology courses.

	Surveys are being used to collect data from teachers and students with regards to technology, getting baselines in order to continue to assess the programs, both use of hardware and programs. Mrs. Kerner also asked about keeping parents engaged and if the Board will be provided with the results of assessment and decisions based on the information that is gathered.

	Mr. Montgomery asked what the plan will be going forward with regards to the flipped learning. Dr. Kor said that it is a learning strategy, and to assess the success, it will be based on student's achievement, and student feedback. It will be measured by measuring the success of the students.

	With regard to implement “bring your own device” (BYOD) in the fall, it is the intent to saturate the campus as Wi-Fi accessibility and a policy will provide the foundation to allow students to bring in their own devices, with and a next step would be determine how instruction will be delivered via technology.

	The enrollment in the high school technology classes was reviewed and it appears that, across the board, there has been an increase in interest, including digital photography. Dr. Kor pointed out that there is not sufficient staff to provide instruction and that it is something that must be kept in mind when planning technology classes, to be sure that sequences are also made accessible to students, especially at the highs school level.

	Mr. Whitney asked about the sequencing at the middle school level with regards to technology instruction and asked if the budget for next year will satisfy the needs of the district to expand Wi-Fi at the campus as well as supplying the needed devices. Mr. Lubinsky said that further assessments must be completed before making any long-term decisions as to what the needs and costs will be to satisfying the instructional programs.

	Mrs. Kerner expressed concerns with the utilization of Edline by teachers which she feels is problematic. Dr. Harrison reiterated that the standards will set the expectations of both the teachers and students and will provide the structure for all classes and will then enable assessment and oversight.

	Mr. Whitney noted that it would be important to develop the technology plan, including the needs of the District prior to the budget season, in order to be able to have discussions of possible costs that would need to be included in the 2015-16 budget. Dr. Harrison reported that the Board will be given the framework of a technology plan.

	11. Presentations/Discussions

	Discussion: 11.1 2014-15 District Goals – Building Ladders to Success

	Prior to a review of the work done in the District to fulfill the 2013-14 District Goals, Dr. Harrison reviewed the District’s mission statement and how goals are used to achieve the mission. He reviewed the Theory of Action (ToA) statements that were used as the framework in the development of the goals, with a focus on curriculum, instruction, college preparedness and technology. The goals have moved the District but will continue to be an on-going process. Of note, has been the creation of consistent curriculum and instruction and alignment of curriculum with the CCLS, the benefits of curriculum mapping and Instructional Design (UBD) and the integration of technology in the delivery of instruction.

	Along with the curriculum, instruction and technology goals were goals concerning the District's facilities and the development of a long-range plan for the District. Mrs. Kerner and Mr. Whitney were in favor of holding additional meetings in order to discuss long-range planning in order to be sure that these objectives are met during the year and time must be made to address these needs

	The questions before the Board were: 1) Did the 2013-14 goals meet the District’s objectives? 2) Can certain goals be carried forward, but with modifications?

	Mr. Whitney put forth removing the facilities goal for next year as a lot of time will be spent on carrying out the items if the bond is successful and is comfortable to focus efforts on curriculum and instruction and simply and streamline the goals that focus on what is desired to be accomplish. He also suggested crafting a goal that would define what the higher standards specifically are. Mrs. Kerner stated that she would like to better prioritize instructional goals in order to help focus budget discussions.

	Mr. Montgomery again asked how the outcomes should be evaluated in order to be sure that what is being done is correct and to determine if the processes are working, although there might not be quantitative data, but that it is important to have the information, noting that test scores and APPR is one way of assessing, as is college acceptances can benchmarks to evaluate and determine success of outcomes which would then indicate that the process is working or not working.

	Both Mr. Whitney and Mr. Montgomery thought that a conversation in September might be worthwhile to define what success looks like and then work backwards to identify what it looks like, etc. Mrs. Kerner suggested that in order to accomplish this, it might be important to begin the conversation during the summer. She is concerned that calendars move quickly and that it is important to get the work done.

	The consensus of the Board was that goals should be higher level and that a goal such as facilities does not need to be continued as it becomes day to day operations.
