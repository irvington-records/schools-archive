	6. Business of the Board

		Action (Consent):  6.1 Approval of Revised 2012-13 School Calendar (Attachment)

		Dr. Harrison provided overview of the impact on the District's calendar due to the five-day closing during Hurricane Sandy and also noted that Main Street School was closed an additional day due to a water pipe break.

		To achieve the 180-day mandate, the District will apply the four Superintendent Conference Days, leaving two additional days to be recouped. Administrators looked at various methodologies as to how district might recoup these two days and three for Main Street School. By returning April 1 to a student day, and having June 21st as a day for Main Street School only, there remains two days to be made up. The rationale for opening February 20, 21, and 22nd, is to provide continuity to the instructional program and, in addition, there needs to be an opportunity to recoup any additional days should further closings be necessary, which would be during spring recess. Dr Harrison noted that State Aid would be lost if the District does not reach 180 days. He spoke about the complexities of closing for mid-winter, spring, and Martin Luther King Day.

		There was conversation at the November 20th meeting and Dr. Harrison recommended utilizing April 1 and the three proposed days in February. MSS would have June 21st as their make up day and Jne 20th would be the last day for the other schools.

		Mr. Grados thanked everyone for providing input and that, for the most part, comments were intelligent and constructive and helped the Board understand the hardships involved with modifying the calendar.

		Resolution: Resolved, that the Board of Education adopt the revised 2012-13 school calendar to include February 20, 21, 22 and April 1st as student days for Dows Lane, Main Street School, Irvington Middle and High Schools in order to ensure that the District comply with the 180 school-day mandate as schools were closed for five days (October 29, 30, 31, November 1 and 2) due to Hurricane Sandy and to accommodate two additional school closures due to weather or other emergencies. Be It Further Resolved, should more than two school closures occur, modifications will be made to the spring 2013 recess. Be It Further Resolved, that June 21st will be an early release day student day for Main Street School to recoup the November 16th closure of building due to broken water pipe.

	10. Presentations/Discussions

		Discussion, Presentation:  10.1 LAN Associates Presentation on Meszaros Field and Traffic Calming Study (Attachment)

		Dr. Harrison reiterated that the Board's priority at this time is to remediate East Field once all testing and direction from DEC is received, however the Board also wishes to continue discussions as to possible options available to renovate Meszaros Field and noted that there is no date set at this time for a referendum for voter approval.

		Richard Westbrock of LAN Associates, reviewed previous information provided regarding the conditions and options at Meszaros and ways to rectify the poor soil conditions and drainage issues, i.e. installation of underdrains and installation of an irrigation system would be necessary, utilizing a sand slit drainage system along with artificial turf. Mr. Westbrock provided the Board with options, including costs of : field turf , astro turf, both both organic and synthetic, as well as costs associated with natural turf as well as play times for each of the possible turfs as well as comparison data for installation time and maintenance costs for each of the five options. He noted that field preparation is at least half of the total project costs in addition to the installation of the rug.

		Mr. McCann noted that natural turf is half the cost of artificial fields and could provide two natural turf fields for the same cost and stated that the community will want more information to compare artificial and natural fields.

		Dr. Harrison noted that he did reach out to Chip Osborne, a natural turf expert, and was told that he was nterested in contributing his time only if District was considering grass and not  artificial turf. With regard to organic in-fill which Mr. Westbrock presented as an option to a turf field, Dr. Harrison asked if coconut fill presents any issues for children with allergies and further information on that topic would be necessary.

		Mr. Whitney said serious obligation to community to look at organic solutions and need to better understand pros and cons of organic field turf. Can we get a list of area school districts and their products that they use in order to have conversations regarding their fields. Mr. Westbrock noted that there are fewer organic fields.

		To alleviate drainage issues onto Riverview Road, Mr. Westbrock recommends taking depth to 18 inches and provided comparison data of the impact of the different depths on peak runoffs. 6" of additional stone base is $100,000 and 12" would be $200,000.

		Mr. Westbrock also reviewed the various track surfaces available. Total estimated cost for construction would be approximately $290,000 with soft costs totaling $290,000 for the 300 level. LAN's recommendation is to go with artificial turf, allowing for multi-sport, multi-season use.
