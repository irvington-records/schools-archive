   a. Activity. Identify and describe the activity, its proposed implementation plan, and

Expanding Advanced Opportunities: RECOVS MHELP and MTSSi Data Lab Regional
workgroup will meet to discuss and collaborate around SEL, Mental Health, and
attendance initiatives through the lens of MTSS.

Guided by the NYSED MTSS-I Pilot, District Teams will investigate and design systems and
structures. Insufficient Mental Health Capacity: Directly linked to engagement and
attendance is student well-being. The regionalization data explicitly identifies that the
demand for mental health and social support often outweighs the district's capacity to
provide it. This gap leaves many students without the necessary support to overcome
barriers to learning.

The primary anticipated outcome of this workgroup is the creation of a unified, data-
driven regional framework to address the critical shortage of student mental health
services. By collaborating through the lens of a Multi-Tiered System of Supports (MTSS),
districts will design a more efficient, proactive, and equitable system to support student
well-being, engagement, and attendance.

A Coordinated Regional Framework for Student Support: This initiative will move districts
from isolated, reactive responses to a coordinated, proactive system for managing SEL,
mental health, and attendance.
     Shared Systems and Structures: The workgroup will develop a common language
        and consistent protocols for identifying and supporting students. This includes
        designing a clear continuum of supports, from universal (Tier 1) prevention for all
        students to targeted (Tier 2) and intensive (Tier 3) interventions for those with
        greater needs.
     Data-Driven Decision Making: A key outcome will be the development of systems to
        use data (attendance, behavior, academic, and well-being screeners) to identify
        students needing support before they reach a crisis point. This shifts the model from
        reactive to preventative.
     Clearer Roles and Processes: The designed framework will clarify roles for teachers,
        counselors, social workers, and administrators within the MTSS structure, ensuring a
        more coordinated and less fragmented approach to student support.
More Efficient and Effective Use of Resources: By working together, districts can overcome
the challenge of insufficient mental health capacity that no single district can solve alone.
     Pooling Regional Expertise: Collaboration allows districts to share specialized
        personnel, resources, and best practices. This regional approach helps maximize the
        impact of limited resources and provides access to expertise that might otherwise
        be unavailable.
     Reducing Redundancy: Instead of each district investing time and money to solve
        the same problems, this workgroup will develop shared solutions, models, and
        professional development resources, creating significant economies of scale.
     Targeted Interventions: The MTSS framework ensures that resources are used
        efficiently. It helps schools provide the right level of support to the right students at
        the right time, preventing the misallocation of intensive resources to students who
        may only need lighter-touch interventions.

Improved Outcomes for Students: The ultimate goal of designing a better system is to
achieve better results for students.
     Increased Access to Support: A more efficient and clearly defined system will
         provide more students with timely access to the mental health and social-emotional
         support they need to overcome barriers to learning.
     Improved Attendance and Engagement: By proactively addressing the root causes
         of chronic absenteeism and disengagement - often linked to mental health and well-
         being - the initiative is expected to lead to improved attendance and greater student
         participation in school.
     Enhanced Well-Being: Providing a comprehensive support system focused on SEL
         and mental health will contribute to a healthier school climate and improve the
         overall well-being of all students.
This initiative will help improve chronic absenteeism by fundamentally shifting the approach
from reacting to absences to proactively addressing one of their primary root causes: unmet
student mental health needs. Instead of just tracking empty seats, this plan builds a system
to support the students who would otherwise occupy them.
Creating a Proactive Support System: The Multi-Tiered System of Supports (MTSS)
framework is key. It's a proactive, tiered approach to ensure every student gets the right
level of support.
     Tier 1 (Universal Support for All Students): The workgroup will help districts
         implement school-wide Social-Emotional Learning (SEL) and mental health
         initiatives. This creates a positive and supportive school climate for everyone, which
         builds a strong foundation of well-being and prevents many attendance issues from
         starting.
     Tier 2 (Targeted Support for At-Risk Students): Using data, the teams can identify
         students who show early warning signs of chronic absenteeism (e.g., missing a few
         days each month, changes in behavior). These students can then receive targeted
         interventions, like small-group counseling or check-ins, to get them back on track
         before the problem becomes severe.
     Tier 3 (Intensive Support for Individual Students): For students already struggling
         with chronic absenteeism due to significant mental health barriers, this plan
         provides a important lifeline. These are often the students’ districts lack the
         resources to fully support on their own.
Solving the Resource Shortage Through Collaboration: A regional workgroup directly
attacks this problem.
     Building a Unified Framework: This collaboration ensures that best practices and
         data-driven strategies are used across the region. It creates a more efficient and
         effective system than each district trying to solve the same complex problem in
         isolation.
         In essence, this plan improves chronic absenteeism by building a robust regional
         safety net. It helps create an environment where students feel supported and
         understood, removing the mental health barriers that often keep them from coming
         to school.

   e. Benefits. Describe how the local school districts believe this innovation would benefit
      students in your region as well as anticipated outcomes. Explain what regional needs the
      local school districts believe it would address.

       This regional workgroup is seen as an innovation that would provide immediate, concrete
       benefits to students while also safeguarding their educational future.
       How This Benefits Students
       While the workgroup focuses on logistics and finances, the districts believe the direct
       positive impacts on students would be significant:

      More Time for Learning, Activities, and Family: The primary student benefit of
       creating efficient bus routes is shorter ride times. For students on the longest
       routes, this could mean gaining 30-60 minutes per day. That extra time can be used
       for sleep, homework, participating in extracurricular activities, or simply being with
       family, all of which are critical for a student's well-being and academic success.
    Improved Health and a Calmer Ride: The transition to electric buses offers direct
       health benefits. Students will experience:
           o Cleaner Air: Zero tailpipe emissions mean students are no longer exposed to
                harmful diesel fumes while waiting for or riding the bus, which is especially
                important for children with asthma or other respiratory conditions.
           o A Quieter Environment: Electric buses are nearly silent. This reduces noise
                pollution and creates a calmer, less stressful ride for all students, particularly
                those with auditory or sensory sensitivities.
    Greater Reliability and Safety: By finding efficiencies, districts can better manage
       the chronic bus driver shortage. For students, this means more reliable and
       consistent transportation. It reduces the likelihood of delayed or canceled routes,
       minimizes overcrowding, and ensures students have a predictable, safe journey to
       and from school.
    Protecting Classroom and Program Funding: The electric bus mandate is
       enormously expensive. By collaborating to find cost savings and secure grants,
       districts can avoid diverting funds from their operating budgets. This directly
       benefits students by protecting funding for teachers, programs like art and music,
       technology, and smaller class sizes that might otherwise be cut to pay for buses and
       chargers.
Anticipated Outcomes: The anticipated outcomes are the tangible results of the
workgroup's efforts:
   1. A Redesigned Regional Transportation Map that identifies shared routes and
       optimizes travel times for students attending both in-district and out-of-district
       programs.
   2. Significant, Quantifiable Cost Savings for participating districts through reduced fuel
       consumption, shared services, and optimized staffing.
   3. A Collaborative, Phased-In Roadmap for Electrification that outlines shared
       infrastructure plans, joint purchasing strategies, and a timeline for the entire
       region's transition.
   4. Increased Success in Securing Grant Funding by pooling resources to submit
       stronger, multi-district applications to state and federal agencies.
   5. A Measurable Reduction in the Region's Carbon Footprint, contributing to a
       healthier environment for the entire community.

This regional workgroup would address two critical needs that are too large and complex for
any single school district to solve alone: immediate operational and financial pressures and
the long-term strategic challenge of transitioning to an all-electric bus fleet.
1. Finding Efficiencies in Current Busing Routes By working together, districts can solve
persistent logistical and financial problems that their individual boundaries create. The
primary needs of these addresses are:

           Severe Financial Strain: Transportation is one of the largest costs for a school
            district after salaries. By collaborating, districts can identify overlapping routes,
            especially for students attending out-of-district placements (like BOCES, private, or
            special education programs). This creates opportunities for shared busing, leading to
            significant savings on fuel, maintenance, and driver hours.
        Chronic Bus Driver Shortage: There is a nationwide shortage of qualified bus drivers.
            Finding efficiencies means districts can better utilize their existing drivers. A shared
            route might allow one driver to do the work that previously required two, easing the
            daily struggle to cover all routes.
        Logistical Inefficiencies: It's common for a bus from one district to drive empty
            through a neighboring district to pick up its own students. A regional plan could
            allow that bus to pick up students from both districts who are headed to the same
            location, saving time and resources.
   2. Collaboratively Planning for the Electric Bus Transition: New York State has mandated
   that all new school buses purchased be zero-emission by 2027, and all buses in operation
   must be electric by 2035. This is a massive, mandate that creates several needs a regional
   group can address:
        Overwhelming Infrastructure Costs: Transitioning to electric buses requires a
            complete overhaul of infrastructure, including expensive charging stations and
            significant upgrades to electrical grids. By planning regionally, districts can achieve
            economies of scale, jointly purchasing equipment at a lower cost and potentially
            sharing charging depots to reduce duplication and expenses.
        Lack of Technical Expertise: Most districts do not have the in-house expertise to
            manage a fleet electrification project. A workgroup allows them to pool their
            resources to hire consultants, share research on the best technology and vendors,
            and learn from each other's pilot programs, avoiding costly mistakes.
        Navigating Complex Funding: While the mandate is unfunded, there are
            competitive state and federal grants available. A collaborative effort increases the
            region's chances of securing this funding by allowing districts to share grant-writing
            resources and submit more powerful, multi-district applications.
        In short, this workgroup addresses the need for districts to work together to save
            money now while tackling increasing expenses that are simply too big to face alone.

Item 2.5.
   a. Idea. Identify and describe the innovative idea that local school districts considered. Feel

       Developing Staff Pipelines Workgroup: Creating "grow your own" programs in partnership
       with local colleges/universities (like SUNY New Paltz, LIU Hudson, Mercy U., Pace U.,
       Manhattanville C. just name a few) to establish affordable, cohort-based pathways for
       Teacher Aides to Teaching Assistants and Teaching Assistants to become certified
       Teachers.

   b. Benefits. Describe how the local school districts believe this innovation would benefit
      students in your region as well as anticipated outcomes. Explain what regional needs the
      local school districts believe it would address.

       This "grow your own" initiative directly addresses the critical need to build a stable, diverse,
       and highly qualified teaching force from within the local community.
       Regional Needs Addressed: Local school districts believe this workgroup would address
       several urgent regional needs:
            The Chronic Teacher Shortage: Districts are struggling to fill vacancies, especially in
               high-need areas like special education, bilingual education, and STEAM. This
               program creates a new, sustainable pipeline of qualified candidates to fill these
               gaps.
            Lack of Teacher Diversity: The current teaching workforce often does not reflect the
               racial, ethnic, and linguistic diversity of the student population. Teacher Aides and
               Teaching Assistants are frequently community members who are more
               representative of the students they serve. This initiative provides a direct pathway
               for them to become lead teachers.
            High Cost as a Barrier to Entry: The expense of traditional master's degree and
               certification programs is a significant barrier for many talented individuals,
               especially those in lower-paying support staff roles. An "affordable, cohort-based"
               model makes a teaching career accessible.
            Staff Retention: Providing a clear career ladder and investing in the professional
               growth of support staff gives them a powerful reason to stay with the district,
               reducing turnover at all levels.
       How This Benefits Students: This innovation would benefit students in tangible ways by
       improving the quality and stability of the instruction they receive:
            More Teacher Stability: By creating a steady supply of new teachers, students are
               less likely to experience the disruption of long-term substitutes or a revolving door
               of new staff. This stability allows students to build meaningful, long-term

               relationships with their teachers, which is crucial for learning and a sense of
               belonging.
            More Experienced and Effective Teachers: Unlike new graduates with no classroom
               experience, former TAs and aides enter their first year as a certified teacher with
               years of practical knowledge. They already know the school culture, understand
               classroom management, and have existing relationships with students and families,
               making them more effective from day one.
            Teachers Who Reflect Student Demographics: Students thrive when they see
               themselves reflected in their teachers. Having teachers who share their cultural and
               linguistic backgrounds serves as powerful representation, improves student
               engagement, and strengthens the connection between the school and the
               community.
       Anticipated Outcomes: The primary anticipated outcomes of developing these staff
       pipelines are:
           1. A larger, more diverse, and highly qualified pool of teacher candidates to fill
               district vacancies.
           2. Increased retention rates for both support staff (who now see a path for
               advancement) and teachers (who are rooted in the community).
           3. Stronger, formalized partnerships between local K-12 school districts and higher
               education institutions, creating a permanent solution to staffing challenges.
           4. Improved student outcomes resulting from a more stable, experienced, and
               representative teaching faculty.
