	10. Presentations/Discussions

		Presentation:  10.1 Initial 2013-14 Budget Discussion (Presentation)

		Dr. Harrison remarked that there are always challenges and difficult decisions to be made during budget season. The goals of budgeting include providing necessary programs and resources as well as expanding learning experiences while maintaining the community's investment and being mindful of property taxes. Dr. Harrison noted that administrators must continually look at programs, staff development, technology, maintenance of district facilities while finding ways to contain costs through identifying efficiencies as well as areas for sharing of services.

		Dr. Harrison and Ms. Miller, Assistant Superintendent for Business, provided information regarding anticipated revenues and expenses for the 2013-14 school year, noting that to maintain the services currently in place in 2013-14 would require a budget of $52,897,198, an increase of $1,741,198. Costs that are beyond the District's control were reviewed, i.e., pension contributions, health insurance, tax certioraries, transportation of special education and privately placed students and special education services. The District will employ a zero-based budgeting methodology which requires prompt, well informed decisions through the accounting for every penny. In 2013-14, debt service will show a decrease as there is a final payment of $550,000. Most other expenses are estimated as final figures have not yet been provided. If the Board should decide to present a budget that is within the tax cap, approximately $1,679,570 would be needed to be cut from the budget. It was noted that the tax cap does give provide for some allowances, such as the additional pension costs, but tax certioraries are within the tax cap.

		Dr. Harrison noted that there will be painful conversations over upcoming Board meetings and since many support programs and transportation are mandated expenses, there will be conversation points regarding athletics, music, art, clubs, intra-murals, professional development, guidance services, facilities maintenance, world languages, transportation policies, and how the kindergarten program is delivered. The next step is to begin conversations regarding what the constraints will be. Individual components of the budget will be presented, and public engagement is essential as everyone is motivated to do right by the children and by all tax payers, and to ensure that everyone is getting their moneys worth in the schools.

		Board members were in agreement that it is essential that the Board reach out to the community and invite them to help in the budget process as difficult decisions will need to be made in light of the constraints of the tax cap and that it is the community that must decide if the proposed budget stays under or goes over the tax cap. It was reiterated that 60% majority must approve a budget that is over the tax cap. Mr. Whitney stated that he sees this a crossroads budget and that the Board must give the administration direction immediately but doesn't see how the District could cut $1.7 million to stay under the tax cap. Mrs. Kerner stated that the Board will be seeking venues to enhance the budget process by offering opportunities for community engagement.

		Bob Grados stated that Irvington has had the lowest budget increases over the years and that the District is now dealing with some of the repercussions of those cuts, with the District now at a critical juncture. He also commented that previous Board's tried to solve budget issues by getting to a specific number but did not look at what the vision for the Irvington schools would look like in the next ten years, adding that many of the options that could be considered to be reduced or cut is what makes Irvington schools the reason people are moving here. Mr. Grados suggested that there is the need again this year to go over the budget line by line to see where the money is spent, as was done last year which helped the community better understand how the money is spent and then translated into a successful budget vote. Mr. McCann asked that the District's  financial advisor do an analysis of the debt service and possibly refinance to take advantage of lower costs of money and save money.

		The calendar for future budget discussions was presented, with the next meeting on January 8, 2013 addressing business office, debt service, insurance, athletics, buildings and grounds, and transportation budgets.

		Presentation:  10.2 2011-12 Student Performance Review (Presentation)

		Dr. Kor presented the data from the 2011-12 student assessments, including comparison data from 12 area districts. The purpose of a performance review is to answer the question "How are our schools doing?" and "How can student performance be improved"? However, she cautioned that when looking at data, it is not easy to determine what students' need to learn without looking at the actual test questions and that the State no longer provides individual student test data, i.e. cannot determine what questions students answered incorrectly. Dr. Kor reviewed Grades 3 - 8 NY State assessment data for Math and ELA, providing a cohort example of current 9th graders, from Grade 3 - 8th which shows overall consistency in their test scores. Dr. Kor reviewed test results for all Regent's exams, SAT scores, noting that Irvington students obtain scores that are over the national average. In 2011-12, 22 out of 27 varsity teams were recognized as NYS Scholar Athlete teams. Last year, the enrollment at the high school was 608 and Dr. Kor noted that 555 students participated in the 19 AP courses offered, with 381 passed the exams.

		Dr. Kor concluded that it is important to remember that the assessment data is used along with formative and summative data from the classrooms to inform thinking and planning for improved instruction, as well as looking at data over time to ensure that the needs of the students are being met. Dr. Harrison commended the tremendous work being done within the district through staff development and that the principals are aligning curriculum, analyzing student work, etc. as everyone's goal is always to do better. The Board asked that administration continue to seek ways to increase student achievement.
