                           Irvington Union Free School District

                          Proposed Superintendent’s 2012-13 Budget
                                     February 27, 2012

                                            “Q & A”
At the February 7, 2012 Board of Education meeting, and since, a number of questions have
surfaced in regard to the Proposed Superintendent’s 2012-13 Budget. This document attempts
to address those questions.

Q1.    The TAX CAP: The proposed budget shows a projected 2.15% tax levy increase.
       Doesn’t the TAX CAP legislation limit a tax levy to a maximum of 2%?

A1.    The TAX CAP should not be thought of as a 2% TAX CAP. The TAX CAP legislation
       provides a formula which enables school districts to claim certain exemptions when
       calculating the TAX CAP. For this reason, it is likely that the TAX CAP will be different in
       each New York State school district --- some higher than 2% and others lower than 2%.
       For Irvington, the TAX CAP translates to a 2.15% tax levy increase.

Q2.    Exceed the TAX CAP: What consideration was given to exceed the TAX CAP?

A2.    The TAX CAP legislation does provide Boards of Education the opportunity to present
       budgets that exceed the TAX CAP to the community to vote upon. Such “over the cap”
       budget must be approved by the community with a “super majority” vote of at least
       60% of the voters.

       While the Board of Education has the final authority to determine if it wishes to exceed
       the TAX CAP, the administration felt that it was its responsibility to present a budget
       which was below the CAP. The administration was guided by the popularity of support
       across the State for TAX CAP legislation and by conversations between and among
       school administrators in Westchester County. The following also guided the
       administration:

              The administration’s concern for all District taxpayers, both those with children
              in the schools and those without children in the schools.
              Further, the administration is concerned that, if a proposed budget that
              exceeded the tax cap was defeated, voters may defeat a second attempt to pass
              a budget. That would leave the school district in the position to be forced to
              adopt a contingency budget with a 0% increase over the 2011-12 tax levy---
              which would require over $800,000 in additional cuts over the Superintendent’s
              proposed budget.
              The Board of Education’s goal which is: To address the New York State Tax Cap
              Legislation in the Development of the 2012-13 school year budget.

Q3.      TAX CAP in Other Districts: Are other districts presenting budgets that are under the
         TAX CAP?

A3.      It is early in the budget process, and any answer to the above question may change. The
         Putnam/Westchester region has approximately 50 school districts. In two recent
         meetings of Superintendents, the 17 in attendance at one meeting all reported that
         they would be recommending budgets under the CAP. In the second (different region),
         16 superintendents reported that they would be recommending budgets which are
         under the CAP. We have heard that one district is expected to present an over the CAP
         budget, but this has not been confirmed.

Q4.      Close Main Street School: Have you looked into closing Main Street School as a cost
         savings measure?

A4.      No. The administration believes that closing Main Street School may be a viable option,
         but not for the next school year. In the future when BOCES classroom obligations at the
         high school/middle school are removed and projected declining enrollments are
         substantiated, closing Main Street School may be possible. The administration
         recommends that a feasibility study to close Main Street be done either next year or the
         following.

Q5.      Athletic Transportation: How much of the transportation budget is spent on athletic
         transportation?

A5.      Proposed transportation budget = $2,308,782
         Proposed athletic transportation = $141,000 = 6.1% of total transportation budget
         (.27% total budget)

Q6.      Reducing Staff: When staff are reduced because of budget cuts, are they reduced on
         the basis of “last one in is the first one out”?

A6.      Not necessarily. Instruction staffing reductions are complex and take into account many
         factors such as seniority, placement and certified tenure area. The state has very strict
         criteria for reductions.

Q8.      District Music Programs: How will the reduction in music staffing impact
         opportunities for students next year?

A8.      Mostly, the music programs currently available for students this year, i.e. orchestra,
         band, chorale, general music, lessons, etc. will be available to students next year. There
         may be slight increases in lessons’ class size. The only reduction in music offerings will
         occur at the Middle School with the reduction of the .2 FTE exploratory music section.

Q9.      Undesignated Fund Balance: How much money is in the fund balance? Why not use
         these funds to offset the costs?

A9.      Legally, the District can maintain a maximum of 4% of the budget in the fund balance.
         For Irvington this amounts to $2,012,996. The proposed budget includes the use of
         $400,000 from these funds to lighten the tax payers’ burden and help the district to
         have a budget that is under the TAX CAP. The administration does not recommend any
         further use of these funds because (1) future budgets would have to compensate for the
         amount in the current budget (basically, covering a shortfall in available funds); (2) fund
         balance is used to financially support the school district during the summer (expenses
         are incurred during the summer but the district does not begin to receive tax payments
         until approx. October 1st.); and (3) fund balance can be used to cover unexpected,
         unbudgeted expenses that must be paid (i.e. a new high-cost special education student
         who moves into the school district, or emergency building repairs).

Q10.     Study Hall in the Middle School: With the proposed reduction of the exploratory
         program and World Language program at the Middle School, where will students be
         assigned during periods previously used for these subjects? Will students have more
         than one study hall in a day?

A10.     The elimination of the 6th grade language program will result in one open period every
         other day for 6th grade students. While most students will be placed into a study hall
         during this time, this opening will allow some students to receive additional support (as
         needed) or to enroll in an additional performing music group.

         The exploratory program is an 8th grade program that allows those students (with room
         in their schedules) to take an additional class in music, art or technology every other
         day. With the elimination of the exploratory program, students would be scheduled in a
         study hall every other day.

Q11.     High School Guidance: If a 1.0 FTE guidance counselor is reduced from the budget,
         how will this impact the guidance services at the high school?

A11.     The administration has revised its recommendation regarding High School guidance.
         Instead of a reduction of 1.0 counselors, the administration now recommends a
         reduction of .5 FTE. The reduction of .5 FTE counselor would result in a total of 3.5
         counselors instead of four counselors. The administration would maintain .5 counselor
         to serve as Guidance Chairperson. The Guidance Chair would be responsible for dealing
         with any number of department-related tasks from handling special education testing
         accommodation requests and arranging for evening programs.

         This part-time counselor would not have a case load; the 600 high school students
         would be divided among the three counselors, resulting in case loads which are similar
         to caseloads in other Westchester high schools of approximately 200 students per
         counselor. However, the chairperson would help with any number of counseling
         situations which might arise, including those which involve multiple students, as well as
         those which call for a different counselor because one is absent. In addition, the part-
         time chairperson would help with transitioning certain special education students into
         the work force, a function that is required by state and federal law.

         It is important to note that the above recommendation regarding guidance will require a
         further reduction of other expenses from the proposed budget, if the budget is to be
         maintained below the TAX CAP limitation. It is projected that approximately $ 58,800
         will need to be added to the budget to maintain a below TAX CAP budget.

Q12.     Grade 6 World Language Reduction: What is the effect of cutting World Language in
         Grade 6 on the expected student proficiency in Grade 8?

A12.     If the grade 6 World Language program is eliminated, the District would continue to
         have the grades 7 and 8 World Language program, which is common in many area
         middle schools. The 7 and 8 program provides two years of instruction for the purpose
         of completing the first credit of World Language and is consistent with the model that
         was in place in Irvington prior to the creation of the grade 6 program.

         Student achievement had been strong when the previous grades 7 and 8 program was
         in place. We do not expect any loss in student proficiency as a result of this change.

Q13.     Recommended Assistant Superintendent Position: The Budget proposal calls for
         adding the position of Assistant Superintendent for Instruction and Personnel. Could
         the title Assistant Superintendent be changed to a director level and thus save
         money?

A13.     The Assistant Superintendent position recommended is intended to be titled Assistant
         Superintendent for Curriculum and Personnel. The District does not have an
         administrator in charge of the District’s human resources functions. The District has
         completed a human resources office audit by a consultant. The consultant has
         recommended strong administrator oversight of the human resources office.

         The administration believes that the combined oversight functions in curriculum and
         instruction along with human resources warrants the Assistant Superintendent title.

Q14.     School Psychologists: Would it be possible to reduce the number of school
         psychologists by 1.0 FTE position?

A14.     Currently, the District employs 5.0 psychologists. Having reviewed the opportunity to
         reduce the number of psychologists by one position, the administration recommended
         against such a reduction. The administration believes that such reduction could place
         the District at risk in maintaining compliance with State and Federal special education
         regulations. School psychologists, in addition to principals, are essentially the only
         classroom support available; the District does not engage the services of school social
         workers nor does the District have guidance counselors in the elementary schools. In
         addition to such support, the following areas dominate the workload of psychologists:

                Student evaluations for Committee on Special Education (CSE)
                Member of CSE
                Chairperson of CSE Subcommittee
                200 Annual Student Reviews
                Support for Response To Intervention (RTI) requirement
                Counseling required as an IEP service
                Building Level (Non-IEP) Counseling
                Social Skills Groups
                Support to Parents, Teachers, and Administrators
                IEP monitoring

Q15.     High School Classes: How will staffing reductions impact the high school course
         offerings?

A15.     The administration believes that program offerings at the High School next year will be
         very similar to the offerings this year. With few exceptions, students will likely not
         experience any loss of course selection opportunities. The administration believes that
         the following impact may result from staffing reductions.

                On average, class sizes will be slightly larger next year than this year, and on the
                average we can expect favorable class size. There will likely be, as there are
                every year, class size disparities large and small caused by scheduling anomalies.
