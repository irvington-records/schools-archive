5. Presentations/Discussions

Information, Presentation: 5.1 2017-18 Budget: Curriculum, Technology, Athletics, PPS, Special Education and Employee Benefits (Presentation)

Dr. Harrison reported that tonight's presentation will cover considerations in the areas of curriculum and instruction and employee benefits, including instruction, occ ed, guidance, special education, PPS, athletics, co-curricular and technology. He also noted that the tax cap does stifle growing the budget so that the Board will need to make decisions once the entire budget has been presented. Dr. Kor noted that the curriculum/instruction encompasses many components, such as library/media, instructional technology, curriculum development, and even if they look separate entities, everything under curriculum is linked with the goal to provide enhanced student experiences. Dr. Kor highlighted the considerations contained in the proposed budget and include, but not limited to:

- Consideration 1 - +1.0FTE HS Social Studies Teacher to reduce class sizes in Global 1, American Studies, AP US History, Economics, AP Macro and Psychology. (Mr. Cohen noted that an ideal class size would be 20-21. Additional staff would also provide scheduling flexibility.

- Consideration 2 - +.6 FTE HS Math Teacher to reduce class sizes.

- Consideration 3 - +.6FTE Music Teacher to increase ability to expand music program as well as reduce class size. This would restore a previous cut to staffing in the department. The Music Department is a source of pride but the number of students are very high. Mrs. Palmieri noted that she is in support of this increase.

- Consideration 4 - +1.0FTE Instructional Coach, Literacy - K-5 to increase consistency across all classrooms, provide in-house professional development, increase use of data to inform instruction, etc. This position will also provide teachers the ability to dig deeper into the curriculum and will help new teachers to unpack modules to ensure that they are teaching the students what they are expected to learn in a particular grade. Mr. Graeber supports this position.

- As an aside, Mr. Whitney asked how best to measure what the benefit is of all the considerations in order to make the necessary budgetary decisions.

- Consideration 5 - Purchase of additional Chromebook tubs for all buildings. Each tub (5 Chromebooks) is $1,650 and consideration totals $169,950. The addition of Chromebooks would support district's commitment to using G Suite for Education tools, support flexible learning environments and support continued implementation of district's technology plan. (Dr. Harrison addressed the funds that the District was to receive from the Smart School Bond that will be applied to technology initiatives and a % of these funds could be used towards this purchase.) Mr. Lubinsky, Technology Dir. addressed questions regarding the use of Chromebooks in classrooms, how they are currently utilized and that additional ones would provide for more flexibility. (Mr. Whitney would like to see several years sequence in the purchase/replacement cycle of Chromebooks. The life span of Chromebooks is 3-4 years.)

- Consideration 6 - Purchase of 32 laptops ($50,400) for teachers at the secondary level to provide flexibility and accessibility at all times, anywhere. This is a pilot program.

In summation, the new considerations total $450,350 and push ahead expenses totaled $298,041.

The proposed instruction budget represents a 4.3% increase and includes salaries, equipment, supplies, textbooks, BOCES and BOCES Occ Ed. In the area of Media/Guidance,

a consideration is to add a 1.0FTE MS Library Media Specialist position to increase support in instruction, enhance MS study skills and increase technology offerings at the MS. The current Library Media Specialist serves all HS/MS students. In the proposed instructional technology budget, new considerations include:

Consideration 1 - 1.0 FTE Technology Integration Teacher for K-5 which would enable to expand the PLTW programs at DL and MSS and if the district is committed to increasing opportunities for students in the area of STEM, is to have a dedicated teacher at each school.

Consideration 2 -1.0FTE Data Manager/Analysis Position that would provide a higher level of analysis as well as address the NYS reporting requirements. The current support person who handles the reporting to the State (and supports the student data management system) is retiring and the recommendation addresses the needs that are now present. (The Board asked for further information regarding the position which will be addressed.)

Consideration 3- increase Edutek support by .5FTE to support the increase number of devices to effectively maintain our infrastructure.

The new considerations amount to $182,000 with a reduction in push ahead variance of $7,613.00

The proposed co-curricular/athletic Budget includes:

Consideration 1 - additional clubs at Dows Lane, Main Street, Middle School to enhance students' experiences. The new considerations total is $13,000. (Dr. Kor noted that the PTSA has been an incredible partner to the District in providing funding for many clubs). Improved Instructional Improvement/Administration budget supports professional development, supervision, etc. and there are no new considerations.

The Special Education /PPS budget includes:

- Consideration 1 - .2FTE Speech Teacher to provide related services required as the current 5th grade self-contained program to the MS and requires this increase.

- Consideration 2- 1.0FTE Education Evaluator to address the increased number of initial educational evaluations as well as reevaluations as well as classroom observations that are now required.

In the area of PPS, which includes nurses, psychologists and social workers, one consideration is to add a .5 psychologist to support the move of the 5th Grade special class to MS as well as increase services in all schools through reallocation of services across the services. The new considerations in the area of special ed total $92,000 and $40,000 in PPS.

Carol Stein reported on the proposed Employee Benefits Budget include retirements, social security taxes, health insurance (for retirees as well), life, disability, worker's comp and unemployment insurance and Union Welfare Funds for vision and dental The push ahead shows TRS and ERS rates are down from previous years, health insurance rates are up 4.25% and 1.74% for retiree benefits. For the first time, waiver expenses are now being coded to Employee Benefits. The budget category does show a negative of $350,689 due to the reduction in retirement rates which have been high in past years.

Total Budget Summary thus far is:

A total proposed budget of $59,656,098, revenue of $59,000,000 creating a variance of $656,098 over the tax cap. Ms. Stein noted that state aid amounts are still not final. Dr. Harrison noted that the administration will look for further efficiencies, etc. to provide a final proposed budget under the tax cap.

The Board provided further comments including Mr. Graeber noting that the proposals show that the feedback over the course of the year from the Board was taken into consideration in the proposed budget. Deb Hargraves will continue to absorb the information presented and has not yet formulated opinions on any particular. Mr. Whitney also believes that all the considerations are worthy and asks about hiring additional staff who will then become permanent vs. one-time expenses, such as building and grounds considerations, purchase of Chromebooks. Mr. Hanna agrees with the comments made thus far and that instruction would benefit from all the proposed positions, but there is a limit as to what can be done especially with permanent vs one-time expenses that would benefit from the reduction in ERS/TRS this year. Need to have clarity on what items are non-negotiables and unless revenue numbers increase, difficult choices will need to be made. Mrs. Palmieri says the proposed budget reflects 'every student, every day' --- developing interests and agrees that it restores budget cuts made in the past. She also believes the proposed budget is a good reflection of the Strategic Plan and developing students into global citizens. Would like to find ways to support a broader group of students.

Dr Harrison will present the proposed budget on March 21st which will leave only two meetings to fine tune the proposed budget prior to the Board's adoption on April 18th.

Comments from the community include:

- Is the computer replacement cycle is still in existence and actual amounts spent would be helpful. Can BoardDocs be better utilized to communicate more information, such as surveys, backup information to presentations. Other suggestions were made with respect to the data analysis position, enhanced security in our buildings.

- Supportive and addressed the items that offer her children at the elementary level such as Literacy Coach which would touch an incredible amount of students deeming it a decent value; the positive value of the Library Media Specialist at the MS to help students learn how to use research materials; and there appears to be an opportunity to update and support technology at the MS.

- Thanked everyone for the constructive questions but highlights the perception from the audience of what the process is to make decisions on individual considerations and it would be helpful if the Board refrains from stating what they could support as well as what could not be supported that is not spoken.

- Many smart asks and will be difficult to make decisions. Asked for further clarification of the impacts on technology, etc. that the new media specialist would bring to the MS.

- Concerns expressed with regard to communications to parents and important to gain support of the vision as well provide greater detail of budget information.
