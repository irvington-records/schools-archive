			Mr. Grados read the following statement on behalf of the Board concerning an incident during Spirit Week at the High School which involved some members of the football team.

			The Irvington School District explicitly opposes bullying in any form. Whenever presented with any incident of bullying or insensitive behavior that may rise to the level of bullying, each employee of this District must understand that it is their responsibility to report any instance of bullying to which they become aware.  We encourage our students to report instances of bullying or insensitive behavior as well.  As a school community, we must all be agents for positive behavior.

			The Administration has enforced and will enforce the terms of our anti-bullying policy, in each event, in accordance with the terms of our policy and the law.

			When bullying occurs, we all must act with vigilance, but we also must constructively deal with instances of bullying, teaching our children – as well as reminding ourselves – of correct behaviors, acceptance of others, and respect for differences.

			While the School District plays its part, we must all acknowledge that our children first and always learn the proper way to act at home. The Board urges parents to continue to engage your children in meaningful conversation regarding the topic of bullying, along with reinforcing personal values.

			To help your family, please consider revisiting the District’s Dignity Act policy and the Code of Conduct as it pertains to this topic. Together, we will continue to foster the positive social and emotional growth of our students, and, with the help of our professional educators, make our schools a great place to grow and learn. In turn, we as a school district, and as a community, will help to ensure that our schools are safe and supportive for every student.

	4. Presentations/Discussions

		Presentation: 4.1 Implementation of the  New APPR (PRESENTATION)

		Dr. Raina Kor, Asst. Superintendent for Instruction/Human Resources shared the District's  roll out of the new APPR mandate that is now being implementing in all districts in NYS.  Dr. Kor reviewed the scoring components of the teachers' evaluations: 60%  is derived from teacher evaluations of:classroom observations, portfolios, evidence binders and goal setting; 20% is derived from State Assessment Data or district developed student learning objectives (SLO); and 20% is derived from locally selected measures of achievement using standardized data such final exams and other assessments used at the elementary level. New York State hopes that the system will provide each student with "world class educators and world class principals". Dr. Kor provided an overview of the measures that have been put in place by Irvington to achieve this goal, including professional development, reflective practice through goal setting as well as multiple observations and evaluations of teachers twice a year and, at a minimum,  2-15 minute walk-throughs. Administrators are now being trained to conduct observations so that there is a consistent approach to the observations and evaluations. Dr. Kor noted that Irvington is using a program called "Framework for Teaching" and shared a video featuring Charlotte Danielson who developed the program. Dr. Kor also noted that the District must ensure that teachers are trained in the new Common Core Standards as well as the new Science Standards that are going to be in place shortly.

		Dr. Kor reviewed the goal setting process that utilizes four domains (or areas) that teachers will be evaluated on as well as the  SMART (specific, measurable, attainable, results-oriented, time-bound) Goal Model being used for explaining a teacher's specific goal. Dr. Kor also stressed that APPR is a never ending cycle and the district must be sure to provide support and resources for teachers to move to the goal of being highly effective.

		Board members asked about the role that Department Chairs play in this new process and what, if any, impact there would be on teaching time in the classroom, either due to professional development or students taking assessments. Dr. Kor noted that professional development takes place in classrooms with consultants, faculty meeting time, free time as well as out of the classroom.

		Discussion: 4.2 Principal Searches

		Dr. Harrison stated that the District needs to find a successor for Dr. Mosenthal and also Main Street School, however, we are awaiting  the findings from space utilization study currently being conducted and he noted It will be a challenge to find a HS principal as there are many more aspects to a high school principal than to other level principals as well as a smaller candidates' pool than at the elementary school. Dr. Harrison outlined a possible process for recruiting Dr. Mosenthal's successor, including his recommendation to advertise in The New York Times and other national publications, such as Education Week as well as OLAS. Informal dialogues will be planned through district focus groups; a profile will be developed to be used when screening potential candidates; and  interview committees will be established with various community constituents as well as Board representation. Recruitment will begin after the holidays. At the conclusion of the committee interviews, three or four finalists will be interviewed by Dr. Harrison and Dr. Kor, with Dr. Harrison making a recommendation to the Board of one, or possible more if there are equally desirable candidates, to interview with the Board of Education. Dr. Harrison noted that process would be similar to the process used by Hazard, Young and Attea, for the Superintendent search.

		In response to questions regarding the process and protocols for such a search, and in particular, what the Board's role in the interview process will be, it was noted that this discussion was only a preliminary discussion with the Board and that further discussions will be necessary to establish protocols, i.e. the number of many finalists would be interviewed by the Board, how committee members will be selected, etc.
