Disclaimer:

The following was prepared by staff to assist property owners, developers or consultants in submitting
information to support a request for a determination by the Department regarding eligibility for exemption from
Part 360 regulations for projects which involve disturbance of old inactive landfills. This is not official
Department guidance and has no bearing on how the regulations will be interpreted in a particular case, nor
does it preclude the Department from requesting additional information.

Applicability and Background Information:

The following is a generalized summary of provisions from New York State’s (6 NYCRR Part 360) Solid
Waste Management Facilities Regulations which apply to activities involving disturbance of old inactive solid
waste landfills including, but not limited to, the excavation and relocation of solid waste as part of a
construction project. The term “old landfill” and the applicability of this guidance is limited to landfills which
ceased operation prior to December 31, 1988, are not subject to a permit or Order on Consent and do not have a
Department-approved landfill closure plan. Disturbances or construction activities involving landfills which
were closed in accordance with a Department-approved closure plan require Department approval of a
modification to the closure plan and may require modification of the consent order. This guidance does not
apply to landfills which are listed on the New York State Registry of Inactive Hazardous Waste Disposal Sites.

360-1.5(a) requires that solid waste be disposed of only at a facility authorized by a Department-issued permit
or registration, a facility authorized by a Department or Court-issued Order, or at a facility exempt from the
regulations.

360-1.2(a) defines the terms “solid waste” and “disposal” broadly enough to include the relocation or
replacement of solid waste which has been excavated from an inactive landfill and is subsequently replaced or
re-deposited either within or outside of the original landfill footprint.

360-1.7(b)(9) exempts disposal of solid waste from a non-hazardous inactive landfill which has been excavated
as part of a construction project and is being returned to the same excavation or other excavations containing
similar solid waste or otherwise relocated within the landfill's existing footprint, provided the handling,
relocation and disposal practices are deemed acceptable to the department in writing in advance.

The Department’s “Transition Rules”, which are prescribed at 360-1.7(a)(3), generally require that inactive
landfills comply with the closure requirements which were in effect at the time that the landfill ceased
operation. Starting with the initial promulgation of State-wide solid waste regulations on January 1, 1963,
landfill closure requirements have evolved and become more stringent over time. Application of the transition
rules to a specific landfill is a case-by case determination which requires specific knowledge of the landfill’s
history, design and construction. In general, inactive landfills are subject to Department regulation in cases
where the landfill is disturbed or in cases where inspection reveals exposed solid waste, leachate discharges,
impacts to water quality or other impacts to public health or environmental quality.

Construction projects or post-closure uses which involve residences or other enclosed structures on or near a
landfill, water supply wells in the vicinity of a landfill, utility or infrastructure encroaching on the landfill
footprint, or public access to a landfill site raise special concerns with respect to environmental quality, public

Part B – Outline of Requirements for the Landfill Disturbance/Waste Handling, Relocation & Disposal Plan

1. Describe the nature of the project including anticipated use of the site after the project is completed.

2. If solid waste will be disposed of off-site, identify the disposal facility(s).

3. If any of the solid waste will be left on site after completion of the project, describe the type and thickness
of cover material and/or other measures that will be taken to prevent human or environmental exposure to
contamination associated with the solid waste as well as leachate, dust and landfill gases. Describe what
measures will be taken to address potential risks for health and safety both during site development and as a
result of site use after the project is completed.

4. Attach a site plan showing the original waste limits and the final limits of waste after project completion in
relation to property boundaries, buildings, roads, water bodies, water supply wells, onsite wastewater disposal
systems, sewers or other utilities and any other features which may impact or be impacted by the landfill.

5. Attach a copy of the notice which will be affixed to the property deed to ensure that all future owners of the
property are aware of the presence of the landfill and any landfill-related requirements or restrictions which
apply to use of the property. Deed notices must make clear and unambiguous reference to the presence of solid
waste on the property and should not refer to solid waste using vague or misleading terms such a “soil”,
“material” or “fill material”.

6. For projects involving construction of a residence or other enclosed structure within the boundaries of a
landfill, attach a certification signed and sealed by a professional engineer, licensed in the State of New York
which indicates that the special concerns which arise from construction on a landfill have been evaluated and
addressed in the site development plan.

7. Additional information as requested by the Department on a case-specific basis.
