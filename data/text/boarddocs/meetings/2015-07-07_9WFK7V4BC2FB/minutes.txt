	Action (Consent): 10.5 Adoption of Supplemental Defense and Indemnification Provisions

	Resolution: WHEREAS, Section 18 of the Public Officers Law of the State of New York authorizes municipal corporations, including boards of education, to adopt supplemental defense and indemnification provisions to those contained in the Education Law; and WHEREAS, the statute authorizes boards of education to defend, indemnify and save harmless, members of boards of education and officers, agents and employees of boards of education; authorized parent volunteers; members of school-based planning and shared decision making committees; and members of the district’s audit committee, in connection with any claims, awards or judgments obtained against such board members, officers, agents or employees; authorized parent volunteers; members of school-based planning and shared decision making committees; and members of the district’s audit committee in any State or Federal Court, in the amount of any judgment, award or settlement of any such claim, provide the act or omission from which such judgment, award or claim arose or occurred while the board member, officer, agent or employee or authorized parent volunteer; member of a school-based planning and shared decision making committee; or a member of the district’s audit committee was acting within the scope of his or her office or employment or authorized volunteer duties; BE IT RESOLVED that the Board of Education of the Irvington Union Free School District hereby adopts the supplemental defense and indemnification provisions contained in Section 18 of the Public Officers Law for its Board members, officers, agents and employees; authorized parent volunteers, members of school-based planning and shared decision making committees; and members of the district’s audit committee. 	Action (Consent): 11.2 Support of Old Croton Aqueduct Improvements

	Connie Kehoe, trustee of the Village of Irvington, provided the Board with the history and origin of the integrated street scape plan, a master plan for all of Main Street, and unanimously support by the Village Board. One of key components of the plan is the improvement of the intersection on Main Street and the Croton Aqueduct to address safety concerns. The Village can apply for two funding opportunities to improve that location, however, any improvement of the Croton Aqueduct requires state approval as it is designated a state park. To proceed with the project, the State Parks Dept. would be involved as well as the Village and school district. Ms. Kehoe noted that the edge of the parking lot of Main Street School has a little strip and the desire is to improve that area so that walkers, etc. can easily locate the aqueduct and walk on the strip and not through the parking lot. However, it is not confirmed that the land belongs to the school district but in order to apply for the grant, it must be shown that all possible owners agree to the concept. The Village is paying for the grant writer and will not look to the District to absorb any associated costs. The approximate amount of the grant could be somewhere around $300,000. She also noted that the Village has been committed to this project for several years.

	Resolution: WHEREAS, the Village of Irvington produced a comprehensive Main Street Streetscape Master Plan in September 2014 which includes necessary improvements to the Old Croton Aqueduct Trail. The Plan recommends creating a distinct path for the Trail, separate from the Main Street School parking lot, on the north side of Main Street for pedestrian safety. Creating this separation would also remove hikers and other trail users from the school parking lot; and WHEREAS, the New York State Office of Parks, Recreation and Historic Preservation owns the land upon which the improvements will be made, although it is not clear whether or not a portion of it is included in an easement to the Irvington Union Free School District; and WHEREAS, the Village of Irvington is submitting grant applications to the New York State Office of Parks, Recreation and Historic Preservation for funding for these trail and trailhead improvements; and WHEREAS, the engineering phase of the project, to be undertaken by the Village, would include design of this separate trail-path, which will reduce neither the number nor length of parking spaces currently in existence in the school parking lot; and WHEREAS, the School District would not be responsible for the cost of the improvements. NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED that the Board of Education hereby supports the project and the Village’s applications for grant funding for this project; and BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED that, should the easement between the School District and State Parks include land upon which improvements are anticipated, the School District will enter into a legally binding agreement permitting such improvements to the property.

	Motion by Catherine Palmieri, second by John Montgomery.

	Yes: Phil Whitney, John Montgomery, Michael Hanna, Catherine Palmieri

	16. Discussions

	Discussion: 16.1 District's Participation in the Medicaid Program

	Mr. Palermo, Dir. of  PPS, reported that the District can bill for certain related services provided to 14 special education students who are medicaid eligible and receives approximately $7,100 per year. He reviewed the costs associated with filing the paperwork to claim the $7,100, with direct costs (office staff, the therapists time with completing paperwork required, business office staff's time as well as paying for the firm that the District contracts with to submit the actual billing ($2,500) total approximately $9,075.92, the indirect costs are $9,500, which totals $18,575, therefore, it is costing Irvington $19,470.92. He also noted that annual trainings are mandatory for the Assistant Supt. for Business, Treasurer, PPS Director, eight staff members and clerical staff. He is asking that the Board support the suspension of filing for medicaid reimbursement until such time that it might become advantageous again for the District to again participate in the program.

	The Board was in support of discontinuing participation in the program since they believe that there would be no impact on the students' services. However, there should be a review of eligibility of Irvington students to ensure that it continues to be inadvisable to file for the services.

	Discussion: 16.2 Board Retreat and Long Range Planning

	Dr. Harrison began the discussion by stating that he believes there is great value in having regular board retreats and focusing on the dynamics within the Board and the relationships and conflicts that may occur. He noted that there has been difficulty in arriving at consensus among board members and believes there are ways to be more productive, setting common objectives to make progress in developing a long range plan, all benefits to the construction of a new vision to lead the work of the district. In researching who might be best suited to work with the board to facilitate such a retreat, he is recommending Dr. Giselle Martin-Kniep, an expert in organizational change who he believes would be well suited to work on internal dynamics and to possibly help map out a pathway for creating a strategic planning process.

	Mr. Whitney reported that in the past three years, the Board thought it could create a long range plan, and commended Mr. Montgomery who put in time on the subject and he supports the use of Dr. Giselle Martin-Kniep as facilitator. Mr. Montgomery does support the use of a facilitator for a retreat but doesn't have a firm opinion of this person but thinks that the Board could derive benefit from retreat with just Board members to discuss how to manage the administration, with a second retreat that would include Dr. Harrison might be helpful. He is skeptical that a facilitator would be useful for strategic planning and worries that it is possible to get more into process than substance, however, he is open to considering the concept.

	Mrs. Palmieri does agree with Mr. Montgomery that the Board should meet separately to work out differences then bring in Dr. Harrison. She also believes that developing a strategic plan is time consuming and that a consultant could add value to the process of and ensure that all voices are heard. Mr. Montgomery noted that they had received samples of strategic plans and that there is a diversity of approaches to strategic planning. Mrs. Palmieri would also like to include a survey of the community to ensure that the Board's vision is aligned with the community and a consultant would be helpful in gathering the data necessary. Mr. Hanna is open to retreats but is concerned that it all can be accomplished. He believes that there is real need to have the Board discuss their relationship among themselves but also with the community. Dr. Harrison will reach out to Dr. Giselle Martin-Kniep to secure some possible dates.
