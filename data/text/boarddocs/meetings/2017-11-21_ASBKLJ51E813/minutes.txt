2. Presentation/Discussion

Discussion: 2.1 Workshop on Assessment

Dr. Kor presented what student assessments look like in Irvington schools, noting that assessments are ongoing throughout the year. The focus and goal of the presentation will be on how assessments inform instruction and programs in the district, how data sets are being built and/or developed. The goal that administrators have been working is to develop a balanced assessment system, including formative and summative, authentic/performance based assessments, with the expectation to accurately assess students' knowledge, skills and thinking. There is also a focus and value on having students own their learning, i.e. reflect on their work, self-evaluate and set goals for themselves in order to be involved. Types of assessments include standardized state assessments, teacher developed as well as authentic/performance-based assessments - teacher developed but can include student input.

Dr. Kor shared the three data sets that are used in the district to tell how well students are doing, how effective the programming is and to determine if goals being met vis a vis benchmarking data using Aimsweb to build data sets which then informs learning needs and support services. Benchmarking using Aimsweb not only informs struggling students' support needs, but all students, and at this point, both Dows Lane and Main Street School are piloting at 6th grade for the winter the Middle School.

Deb Mariniello, Principal at Dows Lane and Asst. Principal, Andrea Kantor, shared a snapshot of an early literacy summary from Aimsweb and walked through the data in order for the Board to better understand Aimsweb power and how the data is used to ensure students' needs are being supported, highlight  any gaps, after which, instruction can be targeted to fill any gaps. Joyce Chapnick, Principal of Main Street School, illustrated an individual student's benchmark report how it is used to help the student reach success by implementing supports, but and conversly, illustrates high performing students upward trajectory, with a comparison on national norms. Schools are also usingRtIDirect which is a repository of all the data, including state assessment data, other assessments as well as the interventions that have been provided for a student and is available to all teachers to see what has been provided and what next steps might be to aid the student. Examples were provided as to how the data is used not only for students who need intervention and students who are performing at a high level, but also at the class level to inform design instruction to meet the needs of all students.

Dr. Kor provided an overview of the assessments now being used in the World Language program, noting that the district has shifted the goals of the program to a speaking/listening rather than grammar/vocabulary emphasis, therefore, there is only one year of benchmarking available, so no data was shared at this point. Davd Sottile, Principal of the Middle School, shared what has been observed in the classroom, and noted a culture change is that 85% of the time is spent speaking in the target language. David Cohen, Principal at the High School, shared that teachers are learning being trained to make the assessments and to train their students to do the same. He has also observed the level of risk-taking has increased and is exciting to witness. The department has worked as a whole and has been committed to revising the curriculum, grades 6-12, and to better engage the students using topics that they are interested in, therefore, increasing student interest in the target language. Daily learning targets are also new and provide the ability to cement what students have learned and are ready to move forward in their learning.

To illustrate using classroom data as an assessment tool, an exmaple of using mid-module 2 and end-of-module 2 data would look for how a particular student performed at each and if there was growth in understandng, performance, standards can be looked at using the data and is very useful to the teachers as they discuss the individual/classroom performance.

Dr. Kor shared that much of the good work that is taking place in the math curriculum has been undertaken by the elementary math coach, Christine Rosner, who leads the work and collects the data to be used at math learning sessions, looking at the units' successes as well as gaps. Mr. Graeber was very impressed with the work undertaken in the area of math and suggested that the success be demonstrated to the community. The use of data at the middle/high school level was addressed in response to a question from Maria Kashkin and contrasted assessments and the use of data differs from the elementary level.

To ensure that students are measured in different ways examples of authentic assessments include, but are not limited to:

- observation

- essays

- performance tasks

- portfolios

- journals

Also critical is to ensure that there are more opportunities for students to become assessment-capable learners and to learn to evaluate their own learning so that they can use the assessment to improve their learning and again, take ownership and part of their learning, therefore, curriculum must be designed around this type of learning. Another key is to ensure that students can transfer their knowledge in one area to another and not in isolation.

In addition, there is a move to create a culture of data, and this year, it felt important to build capacity of the administrators and the instructional leaders by developing data projects around a topic of their choice, with key project categories emerging such as in programs, placement, achievement, process/approach and curriculum and instruction, correlations and predictors of success in a particular topic.

Dr. Kor concluded that any change may take at least 7-10 years, and the work will continue in looking at data, assessments, with ongoing dialogue in faculty meetings, grade level meetings, and thanked the administrative team who is so amazing at this time, resulting in the good work taking place in the District. Goals for assessment are for both teachers and students, using instructional design, offering a broad range of opportunities to students and have them own their learning.

Mr. Hanna expressed concerns that some parents have shared with the concept of student self-assessment and would prefer to hear from the teachers as to how their child is doing in a particular area. Dr Kor noted that the idea is to move from a teacher saying what is right or wrong, to determinng if the student believes they have met the goals set out by the teachers and achieved success and are partners with the teachers in measuring their learning.

The Board thanked everyone for the powerful presentation and Dr. Harrison also acknowledged not only the administrators for their work in this area, but also acknowledged Dr. Kor's work in this area.
