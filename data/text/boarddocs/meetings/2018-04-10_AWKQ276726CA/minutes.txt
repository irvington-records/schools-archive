6. Discussions/Presentations

Discussion:  6.1 2018-19 Proposed Budget Discussion

Dr. Harrision noted that this is the night that the Board must provide final direction to administration as to the proposed budget that they will be asked to adopt on April 17th. He reviewed the District's vision, the District's objectives and the budget process thus far. He noted that without any new initiatives, the 2018-19 budget would increase by $1,716,044 which includes salary increases, health insurance, transportation bid for out of district transportation, BOCES increases, pension increases, etc.

The budget is comprised of two components: Business & Operations and Curriculum & Instruction and he outlined the areas that fall under each component. There was some increase in State Aid which is appreciated and thanked those for their advocacy efforts. Fiscal sustainability and efficiencies were at the forefront of the budget process and considerations that were put forth align with the strategic objectives and district's vision. Dr. Harrison reviewed the new considerations which amount to $495,462. Of note, two new considerations that are cost neutral: additional roving guard and a 7/8 special class. Follow up to previous conversation on the late bus survey, 105 responses were received of which 42% answered that the lack of late bus did limit their child's participation in after school activities and 49.5% said their child would make use of a late bus. The proposal has been amended to have it run Monday-Thursday only.

Foundation Aid increased to $63,567 but excess cost aid decreased by $31,272 and instructional materials decreased by $1,205 which totals a net increase of $31,090 in state aid.

Dr Harrison reviewed the state aid that has been lost since 2007-08 and the District has lost $9,114,117, part of which is due to the State not being fully funded by the State and has a negative impact on the District.

In summary, the current proposed expenditures equal $61,312,000 and projected revenue of $61,348,175, creating a difference of $36,175 and is recommended to add that to the either tax certiorari and/or security initiatives. However, Dr. Harrison noted that the budget is very tight and does not allow for any emergency spending, new students with needs, etc.

Expense trends in both dollars and percentages over the past five years were reviewed as well as revenue trends, actual expense trends in general fund expenses by function were reviewed. Dr. Harrison noted that he is proud of the proposed budget as it provides for enhancing experiences for all students, both academic and social/emotional, secruity enhancements, professional development, district facilities, etc. and remains under the tax cap. The budget to budget variance is 3.8% and the allowable tax levy increase is 4.24% and the tax rate per M is estimated at $19.41 with an estimated increase of 1.42%.

The Board then asked any specific questions on the budget, including security initiatives included in the budget, late bus expense, i.e. could the bus be eliminated if it is found that it is not being utilized by the students. Deb Hargraves expressed concern over the number of students vs. the number of staff and what is the tipping point where we say we can no longer add staff to the budget. Dr. Harrison addressed her concerns and highlighted how mandtates, student demographics, etc. could impact hiring in the future but also as a district will want to provide educational opportunities for all students but is committed to continued discussion. Dave Graeber also noted that he thinks about what will benefit the greatest number of students when making decisions, but stands firmly behind the PLTW position which is vital to begin at the elementary level. Brian Friedman agreed with the other comments, but also wanted to address the expenditures for staffing and what it might look like four or five years out, and where money might be best spent. Maura Gedid addressed the safety issues with respect to busing and it is important, but and noted that Irvington doesn't have benefits of scale since district is so small, and asked what the ridership number would be to determine continuation  or elimination of the late bus. With respect with the staffing of PLTW and children's time is limited and how will the benefits/effectiveness of the program to the children be assessed, and hopes there would be flexibility in this hire to teach other subjects or what would they be able to do should the PLTW prove not as positive. Michael Hanna noted that as a Board they must look at the big picture and the expectation is to look at budget in new ways next year with ongoing conversations. One of the main goals as a Board is to be able to assess programs and be able to answer "How are we doing?" He suggested that the Board allow for the opportunity for the late bus but asks administration to assess the ridership and eliminate it low ridership.

Maria Kashkin would agree to pilot the late bus, but also feels that security needs to be considered as well as tax certs. It was agreed to pilot the late bus but  conduct a survey to ensure money is being well-spent. It was again noted that there are other initiatives that might better serve a greater number of students. The Board also agreed that the additional $36,175 be allocated to security initiatives. Dr. Harrison thanked his colleagues and the work that  has been done over the past six years by the administrative team has taken the District to a different point with the conversation moving forward to now address the question, "how are we doing", and "where is the capacity"as the administration is committed to the conversation.

Community Comments on the Budget:

Administration was commended for the budget process and the amount of work that goes into the process, but wonders how many other $20,000 items are in the budget but aggregate to larger amounts that might be looked at as well. She asked about the student enrollment as the number of students per class at the kindergarten level appear low this year. Can we benchmark our spending against other districts to have a sense of perspective? Dr. Harrison noted that the registration of kindergarten is monitored very closely but projection is consistent with where we are this year. (Maria Kashkin noted that historically, it has been a conscience decision to keep the numbers low in kindergarten).

The Board thanked administration for the thoughtful budget and the work that went into its development.
