Option 1. Elimination of Bus Monitors
Currently all eight Irvington buses have monitors on all routes.
By comparison, Hastings and Ardsley do not have monitors; Dobbs Ferry still uses them.

Pro:  Quickly realize significant savings without the need for a referendum.
Con: Less supervision on buses.
Total potential savings = $100,000
Source: Joe Urbanowicz, Director, Facilities & Transportation, Ardsley School District and Director of Quad Village Transportation Consortium.

Option 2. Dows Lane Bus Reduction
Based on actual usage, Dows Lane can immediately bring bus count down from eight to seven.

Pro:  Quickly realize significant savings without the need for a referendum.
Con: Potential for limited increase in pupil commute time, per Joe Urbanowicz, Director of Quad Village Transportation Consortium.

Total potential savings = $80,000
Source of savings figure: Joe Urbanowicz, Director, Facilities & Transportation, Ardsley School District and Director of Quad Village Transportation Consortium.

1. Increase Distance for Ridership Eligibility
Current busing limits include all students who are more than .25 miles from Dows Lane/Main Street or 1.25 miles from the Middle/High School.
By increasing these limits to 1.25 miles for Dows Lane/Main Street and extending busing for Middle/High School to state limits, the district could realize substantial savings.
Dobbs Ferry and Hastings have already stretched busing to state limits for middle/high school students.

Pros: 
Four buses (instead of eight) still transport Dows Lane students – the largest group of bus riders.
For eligible students, no change in time spent on buses – per discussions with Joe Urbanowicz.
Cons:
Potential for increased traffic at Dows Lane due to drop-offs/pick-ups.
Increased inconvenience for affected families.
Potential Ameliorations – costs to be determined
Staggered start times for schools and grades within schools to alleviate traffic.
Increase crossing guards at strategic sections of busy streets.





Total potential savings = $320,000

--- slide 16 ---
2. Maximize Distances for Ridership Eligibility
Current busing limits include all students who are more than .25 miles from Dows Lane/Main Street or 1.25 miles from the Middle/High School.
This option moves the limits to the state maximum:  2 miles for elementary/middle school students and 3 miles for high school students.  
This reflects strategies already in place in Edgemont and other Westchester districts.
Private school students will also be impacted; transportation for these students would be reduced.

Pros: 
Significant savings by eliminating at least five buses and the associated costs.
Cons:
Potential for increased traffic at all schools due to drop-offs/pick-ups.
Increased inconvenience for affected families.
Potential Ameliorations – costs to be determined
Staggered start times for schools and grades within schools to alleviate traffic.
Increase crossing guards at strategic sections of busy streets.



Total potential savings = $400,000
16
--- slide 17 ---
3. Maximize Distances for Ridership Eligibility at Middle School/High School & Stagger Start Times at Dows Lane/MSS 
Current busing limits include all students who are more than .25 miles from Dows Lane/Main Street or 1.25 miles from the Middle/High School.
The district uses eight buses to serve all schools by running these buses consecutively.  Little savings can be realized unless the number of buses in use is reduced.  
This option moves the limits for Middle School/High School students to maximum levels while maintaining current distances for elementary school students.
The current elementary school student load requires eight buses.  This option cuts that number to four buses that run in two consecutive shifts.  
This reflects strategies already in place in Hastings and Dobbs Ferry.
Private school students will also be impacted; transportation for older students would be reduced.
Pros: 
Significant savings by eliminating at least four buses and the associated costs.
Traffic at Dows Lane and Main Street School will be spread across two separate start times.
Cons:
Traffic at Middle and High Schools will increase.
Potential Ameliorations – costs to be determined
Staggered start times Middle and High Schools to alleviate traffic.



Total potential savings = $320,000

Further Consideration – Safe Routes to School
To somewhat offset the proposed cuts to transportation, it is useful to consider that Irvington recently won a $36,250 Safe Routes to School grant that covers the following initiatives:
A coordinator to develop, implement and evaluate walking and biking programs for K-8 students. 
Materials & supplies for coordinator to develop programs and provide incentives, activity accessories (e.g., reflective straps) and materials (e.g., T-shirts) for participation.
Three (3) Walking School Bus adult leaders for Dows Lane (Sept-Nov; April-June)



Source: Bev Miller, Irvington District Assistant Superintendent for Business

Further Consideration –Private School Students
Private School Student Transportation
 
Currently, Irvington provides transportation for 173 students who attend private school. There are several ways to bring down the associated costs:
 
Options that Don’t Require a Referendum:

1.     Continue to pursue partnerships with other districts so students who attend the same private school, but live in nearby districts, share the same bus.  This is already in place, but should be reviewed. 
 
2.     Offer students public transportation at the District’s expense, instead of a school bus.  This would allow older students the flexibility to travel on their own schedule.  For example, Joe Urbanowicz has said that some high school students have chosen this option (where offered) to facilitate travel back and forth to jobs and/or internships in New York City.  
 
Option that Requires a Referendum:
 
1.  Require the use of public transportation for private school students who meet specific requirements based on distance from their schools and public transportation.   For example, if the District stretches distances for public school transportation eligibility, the distances would equally increase for private school students getting themselves to public transportation.




Source: Joe Urbanowicz, Director, Facilities & Transportation, Ardsley School District and Director of Quad Village Transportation Consortium.
