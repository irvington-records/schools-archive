	13. Presentations/Discussions

		Discussion: 13.1 Next Steps: Transportation Recommendations made by Budget Task Force

		Mr. Bob Grados stated that the administration has asked the Board to re-review the presentation by the Transportation Subcommittee of the Budget Task Force at the end of the 2011-12 school year and provide the administration with guidance or direction as to where they would like administration to focus their energies  relating to cost saving options that the Transportation Subcommittee presented. It was noted that some items would require a referendum for voter approval. Mr. Joe Urbanowicz, the Quad District's Transportation Coordinator, attended meeting to answer any questions.

		Mr. Grados asked if Board Members have thought about the items and if they are prepared to talk and make any decisions. The Board discussed the non-referendum items, i.e. the elimination of bus monitors, for a potential savings of $100,000 and the elimination of bus at Dows Lane for a potential savings of $80,000. Comments from Board members included:

		Jim McCann: Do we have cameras on all buses and if so, they might make monitors unnecessary? Mr. Urbanowicz reported that all in-district buses have cameras. Tapes are held for approximately 1 week.

		Phil Whitney: Need to gather information from Ardsley and Hastings school districts as to how it has gone without monitors. Mr. Urbanowicz reported that initially  concerns were raised by parents and noted that it is important to understand what monitors do, i.e. assist students on and off buses. He also noted that a lot of what happens on a bus is not seen by monitors which is a misconception parents often have.Dobbs Ferry still uses bus monitors.

		Robyn Kerner noted that almost all parents of younger children are in favor of bus monitors and suggested getting the viewpoint of the Dows Lane principal as to what issues arise, etc. Ms. Kerner personally feels that monitors often try to umpire while bus driver is driving. Mrs. Kerner indicated that she is not comfortable making changes for the upcoming school year as it was discussed by last years's Board as part of the 2012-13 budget. Would need to give the community time to weigh in and it should be part of next year's budget discussion.

		Phil Whitney's recollection of previous discussion was to table the discussion until the summer in order to address possible referendum options and not to discuss elimination of monitors as they are already budgeted for. Mr. McCann noted that if monitors were eliminated for financial reasons, a system must be set up to deal with conflicts that arise, which is a bigger issue than saving money.

		Concluding the discussion, Mr. Grados stated that it did not appear to be consensus to move forward on the elimination of bus monitors at this time.

		Possible Elimination of Bus

		Mr. Urbanowicz reported that the District could eliminate a bus based on ridership. He also noted that the elimination of one bus would impact most routes and also impact start/end time at schools. Maria Kashkin commented that parents tend to drive students to school when we've eliminated a bus which causes longer routes. Ms. Kashkin also noted that currently the morning bus to Dows Lane from the High School barely makes it on time. Mrs. Kerner also noted that there is a safety issue involved which possible overcrowding of buses due to eliminating a bus.

		Dr. Harrison stated that it is important to find the opportunity to have Board retreat sooner rather than later and asked that Board Member's find ways to be flexible with scheduling.

		Robyn Kerner noted that the Board needs to find ways to communicate to the public and to reach out in meaningful ways.

		Phil Whitney -  With regard to identifying issues for the Board to discuss at meetings, Mr. Whitney stated that curriculum will need to be addressed and that Dr. Kor will take the lead. The other two issues will be the fields and Main Street School which will require analysis and noted that there are not that many meetings before the Board gets into budget. Fields are more challenging as there are many steps to get through to plan out agendas over next couple of months to discuss in orderly fashion. Robert Grados noted that hopefully the Buildings and Grounds Committee will take charge.
