7. Presentations/Discussions

Presentation: 7.1 Main Street School Presentation (Presentation)

Ms. Chapnick, principal of Main Street School, began by noting that MSS is a continuation of the experiences that the students have at Dows Lane, building on the ideas of the Being a Good Citizen program, etc. and highlighted the MSS Theory of Action: If students are communicating effectively in whole class discussions, small groups or partnerships, then they will successfully engage in discussion and questioning that promotes clarity, comprehension and higher level thing…and develop citizens of the world. MSS fosters higher level thinking throughout the students experiences. Along with highlighting the shared the work of teachers and the work that the teachers do with students, such as integration of essential questions and enduring understanding, small group station work in math, and accountable talk, which is teaching students explicitly how to talk in discussions to push their thinking and how they think about essential questions that fosters higher thinking to get to deeper meaning.

There is much work among teachers for planning, learning and collaborating through early release/delayed openings as well as in faculty meetings. Teachers in the classroom are integrating essential questions, the big idea/what is the thinking we want students to take with them. Within ELA, there are book clubs, with students reading independently then move onto group discussions and to ensure that students are understanding the text. She spoke of the PLTW activities that are taking place at MSS.

Much of the work at MSS addresses the social/emotion learning which go hand in hand with academics, using Habits of Mind, which helps the students understand who they are as learners as well as helping teachers to understand their students. Essential questions have been developed around character building, global citizenship, and all of which are used to push the students' level of thinking and fosters the desirable characteristics of learners.

Opportunities at MSS that are beyond the classroom include Hike for Humanity, Minute to Move, academic challenges such as Geo Bee, Spelling Bee and 24 Tournament, as well as many clubs, all opportunities for students to begin to think of who they are and what their passions and beliefs are. Opportunities are student-interest driven, such as the Garden Club, the idea to create a quiet space, a mindful garden. The PTSA has funded the garden clubs, with fundraising by the students and half the funds were raised needed. The IEF did fund the other half of what is needed to create the garden. Students at MSS engage in various activities which develops the concept of global citizenship through current events, collaborative projects with other schools around the world, skyping, assemblies, Hike for Humanity fundraiser and the recent screening of the movie, He Named Me Malala, with students planning and organizing the screening and fundraiser which raised $1,200 for the Malala Fund. These are examples that provides students’ with opportunities to identify their interests and passions.
