	6. Board Committee Reports

	Information: 6.1 Buildings & Grounds Committee - May 29, 2014

	Mrs. Kashkin provided an overview of the Buildings & Grounds meeting discussion on May 29th and included final conversations as to the recommendation for facilities projects to be included in a referendum. There was also conversation regarding the district's logo and review  of some possibilities but no recommendations are being made at this time for any particular design. The committee discussed the facilities report card as well as the five year plan that must be addressed each year. This will be done in-district and she noted that as mandated, the architects must create the plan which will be undertaken in the 2015-16.

	In a brief PowerPoint presentation, Dr. Harrison provided an overview of the projects that are recommended to be included in a bond referendum and highlighted the items not included in the bond, i.e. conversion of the upper lot to gravel parking lot, installation of radiator covers at MSS, campus lighting project and new storage shed at Meszaros Field. Some items will be covered through the general operating budget, i.e. renovation of boys and girls bathroom at the high school, and the installation of speed bumps on high school hill.

	The new annual debt service will be nearly $400,000 for the bond package, and noted that some debt will be retired, $315,425, so the net estimated in first year would be $84,163 or an increase of $28 per average home valued at approximately $700,000. There will also be approximately 24% the District would receive in building aid once the project is completed.

	Mrs. Kerner noted that she was under the impression that the Board would have discussions about what is to be included in a bond referendum and the recommendations made by the B&G Committee and shared her thoughts: 1) if there is a way to add the $80,000 (for bathroom renovations) to the bond which would then provide additional funding available for educational programs;. Mrs. Kashkin noted that the money would come from the facilities budget in 2014-15 and 2015-16 (one bathroom per year as the work can only be done during the summer) without impacting money that would be used for students. There was discussion on the pros and cons of funding the renovations via bond or general fund; and 2) what is the follow-up to the conversation that Dows Lane needed to be included in any renovation project and had asked if money could be included to ensure that they are also maintained in good shape and makes sense to include in the proposed bond. Mrs. Miller noted that upper Dows Field has been overused and that once the campus fields are renovated, Dows will be in good condition. In order to irrigate lower Dows, water lines would need to be installed and the cost could be handled through the general operating budget. Mrs. Kerner asked for a breakdown of the costs of the components that would be included in the bond and asked why there were differences in the architectural percentages. Ms. Miller explained that they are based on the level of individual components. John Montgomery and Robyn Kerner supports including $80,200 in the bond, Maria, Phil and Bob do not support doing so. Mr. Montgomery asked for the costs for architectural services, LAN, and it is expected the cost approximately $218,661 which is calculated on a percentage basis. Additional monies already expended $260,000 which has been paid through a fund balance which was primarily due to the necessary East Field analysis.

	14. Presentations/Discussions

	Presentation: 14.1 School Facility Report Card

	Beverly Miller, Asst. Supt. For Business, stated that it is an annual requirement to file a Facilities Report Card, stating that the District is in compliance with all mandates for safety. The District was found to be in compliance.

	Presentation: 14.2 Instructional Technology and Technology in Classrooms (Presentation)

	Dr. Kor and Jesse Lubinsky presented the current status of technology in the District, including examples of how students are currently utilizing technology in the classroom and also technology instruction at all grade levels. It was noted that a common understanding and language has been developed among staff and that technology is now part of any conversation that takes place when discussing curriculum. The presentation covered the infrastructure that is essential to integrating technology in the classroom as well as teaching technology. The Common Core has mandated that technology being integrated in the classroom, specifically Anchor Standard 6 and the various grade level requirements. It was noted that technology courses are offered as electives and that there are no specific requirements at the high school level. The District’s goal is to offer many higher level courses, such as engineering courses, however, staffing is an issue as there is only one teacher in the district who is teaching the courses. The ISTE standards are the framework that teachers will follow in providing learning experiences in the classroom.

	Professional development was also highlighted including the Quad Village consortium that provides course offerings taught by individuals and is open to teachers from the quad districts. Mr. Lubinsky also provided an example of a flipped learning assignment, and the Chromebook pilot. It was noted that the District is still looking at what is the best approach to how instruction will be delivered vis a vis technology. Infrastructure is a four pronged approach: hardware, software, communication and policy.

	In planning for the future, there will be continued dialogue with a broad range of thinkers that include staff, parents, the Technology Think Tank will meet on a regular basis next year and a Technology Leadership Team of teachers, students and administrators who will also be assembled to meet on a regular basis to assess the components of instruction, etc. with regards to technology in the District.

	The Board will provide questions regarding technology and there will be a follow up conversation at a later meeting.
