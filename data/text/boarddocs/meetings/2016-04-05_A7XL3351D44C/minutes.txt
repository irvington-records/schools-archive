	5. Presentations/Discussions

	Information, Report: 5.1 Elementary World Language Opportunities (Attachment)

	Dr. Kor reviewed the chart that was developed providing four possible models for delivering world language at the elementary level: immersion which would be costly and difficult to implement; FLES would require 4.0 staff and expensive to maintain and would replace other core instruction time, the FLEX model schedules 20 minutes every other day and only requires 2.5 staff but would also replace some core instruction. The exploratory program is geared for 5th graders and would be seen as a special, and similar to what was in the district previously, as an introduction to languages and would require a .4 FTE. Dr. Kor noted that a more detailed program will be developed with a strong focus on exposing students to global languages if moving forward with this model. Following the review, the Board and administration engaged in conversation on what the expectation is of what a student would gain from the programs along with the major issue aside from cost would be finding the time in the day. To offer it as an opt-in program would require a larger conversation and challenging in determining how many sections would be available to students as well as a shift in philosophy. Mr. Montgomery believes that the question to answer is "what do we want our graduates to leave high school with?" and take the time to look at our language program in a systematic way which might require reconfiguration. Dr. Kor noted that one area of growth needed is to leave the program speaking the language. The work with the consultant is focusing on developing units to strengthen speaking. Mr. Grados asked if there is any thought to move away from offering French to offering languages that are more global in nature such as Mandarin. Mr. Hanna asked if there was any conversation about offering a study abroad, which at this point there is no effort beyond providing opportunities for students to travel abroad. It was noted that if a language such as Mandarin would be offered, would be more complex in staffing, etc. then some of the other languages. Mr. Hanna believes it will be necessary to prioritize what is taught at the elementary level, i.e. STEM, languages as there is the time factor.

	Information: 5.2 Special Education Cost Analysis (Attachment)

	Mr. Whitney presented a Price/Volume Reconciliation analysis of special education data which he compiled illustrating how the costs in this area have risen from 2005-06 through present, using data points from 2005-06, 2010-11 and 2015-16, with a growth rate, excluding special ed, of 3.7%, and a 10.1% rate of growth in special ed., a little less than 3x greater than the overall budget growth. He reviewed the different student populations in special education, i.e. 504, residential placements, out of district day schools, etc. and noted that there is little change in the overall population, 175 in 2005 vs 181 currently. He also reviewed student data for John Cardinal O'Connor, from other districts and preschool, noting that these costs are reimbursable to the district. In reviewing staffing, he noted that there should be a correlation between student population and staffing, and noted that approximately seven additional staff have been added, or a growth rate of 2.2%. Non--instructional staff growth is approximately 1.8%, with the addition of quite a few aides.

	In reviewing what is driving the costs, a 12.7% growth rate, a 2.2% average annual growth rate in staff and 7.4% in cost/ FTE, and is clarified in the analysis of costs of instructional and non-instructional average costs. Also reviewed were the cost per students placed in district as well as out of district placements.

	Mr. Whitney provided comparison data with other districts. The benchmarking data was obtained from other districts' websites, looking at six area districts, comparing staffing, staff costs, cost per FTE as well revenue from incoming students. In reviewing Ardsley's data, he noted that their programs require greater staffing, however, their revenue is greater for incoming students, $2,976 vs Irvington's $709. Mr. Grados noted that each of the costs per student is based on each of the individual students and the costs related to each, as you may not be comparing apples and oranges and cautions that it is not as simple as just looking at numbers as there are many variables that go into the data. Ms. Palmieri noted that she sees value in looking at comparable data. Dr. Harrison also noted that not everyone budgets in the same way and that also there may be some who will be reluctant to provide full disclosure on their data and that there might be different accounting for such expenses such as legal expenses associated with special education. Scott Palermo and Gail Krieger provided commentary about the impact of JCOA as well as preschool on manpower as well as the types of disabilities and the level of services needed by our students.

	With respect to preschool, Gail Krieger noted that there are 67 preschools currently, with each requiring testing when transitioning into kindergarten. She also noted that the services are outsourced by the county but then become the District's responsibility when entering kindergarten. With JCOA, services are contracted out and billed back to home districts, however, District is responsible for management for all CSE meetings, which generally happen during the summer, and necessitates paying staff over the summer to sit in on meetings which are district costs. Ms. Krieger noted that who must be in attendance at meetings is governed by law. Shifts within the types and levels of disabilities were reviewed, with an increase in Other Heath Impaired and a decrease in Speech Impaired. Staff would be impacted as the nature of the needs of students are greater.

	Mr. Whitney's analysis was to attempt to answer the Board's question as to what has changed in the 10 year period to account for the increase in special education budget.

	Discussion: 5.3 2016-17 Budget Discussion (Attachment)

	Dr. Harrison noted that this is the sixth presentation relating to the budget. The proposed budget was approximately $500,000 over budget. Since the last meeting, $158,14 in additional aid will be received, reduction in special education expenses as well as a decrease in building and grounds expenditures and reductions in professional development have been made, totaling. $512,990. Dr. Harrison believes it is a balanced approach to maintaining current programs, deliver support to teachers as well as maintain facilities. The addition of .4 FTE in world language might help expand the program to fifth grade, increase of .2 FTE to support PLTW and provides funds for a replacement network switch. Total additions would increase the budget by $124,635 creating a total budget total of $58,300,000, or a 1.10% increase, bringing the proposed budget under cap by $304 or a budget variance of $636,000. The actual tax rate if assessments remained the same would be .12% but the average home would see $214 increase in their annual school taxes. He also noted that this would be the lowest increase in six years.

	There are other budget considerations that are not included in the data provided include:

-
		window air conditioners in classrooms - 40 @ $102,600;

-
		grounds foreman or outside contractor = $91,000

-
		1.0 FTE library media specialist = $106,000;

-
		1.0 FTE technology teacher = $106,000; and

-
		5 FTE school psychologist = $53,000

	The above total $458,600 and would require reductions in other areas or increase use of the fund balance.
