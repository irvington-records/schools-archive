SEPTEMBER 2015


Liaison Report: New York Suburban Consortium for Public Education!
by Michael Hanna!
!
The New York Suburban Consortium for Public Education held two meetings this past
month that I attended. The Consortium is a coalition that includes parent advocates,
school board members, and superintendents from 38 school districts in Westchester
and the Lower Hudson Valley.!
!
The first meeting on September 17th at the Ardsley District Office was an opportunity to
discuss the current issues. Following tremendous opposition from school boards,
teachers, superintendents, and parents last spring regarding the recently approved
Educational Transformation Act.        the Governor has suggested shift on assessing
Common Core with the creation of a review commission. We also heard from members
who observed the State Regent’s meeting the previous day in which while moving
forward with a split 10-6 vote to approve new professional review (APPR) regulations
that are increasingly reliant on state tests, also included a unanimous vote to express a
lack of confidence in the new system. !
!
The second meeting on September 24th also in Ardsley featured a sit-down with our
region’s state legislators and our local Regent Judith Johnson also participated. In
addition to myself, there were two members of the Irvington PTSA present, Debra
Nathan and Beth Propper. I had the opportunity to sit at the table with the legislators for
the discussion. !
!
At the meeting --!
! •! We explored with legislators the collective disappointment with mandates
       particularly and particularly the recent passage of the Education Transformation
       Act of 2015.!
! •! In fact, it was suggested that despite the passage of the ETA; the opposition was
       so great that there appears to be consideration on the part of the Governor to
       make some adjustments. The point was made by the legislators that the
       Governor has tremendous control over the budget process and tying desired
       legislation to it. That it is in the fall that his team plans the budget and as reported
       in the news the Governor has also put together a commission to review aspects
       of state     testing, so it is important for those who are concerned look to
       opportunities now to help shape what will be proposed in January by the
       Governor at his State of the State.!
! •! The legislators also suggest this is the year to push for finally restoring state aid
       to pre-recession levels by ending GEA - the Gap Elimination Act. They believe
       that there is political alignment to fully restore funding going forward.!
! •! There was also concern shared by some districts regarding a potential sub 1%
       tax levy cap this spring.!
Afterwards, I spoke with Assembly-person Tom Abinanti and State Senator Stuart-
Cousins and both expressed interest in holding a public discussion in our area perhaps
in coordination with Rivertown Parents about the impact of state policy and funding on
our area schools.
