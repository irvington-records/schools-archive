3. Presentation/Discussion

Discussion: 3.1 Text-based Workshop on Rigor

Dr. Harrison opened the meeting by noteing that the conversation on rigor will build upon the initial conversation that took place on goals, both District and school-based on September 6th, and will be followed by a discussion on assessments at the November 21st Work Session. Dr. Harrison noted that rigor is a difficult concept to understand in an educational setting and therefore it is important to have a dialogue in order to come to a common, deeper understanding on what rigor is in a school setting; language that is used in the classroom, how people look to stretch themselves. In advance of the meeting, the Board was provided with an article by Tony Wagner, Expecting Excellence as well as a chart illustrating 10 examples of rigor along with examples of the instruction created to provide academic rigor. He also noted that rigor exists at all levels, not only at the secondary level. The article highligted a list skills that industrywill be looking for from studentsm including, but not limited to: initiative, spirit, strong communicators, both i8n speaking aned writing, innovators, ability to process and analyse large amounts of data, but also students who pocess imagination, creativity, and empathy.  Dr. Kor facilitated the learning session that included board members as well as building principals and noted that a goal of the district has been to move toward being a professional learning community that will ensure that all teachers are provided with opportunities for ongoing dialogue, which is a true learning community, with one goal to develop a deeper understanding which in the end builds culture and a common understanding of what is needed in the district and in the classroom. Dr. Kor provided a chart that illustrate environments that will promote rigoe to be used as talking points as well as a common language but to ensure that everyone has a voice and to share their thinking, in a respectful way and to really listen to each other's voice. Copies of the 4 As: Assumptions, Aspire to, Agree and Argue, was distributed with the purpose of participants taking on of the examplees in the chart, wiith the3 e3xpectation of each to express which examles speaks to them and will think throuwn own values and beliefs.  The exercise is not to convince others of one's thinking but to think about they would like to aspire so that sense of what resonated for each participant, what best defines what rigor means.

Dr. Kor concluded that the goal is to have the Board have a deeper understanding of the concept of rigor when the principals share examples of the work taking place in the schools, and that the goal of ensuring that the practice of providing rigor is going on in all classrooms and continue to build upon a common understanding of what rigor looks like in the Irvington Schools.
