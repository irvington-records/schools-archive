3. Presentation/Discussion

Information: 3.1 2017-18 District and Building-based Goals (Presentation)

Dr. Harrison began the presentation by describing the work that has been taking place in the District over the past several years with a K-12 focus on providing enhanced learning experiences for all students. The District developed a framework to guide the planning along with the creation of three overarching goals that align with the Theories of Action and strategic goals and are curriculum and instruction, operations and finance and communications. He outlined the goals along with each outcome, activities/strategies, evidence of attaining goals along as well as the timeline for completion for each.

Dr. Raina Kor, Asst. Supt. of Curriculum/Human Resources, reviewed the Theories of Action that provide the foundation of the District's goals. She stressed that the mission of high achieving districts is to make sure that courses, curriculum, and classrooms are working toward the creation of an environment which supports the elevation of student thinking and understanding. The common goal is "a well-developed brain through thinking, metacognition and automaticity, wanting students to think, make good decisions/choices. Dr. Kor reviewed the tools that are used to implement instructional practices that will elevate student thinking and understanding and ensure that students receive consistent instruction, along with the use of data that will information instruction and planning.

The principals, assistant principals, and department administrators had the opportunity to speak to their school’s/department goals, desired outcomes as well as examples of evidence that will ensure that the desired outcomes are being achieved. Each spoke to the important role that staff development and collaboration among staff, through the use of study groups, instructional walk throughs, etc. that will ultimately create the instructional practices to foster deeper level of thinking and rigor in the curriculum at each grade level. Each reviewed assessments systems to measure content knowledge, skills, etc. Joyce Chapnick, Principal of Main Street School and Deb Mariniello, Principal at Dows Lane, shared the work that is taking place in their schools, noting that even though in separate buildings, there is a K-5 continuum.

Irvington Middle School Principal, Mr. Sottile, and Asst. Principal, Allison Daley, spoke to the work taking place at the Middle School that elevate teaching and thinking with everything being influenced to meet the end goal of promoting student thinking, engagement and ownership of their learning. Again, professional learning is the cornerstone of ensuring that students are being provided with the tools needed that will ensure achievement of the goals set forth. Mr. Sottile began with the questions "How do we know what we think we know" which is at the heart of assessing and what do we know about students as thinkers.

Irvington High School Principal, Dave Cohen and Asst. Principal, Matthew Samuelson, reviewed the practices taking place at the high school to achieve the instructional practices and assessment goals for this year. Mr. Cohen noted that the work of developing department chairs into instructional leaders has been a critical component taking place at the high school. At the high school level, the work is to prepare students to move forward, with dense, rigorous curriculum. Unit planning is essential and has the teachers thinking about how the unit elevates student thinking. Assessments will also help to determine if  design of the curriculum is meeting those objectives. Additionally, there are post assessment activities which allow students to reflect upon their own process in learning and for them to begin to own their learning. Data is essential in determining whether interventions are necessary to support a student's learning.

Gail Krieger, Director of PPS and Kimberly Mosca addressed the PPS goals Ms. Krieger stressed that PPS supports K-12 learning for all students and outlined the services that fall under the department. The foundation of this year’s goals began with conversations between the buildings over the summer in order for Ms. Krieger and Ms. Mosca to learn what the administration saw as the department's strengths and where there is room for growth, PPS also will look to implement instructional practices that elevate student thinking and learning. With respect to assessments, Ms. Mosca noted that the goal is to develop a balanced assessment system to identify students’ needs by utilizing multiple measures to assess the supports necessary for individual students as well as using data to determine a students present level of performance.

Jesse Lubinsky, Dir. of Technology, spoke of the activities that take place in classrooms that promote student voice, choice and inquiry. He outlined the PLTW courses which continue the growth of STEM that are now available at each school that will expand the experiences of students. He added that the Innovation Fund allows students the opportunity write a grant to seek funding for projects that benefit a wider segment of the student population. Technology-based tools will be utilized to assess student learning and the efficacy of instruction in the area of technology.

Artie McCormack, Dir. of Athletics, could not be present, therefore, Dr. Harrison addressed the goals of the athletic department which is to implement practices that will increase opportunities to elevate pride and spirit within the department, the launch of the Booster Club, to seek student feedback of their experiences and to develop a District philosophy around athletics. Surveys will be created to identify student interest and opportunities to create new experiences.

Following the presentation, board members asked specific questions regarding individual components of the presentation, i.e. assessments, what changes in practices have been implemented to ensure balanced assessments, if data from assessments inform teaching in the current year, how teachers utilize the previous year's assessment results for their current students. Mrs. Palmieri asked for clarity on how the Theories of Action align with the Strategic Plan as well as the vision for the District, as the presentation focuses on learning and the other goals weren't as transparent. Dr. Kor noted that each school/department's goals does touch upon the six overarching goals. It was suggested that a reference chart would easily map out that alignment and it was agreed that one would be developed. Mr. Graeber also noted that last year's presentation on unit planning was very informative and might be helpful to parents to gain a better understanding of the curriculum at each grade level. Mrs. Palmieri asked questions on behalf of emails sent to the Board asking: does Irvington have programs for gifted students, support for providing Spanish at an earlier age, assess students who might be advanced in Spanish or French, and to start Latin at the middle school. Dr. Harrison addressed these questions regarding the language program with respect to building a program and scheduling it in the school day so that it is impactful. With respect to gifted programs, Dr. Kor noted that there are no pull out programs and that instruction is differentiated to provide deeper learning. Mr. Graeber reported that the email the Board received from community regarding the expansion of the language program did note that there would be support for an increase in taxes to support an elementary language program.

The Board gave an opportunity for community members to comment on the presentation:

Erin Burnstein spoke on behalf of parents of elementary students in strong community support to introduce an early language program at the elementary level, noting the benefits of such a program and especially as it relates to the desire for the district to develop students into global citizens.  A petition has been circulated that contains approximately 300 signatures of parents asking the Board to consider this request.  Other parents spoke to their desire for such a program and the benefits of such a program, one noting that Irvington is not competitive in this area as are other nearby schools, global competition, etc. They also noted that many parents would be in favor of increased taxes in order to provide an early language program.

Mr. Hanna noted that the board had received an email from Beth Proper regarding providing foreign language in the lower grades as well as enhancing the secondary program to allow students proficient in a language to move ahead.
