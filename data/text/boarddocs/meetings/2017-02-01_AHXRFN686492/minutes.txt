2. Presentations

Presentation: 2.1 Draft of District's Strategic Plan (presentation)

Mrs. Palmieri thanked the community members in attendance as well as those that participated in the community forums to help develop the foundation for the plan, stating that the expectation for this meeting is to put the plans in place that are part of the vision that the community provided vis a vis the forums, with the request for feedback from the community.

Dr. Harrison spoke of his first impressions of the Irvington community prior to becoming the Superintendent five years ago, noting the feeling that Irvington was a community with a strong connection to the schools and their sense of ownership of their children’s education. The dedication of administrators, teachers and staff and the students’ passion for learning became evident once he became Superintendent.  He provided a synopsis of the process of creating the strategic plan whose purpose is to bring the schools to the next level. He also highlighted the academic iniatiatives over the last five years aimed at reaching the whole child. He addressed the investment made in facilities, such as a campaign to beautify school grounds and enhanced communications with the community. Financially, the District has maintained a healthy standing, with a low fiscal stress indicator, created a capital fund reserve while maintaining responsible school budgets.

Dr. Harrison highlighted students’ academic achievements, including SAT/ACT scores that ranked at least 23% higher than the national average, as well as 14 athletic teams recognized as NYS Scholar Athletes in 2015-16. Also highlighted were the many opportunities for student success, including 20 AP classes, 18 honor level classes, expanded PLTW STEM/technology program in all schools, focus on Character Building and the many enriching opportunities through electives, clubs and parent-based programs.

The following Mission Statement has been drafted that provides the foundation for the District’s vision:

"The mission of the Irvington School District is to create a challenging and supportive learning environment in which each student attains his or her highest potential for academic achievement, critical thinking and life-long learning. Our schools encourage the discovery and development of students’ individual strengths, skills and talents, and foster social and civic responsibility."

Through the focus groups, strategies to achieving the vision were created with key action and indicators of success established through which to grow programs that are of value to the community over time. “We Will…statements were also drafted which will provide expectations and direction to meet the goals at each level and provide the foundation for each building’s goals and action plan that will be aligned with overall vision for the district. Following the overview of each of the components of the strategic plan, Dr. Harrison outlined the critical steps to implementation: finalization of goals and objectives; development of actions plans on a District and school level; semi-annual updates from leadership; ensuring the plan remains flexible to accommodate new and/or changing needs.  An annual report will be provided to the Board and community as well.

Following the presentation, Board members thanked the community for their time in helping to create the Strategic Plan and concurred that the draft presented is a tremendous first step toward finalizing a plan. Mr. Whitney noted discussions to create a strategic plan has been ongoing for six years and he is happy that the plan is comprehensive and that it addresses the principles and values that came from the community. Mr. Whitney and Mr. Graeber both believe that the plan’s indicators need to be turned into measurements of success. Deb Hargraves noted that she participated as a parent on the committee and that members often began with their own set of personal wants, but that in the end, the plan connects the community’s vision through goals, action items and indicators.  Mr. Hanna agreed that  setting forth the values of the community is important but cautions to look carefully at the words used as they might have different meanings at different stages, i.e. the word rigor might mean something different at the elementary level and at the secondary level.

Mrs. Palmieri noted that in looking at other district’s plans, she saw that many, even high performing districts, address where they are failing, however, Irvington doesn’t have that problem, but rather needs to address how can Irvington differentiate itself, especially when competing against high performing districts with more funds. She believes that there are opportunities in Irvington through some of its local industries, i.e. Nevis Labs, that could provide unique opportunities for our students.

Courtney White, a member of the committee also agreed that the plan needs to include measurable results that can be benchmarked and urged the District to “put a stake in the ground” by establishing a date for the plan to be finalized. She also recommended that a summary document be created, which Dr. Harrison acknowledged would be written.
