5. Presentation

Presentation:  5.1 2019-2020 Budget Presentation: Budgetary Climate/Operations & Finance

Dr. Harrison shared that the vision of the district is to be "an ever stronger district", not just today, but long range as well in the areas of academics, professional development, fiscal health as well as providing a safe environment for staff and students, while being mindful of tax rate. He highlighted the correlation between the strategic objectives and the budget proposals. It is also important to plan for challenges that include not only national and state landscape, uncertainty over state aid, tax levy formula, unpredictable tax certioraries, special education needs, changes in enrollment, as well as contractural obligations.

Carol Stein addressed foundation aid and noted that the governor's proposal would be dangerous to a district like Irvington and advocacy will be necessary. She also noted that there is a proposal to make the tax cap permanent and believes that there changes would be necessary in the formula. A 10-year history of Foundation Aid was reviewed and noted that Irvington is owed $10 million dollars in Foundation Aid. An overview of budget history was provided, including past reductions and recent increases to programs, staff, etc, along with general fund expenditures over 10 years. Ms. Stein addressed the fund balance, explaining what it is, how it is used and what are ramifications for using it. Under State law, 4% is able to be carried over. If under, the district could be designated in fiscal stress by NYS. Dr. Harrison shared upcoming budget presentations and discussions and noted that it is the Board's responsibility to determine the budget ceiling that will ultimately result in the proposed budget for voters' consideration on May 21st.

Business Operations Component

Board of Education, Chief School Administrator, Finance, Legal, Human Resources, Facilities & operations, Central data processing, special items include insurance, BOCES, transportation, debt service and transfer of other funds. Ms. Stein provided areas where there would be variances even without any budget increases such as salaries, BOCES, insurance costs, etc. She also noted that there is a bus driver shortage and transportation companies are looking to increase cost of services and will require district's to go out to bid.

New considerations include:

- expanded security costs in afternoon and evening ($76,650),

- shared services in security consultant ($69,000)

- a new phone system ($60,000 per year for 5 years),

- floating custodian ($79,800) and facility vehicles ($70,000).

An overview of the current budget and the proposed budget in the components were provided. Ms. Stein noted that all the detail of the expenses in the categories is available on the web

The Facilities Operations & Maintenance proposed budget was provided with an increase of $281,562 which includes the new considerations as previously noted. Presentation also included proposed facilities equipment and building repair projects in each school. Building repair and capital repair projects that are not included due to fiscal restrictions were noted.

The other budget areas were addressed, including: central data processing, special items, and transportation. There are no new considerations in these areas, but again it was noted that transportation could be higher should it be necessary to go out to bid. However, we are actively seeking to add other students from districts to some private schools to possibly reduce costs. Debt service, inter-fund transfers were reviewed. In the above categories, new requests total $312,215.

Dr. Harrison concluded the presentation by noting that it will be a difficult budget year as the district does not receive much state aid, but it addresses student needs, security initiatives which he feels are non-negotiable, but that all considerations are under discussion over the next several months.
