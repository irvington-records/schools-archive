	3. Superintendent's Report

	Information: 3.1 District's News and Events

	Dr. Kor shared that it has been decided not to administer the new Common Core English Regents in addition to the old Regents to 9th graders. Since the Common Core Regents is optional, administrators believe that administering another assessment in light of the other demands placed on students was not in their best interest.

	The Common Core Algebra Regents is required this year for all students taking Algebra 1 and Districts can also choose to administer the old Regents exam as well, with the higher of the two exam scores recorded for each student. In addition, 8th grade students would have to take the NYS 8th grade Assessments, having students in accelerated math sitting for three exams, which would be unacceptable. The District is waiting for SED to make a decision that would allow 8th graders in accelerated Math to take only the Regents.The next Generation of Science standards will be considered and voted on by the Regents in February, but there has been talk that there might not be the funding to roll them out sooner rather than later.

	Districts have received the new framework for Social Studies Standards which will also be voted on by the Regents as well. State assessments will be developed and Dr. Kor guesses that they might be made available in 2014 for administered in 2015.

	Dr. Harrison reported that additional Board meetings will need to be added to address other issues concerning the District, such as the fields and a broader discussion on the District's facilities. Budget discussions will begin on January 14th and there will be discussions prior to the budget with respect to specific areas in order to have deeper conversations regarding the budget.

	Dr. Harrison also noted that it is important to also focus on the good things going on in the schools. School concerts are beginning on December 4th with the HS/MS Band. On December 12th the HS Wind Ensemble and chorale will perform at the MetLife building in NYC and the MS/HS Winter Chorus and Orchestra will perform on December 18th.

	5. Student Performance Report (Presentation)

	Presentation: 5.1 Student Performance Report

	Dr. Kor provided an overview of the 2012-13 Grades K-8 Math, ELA, Grades 4-8 Science NYS assessment results, and included the 2009-2012 results for comparison purposes as well as comparisons with 11 other area districts of similar size, with the exception of Scarsdale and Chappaqua, and that there are many variables to keep in mind when comparing assessment results, i.e. number of students tested. The comparison is provided only as a snapshot and not the entire picture. Dr. Kor stressed throughout her presentation that these assessments are one of many ways students' performance is looked at. She also noted that it is important to keep in mind that in 2010 New York State revised their definitions for achievement levels which resulted in a drop of in scores, and other assessment modifications that can be reflected in the scores. She also noted that Irvington's 4th graders were 4th in NYS in achieving levels 3/4 but that noted that fluctuation of scores from 3-8th grade and feels that lack of assessment consistency in design, and are not consistently assessing each year which would reduce the fluctuations in achievement as well as particular cohort of students. Staff will look in depth at certain data points to see where we might need to improve.

	A five year history of the English, Sciences, and Social Studies Regents scores were provided and shared the science assessments, beginning in 4th grade and noted that Irvington generally scores well on the science assessments. Students generally do well in Social Studies Regents, Global History and US History. She provided data on the number of AP courses offered and it has grown to 19 courses over the last 12 years with participation remaining consistent and provides only an indicator of what our students are taking and it was noted that not all students taking AP courses sit for the exam).For the 2013 school year, 594 students were enrolled in AP courses, with 368 out of 590 passing the exams. In addition to the assessment data, Dr. Kor noted that Irvington had 22 out of 27 varsity teams recognized as NYS scholar Athlete Teams in 2012-13. Dr. Kor also reviewed the data by looking at a particular cohort of students as some might score higher as they go through the grades.

	Dr. Kor reiterated that the data is used to inform thinking for planning instruction and data will continued to be used to guide knowledge and understanding on how Irvington compares with neighboring districts and will continue to look at the data longitudinally. Irvington needs to look at how foundational skills are being delivered to our early learners, K-1 to ensure success as they move forward. Currently there is no standardized approach to learning basic skills in the early grades in Irvington and expect that all teachers have a tool box of all the methods. This will also help to fill the gaps of our struggling early learners.

	In response to a question from a community member if the Board was satisfied with the assessment results, Robyn Kerner responded that that NYS assessments is only a benchmark and one of many and not that important in the grand scheme of things, however, must look at deficiencies and strengthen them where warranted, but that in her view the Regents are a better benchmark to see how our students are doing. Mr. Montgomery noted that he is struggling as to how we really assess the value added and if Irvington is providing students all that we can but he is not sure what the right metrics would be to measure success of students. Mr. Grados and Mrs. Kashkin both agreed with the previous comments from Mr. Montgomery and Mrs. Kashkin, with Mr. Grados noting that he feels that to put value on the data, you must look individual student’s performances to see how they are doing in a particular area. Mr. Montgomery would be interested in looking at other district's Regent's scores for comparison.

	Catherine Johnson asked when the breakdown of the APPR scores will be available. Dr. Kor said that they would be available end of this week or early next week.

	Mr. Grados noted that the question that remains is "who does Irvington want to be"? But the grander goal is to focus on what the District can control and what we want the curriculum to be and this is the time to do answer these questions. Robyn Kerner asked if the SAT informs the curriculum and what our students' scores might tell us. Mr. Cohen noted that it was a point to look at but it's not typical to align curriculum with SATs. Mrs. Kerner would like to see a comparison of the scores of 8th graders taking Algebra Regents and 9th graders. She also would like to look at students Regents scores and AP scores, if any.

	11. Presentations/Discussions

	Information: 11.1 APPR Update (Presetiontan)

	Dr. Kor provided an overview of what the Annual Professional Performance Review (APPR) is and noted that Irvington has had an APPR plan in place for 12 years. She also noted that Irvington utilizes the Charlotte Danielson Framework for Teaching for teacher evaluation and the mandated components for the APPR, using 60% classroom observation, 20% state assessment data or district developed student learning objectives (SLO), and 20% locally selected measures. It is estimated that the process takes approximately 10 hours per teacher over the course of the year. The plan must be reviewed annually and each teacher's review must be completed by September 1st of the school year that follows, resulting in their individual composite scores. Dr. Kor highlighted the accomplishments of the District, trying to make it a positive, learning experience for all teachers. She also noted that there are concerns and challenges with the regulations in the law and that there is no consistency in the plans with other districts, as they are all individually negotiated. She also noted that guidance and psychologists do not fall under the APPR mandates at this time, but they are subject to observations.

	Dr. Kor provided a visual to show the relationship between the Common Core Learning Standards and the APPR.

	Robyn Kerner asked if input from parents would impact a teacher's final score. Dr. Kor noted that it is not built into the plan but would, of course, be taken into account if the observations showed something different. Maria Kashkin asked if the use of tutors might be factored into evaluations of staff, especially if students in a particular teacher's class tend to utilize the services of teachers. Dr. Kor stressed the time spent in classrooms, both formally or with administrators going into classes and identifying what is going on and it enables to be sure that students are getting a consistent experience.

	11. Presentations/Discussions

	Information: 11.1 APPR Update (Presentation)

	Dr. Kor provided an overview of what the Annual Professional Performance Review (APPR) is and noted that Irvington has had an APPR plan in place for 12 years. She also noted that Irvington utilizes the Charlotte Danielson Framework for Teaching for teacher evaluation and the mandated components for the APPR, using 60% classroom observation, 20% state assessment data or district developed student learning objectives (SLO), and 20% locally selected measures. It is estimated that the process takes approximately 10 hours per teacher over the course of the year. The plan must be reviewed annually and each teacher's review must be completed by September 1st of the school year that follows, resulting in their individual composite scores. Dr. Kor highlighted the accomplishments of the District, trying to make it a positive, learning experience for all teachers. She also noted that there are concerns and challenges with the regulations in the law and that there is no consistency in the plans with other districts, as they are all individually negotiated. She also noted that guidance and psychologists do not fall under the APPR mandates at this time, but they are subject to observations.

	Dr. Kor provided a visual to show the relationship between the Common Core Learning Standards and the APPR.

	Robyn Kerner asked if input from parents would impact a teacher's final score. Dr. Kor noted that it is not built into the plan but would, of course, be taken into account if the observations showed something different. Maria Kashkin asked if the use of tutors might be factored into evaluations of staff, especially if students in a particular teacher's class tend to utilize the services of teachers. Dr. Kor stressed the time spent in classrooms, both formally or with administrators going into classes and identifying what is going on and it enables to be sure that students are getting a consistent experience.
