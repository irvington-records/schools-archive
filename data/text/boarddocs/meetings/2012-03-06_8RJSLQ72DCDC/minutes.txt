		Report:  3.2 Human Resources Audit Report (Presentation)

		Dr. Roelle reported that District contracted with Dr. Leonora Boehlert to perform an audit of the Human Resources Department in order to ensure that all functions are being done properly, Dr. Leona Boehlert, was contracted to perform an audit of the department. Dr. Roelle noted that Dr. Boehlert spent quite a lot of time in the District Office going through the personnel files and reviewing the current practices to ensure that the District is in compliance with both State and Federal laws. The oversight for the department is performed by the Superintendent and the Assistant Superintendent and a secretary for the department.

		Dr. Boehlert provided overview of what was included in the review, such as personnel files, Board policies related to human resources, compliance with state and federal regulations, and technology and the human resource function. Among the strengths of the District is a supportive administrative team, collegial relationships between staff, the technology framework as well as a clerical staff member in the department willing to research and implement up-to-date practices.

		Dr. Boehlert's recommendations include:

-
			continued streamlining of personnel files and tracking systems;

-
			monitoring of certification and seniority areas;

-
			restructuring appointment information;

-
			implementing systematic processes for Board appointments; and

-
			increasing the current substitute pool and review the possible use of an electronic substitute calling system.

		Dr. Boehlert also recommends continuing to update current use of technology and maintain an electronic seniority list as well as ensure that up-to-date information is disseminated to staff. Dr. Boehlert also noted that it may be beneficial to reinstate the position of Asst. Superintendent of Curriculum and Instruction to provide oversight to the human resources function.

		3.3 2012-13 Budget Discussion (Presentation)

		In response to a question raised at the last Board meeting regarding the possibility of having two budget propositions for the community to vote on in May, Dr. Roelle noted that a school district may submit two separate propositions, but, if both propositions collectively exceeds the Tax Cap, then both propositions would need to pass by 60%, a super majority of voters. Dr. Roelle noted that there is no case law currently on this matter and the State Education Department has only issued a verbal opinion. The District in the area who was thinking about posing two propositions have abandoned this idea.

		Restoration of .5 Guidance Counselor and the Grade 6 World Language - .6 FTE  total $113,500 along with an addition $85,0000 for other necessary additions total $199,000. Ms. Miller reviewed the reductions that would offset the additions to the budget, totaling $118,000, leaving a net adjustment of $81,000 to be added to the budget, resulting in the maximization of the tax cap as well an adding $22,000 from the fund balance. The proposed budget totals $51,156,000, a percentage increase of 1.65%, and maintains the budget under the tax cap by $718.

		With regard to the recommendation to restore the Asst. Superintendent for Curriculum and Personnel position, Dr. Roelle provided the rationale for this recommendation. Dr. Roelle noted that Dr. Kor is doing a wonderful job for the district but that there are limitations in a stipend position. Dr. Roelle also noted that there are areas that have been placed on the back-burner such as implementation the Science recommendations following a complete audit of the District's science curriculum as well as providing consistency through all grade levels. Dr. Roelle provided an overview of the responsibilities that would be under the leadership of an administrator for curriculum and instruction and again stressed that the position also provides leadership for change and innovation as well as provide support of the Superintendent's vision.

		In response to the request at the last Board meeting, Dr. Roelle reviewed the impact of the budget reductions recommended such as Grade 5 World Language and the 1.0 technology position at the elementary level. Class sizes remain the same at the elementary level. Dr. Roelle reviewed the impact of the reductions at the middle school, and noted that the recommendation to eliminate the Grade 8 Exploratory Program might place some students in a study hall every other day. At the high school, class sizes may be slightly higher, but continue to be favorable and also reviewed the impact on sections of subjects. Dr. Roelle noted that there might be a possible reduction in AP French due to enrollments and there will be the reduction of the photography class. There are no expected changes to the district-wide music program, except that some lessons might have slight increases in class size.

		Action (Consent):  9.5 Quad-Village School Districts Local Government Efficiency (LGE) Grant

		Resolution: WHEREAS, funds are available through the New York State Department of State, Division of Local Services, 2011-2012 Local Government Efficiency Grant (LGE) Program for studying, planning and/or implementation assistance for consolidations, mergers, dissolutions, cooperative agreements and shared services between two or more local government entities, including school districts; and WHEREAS, the Ardsley Union Free School District (“Ardsley”), Dobbs Ferry Union Free School District (“Dobbs Ferry”), Hastings-on-Hudson Union Free School District (“Hastings”) and Irvington Union Free School District (“Irvington”), collectively referred to as the “Quad-Village School Districts,” wish to jointly submit a grant application with the New York State Department of State, Division of Local Services for a Local Government Efficiency Grant to undertake a study to determine the efficiencies, requirements and benefits of consolidating two or more small school districts, to be known as the “Quad-Village School District Efficiency and Consolidation Study”; and WHEREAS, upon authorization by the Board of Education of each of the Quad-Village School Districts, an application will be submitted for an LGE Grant in an amount up to $90,000, with Dobbs Ferry serving as the lead applicant, and Ardsley, Hastings and Irvington participating as co-applicants; and WHEREAS, Dobbs Ferry and the other three co-applicant school districts shall together commit the sum up to $10,000 or up to $2,500 per school district participant, as the Local Cost Share; and WHEREAS, Ardsley, Hastings and Irvington, as co-applicants, have agreed to enter into an intermunicipal agreement with Dobbs Ferry upon approval of the grant application which will detail the services to be provided and the responsibilities of each of the Quad-Village School Districts; THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED, that the Board of Education of the Irvington Union Free School hereby authorizes the Dobbs Ferry Union Free School District to apply as the Lead Applicant for the Local Government Efficiency Grant for the “Quad-Village School District Efficiency and Consolidation Study” in an amount up to $90,000, with the Quad-Village School Districts committing up to $10,000 as the Local Cost Share towards the Study (up to $2,500 per school district) and making a commitment to enter into an intermunicipal agreement with Dobbs Ferry upon approval of the LGE Grant; and BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED that the Board of Education hereby authorizes Dr. Lisa Brady, Superintendent of Schools of the Dobbs Ferry Union Free School District or designee to submit the Grant Application on behalf of the co-applicants, and further designates Sylvia Fassler-Wallach, Director of Finance and Facilities of the Dobbs Ferry Union Free School District to serve as the contact person with respect to the LGE Grant, with authority to execute all financial and/or administrative processes relating to the Grant.
