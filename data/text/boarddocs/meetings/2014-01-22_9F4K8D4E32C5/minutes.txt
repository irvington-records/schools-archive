	3. Presentations/Discussions

	Discussion: 3.1 Fields, Facilities, and the Future

	Dr. Harrison presented data regarding the District’s current fields’ utilization, including physical education classes, recess and school-based athletic teams’ usage as well as community usage of the district’s facilities which included the hours per week for each of the district’s fields. Dr. Harrison also reviewed the benefits of reopening East Field and reviewed the three options, including their associated costs that had been presented at the January 7th meeting which would also satisfy DEC/DOH requirements to remediate the field.

	Also presented was the concept of creating a baseline of need for East Field, Meszaros Field as well as replacing Oley Track. Comparison of the costs associated with installing natural turf, artificial turf and organic turf, along with play time for each surface as well as maintenance costs and longevity with presented.

	With regard to the needs of the District’s facilities, Dr. Harrison stated that the Board of Education Buildings & Grounds Committee has established a prioritized listing of needs and in determining priorities considers: health and safety, energy efficiency and building maintenance and preservation. Considerations include:

-
		Energy conservation projects, totaling $1,058,450

-
		General facility projects, totaling $2,051,600.

	To fund any field and facilities projects, the District would ask voters to approve a bond in a referendum being considered for Fall 2014. Dr. Harrison reviewed the cost impact of a potential project cost of $1.9-6.2 million depending on the scope of the projects as determined by the Board of Education. He also reviewed the debt service impacts on the 2015-16 budget with principal and interest estimates based upon a 15 year term at 4% interest, and noted that a bond retiring in 2014-15 will result in a $314,425in debt service reduction for the 2015-16 budget.

	In concluding the presentation, Dr. Harrison charged the Board with establishing a baseline or minimum amount of work that must be accomplished, i.e. remediation to East Field, renovations to Meszaros field and infrastructure needs to District facilities. In addition to establishing a baseline for the above, potential enhancements must be addressed and how best to seek community input for this aspect of the process.

	To provide community input, a Fields Enhancement Committee would be appointed that would include 10 residents with civic or professional expertise and their charge would be to examine potential enhancements such as artificial turf and lights and their findings presented in a report to the Board of Education which the Board will consider in making their final determination as to the scope of the athletic facilities project to be included in a bond referendum.

	Following the presentation, Board of Education members asked for further information regarding aspects of the presentation, including but not limited to: annual maintenance costs of turf, grass vs. turf and if two grass fields would be sufficient, cost/benefit analysis of proposed energy projects, if any more bonds would be retiring to cover costs.

	Community members in attendance also inquired about the tax implication of bonds, actual construction timeline, if sod was an option, etc. It was observed that the meeting turnout was low and asked that the issue not be rushed without community engagement.
