	1. Public Hearing - Code of Conduct

		Procedural:  1.1 Revised Code of Conducts (Document)

		Dr. Roelle stated that the new requirements under the Dignity for All Students Act (DASA) are now incorporated into the revised Code of Conduct. The Code will be presented at the July 10, 2012 reorganization meeting for adoption.

		Carolyn Pryzbylo, Legal Counsel, noted that she and the administrators worked on revising the Code in order to be in compliance with DASA and other mandates. Carolyn noted that it must be adopted by law by July 1, 2012.

		Mr. McCann asked to see where in the Code of Conduct it addresses how issues regarding bullying, etc. will be addressed. Ms. Pryzbylo responded that DASA provides more sensitivity to bullying issues, not just issues of  race and religion. It also calls for bringing heightened awareness to students and staff. Mr. McCann asked that the word "stop"  be used instead of "correct" on page 13 where it speaks to remedial responses. He is concerned that students who are bullied tend to withdraw from school and feels that Code does not have enough "teeth". He asked if other Board Members felt the same and if it should be held until  the language is sufficient to the Board's desire.

		Dr. Roelle noted that aggressive steps are taken to deal with bullying. Parents often come forward but do not want to use their child's name. The policy is an educational issue to address the problems. Training is inherent within the policy. Part of the DASA also includes education for students to understand the issues

		Mr. Grados noted that the code must be looked at as a whole to see that it does have consequences. The law acknowledges that this is an educational institution, but we do not have legal authority to act as a police.

		Board Members John Dawson, Phil Whitney and Bob Grados all noted that they are fine with language as it stands. It was noted by Mr. Whitney and Mr. Grados that the Code is a legal document. Mr. Whitney suggested that plain language text be provided to the students.

		Comments from Community

		Mr. Knowler noted that he is disturbed by Mr. McCann's assertions that there are bullying issues in Irvington and would like to discuss if there really is a district problem.

	       Richard Cole cautioned the Board that by using the word 'stop' the Board might be opening themselves and the District up to massive lawsuits.  Mr. Cole personally has seen that the school has been more than responsive in bullying issues, opposite than what Mr McCann claims.

	4. Presentations/Discussions

		Discussion, Presentation:  4.1 Technology Lease Agreement and Overview of EduTek (Presentation)

		 Jesse reported on his responsibilities as both Technology Coordinator and Chief Information Officer. The CIO position is responsible for providing all student data to the state reports as well as verification of all testing data. Mr. Lubinsky also noted that the new APPR mandates will increase data submission requirements.

		An IT audit was requested several years ago and conducted by Accume Partners. The District was rated in seven areas, including IT strategy and planning, outsourced vendor management, and systems operations. The District was rated as adequate/satisfactory in most categories, with the exception of outsourced management (which was more connected to third party vendors, such as Lower Hudson Regional Information Center) and information security were noted to need improvement. Mr. Lubinsky noted that these areas were immediately addressed.

		The current infrastructure was reviewed, i.e. bandwidth and data security. Mr. Lubinsky noted that the District is using over 100% of our bandwidth, mostly due to an increase of web-based programs being used, such as eSchool. The new package will bring us to a bandwith of 50 at a cost of $469 per month. With the increase in wireless infrastructure, the usage must be monitored to be sure that we have adequate bandwidth for classrooms and offices.

		Also reviewed was the 2012-13 Technology Lease  and it was noted that there has been a 30% ($75,000) reduction in hardware costs. The District currently has approximately 880 workstations but through the use of virtualization of host computers, there are only 600 physical computers, thereby reducing the costs. Mr. Lubinsky noted that if the district moved to a six year replacement cycle as opposed to current 5 year, it might result in larger repair costs of computers.In response to a question regarding the use of laptops, Mr. Lubinsky noted that laptops seem to have only a four year replacement cycle and are more expensive, therefore, therefore are not generally used as host computers.

		Among future initiatives, is making provisions to allow students to bring their own device, cloud storage and realizing savings in green power initiatives, paperless initiatives and asset recovery. The District has implemented a Power Management system which allows us to remotely program that all monitors are off at 6:00 p.m. as well as to monitor the electric usage.
