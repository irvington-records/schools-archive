5. Presentation

Presentation: 5.1 2018-19 Budget Presentation: Budgetary Climate/Operations & Finance

Prior to the presentation, Mr. Hanna pointed out that the first slide in the presentation highlights the district's vision for tomorrow to be an ever stronger district: and includes a bullet: "in which our staff is supported with consistent high-quality professional development and fair contracts." Dr. Harrison noted that there is a broad set of responsibilities imbedded and very importantly, includes a tremendous commitment to supporting faculty. Highlighted were the challenges of maintaining fiscal health with the need to do more, maintain facilities, navigating the tax cap, government issued unfunded mandates, national and state political tax law changes, etc. while allowing the Strategic Objectives to achieve the District's mission and vision. Other challenges include unpredictable tax certioraries, contractural obligations beyond control, such as pension contributions, health insurance costs, utility cost increases and BOCES fees. Carol Stein spoke of the aid received by Districts and provided a 10 year history of Foundation Aid, noting that the District has lost nearly $9,769,958, and addressed other possible changes that are being discussed in Albany which would be detrimental to the District and expressed her concerns should they be realized, including increased contributions for summer special ed tuition, proposed caps in Transportation/BOCES/Building Aid, which would hurt Irvington. Dr. Harrison also noted that there is always a degree of uncertainty with respect to enrollment at this point in the budget development process.

Dr Harrison noted that the District has been in a rebuilding phase over the past 5 years to offset the prior cuts to staff and programs and highlighted the expenditures year to year by component since the 2008-09 school year and shared the budget trends since 2011-12. Tax certs expenditures over the past 10 years were reviewed along with the impact on the district. Carol Stein provided an overview of the fund balance, and noted that District's are only able to keep 4% of their total budget and if under that, the State Comptroller deems a district in fiscal stress.

Following the overview of the budget process, the focus of the presentation was on the operations and finance portion of the budget and focusing on Strategic Objective #6: Ensure the fiscal health of the District and to provide for a high quality learning environment." Dr. Harrison noted that the areas covered include: BOE expenses, superintendent's office and staff, business office staff, legal services, human resources, facilities and operations, central data processing, special items, (insurance, transportation, debt service, etc.) and noted amount to only 23% or $14,354,523, of the total budget. New considerations include:

- a part time District Clerk ($31,500) but coupled with other responsibilities such as Public Records Officer, Registration

- annual community-based surveys ($20,000)

- recycling supplies for Dows Lane/Middle School ($113,000)

- late bus transportation ($25,800)

In the area of facilities, considerations include:

- an additional maintenance mechanic with HVAC/plumbing expertise

- contractural maintenance for second window cleaning and rain gutter cleaning ($12,000)

- contractural preventative maintenance ($28,000)

- architect fees: capital project planning $32,000)

Mrs Stein noted that several of the expenditures would be lower as additional savings are realized/offset with having in-house maintenance and would benefit the health of the facilities. It was reiterated that what is being presented are the requests that administrators deemed important and are being put forth for the Board's consideration. No decisions have been made as to what will be included in the final proposed budget.

Other considerations are for funding a tax certiorari reserve in the amount of $400,000 as well as transfer of $200,000 to the capital fund to ensure the fiscal health of the district.

The individual budget areas were reviewed by category as noted above with detail of each area available. Mrs. Stein reviewed the facilities operations and maintenance budget lines requests and include equipment, capital repair projects as well building repair projects with the requests/needs for next year. Additionally, the Central Data Processing, special items, transportation, debt service, which is being reduced as some debt is retiring, and finally inter-fund transfers were reviewed. In summation, the total proposed budget for the areas addressed is $14,354,523, which represents an increase of $576,029 or a 4.2% over the current budget, with new considerations amounting to $749,526, or a reduction of $173,497 with no new considerations. Dr. Harrison concluded that the proposed budget does meet the needs of the District and is aligned with the Strategic Objectives. At the March 6th meeting, the instructional areas will be addressed.

Board members had the opportunity to ask for clarification and/or comment on particular items such as the number of FOIL requests that are received, projects that are not being included in the proposed maintenance list and if the some items will never be addressed, changes to transportation and the increased costs to private school/special education transportation, detail of debt service, funding of the capital fund and how the amount to include was determined, should the overall requests in the area of maintenance be increased or is what is included sufficient and responsible as there had been cuts in previous years. Board members noted their appreciation that the presentation was very clear and easy to follow and understand. Mr. Hanna noted that there are needs that are not included, such as garage for trucks and important to pay attention to what is being done in Albany with the need for advocacy and to reach out to legislatures to let them know of the impacts on school districts on decisions that might be made. Mrs. Stein noted that some of the considerations at the state level would impact the budget negatively but will be following very closely. Dr. Harrison noted his disappointment in advocacy in this area must again become involved ito ensure that our voices are heard in Albany.
