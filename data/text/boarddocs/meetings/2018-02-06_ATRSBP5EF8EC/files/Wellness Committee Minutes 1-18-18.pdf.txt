                                                   Irvington UFSD
                                  School Food Service/Wellness Committee Meeting

                                                    January 18, 2018

Present: Dr. Kristopher Harrison, Dr. Raina Kor, Carol Stein, Arthur McCormack, Gail Krieger, David Cohen, David Sottile,
Joyce Chapnick, Deborah Marinello, Maria Kashkin, Maura Gedid, David Alberts, Meg Tubman, Phil Whitney and Doreen
Morandi

Introductions/Overview:
The meeting opened with an introduction by Carol Stein, Assistant Superintendent for Business, as well as, all members
introducing themselves to the rest of the committee. There are 4 new parent representatives. Unfortunately 2 were
unable to be present with the rescheduled meeting. With new representation, the committee reviewed the previous
years’ work. Carol explained how the overall health and wellness initiatives play a large role in the District’s Mission
Statement. We distributed our policy, a presentation from last year that outlined the many wellness activities that are
in place. We also distributed the summary of the food services survey from 2015-16 and a presentation made
summarizing the survey. Some background items noted were:

       Dr. Harrison mentioned that the front page of the Enterprise has a photo and article about Middle/High School
        students participating in a meditation session shows the District’s commitment to wellness.
       Phil Whitney spoke about the survey that was conducted last year and that it addressed quality and timeliness
        of food offerings. He described that there are different levels of satisfaction depending on the age and school
        attended by the students and that there is a need for more participation in order to improve the overall Food
        Service program.
       Maria Kashkin said that the portions are too small or are the same for all students grade 6 to 12 so many
        students order food from outside. Dr. Raina Kor noted to the group that since there was not always a cafeteria
        in the high school, the culture was to order lunch from the outside.
       Carol noted that the portions and components are guided by the National School Lunch Program which the
        district participates in. https://www.fns.usda.gov/nslp/national-school-lunch-program-nslp
       Phil Whitney told the group that Chappaqua High School (only) does not participate in the National School Lunch
        Program and thus are not similarly constrained.

Carol provided an update on changes this year and last year to our Food Services program.

       The District has strengthened its “No Lunch Shaming” program. All students without sufficient funds may charge
        the daily meal (not just a substitute meal). For Dows Lane and Main Street students, parents get follow up and
        low balance alerts from the Business Office. For Middle and High School students, low balances are gently
        communicated to the students so they can tell their parents, along with business office follow-up. Students will
        be asked to put a snack back but this is common since several parents do not allow snacks for their children and
        thus wouldn’t be seen as shaming. The district does expect a higher accounts receivable and potential small
        write-off but we will manage this with frequent behind the scenes communication to parents about account
        balances.
       Main Street School-improved flow of line (Joyce Chapnick noted the line flow change has dramatically helped)
       Introduced yogurt based smoothies at the campus
       Introduced Action Station (students pick ingredients and food is cooked in front of them) which is very popular
       Expanded selections at the salad bar and daily soup

       All white chicken meat is now served in all chicken meals as a result of the survey at all schools
       New equipment at the campus allowing a slight redesign of the serving area to accommodate the action station

The committee next discussed some concerns.
     Deb Mariniello spoke about the concern parents have about snacks for the students at Dows Lane. She
       explained the procedure they have in place, a student must first get and eat lunch at least half their lunch before
       they can go back a second time for a snack. We are piloting this process to see how it fits in with the lunch
       period timing. We will also be adding a limit of one snack per student at Dows.
     Maria Kashkin and Phil Whitney wanted to know there were healthier snack options such as Cheerios instead of
       Fruit Loops or Pretzels in place of Doritos. Deb Mariniello thought the students would still buy a snack based on
       what is available to buy and is in favor of healthier snacks for sale.
     Phil Whitney suggested that better/different snack choice discussion to be added to an agenda for a future
       meeting.
     Phil Whitney asked about the possibility of pulling out of the National School Lunch program and the effect it
       would have on the lunch program. Carol said she would get the cost of the financial impact of dropping the
       program to the group.
     Carol spoke about the District’s Farm to Table Program and the need to promote it more to the parents. The
       new website will have a Food Service tab provide more details about the program to parents. Also add more
       “did you know” types of communications in weekly bulletins in the future.
     Meg Tubman would like the menu to show the calories of a meal so parents would know what their kids are
       eating.
     Phil Whitney wanted to know if it was possible to arrange a cafeteria facility tour for the parents on the
       committee.


Carol spoke about the upcoming bid for a Food Service company. We have reached the 5 year mark where our contract
must be rebid. The District has hired a Food Service Consultant (HMB Consulting) who will help develop the bid specs
with all the requirements from NYSED. The consultant will attend the March 23, 2018 Wellness Committee meeting and
explain what is allowed to be changed and what is not by NYSED-Child Nutrition Program.

       The timetable for the bid is as follows:
            o March 23 – consultant to attend next wellness committee meeting to assist in gathering requirements
                and meet with principal and administration late March
            o Mid April – draft copy of detailed specifications developed
            o Late April, early May – send specifications to NYS Bureau of Child Nutrition for approval
            o Late May – advertise and send out specifications for bidding
            o Mid June – bid opening
       Carol will develop a “Healthy Snack” google doc that will be open to the committee and allow the consultant to
        use doc as part of the process.
       Deb Mariniello suggested finding out what parents are willing to pay for a school lunch.


Next meeting March 23, 2018-10:15 a.m. in the CPR
