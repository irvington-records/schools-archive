Dear IHS and members of the Irvington Board of Education,

First, my family and I would like to thank you for allowing us to submit this application to create the Philip L. Friedman
Achievement Scholarship. It would come with a prize of $280, given to one senior each year.

IHS has long had a Philip Friedman Achievement Award, given annually to a deserving senior, but with no monetary prize.
This new scholarship would continue that tradition, but now with the money to go alongside.

A bit of background: Irvington High School thoughtfully inaugurated the Philip Friedman Achievement Award in the spring
of 1994, after my father’s death in late 1993. The prize money that went to students at the time, along with the award, was
generously donated by my father’s friends, family and colleagues at Irvington High School in the months following his
death. After that money ran out, the award remained as a certificate of merit only.
Also worth noting, the high school presented the original award in 1994 with a beautiful ceremony that included some of
my father's students. My family and I attended, and it was very moving.
The IHS students and faculty also dedicated their 1994 yearbook to my father, another incredible gesture.
With this modest scholarship, which we will fund, my family and I would like to give something back to the school and the
district for all the kindness shown.
My mother, sister and I are grateful that the Philip Friedman Achievement Award at IHS still exists today, and is given out,
as a certificate of merit, to a deserving senior each year. We ask that if this scholarship application is not accepted, the
Philip Friedman Achievement Award be allowed to continue, as it now stands, as a certificate of merit. Students who
receive this certificate feel a sense of pride, as I have learned in my research for this scholarship application. And I'm glad
about that.

*Here are answers to questions for the application to create the Philip L. Friedman Achievement Scholarship. We know
this process requires approval by the Irvington Board of Education. If there is a more formal application needed, I can fill
that out, as well.

1. Question: What is your scholarship about?
Answer: The Philip L. Friedman Achievement Scholarship will carry on the tradition of the existing Philip Friedman
Achievement Award, which has been given to one deserving IHS senior each year since 1994. It honors Philip Friedman,
who was an English teacher at IHS for 28 years, and who died of cancer on December 25, 1993. He loved his students
and his school, and was always searching for new ways to bring his passion for literature, language and travel (including
his beloved Greece and the United States Southwest) into the classroom.
The Philip L. Friedman Achievement Scholarship will have the same accompanying description as the current award:
This award is given to a senior who demonstrates a love of language and literature that is exemplified by high
academic achievement in English courses. The student who receives this award must exemplify kindness,
empathy, integrity and conscientiousness.

2. Q: When is this scholarship available to be awarded:
A: It would be awarded at the end of each school year, to one deserving IHS senior.

3. Q: Who can apply:
A: All IHS seniors who show an interest in English/literature courses.

4. Q: Qualifications for those who apply:
A: There would be no specific qualifications, but students who are serious about and show special interest in their English
courses, literature and language should be encouraged to apply.

5. Q: Who is determining the award within the school:
A: The faculty of the IHS English Department would select one worthy scholarship recipient at the end of each school
year from the applicants. The idea would be not to simply select the student with the highest grades, but to choose
someone who embodies the spirit of the scholarship. As it stands now, the award recipient is chosen by the faculty,
without an application process. For this scholarship, students will apply themselves. If no one applies but a
worthy recipient is identified, members of the English Department faculty could select a scholarship winner on their own.

6. Q: The number of awards and amount per awardee (award will be presented as a check):
A: Answer: One winner per year. The annual amount would be $280.

7. Q: Will you agree to fund this award annually or is this a one-time donation:
A: We agree to fund it. We will provide a five-year lump sum payment in advance. Any accrued interest would be used
toward the next cycle of payments. At the end of the fifth year, we would provide another five-year lump sum payment,

and we would continue doing that. Alexi Friedman would be custodian/administrator of the scholarship for the Friedman
family.

8. Q: You agree to allow the school board to hold the funds in a separate holding account for this scholarship:
A: Yes.

9. Q: Do you allow anyone to add additional monies to this scholarship fund by gift or cash equivalent to be held by the
Irvington School District Board of Education to be awarded in the future:
A: Yes. I would presume the extra money would be transferred into the Philip L. Friedman Achievement Scholarship
account, but the school board/IHS could decide that.
