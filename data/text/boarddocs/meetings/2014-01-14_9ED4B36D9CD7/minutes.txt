		5. Grades K-5 Common Core Learning Standards Presentation

		Presentation: 5.1 Grades K-5 Common Core Learning Standards (Presentation)

		Dr. Kor, Assistant Superintendent for Instruction/ Human Resources, Eileen Casey, Interim MSS Principal, Randy Lichtenwalner, Dows Lane Principal, Meg Benedetto, Dows Lane Assistant Principal, and Christine Rosner, Math Coach, presented an overview of the elementary implementation of the Common Core Learning Standards, stating that the CCLS is a set of learning standards with the focus on Math and ELA, but has branched out at the secondary level in Science and social studies. Ms. Casey referred to the six shifts in ELA/literacy within the CCLS and noted that there is increased use of informational texts with greater use of evidence in students' writing of the texts. Each grade level will build on the previous year's grade. ELA standards include reading, writing, speaking, listening and language. Student's writing is expected to delve deeper into the story and characters, with greater analysis of the story. Mr. Lichtenwalner reviewed the foundational skills that students will acquire Grades K-5. The expectations will be reading both informational and literary texts in equal amounts and by grades 7-12 that balance will be 70/30, noting that informational texts is more difficult to comprehend. One important shift is to “reading to learn” from “learning to read.” Ms. Benedetto reviewed the shifts that ensure students understand what is being read, the increase in the complexity of the texts, and the strategies employed to enable children to comprehend complex text. Ms. Benedetto also highlighted the shifts in using vocabulary to better understand the text by learning multiple meanings but also how to apply the words to a particular text.

		With regards to the Math Common Core, Ms. Casey highlighted the shifts along with the expectations for each grade level, with the work being narrow and deeper with all learning building year upon year. Students are gaining a deeper understanding of math concepts, learning more than just getting the right answer and applying their math facts throughout all their math operations.

		Ms. Rosner explained the fluency expected for each grade level, beginning in kindergarten to add/subtract within five and in grade 5 using multi-digit multiplication, including sprints to improve fluency, noting that children find them (sprints) to be fun and very active. Another shift is that language is now used to explain problems and diagrams are used to solve all types of word problems. Tape diagramming is also new with the CCLS whereby students draw exactly what they are reading in order to solve the problem.  The Math modules being used in the District have been developed by NYS/Engage New York. Within each module, there are four components utilized: fluency practice, application problems, concept development and student debrief, which is very important for teachers to understand what the children understand and what they don't know.

		Dr. Kor noted that CCLS and state assessments will be aligned, adding that professional development is critical to the successful implementation of the CCLS and she is very proud of the work taking place across the district between teacher, students and administrators.

		Mr. Montgomery stated that the presentation was informative and asked how the work done will be evaluated. Dr. Kor noted that there are more assessments, measured success and standards that are the guide to where the students did well and where they are struggling. Mr. Montgomery asked if the shift from literature to information texts might cause students to miss important texts. Dr. Kor noted that the importance of literature and the different types of writing will help to articulate and make a more thoughtful plan as to what needs to be accomplished.

		Mrs. Kashkin asked how students are being evaluated to determine their reading level. Fountas and Pinnell is still used to assess an individual's reading level in grades k-5. She also asked questions concerning vocabulary. Mr. Whitney asked how the six shifts intersect with the actual information being taught and at what point do the shifts actually cross over, i.e. will algebra be taught in earlier grades since the impact of the teaching shifts? Dr. Kor noted that the assessments are still being given at the appropriate grade level, such as algebra being assessed in the 8th or 9th grades even though the concepts are introduced at the elementary level. He also asked about the "pain points" for teachers’ learning curve. Ms. Casey noted that initially teachers were apprehensive that the material was too difficult for students but now view the students as being fully capable of the work, once they have been given the foundation. Ms. Rosner noted that a lot of time was spent in setting up the classroom culture to accommodate the different concepts, noting that there has been an increase in the stress level on the part of the staff. Mrs. Kerner asked how the teachers will handle the transitions will be to the middle school level and the high school level.

		12. Presentations/Discussions

		Discussion: 12.1 2014-15 Push-Ahead/Maintenance Budget (Presentation)

		Dr. Harrison noted that the purpose of a push ahead budget is to help set as well as provide a framework for future budget discussions. He spoke about the mandates imposed upon the District as well as the limitations that the tax cap imposes on the district while wanting to preserve and expand programs, provide professional development, and maintain the facilities. Dr. Harrison also pointed out that it must also be understood that a maintenance budget is based upon known data at the time and that figures could shift and change. Over the next several months, requests and priorities will be addressed after a component by component review and will then need to determine what the proposed budget will contain.

		Dr. Harrison reviewed the revenues, and using a calculation of a 2% increase in State Aid, the District might have an overall increase of $619,822 in revenues and using an estimate of $425,000 from the fund balance, there will need to be $49,605,568 levied to balance the budget, resulting in a $55,852,568 budget.

		Highlights of the push ahead budget including:

-
			Anticipated budget expenses = $55,852,568 or a budget to budget increase of 3.30% with major increases coming from salaries, NYS Employee Retirement and Teachers Retirement pensions as well as in health insurance. Most are aspects that cannot be controlled at this time.

-
			The maintenance budget of $49,605,568 would be $37,948 under the tax cap, however the final tax cap has not yet been provided to the District. Because the District has less debt, it works negatively on the impact of the tax cap of $82,444.

-
			New initiatives in 2013-14 included curricular enhancements, technology initiatives and investment in professional learning/teacher training and that the conversations will be similar in 2014-15 school.

		The next steps include presentations of component by component review, prioritization of needs, a look at the delivery of services, identification of possible savings as well as engaging the public in the conversations. There will be many opportunities for conversations among the community.

		Mrs. Kerner asked if we would use the $425,000 of the fund balance and Mrs. Miller noted that if there is a remainder in this year's budget, we could apply that towards next year's budget and she noted that we are at the maximum of 4% of unappropriated budget. Mrs. Kerner noted that she would look at the budget without the $425,000 so that the Board can make a decision as to how much to put in the budget from the fund balance and feels that it is best to balance a budget without using the fund balance in order to see what the real budget figures. The actual amount spent this year will be presented at each budget meeting and the Board will be better able to see how much will be left from this year's budget and what might be available to allocate to the 2014-15 budget.

		Mr. Montgomery asked about the increases in salaries and would like to see greater detail included in the salary lines. Mr. Montgomery also asked about the enrollment what impact declining enrollment would have on staffing. Dr. Harrison noted that a difference of 14 students at the high school would not impact staffing as it is an insignificant decrease over the many sections at the high school, however, there might be an impact in kindergarten if the enrollment remains low. He asked for a two year history of the fund balance and also too look at the current tax certiorari amounts. Mrs. Miller also explained that there is a budgeted item to cover a five year BAN and once that BAN is paid off, that money could be transferred to the tax certiorari budget line without any impact to the budget.
