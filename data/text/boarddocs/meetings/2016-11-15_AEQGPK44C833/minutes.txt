6. Presentations/Discussions

Presentation: 6.1 Overview of Rubicon Atlas

Dr. Kor provided a look at Rubicon Atlas, which will provide parents with link to each school's website and curriculum page. She demonstrated what parents will see when going to the link, and how to navigate the site to view the curriculum maps that are loaded into the system. She noted that the expectation is that every high school teacher upload at least content from one course as they could have up to four different courses which requires more time then at the elementary level. She provided examples of curriculum maps within a grade and subject which contain the unit, the Enduring Understanding, Essential Questions, Content Knowledge and skill set that are expected for a particular unit. Mrs. Palmieri asked if the definitions will be provided in communications to parents so that they better understand the maps and suggested adding a link to the NYS Learning Standards. Mrs. Hargraves asked what the parent will gain from the site and what will be the value. Dr. Kor believes that it is the Essential Questions which provides the skills and the bigger questions of a lesson that will help parents understand what their child is learning. In response to Mr. Whitney's question if sequencing can be viewed via Rubicon Atlas, Dr. Kor said that that  is not the point, but rather the power of the system lies in the reports and for vertical articulation (to look at curriculum across grade levels to see content, redundancy, skill sets achieved, etc.).

The Board appreciated the amount of work the teachers have undertaken to create the curriculum mapping. Dr. Harrison gave kudos to Raina Kor for all her work over the last four years to get this powerful program up and running.

Information: 6.2 Social Studies Learning Standards

Dr. Kor provided the scope as well as the complexities of the new social studies framework created by New York State. The anchor standards in social studies include writing standards and are the same found in the ELA standards, i.e. speaking, listening, and writing. The Inquiry Arc tries to integrate a set of interlocking and mutually reinforcing ideas that feature the dimensions of informed inquiry. The components of the learning standards framework are content specifications, key ideas and conceptual understandings, common core literacy skills and social studies practices, unifying themes and inquiry arc. She highlighted the social studies practices that were created based on the NYS learning Standards and how curriculum is developed to meet the standards. She shared the vertical articulation from grade to grade, noting that Rubicon Atlas will be helpful in writing the social studies curriculum for each grade level, building from one grade level to the next. She reviewed the grade level inquiry units of which there are six. She spoke of the changes at the middle school level, noting that there are fewer levels. In 2018 the new Global Regents will be rolled out, followed by the History Regents in 2019. Learning expectations for AP level courses will be steeped in higher level thinking as is  expected at the college level. She noted that the Tri-State Review of the District's social studies program and essential questions must be developed for the visitors who will explore the evidence to answer:

- To what extent do we use social studies content to support a curriculum that develops students' historical thinking skills?

- To what extent does our program result in student writing that demonstrates effective communication of thinking?

- To what extent does our program result in a defined progression of knowledge and skill for all students 6-12?

The Board thanked administration for the work that is being done and asked how they can help to continue this work. Michael Hanna believes that the work being done is helpful to parents in order for them to speak to their children and support their learning. Dave Graeber asked if there is thinking that assessments will begin to involve project-based work to ensure true inquiry work vs assessments focused on memorization of history facts. He also wants to be sure that there is also attention paid to the lower grades units link to deeper thinking as well.
