		The Board requested that the above resolution be removed from the Consent Agenda in order to have a brief explanation as  to the contents of the resolution.

		Dr. Harrison explained that the bond would represent authorized settlements and recognize they create significant challenges to budget. If the District were to pay for settlements in  one fell swoop it would require additional funds on top of the current budget --- looking at a tax rate of over 11.5%., which would not be palpable to the community. It would also require a reduction to the budget by $3 million in order to pay for settlements and the cuts would include mass eliminations to extra-curricular programs, arts, sports, etc. This leads to the option in the proposed budget to look at short term financing to pay for over five years approximately.

		Ms. Miller reviewed the resolution which identifies settlements already approved by the courts. Attorney provided for staggered payments over one or two years. General Finance law states that the Board must authorize serial bonds and can issue bond anticipation notes for one year in the full amount and renew for up to five years,. The proposal will be to pay down over five years (with the anticipation of $700,000 to will be included in subsequent recommended budgets over the next five years). It was also noted that the issuance of a serial bond provides flexibility in financing current and potential future settlements.

	13. Presentations/Discussions

		Discussion: 13.1 Review of Proposed 2013-14 Budget (Presentation)

		Dr. Harrison provided an overview of the Budget process began in mid December and explained that line by line expenditures and revenues were reviewed. Many opportunities were made available for the community to engage in the process and provide feedback. Throughout the discussions, budget constraints were outlined and often noted that there would be need to exceed tax cap to provide programs needed. It was also stated that Irvington had the lowest tax increases in the area and that the five year average tax increase was 2.55%, but came at a cost by having to eliminate many staff and other positions as well as reducing opportunities for students.  Both the Community and the Board has voiced their desire to invest in programs and resources and to exceed the tax cap as needed. Recommendations included: restoration of a .5 guidance position, restoration of AP at Dows Lane, restoring three 10 month clerical positions to 12 months, add electives at the high school, etc. The proposed budget focuses on children, classrooms and experiences for all students. Revenues will increase with increases in state aid, health reimbursements and sales tax.

		In summary, the budget to budget increase of $2.9 m, includes $700,000 for financing of a bond and is $1.2 over the tax levy cap. The impact is attributed to pensions, health costs and tax certs and are driving the increase. The estimated tax rate increase will be approximately $29.77 per thousand assessed value. Over the last five year, an average of 1.37% increase, the lowest in the area. The averaged assessed valued home will have an impact of $739 increase in their taxes. A tax calculator will be available on the website to calculate personal tax impact.
