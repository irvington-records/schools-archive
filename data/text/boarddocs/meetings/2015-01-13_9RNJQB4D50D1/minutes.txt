	10. Presentations/Discussions

	Presentation: 10.1 Districtwide Technology Planning (Presentation)

	Dr. Harrison noted that the presentation tonight is an update to the June 13, 2014 presentation on technology. He highlighted the progress made in the area of technology in our schools and shared where the District might go in the future, outlining the opportunities for program growth, vis a vis, District goals and the Technology Framework. Dr. Kor then highlighted the planning and development of the work being undertaken in the schools, including the Technology Leadership Team – ETDT -- consisting of a group of teachers, students, and administrators that meet to assess the status of the curriculum, instruction, assessment and professional development. The Technology Think Tank is another tool used in the planning and developing, which provides a broad range and level of expertise of its participants, consisting of parents, students, teachers and administrators.

	Dr. Kor reviewed the revised technology framework goals and noted that Goal 5, which concerned students bringing their own device, is now a sub-goal of Goal #1, mobile student centered computing and highlighted the action steps to be taken to both enrich curricula and building out the infrastructure that will support technology-rich learning. She also noted that classroom technology, software and hardware, should be designed to meet the needs of individual teachers.  The ISTE Standards were highlighted as it relates to the District’s Goal #6 --- Curriculum Integration and what would be observed in elementary or high school level classrooms, curriculum design and the outcome for students at each age-appropriate level. She provided further information on the STEM education - science, technology, engineering and mathematics using the Project Lead the Way program. The District has introduced curriculum units starting in third and fourth grade --- called LAUNCH K-5 using various modules such as stability and motion, sciences of flight, the human brain. The middle school program is called the Gateway Program, which includes modules on robotics and short 10 week exploratory options intended to tap students' interests. At the high school, the PLTW Engineering will continue and possibly branch off into specific areas, such as biomedical science. In order to expand student options, it is important to ensure that there is qualified staff available to teach such specialized courses and ultimately, provide students with sequences. Expansion of the high school's current offerings in areas such as computing are also being explored. There was discussion among Board members regarding this area of the curriculum, with Board members wanting to ensure that our current math and science curriculums are as strong as they need to be. Mr. Montgomery noted that he is more focused on the teaching of technology (STEM) rather than the use of technology to education and asked rhetorically, what is the point of focusing on STEM --- is it to equip students to go on to higher education in engineering or computer science or something else, as the answer to that question would help inform the right educational approach. He would like to see a serious discussion of both the current math teaching and science curricula to ensure that students are being prepared for a more technological future if that is what is needed. Knowing the different PLTW initiatives would be helpful, at the 3rd-4th grade and MS, including the amount of student time needed. Dr. Kor stated that they are 10 week programs, similar to the rotations utilized in the technology curriculum so that no additional time is needed. Mr. Whitney suggested that it might be interesting to see the offerings over multiple years, starting with today to see the progression of our programs.

	Mr. Lubinsky provided an overview of professional learning opportunities, including the building level committee (ETDT) that look at their specific needs. He also highlighted the rollout of the Google Apps for Education (GAFE), and related staff development including PD teams who will then be resources in their buildings, and --- Google Bootcamp --- taught by leaders from the other Quad village districts.

	Mr. Lubinsky reviewed the District's infrastructure, both hardware and software, including how they impact teaching and learning. He then reviewed the existing hardware, including the use of the Chrombooks by teachers to work with Google apps. He noted that the campus’ wireless is at saturation, with 100Mbps bandwidth, 1Gbps building-to-building, meaning that the system can cover anyone wishing to connect to the wireless network anywhere on the campus. Mr. Lubinsky addressed the expansion of the infrastructure which has approximate cost of $360,000 for the Technology Lease and installation of wireless for Dows and MSS, annual Smart Boards, projectors, printers, etc. for each building. The replacement cycle that the District previously utilized will be discontinued for the purchase of desktops as the future needs are not certain as wireless becomes available and the use of Chromebooks, etc. becomes more viable. Online testing, which will be mandated, will also require the use of wireless computing.

	Dr. Harrison noted that Irvington is eligible for approximately $277,000 through the State's Smart Schools grant but school districts have not yet received any further directives from the State but hopefully the monies could be applied to help offset the $360,000 in anticipated costs.

	There was conversation regarding the use of Chromebooks and how teachers access them and if used as a collaborative tool to share work using Google Apps. Chromebooks are heavily utilized and reserved but the applicable uses differs depending on subject. The goal is to have more available to be used at the lower grades as well.

	Questions were raised regarding the ISTE standards and the amount of change to teaching practices that are needed to meet the standards. Dr. Kor noted the expectation is that teachers are implementing certain standards as some areas are easier to address naturally and stressed that technology is not an add on but that technology has a place in the enhancement to learning. Mr. Montgomery believes that the learning is more important than the technology and to think of technology as a tool that is appropriate and useful but doesn't see that in the presentation.

	Mrs. Kerner stated that she sees technology in four distinct areas, professional development, teaching technology, integration of technology in teaching as well as infrastructure. Her concerns, as a parent, is that there are certain technology pieces being laid in (curriculum) but that it is at a cost of learning, and wonders if the teachers are getting the message that they must use technology to deliver instruction and if so, best to step back and evaluate how and when technology is used and ensure that it makes sense in a particular area or class. Dr. Harrison stressed that efforts have been to provide teachers with the tools and information to enrich learning only and sees teachers as being thankful. Mr. Whitney suggests that perhaps a districtwide or school wide poll would be helpful to determine what teachers are using, i,e. Chromebooks and report back to the Board so that they can feel that they have a better sense of what is happening in the classes.

	Mrs. Kashkin reported that two math teachers utilized Chromebooks and she saw enthusiasm from their students when used, but her concern is with oversight of differing experiences among the teachers and who has the final say as to teachers’ approach and to ensure that students are getting the same experiences. Mrs. Kerner also expressed concern that there needs to review of how instruction is being delivered, managed and if it is successful. Also discussed were current electives being offered and perhaps there is need to update what is offered. Mrs. Kerner would like to see what other courses might be offered instead of PLTW.

	In response to questions regarding the purchase of iPads and use of SmartBoards  Dr. Kor noted that technology use is being targeted, especially at the elementary level, and assessment is being made at each building to look at what teachers really need and what makes sense for their class. Mr. Lubinsky noted that there are no plans to build any new inventories but waiting to see what direction will be taken regarding the use of technology as a teaching tool. Mrs. Kerner also expressed concerns, from a budgetary point of view, for such initiatives as PLTW, and hopes that the rational, costs and comparisons would be helpful to budget discussions. Again, Board members stressed that when considering STEM as a whole the current math and science programs need to be reviewed to ensure that the curriculum and teaching is as strong as it needs to be as it all ties into the vision of what will be needed at the college level. Mr. Montgomery  asked if PTLW is the best program and what other programs were considered and why PTLW was chosen, expense, etc?  and if a pre-designed curriculum is needed or could we develop a more rigorous program.  Dr. Kor noted that PTLW has the most comprehensive program and the District’s experience has been positive, as has our students' experiences with the program. It is designed to align with the science curriculum and allows the current program to expand.

	The administration will provide the Board with responses and follow up to questions and concerns expressed by the Board regarding PTLW, electives, etc.
