Increasing Daily Student Attendance
The Evolution of the Irvington UFSD Reopening Plan




                                       February 23, 2021



                                                           1

The Irvington UFSD Mission & Vision

We believe that in order for our students to achieve their personal best and for the
District to attain its Mission and Vision, our students must be able to access their
learning in the most effective manner. This requires:

 ●   In-person attendance,

 ●   Interaction with their teachers, support staff and peers, and

 ●   Regular participation in co- and extracurricular activities.




                                                                                       2

Tenets for Local Decision Making
For the IUFSD to consider increasing student attendance:
1.   All staff must have access to receiving the COVID-19 vaccine*;
     a.   Those who choose to be vaccinated; A district can’t require one to be vaccinated
     b.   Presumed immunity among staff (both vaccine doses) - End of March

2.   The District must have unfettered access to NYS-sponsored surveillance
     testing; and
     a.   Initiate testing model in March with launch of high-risk athletics

3.   NYSDOH guidelines must be clariﬁed.


                  *County-sponsored vaccination clinic was facilitated for IUFSD staff on February 15, 2021

                                                                                                              3

Effect of the COVID-19 Pandemic on Students
The Irvington UFSD faculty and staff have done an impressive job of providing
for the needs of our students during the pandemic. However, we believe that
our students:

 ●   Need more time learning in-person or on-site, as it will result in deeper learning and
     will provide teachers with more opportunities to address students’ personal needs and
     assess their growth as learners;

 ●   Must resume social interactions with peers; and

 ●   Have opportunities to engage in enriching, school-based experiences that will support
     their emotional and physical well-being.




                                                                                              4

Exploring the Expansion of Student Attendance & Experiences
The Irvington UFSD “Reopening” Plan has served us well by:
 ●   Providing for in-person learning experiences through a hybrid model;
      ○   Grades K-1: Cohort-based attendance, 4 days/week, AM/PM Model
      ○   Grades 2-12: Cohort-based attendance, 2 days/week, M/T or Th/F Model
      ○   All students: Remote on Flex Wednesdays
      ○   Cohort C - 4 days/week; all special classes
            ■    Elementary ICT classes (students with IEPs) - 4 days/week - Feb 8, 2021

 ●   Offering 100% Remote Learning Option
      ○   ~14% of students enrolled; changes by grading period

 ●   Utilizing technology to support distance learning
 ●   Providing extensive professional development and collaboration on new learning models.
 ●   Demonstrating ﬂexibility by altering attendance calendar to maximize in-person learning
     during two of the weeks when there were snow days
 ●   Provided for the health of students and staff
      ○   No examples of in-school COVID-19 transmission
                                                                                               5

Need to Enhance the “Reopening” Plan
The District recognizes that it is time for the current student daily attendance
model to evolve to provide for more in-person learning. However, ﬁrst the
following must be considered:

 ●   Compliance with with all New York State Department of Health (NYSDOH), Westchester
     County Department of Health (WCDOH) and New York State Education Department
     (NYSED) requirements;

 ●   Continue to maintain safe and healthy learning environments;

 ●   Ability to provide essential stafﬁng to educate and supervise students; and

 ●   Obtain all equipment to support teaching and learning

 ●   Acquire all materials necessary to promote health and safety.



                                                                                          6

Who Has the Authority to Make Decisions Regarding
Daily Student Attendance?
While the Superintendent of Schools has the authority to recommend to the
Board of Education the plan by which students will attend school, the District
has the obligation to adhere to all NYSDOH, WCDOH and NYSED
requirements.

Speciﬁcally, the District must follow published directives pertaining to:
 ●   Social distancing
 ●   Mask wearing
 ●   Occupancy limitations
      ○   Classrooms, buildings, cafeterias, gymnasiums, etc.


           The guidelines from state agencies cannot be ignored
                                                                                 7

Conﬂicting Guidelines
School districts need to have a clear set of guidelines from which to develop smart, safe plans
for student attendance. The greatest single area of question is regarding social distancing.

“The CDC guidelines recommend six foot distancing in schools as the default — even though
the WHO, the American Academy Pediatrics, Harvard and Brown experts, and the
National Academies of Science, Engineering, and Medicine support 3-foot distancing
when masks are being used. Only the US promotes 6-foot distancing. JG Allen of the
Harvard Chan institute noted that six foot distanding “has a weak scientiﬁc basis, and others
concur.”1

Key question that must be answered:

Is six feet of social distancing with mask wearing necessary or is three feet acceptable?

On February 22nd, the WCDOH stated that they will support school districts to safely
implement their plans, regardless of the social distancing “standard” that is being applied.

Conﬂicting Guidelines
Current NYSDOH Guidelines, August 26, 2020:
“...appropriate social distancing means six feet of space in all directions between
individuals or use of appropriate physical barriers between individuals that do
not adversely affect air ﬂow, heating, cooling, or ventilation, or otherwise present
a health or safety risk.”2

Center for Disease Control (CDC) Guidance, February 12, 2021:
“Physical distancing (at least 6 feet) should be maximized to the greatest extent
possible. To ensure physical distancing, schools should establish policies and
implement structural interventions to promote physical distance of at least 6 feet
between people.”3

Considering How to Increase Student Attendance
With health and safety as the top priority, how might daily student
attendance be increased?
●   Phased-in approach
     ○   Reconsider use of Flex Wednesdays
     ○   Speciﬁc grade levels phased-in through a plan to achieve 100% daily attendance
     ○   Expand AM/PM attendance model
     ○   Other concepts to increase daily attendance from the current hybrid model
●   Transition directly to 100%
●   Maintain the 100% remote learning option
●   Establish a COVID-19 positivity rate threshold that would be adhered to in
    order for schools to remain open for in-person learning through an expanded
    daily attendance model
     ○   Guided by input from the Medical Director and WCDOH
 Recommendation to be informed through collaboration with the Reopening Steering Committee
