	3. Board of Education Recognition

	In recognition of Board of Education Recognition week, Dr. Harrison thanked the Board for their commitment to the children of the Irvington Schools. On behalf of the PTSA, co president Shari Wels, thanked the Board for their dedication to the community and students and presented each of the members with a cookie platter. On behalf of the Irvington Administrators Association, Mr. Sottile and Mr. Palermo,  spoke of the relationship and support that the Board provides to administration which is valued and appreciated. The Board was presented with blankets on behalf of the IAA. Doug McClure, on behalf of the IEF, also expressed the IEF's appreciation of the support that they are given from the Board and stated that a donation had been made in each of the members' names to the Friends of the Irvington Library. Dr. Harrison presented the Board with a book and thanked them for all their time and energy they give to the District.

	4. Superintendent's Report

-
		Dr. Harrison reported that the District's APPR waiver has been approved which allows the District to continue to negotiate with the faculty and administrators’ bargaining units to update the current APPR plan.

-
		The work on the MSS roof will begin this week.

-
		Since the District's Government Efficiency Plan demonstrated a 1% savings in the tax levy(and remained under the tax cap), the District's plan has been approved from the State. The result of which is that some Irvington residents will realize a tax rate rebateunder the State's Tax Freeze program. Information is posted on the District's webpage as well as Facebook.

-
		277 students sat for the PSATs on Wednesday, which contrasts with the 153 students who sat for the test in 2014.  HS administration will review and make recommendation as to whether to continue offering the test during the school day.

-
		Enrollment is trending higher with 1773 students enrolled vs 1753 enrolled in the spring.

-
		The fall Book Club will launch on November 17th, to discuss ‘The Gift of Failure’, with author, Jessica Lahey. The PTSA will host the event at a member's home. (Note, the date has been subsequently changed to Dec. 1)

-
		The second forum for strategic planning will be held on Thursday, October 29th at 1:00pm at Dows Lane Multi-purpose Room.

	Dr. Kor reported that during Friday's early release day, the faculty was focused on curriculum work which  then continued on Monday's Superintendent’s Conference Day. Initial feedback from teachers, via an online survey (80% responders thus far), reflected that most felt positive about having 1.5 days to work on curriculum and that progress is being made towards the goals. All administrators worked actively with teachers to think through curriculum goals based on the instructional model. In addition, a group of K-5 Response to Intervention (RTI) team members worked with Jim Wright, well-know expert in the area of intervention,  to develop a roll-out plan with goal to have cohesive plan in place by next September.

	5. Board Committee/Liaison Reports

	Report: 5.2 Curriculum Committee

	Mr. Hanna and Mr. Montgomery reported that the conversation at the Curriculum Committee meeting that was held on October 27th focused on curriculum design using Rubicon Atlas software. Committee also discussed the world language department’s work with a consultant and is looking at ways to improve the program. Mr. Montgomery also noted that the conversation began with defining what the purpose of the Curriculum Committee is and he stated that it is his belief that the administration views the committee as a way to educate two board members that will then lead to a better informed Board, not as a place to discuss/deliberate on curriculum matters. With respect to World Language, Mr. Montgomery believes that one of the issues is what the focus should be on world language and would like the Board included in any discussions. World Language will be addressed at a future Board meeting.

	Report: 5.3 B & G Committee

	Mrs. Palmieri reported that the committee discussed the:

-
		possible cross-walk at the high school;

-
		the Dows Lane modular unit - movement/replacement is more complicated due to storage and heating requirements, noting that any short term options would be as onerous as long term solutions, as both requires SED approval, possibly taking a year or so to gain approval, and an expense of $20,000-$30,000. The committee concluded that any short term solution is not viable at this time.

-
		Installation of air conditioning units in classrooms - this issue has been raised by community members, 40 classrooms were identified by administrators as desperately needing air conditioning, with an approximate cost of $62,500 for wiring, etc. and a cost of $36,000 for the actual units.

-
		Campus beautification program - the PTSA doesn't have the funds to support at this time, but has come up with a fundraising opportunity that would have community members buying commemorative bricks to be installed in a patio in the quad.

	Report: 5.4 Policy Committee

	Mrs. Palmieri reported that the committee began review of policies as part of the policy manual review process that is being undertaken. NYSSBA representative (Jessica Goldstein) was present to help with the organization of the process and identify where there are gaps and/or where language changes are required . Mr. Grados also noted that another purpose of the review is to streamline the policies and to use the NYSSBA representative to help with what is required by law or is strongly suggested.
