6. Presentations/Discussions

Information: 6.1 Principals' Update on Schools Goals Related to the Strategic Plan

Dr. Harrison stated that he is proud of the work that has evolved over the years. The school year started with the administrative team presenting a list of school goals and objectives, a mid-year a round table discussion with updates on and tonight’s end of the year summary which will meld the work together that is taking place over the schools. The focus is aligned with the mission of the district and the Theories of Action which is the root of all that is being done including curriculum, professional development, enriching learning experiences. Dr. Kor provided the backdrop of the Theories of Action with the administrators speaking to their building's work this year and stated that the fruits of the five years of curriculum work are now being seen in the work being done throughout the district with this year’s focus on continuing to develop instructional design viś a viś unit planners to ensure students in a grade are engaged in the same learning. She highlighted the stage 1 of the four learning goals (TMKS): T=transfer goals, M=making meaning; K=knowledge facts, definitions and S=discrete skills , all leading to the concept of a well-developed brain, thinking, metacognition, automaticity. Dr. Kor highlighted professional development activities in secondary and elementary levels, including work in the World Language and Math Departments at the secondary level and K-5 Math Coach, Fundations, Aimsweb, etc.

Principals and assistant principals then highlight the work that has taken place in their buildings in alignment with the Theories of Action. At Dows Lane, Deb Mariniello and Jackie Salcito spoke of accountable talk and student discussions, collaboration to support inquiry, and having students think about What Makes A Community, and to foster curiosity, creativity and inquiry through garden and outdoor classroom experiences, PLTW and math and literacy nights are some of the examples of the work taking place at Dows Lane

Joyce Chapnick provided examples that fulfill the goal of implementing instructional practices that elevate student thinking and understanding including fostering and supporting teacher practice. the integration of big ideas in and out of the classroom that drives the culture at MSS such as Global Citizenship, using math stations as a collaborative, overarching connections and PLTW, Stone Barns, Immigration Day, Clearwater Sail, etc. provide the experiential applications - all towards achieving Making Meaning that enrich student learning. Unit assessments are being revised to align with the essential questions.

David Sottile and Allyson Daley spoke to the work being done at the middle school, among them: essential questions, which are broad, open ended divergent questions; use of small advisory groups allows for the social emotional learning for students in 6th grade to take the rich thinking in core classes and apply to real-life scenarios;. expansion of PLTW with automation & robotics in Grade 8; focus of the world language program is on developing speaking proficiency and not so focused on writing structure. With respect to assessments, Mr. Sottile provided examples that are project-based and provide teachers the opportunity to assess meaning-making and transfer of information that students gained in a particular lesson and to ensure that students are developing higher level thinking.

David Cohen and Matt Samuelson provided an overview of the work taking place at the high school to elevate student thinking:  targeted work being done at Math through Metamorphosis to get students to think about math through habits of mind; use of interactive Science Notebooks, student presentations and student-developed essential questions.

Mr. Cohen spoke of the opportunities at the high school that impact transfer of knowledge through activities that take place out of the classroom, such as debating, the Innovation Fund opportunities, installing water fountains that will have a positive environmental impact, along with other areas that students are taking ownership of their education and are powerful experiences that highlight transfer. Also, taking place behind the scenes  is the work of developing a master schedule, anticipated to be rolled out in 2018-19.

Following, the Board and administrators discussed  how student's learning is assessed, if programs are achieving the desired goals, and how the curriculum is assessed to achieve optimal student learning or to determine where there is need for improvement in the delivery. Mr. Graeber believes it would be helpful for the Board to delve into what rigor actually means as it would be helpful for parents in determining their children's successes as well as their needs. Deb Hargraves suggested that in the future, presentations be done individually as this is the important work of the Board. Administrators will think about how to demonstrate what the instructional models really looks like.

Information: 6.2 Update on District Logo Design Competition

Catherine Palmieri reported that the student body was enthusiastic about having the opportunity to have a voice in the logo selection process with 17 designs being submitted. High School students Jordan Schwed and Dorian Barber spoke of the process in conducting a logo design competition to represent student and staff input. Of the 17 designs submitted, six were selected for students and staff to vote on their favorite. The Board will then make the final selection from the top three designs. The students thanked the Board of Ed for reaching out to the student body to give them the opportunity to have a voice in the selection and nice to see creativity of the students as well as to push for the democratic process.

Dr. Harrison thanked Jordan and Dorian for taking a leadership role in the process and thanked all the students who submitted designs with representative students from elementary through the high school. Some designs were from students who take Mr. DeBenedetto graphic design class.

The Board commented on the designs submitted, with the overall objective achieved with each final design, the process was a home run, and the students were involved in all levels of the contest and truly a school wide effort, K-12. Mr. Hanna noted that some of the designs could be adopted in the classrooms or schools but many are historical in their design. Mrs. Palmieri pointed out that designs often go through various modifications, so the next step will be for the designers of the winning logo to work with the professional designers to make any needed modifications.

Would like designer to work with the designers to make any modifications and if the Board is amenable to having the students work with the designers to make some modifications to the winning logo. Members of the Board agreed to select the 1st place logo. The students who created the design are: Alexandra Powell, Maya Castiblanco, Julia Herceg, Katherina Kovalevskaya and are in Mr. DeBenedetto’s class.

Information: 6.3 Possible Revisions to 2017-18 District Calendar

Dr. Harrison noted that there will be necessary changes to next year’s calendar if the State Education Department’s recent memorandum must be adhered to regarding the number of instructional hours that constitute a day.  He said that half days at the end of the year, the early release for kindergarten at the beginning of the year, as well as early release prior to a holiday will need to be eliminated.  The district’s legal counsel is reviewing the matter and an update will be provided following their determination.
