		10. Presentations/Discussions

		Discussion, Presentation:  10.1 Overview of Guidance Services (Presentation)

		Dr. Mosenthal provided a brief biography of Lester Eggleston and noted that Irvington was fortunate to find someone who had seven years of experience as Department Chair. Mr Eggleston works part time for Irvington and part time for BOCES, therefore he does not have a student case load. Since arriving in IRvington, Mr. Eggleston has mainstreamed processes and is developing a vision for the department.

		Mr. Eggleston reviewed the national model that was developed in response to a time when there was a call to eliminate guidance counselors from schools. There are four main components, or domains, of a counseling program:

-
			Foundation

-
			Delivery System

-
			Management System

-
			Accountability System

		Irvington's counselors are currently evaluating the present program and seeking ways to improve the current program. A Mission Statement has also been created.

		Mr. Eggleston reviewed what the college process looks like at IHS which begins with Junior Night. New programs this year include Naviance Night for Junior Parents and Family Connection Workshop for Juniors. The goal is to provide the students with tools to be involved in their process. Mr. Eggleston noted that 97% of this year's seniors have applied to college and there are currently acceptances at over 80 different colleges. There is approximately seven applications per students and approximately 26% have received early decision admission.

		Other department programs include but are not limited to freshman group meetings, 8th and 10th grade parent night, Administration of PLAN and PSAT's to over 150 students, as well as providing character building lessons for students.

		Personally, Mr. Eggleston has taken this year to learn about Irvington High and to revise the school profile, move towards electronic submission of transcripts for next fall as well as development of an internal department Master Calendar. Mr. Eggleston sees his role as leader, manager and advocate for the department. Mr. Eggleston reported that this year he has been identifying areas of greatest need and to evaluate current programs to ensure that programs are providing what students require.

		Mr. Grados noted that previous budgets has taken away from the Guidance Department and asked Mr. Eggleston if he feels the department is being given what is needed to run successfully and looks to the administration to answer that question during budget discussions.

		Mrs. Kerner asked about the college process beginning in the Junior year and not in 9th grade. Mr. Eggleston clarified that the actual school selection begins in junior year, but that in 9th grade learning and interest inventories are given to begin to have the students gain awareness of themselves. She also noted that the first goal is quite broad and asked about the specific objectives of that goal.

		Mr. Whitney asked what means will the department employ to evaluate their effectiveness and provide accountability to the administration, Board and parents. Mr.McCann noted that perhaps more reporting and/or engagement into the home would be valuable to parents, especially when students are moving onto college.

		Discussion:  10.2 2013-14 Budget Discussion (Presentation)

	      Dr. Harrison noted that prior to 2009, the district was expanding programs and growing but that beginning in 2009, programs, co-curricular and staffing was reduced, thus diminishing student experiences. He reviewed

	 Dr. Harrison then reviewed Level 1 Proposed Reductions which would provide for a proposed budget of $53,781,582 or a 4.34% tax rate increase. The Board's and parents who have been in attendance at Board Meetings, requested that more be invested in the schools and the Administration have proposed reinstating a full time Assistant Principal position at Dows Lane (currently a .5 position) as well as reinstating the proposed maintenance position that was recommended but then removed from the proposed budget. The cost of $52,000 would be partly offset by expected savings in eliminating the need to hire outside contractors for projects. The cost of these two additions total $115,838. Along with other adjustments made to the proposed budget, the total budget would be $53,897,420 which would be $1,263,474 or a budget to budget increase of 5.36%.

	On the revenue side of the proposed budget, it is expected to be $5,498,001 or an increase of $272,545, with the largest increases in the area of State Aid (+$128,291) and BOCES Aid (+109,790). It is important to keep in mind that  aid amounts could move higher or lower.

	In conclusion, the estimated tax rate change over the 2012-13 budget, with the new adjustments to the 2013-14 proposed budget, would be 4.59% with estimated tax increase on the average assessed value of $24,815 resulting in an approximate tax increase of $699. Dr. Harrison stressed that the proposed budget and adjustments will preserve Irvington's commitment to a high-quality education for every student and also demonstrates the commitment to fiscal prudence demonstrated by 1.31% budget growth over the past five years. Dr. Harrison again noted the challenges that the District faces in presenting a budget that continues to provide quality instruction and is fiscally responsible. He noted that the budget provides for $700,000 to pay for tax certiorari settlements and $1.3 million in pension and benefit costs. He believes that the proposed budget communicates the commitment in what the District values in education, adding that currently the enrollment is 1,800 and that the trend seems to be that enrollment is growing, with 107 students registered for Kindergarten and expected to increase.

	Dr. Harrison reminded the Board and the audience that the April 9th Board of Education meeting will be a critical meeting as the Board must communicate final directives to the administration with regards to the proposed budget. The Board must adopt the proposed budget at the April 23rd meeting
