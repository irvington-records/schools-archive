                   The Tax Cap is Flawed
                                    6


 The tax levy cap fails to consider basic operational needs
 It is reliant on inflation (CPI) and other factors that don’t reflect
  local needs & finances including:
   Tax certioraris

   Changes in enrollment

   Mandated SED programs

   Contractual obligations out of our control - such as increased health
    insurance costs, utility cost increases, BOCES fees
 The CPI used in the formula does not consider wage data, which
  is 70% of our budget
 Uncertainty of tax levy cap from year to year makes long range
  planning difficult

         Understanding the Tax Levy Cap
                                9



             The Tax Levy Cap IS NOT a flat 2% !

It is a formula that includes:
 Allowable CPI (inflation) as set by NYS Comptroller
 Tax base growth factor
 Changes in other items: capital debt; PILOTS (Payments in lieu
   of taxes); & excess increases in pensions

             Impact of PILOTs on Tax Cap
                                  11

 The tax levy cap formula adds prior year PILOT payments to the
  previous year levy and subtracts current year PILOT payments.

 For 2017-18, there are no PILOT payments expected, therefore, the
  subtraction of $689,000 from the 2016-17 PILOT does not occur.
  This contributes to the higher tax levy number of $1,501,275.

 However, the Revenue Budget previously included the PILOT and
  now is reduced by $689,000. So in essence, the tax levy revenue
  makes up for that reduced revenue.

 The PILOT property has now gone back to the total assessment roll
  and therefore will not impact the tax rate.

           Demystifying the Fund Balance
                                    19

 What is the fund balance?
   State allows 4% of budget to be retained by school districts
   End of year difference between expenses & revenue is transferred to
    fund balance or reserves where appropriate
 How is it used?
   To cover emergency (unbudgeted) necessities
   Used for cash flow from July 1 until October (when District receives
    tax revenue) to pay bills and salaries; reduces interest expense by not
    needing to borrow via a Tax Anticipation Note (TAN)
 What are the ramifications for using it?
   Not a sustainable source to balance budgets
   Could lower bond rating, resulting in higher interest rates
   District could receive “Fiscal Stress” designation from NYS
