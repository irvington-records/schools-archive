I. Background
Routhier Office hired by District to help identify opportunities to strengthen district
website and the salience of the Irvington Schools Brand. District team identified
broad project objectives such as improving communication consistency and
transparency across buildings and developing a more distinctive and overtly
academic school brand.

As a first step, Routhier Office team worked with District head of Technology to
conduct focus groups among faculty, parent and students to gain insight to support
site and brand objectives.

Description of Focus Groups: Five focus groups were held w/o December 5, 2016
to gain insight to inform the update of the District’s website and the positioning of
the Irvington School District “Brand.”. These included Teachers and Parents across
all 4 schools and High School Students in groups of 6-8, each lasting 40 - 60 minutes,
depending on availability. Each group consisted of prompts by the moderators to
stimulate general discussion, individual responses written confidentially, and
exercises for sub-groups of 2-3 people each.

In this document: Below you will find an executive summary of observations for

our work to come, followed by topline observations about each of the five groups,
including some quotes and paraphrases to illustrate main points of thinking.

II. Executive Summary
   ● CONSISTENCY OF GROUP FINDINGS - findings were broadly consistent
     across parent, faculty and student groups. Groups were insightful, yet not
     shocking or surprising.

   ● SITE UPDATE APPLAUDED - All supported the obvious and good idea of a
     site update. All expressed a level of pain, frustration with site as it currently
     stands. Tactical ideas for improvement were often similar across groups,
     sometimes specific to group, but presented no clash or irreconcilable aims,
     per se. Overall the site update seems a low risk project where any meaningful
     improvements will be welcomed.

   ● CURRENT SITE TOLERATED - Website is tolerated as an unpleasant
     necessity rather than truly useful resource. Most constituents have a
     resigned expectation and accommodation to a status quo unacceptable in the
     rest of their digital lives. Cost to play of information accuracy, completeness,
     consistency, current-ness, and overall utility falls short of delivery they’re
     used to in the wider digital world. Easier, faster, intuitive is in universal
     demand. The current site has parents feeling less ‘connected’ to the district
     and their kids than they’d like. This disconnectedness has emotional and
     rational parts.
   ● DISTRICT COMMUNICATION - Parents think Schools have strong intention
     to keep them well informed. Site does not deliver on that intention; weekly
     principal and superintendent messages do.
   ● AN INCREASING LIABILITY: Younger parents, faculty and high school
     students share an expectation of digital technology that provides the
     straightest, fastest line between A and B. This is a largely unseen liability
     right now. It will increase across time as older parents age out of the system
     and younger newcomers thinking of moving to the district are increasingly
     digitally fluent or native. (“The first place a new person looks is the
     website.”)
   ● ‘IRVINGTON-NESS’ NOT REFLECTED - Pride in Irvington Schools is widely
     shared. The pride is partly informed by a strong sense of community and

    website.
