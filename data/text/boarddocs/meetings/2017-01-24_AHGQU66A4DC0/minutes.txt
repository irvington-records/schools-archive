6. Presentations/Discussions

Presentation: 6.1 2017-18 Push Ahead Budget (Presentation)

Dr. Harrison spoke to the politics impacting education and local control, such as government mandates without funding, i.e. lead testing, special education, etc. Foundation Aid remains underfunded and the proposal from NYSED doesn't factor in surburban school districts, and the tax cap levy negatively impacts the district. Ms. Stein noted that Foundation Aid is based on a formula and that the bottom line is that it replaced different types of aid and Irvington has lost $8.2 million since 2007 as we are only receiving 60% of aid due. Currently, the Governor is proposing to eliminate the formula and it is speculated that it will favor schools with higher poverty levels and threatens year-to-year sustainability.

Dr. Harrison spoke to having to balance enhancing and expanding programs, dealing with increased poverty, noting that free and reduced lunch has increased from 3% to 9% of the student body, and an increase in students with social and emotional needs with fiscal responsibility to the community. With the challenges we are facing, the tax cap fails to consider the changing needs of our district. In addition, the District continues to deal with tax certioraries which drain funds from the district. Dr. Harrison reviewed the recent history of the District's budgets where prior to 2013-14, there were large scale reductions and programs eliminations but that in 2013-14 the sitting Board voted to overrun the tax cap to enhance student experiences and have since been investing in programs and opportunities for students. A chart was provided illustrating the expenditures over the past nine years as well as the expenditures projected with the push-ahead 2017-18 budget. Mrs. Stein reviewed the tax cap formula and stressed that it is not a 2%, and it is projected that next year's levy would be a 2.95% increase over last year. Concerns regarding the tax assessment information from the Greenburgh Tax Assessor's office were expressed along with the impact of PILOT payments of $689,000 that are subtracted from the revenue line. The tax cap will provide insufficient funds to enhance current programs, but Dr. Harrison again stressed that the numbers are still uncertain so the push-ahead budget is based on what is known today and is an estimate and preliminary.

Mrs Stein reviewed the push-ahead budget revenues and expenses resulting in a $333,920 reduction to stay within the cap. The total amount of the push-ahead budget is $59,240,728, compared to this year's budget of $58,330,000. It was noted that there are currently three expired contracts and one that will be expiring and these figures does not include any possible settlements, only the obligated step increases. Potential considerations to stay within the cap were reviewed and the majority would be to reduce staff, with additional cuts to maintenance of buildings and grounds, professional development, technology and increased use of the fund balance. Dr. Harrison addressed the ramifications of depleting the fund balance and noted that it is not a sustainable source to balance budgets. It also would put the district under "fiscal stress" which would impact the interest rates that we would be charged if you go below the allowable 4% of budget in your fund balance. Mrs. Stein would recommend the District not relying on using the fund balance and to move away from it.

Dr. Harrison highlighted the needs of the District to continue to move forward, such as in the area of technology, growth of programs, both academic and co-curricular, building maintenance and possible enrollment increases. Dr. Harrison posed the questions that the Board needs to determine, i.e. staying under the cap or if going over, how much, new programs vs program reductions, etc.

Mr. Whitney asked to have the District's estimated exposure on tax certs as the fund balance was used in payment. Ms. Stein noted that the District has approximately $6 million in possible refunds.

Discussion: 6.2 Discussion on Athletics Program (Presentation)

Mr. McCormack provided an in-depth review of the Athletic Department and believes this is a good opportunity to highlight the current program prior to discussing the athletics budget. He reviewed the philosophy of the department, with the goal to promote and ensure the value of education, school spirit and fair play. For a district of Irvington’s size, many opportunities are offered for our students to participate, and the participation rates for the Spring 2016, Fall 2016 and Winter 2016-17 sports were reviewed. There are 58 coaches for 56 teams, 60% of coaches are not Irvington staff, requirements for coaching and the hiring practices were highlighted. The presentation also included:

- goals and/or outcomes of each level

-

- captain, code of conduct, academic probation

- medical clearance

- role of the athletic trainer

- District's concussion testing and protocols

- equipment and uniforms

- booster clubs, school spirit, etc.

- Clerical support and communication tools

It was noted that a committee will be convened to look at current practices within the Athletic Department, along with the creation of a new mission statement or philosophy of the Athletic Department.

Mrs. Palmieri noted that letters have been sent by parents, along with other schools' protocols, forms, etc. and believes it would be a good model to follow. She also noted that the Activity Code of Conduct was last updated in 2010 and would like it to be made clearer as to penalties for certain infractions. Mrs. Hargraves also noted that the current activity code does not addresses specific consequences for captains. Dr. Harrison will meet with Artie to develop expected timelines and expected outcomes and will update Board next week.

7. Business of the Board

Action: 7.1 Leadership Statement to Legislators

Be It Resolved, that the Irvington Board of Education adopts the following statement to be recorded in the Official Minutes.

We, the Board of Education and Administrators of the Irvington School District, agree fundamentally and philosophically that quality education is an inalienable right for every student, and that parents should have a say in their children’s education.

We believe in the power of our current public educational system to address the needs of our children in the provision of free and appropriate public education for all students. We believe that traditional public education has been the hallmark of success for our students both locally and nationally, and that it has contributed to the many successes of our country.

We believe that vouchers, school choice programs, and charter schools are not a tenable long-term response to our educational challenges, and that the diverting of taxpayer monies away from public education is counterproductive to our educational objectives.

We believe proper funding and support of our current public system, with a continued focus on academic rigor, teacher excellence, global learning, and emotional wellness, is the right path to long-term success for all our children.

Therefore: Believing we must work together for the common good of our children and their future, we respectfully ask our State and Federal Representatives and Administrators to find common ground for our educational challenges and for the future success of our children. We look for a long-term vision that supports public education with appropriate and adequate funding and that enables all students, regardless of socioeconomic status, ability, race, gender, or background, to go forth and serve their community and nation at large.

Motion by David Graeber, second by Deborah Hargraves.
