         Ironically, Claudia Rodriguez never liked being the center of attention. If her birthday was
mentioned over the PA or her track coaching achievements were mentioned at a faculty meeting, Claudia
immediately shuffled the attention to someone or something else. However, center stage is where she was
most alive and happy. Claudia´s love for Irvington High School and her Bulldogs was second to none,
particularly each year when the Otto Awards came to life. Her desire to recognize kids and make sure
each knew how very important they were to all at the high school was the driving force behind her work,
both as a counselor and as the co-coordinator of the Otto Awards. Dressed as a cavewoman, a member of
the Spice Girls or doing the YMCA dance, Claudia enthusiastically sang Aretha Franklin’s “Respect” as
the audience rolled in laughter and cheer. After creating a script, naming the stage crew, identifying kids
to be the pit crew and music makers, forming a costume crew, ordering awards, rounding up artists to
decorate the entire theater, only then was Claudia ready…..ready to watch student acts audition, teachers
scramble for the best acts and costumes, accept calls from parents who eagerly awaited the sales of tickets
for their Friday family entertainment. Claudia manned all operations with a smile and utmost joy. Her
sidekick, Sara Lake-Garcia and she would spend hours revising their plans to ensure inclusivity and fun
for all. This was Claudia’s special gift. She found the right opportunity to make everyone feel welcome
and loved. A student who did not like theater acts? That student was assigned ticket sale duty at the door.
A teacher who was shy and not able to get himself on stage? He was given the task of drawing the curtain
between acts. The Otto Awards for many, many years signified fun in our school community, a
togetherness that only genuine love and care can create and carry on through so many years. This was not
about the best act, the greatest number of awards, the loudest applause. Instead, the Otto Awards were
Claudia’s time to give back to the students, to recognize our family bond through the hundreds of
students, staff, teachers, and administrators who not only helped each year, but eagerly awaited the next
when one show was finished and the final curtain was drawn.
         It is our desire to honor Claudia in a very special way and we ask to do so, that the Theater
Dressing Area be named the Claudia Rodriguez Dressing Room. Through this dedication, it is our hope
that when people enter the theater for years to come, they will remember the fun loving soul that Claudia
was, the love for bringing the school community together that she shared and the utmost gift she gave
everyone in Irvington - her heart and the joy within it.
         Funding for this incredible opportunity to honor our friend Claudia will be provided by private
donations from our colleagues at the high school. We would like to provide alphabet letters similar to
those in the art wing, library and music wing, all of which are named after former friends/colleagues. We
will also provide a framed collage of pictures from past Otto Award celebrations to hang within the room
or perhaps a piece of student artwork, both of which would also be funded through private
donations/voluntary giving.

Thank you for your consideration.
