	Discussion: 4.2 Data Dashboard

	As has been stated at prior meetings, a parent portal is required by the Race to the Top (RTTT) initiative. The deadline for District’s to choose a portal is October 31st.  Over the past several meetings, Dr. Harrison discussed with the Board to consider opting out of RTTT, which would then eliminate the requirement to select one of their selected vendors and to use the existing student program, eSchool Plus.

	Dr. Harrison noted that there has been limited access with the SED to discuss this issue. There has been a request from Superintendents in this area to have conversations with SED as to what their intentions are as there are many gray areas surrounding this issue. There will be a meeting on October 24th where it is hoped that more direction will be provided. A large number of comparable districts to Irvington are not opting to participate with the initiative. It is not required by law and it is an underfunded mandate, and there are security concerns surrounding personal information being provided to outside organizations, i.e. demographics of students, etc. as well as the information being used to market to students and parents. There is also debate on who would own the information that is being disclosed.

	Of concern is the financial implications associated with this mandate, however, revisions to the initial costs appear to be lower, and appear that at year three costs could be upwards of $15,000 per year which might impact budget decisions. Another concern expressed by Dr. Harrison is the impact on our current staff and an increase to their responsibilities. eSchool Plus could be built to include more testing information and more information to student and parents. Dr. Harrison noted that District’s will still be required to carry out the mandates.

	Mrs. Kerner stated that the District had originally opted out of the RTTT but when the State was awarded funding, the District did decide apply in order to take advantage of some funding. She also asked that we compare what eSchool Plus can provide versus what the other vendors' products’ can deliver. Dr. Kor stressed that the products are not student information systems and that there is no connectivity between the systems. The parent portal would need to be populated with the data for parents to view. The Board members would like to hear the feedback from Dr. Harrison and the District's CIO following the October 24th meeting before making a decision.

	Dr. Harrison stressed that there will need to be balanced and that there will be a management costs associated as there is the potential to have three different systems, but perhaps there might be a program that encompasses all areas, parent, teacher and student data. It was requested that costs of our current systems, i.e. eSchool Plus and Edline be provided. It was also agreed that Edline is cumbersome to navigate. Mrs. Kerner would like assurances that Irvington would not lose out on any future funding that might be provided to schools by opting out of RTT at this time.

	Information: 5.2 Long-Range Planning Committee Update

	Mr. Montgomery reported that he and Mr. Whitney have been engaged in reviewing proposals and samples of reports and plans developed and as well as conference calling with three possible firms to provide strategic planning to the District: Cambridge, PLC and the ECRA group and have concluded that ECRA would be best to provide a professional effort which would include initial conversations with the Board and Administration, a survey, analysis of test scores and coordination of focus groups that would include students, parents, and staff. Mr. Montgomery also noted that he is not sure that the test data analysis will provide much information as Irvington is not a very diverse district to do a statistical analysis. A strategic plan and a set of objectives would be realized at the end of the process. The benefit of hiring a consulting firm would be the overseeing the process, putting a timeline on the process as well as a structure to get input from the stakeholders. Of concern to Mr. Montgomery is that the output could be watered down as you will get a variety of answers from the stakeholders and it wouldn’t get us to answer curriculum, fields or other issues we are facing. Mr. Whitney said he feels that if it is a democratic process and if the District is formally asking people to contribute to the process, his feeling is that a third party is necessary to fulfill the work. The alternate is to talk about the strategic plan and goals and initiatives at meetings but if we are looking forward to answering how to define the success matrix of the district by using a third party with their data focus and impartiality will provide a better chance to define the District's success measures as we do not have expertise in doing so. It is necessary to work hard to get the strategy and goals that will drive our District to a higher level. The cost would be $25,000 plus expenses.

	Mr. Montgomery noted that he is still skeptical that it is necessary to go through a complicated process to develop a set of objectives and that our a mission statement could be used to develop a set of goals around that and wouldn't need a consultant to work up a set of long-range plans as he doesn't believe that it needs to be that complicated for administration to perform.

	Dr. Harrison's recommendation would be to move forward with contracting with ECRA and establish an aggressive timeline to kick off the process and that everyone should have an influence in the process. Mrs. Kashkin stated that she would be in favor of going with a consultant as it would be impartial, engage all stakeholders and feels that this goal will be met as there is much to tackle this year. She also stated that without a consultant, the process might go on the back burner. Mrs. Kerner asked about when the process would be completed and that the plan would need to provide a road map for the District to move forward. In response to concerns about the depth of the report, Dr. Harrison stated that it is the District that will set the tone and process and if, as a District, we desire lofty, poignant goals, ECRA will see this through the process. Mr. Whitney noted that there is a burden to lead and show what we want and he has serious doubts that we could accomplish creating a strategic plan given the other obligations of the Board during the course of the year. Mrs. Kerner also agreed that to develop a strategic plan, a consultant is needed to steer the process. There was majority agreement to go forward with engaging a consultant.

	11. Presentations/Discussions

	Discussion: 11.1 2013-14 District and Board Goals

	Dr. Harrison noted that over the past two meetings, the Board has worked to develop meaningful goals that would have impact on students’ learning and have attempted to streamline the thinking of the Board of Education with three different goal areas: Curriculum & Instruction, Facilities and Long Range Planning. Dr. Harrison asked if the goals reflect the dialogue of the last month and if there are edits or modifications to them.

	Mrs. Kerner feels that some of the items under the Curriculum & Instructional Goal are expectations or operation and not a goal, and would like to see Instruction as its own bullet point as she sees it as the most important aspect of the District and is concerned with instructional quality. The Board then discussed linking curriculum with instruction or if they should be separate bullet points.

	In response to the comments by Mrs. Kerner and Mr. Montgomery questioning the need for curriculum mapping as a goal, Dr. Harrison respectfully answered that this is the foundation of the District and that at this point, the District does not have such a foundation and it is critical to the District. Dr. Kor stated that curriculum mapping supports instruction and builds in efficiencies which we do not currently have. Mr. Montgomery asked to know the costs of such an endeavor. Mrs. Kashkin believes that the mapping is critical and very often criticisms from parents concern the inconsistencies that occur across the grades and between classes. The members agreed to remove the goal regarding curriculum mapping. There were several other suggested changes to the Curriculum Goal, including the use of the word “adopt” vs “align” when referencing the Common Core Learning Standards; adding that a technology plan will be developed, and changes to the language regarding college preparedness and admissions. Mr. Whitney will rework the latter goal to give it more weight.

	Regarding the facilities goal, Mrs. Kerner noted that to evaluate the existing needs for the buildings and grounds is a function of District operations and feels that the goal should be about prioritizing what the needs are. It was suggested to add "an action" plan to the second bullet for clarification.

	Looking at the suggested edits from Mr. Montgomery for the long-range planning goal, the Board was in agreement with his edit: Review and refine the mission and vision of the District" and eliminate further wording.
