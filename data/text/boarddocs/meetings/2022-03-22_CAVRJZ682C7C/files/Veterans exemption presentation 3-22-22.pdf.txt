             Legislation Background

Section 458-a of the Real Property Tax Law was amended on
December 18, 2013, authorizing certain partial tax exemptions from
school taxes for eligible veterans on their primary residence located
within the School District. It has previously existed for other taxing
jurisdictions since 1984.

The Board of Education in each school district must vote and pass a
resolution to grant this veteran’s tax exemption.

The Board of Education can elect the basic exemption amount or
increase to a maximum allowable amount. To increase to a
maximum allowable amount, a second public hearing must be
held.

               Exemption Qualifications
• Veterans - who served during a period of war or who meet other
  qualifications.

• Combat Veterans – Veterans who served in war are entitled to the
  basic exemption plus an additional amount for combat.

• Disabled Veterans – can receive the basic exemption plus,
  combat exemption if entitled, plus the disabled veteran
  exemption.

• Gold Star Parent – the Board may elect to include a Gold Star
  Parent exemption for those parents who lost a child in the line of
  duty during a period of war.

           Implication of Exemption
Exemption dollars are borne by other taxpayers

What does that mean for Irvington:

  • Presently there are approximately 100 eligible properties;

  • Total School District Exemption at the minimum rate is
    ~$2,218,000

  • This equates to .075% of the assessed valuation that the tax
    levy is based on which would be allocated to the other
    residents of the Irvington School district causing their taxes to
    go up by that amount

  • This equates to an approximate increase of $16.60 of taxes
    annually for the average assessed home in Irvington

                    Exemption Options
What are the Board of Education options?

    • To approve the basic exemption for 2022 tax assessment year;

    • To hold a second public hearing and change the allowable
      exemption amounts which will be higher than the basic minimum;

    • To delay making a decision until a future year.*



*Currently school district decisions are irrevocable. The legislation allowed
 other municipalities the ability to change their decision in the future but
 they neglected to afford school districts that same option.

                            Eligibility
Eligible veterans are those who served during a period of war (e.g.,
Persian Gulf Conflict, Vietnam War, Korean War, World War II) or who
did not serve during a period of war but who received an expeditionary
medal from the Armed Forces, Navy or Marine Corps, or a Global War
on Terrorism Expeditionary Medal or who is a member of the reserves
and meets certain additional qualifications;

There is no statutorily prescribed minimum period of service
requirement;

The Veterans’ Exemption is permissive and requires affirmative action
by a Board of Education, by resolution, in order to provide the tax
exemption. Prior to approving the resolution, a public hearing must be
held with at least five days notice to the public in the legal notice
section of the designated newspaper(s).

                       Eligibility Continued
To be eligible for the exemption, the property must be owned by the veteran, the veteran’s
spouse, the un-remarried surviving spouse of the veteran or, if permitted by local option, a
Gold Star Parent. It also applies to a legal life tenant of the property. In the event the veteran
dies with no spouse, the property may qualify, if it had been the primary residence of the
qualified veteran prior to his or her death, title becomes vested in the dependent mother,
father or child under 21 years of age, and it remains their primary residence.

Primary residence – an owner can have only one primary residence. When a veteran has
more than one residence, factors considered are where he/she votes, length of time spent at
each place, nature and amount of personal property in each location and other conduct and
behavior evidencing which he/she considers to be his permanent home.

The property still qualifies if the veteran, un-remarried surviving spouse or Gold Star Parent is
absent from the property due to medical reasons or is institutionalized.

Only the residential portion of a large tract of land may qualify for the exemption.

A one, two or three family residence may be considered exclusively used for residential
purposes and the alternative veterans exemption may be granted to the entire structure.

                     Common Questions
1. If a homeowner is receiving the Alternative Veterans Exemption for another
taxing purpose (ex. town or county) do they need to submit an application to
receive the alternative veterans exemption for school taxes?
No. If a school district opts in to RPTL 458-a, a homeowner who is already
receiving the Alternative Veterans Exemption for another purpose does not
need to submit a new application to receive the exemption for school purposes.
The exemption may be granted on the basis of the veteran's original
application for the alternative veterans exemption.

2. If a homeowner is receiving an eligible funds exemption for another taxing
purpose, does that preclude that homeowner from receiving an alternative
veterans exemption for school taxes?
No. If a school district opts in to 458-a, a veteran may receive the Alternative
Veterans Exemption for school purposes even if the homeowner is receiving
the eligible funds exemption for another purpose. However, the homeowner
will need to submit an application to receive the Alternative Veterans
Exemption for school purposes (unless the veteran was receiving the
Alternative Veterans Exemption for another purpose, see question #1).

           Common Questions Continued

3. Can a school district select limits that are different from the limits set for
town purposes?
Yes. The limits that the school district selects will apply to all the
homeowners in the school district for school tax purposes;


4. By what date does a school district need to opt-in to 458-a?
Our recommendation is that the school district opt-in to 458-a on or before
the applicable taxable status date, generally May 1 of each calendar year.
