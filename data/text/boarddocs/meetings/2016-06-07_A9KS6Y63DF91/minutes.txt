Presentation: 6.2 Irvington Middle School Presentation (Presentation)

Principal Sottile spoke to the Theories of Action that are in play at the Middle School providing for rigorous, authentic learning, professional learning and reflection and feedback, with teaching for understanding at the core of all subjects and extra-curricular activities. Ms. Daley, assistant principal, spoke to instructional design which has been the emphasis of the curriculum mapping that all teachers in the District have been involved in over the last several years. Deep, complex understanding for students is through Understanding By Design (UbD) with the framework and lens to seek how to design curriculum by developing essential questions (EQ) to meet the goal of providing deep, complex understanding as well as enabling students to connect facts and not just facts in isolation.

In the area of technology, a high school level computer programming course has been introduced at the middle school this year and will be continued next year. The use of Google Classroom will be further implemented next year and brings together many of the tools that Google offers and further meets the goal of integrating technology in the lives of students. An exciting, authentic learning experience is the design and modeling course offered through the Gateway program of PLTW. The next level of PLTW will be automation and robotics that will be offered next year. Ms. Daley spoke of authentic learning experiences at the middle school such as the collaboration with the Jacob Burns Center thanks to the IEF for providing funding, such programs as Digital Storytelling and Reel Change offers authentic learning experiences, as does Socratic Seminar and accountable talk. Character building is commitment and focus for Grades K-12 in Irvington with five essential questions the provides the framework for the program. Two programs, character building facilitators (CBF), a partnership with MS and HS, with students apply to be a CDF and engage with adults in training sessions to push into middle and the Advisory Program which is new this year, are examples of the programs at the middle school. To meet the goal of teaching the whole child, extra-curricular opportunities are offered to middle school students to be sure that they feel connected to the school in an area of interest to the students, which often drives the design and birth of club opportunities.

Mr. Sottile and Ms. Daley touched upon the professional learning that happens in the building through monthly faculty meetings which focus on an particular initiatives. These meetings push all staff's thinking and build collective capacity. There are also study groups, led by teachers or administrators. Recently, teachers spent two days immersed in practices to allow children to think more deeply about texts, etc. and connect it to their writing. Ms. Daley spoke to the benefits of the Instructional Coach, Sara Rust, which has had positive impacts on the teachers' design of ELA units as well as assisting science and social studies teachers to enhance student writing skills.

Board members questions were addressed following the presentation tied to curriculum design, computer course sequences being offered, future plans for courses in mobile design, clubs, gender differences in particular classes, i.e. computer courses and studio arts.

Discussion: 6.4 Follow-up on Objectives in World Language

Dr. Kor noted that the work being undertaken by the World Language Department is a result of the Tri-States visit several years ago. The work being done Grades 6-12 is a shift to focus on a program that will have students speaking the language which is lacking in our current program, but not to lose the program's other focus. Other work in the Department includes benchmark assessments in place for 8th and 10th grade, looking to re-define the curriculum targets as to what areas students would be interested in speaking about, such as social media, and not necessarily cultural conversations, such as food, etc., a commitment to a parallel program, i.e. same themes in both Spanish and French or in any other language that might be offered in the future to ensure consistency across the languages. Another approach to build the language program is to assess our teachers’ skills. Another piece being explored is looking at the construct of naming courses as level 1, 2, etc. as opposed to naming the proficiency level and how to differentiate learning for students who have natural tenancy towards language. Lessons are being designed to embed greater speaking in classes and to ask teachers to speak mostly in the target language which needs to be reinforced to the teachers. Parents have expressed that their children are coming home speaking more in the language they are taking in school.

The recent Tri-States revisit looked at what the initial report suggested and examines the program today to see how the program has changed and evolved in the two years. Commendations were given for the parallel curriculum work, the implementation of the benchmark assessment and the overall commitment and collaborative work of the department in building the curriculum and looking at students as language learners. The Board and administrators discussed time constraints in providing a language program in lower grades but how opportunities might be provided.

Discussion: 6.5 2015-16 Fund Balance

At the end of each school year, the Board decides how to allocate any remaining funds for the school year. Historically, the District has maintained the 4% allowable in the fund balance and allocated remaining monies into the tax certiorari reserve. Information provided is just an estimate at this time due to changing expenses and liabilities:

4% Fund Balance into 2016-17          $2,333,300

Appropriated for 2016-17 budget        $   530,000

Est. Unassigned                                  $1,200,000

It is being recommended that between $150,000-$200,000 be allocated to the Capital Reserve Fund that was established last year and can be used, with voter approval, for expenses, such as turf replacement, again with voter approval.  As there is currently significant exposure in possible tax certs in the amount of $8 million as well as settled claims amounting in $500,000, it is being recommended that the balance be allocated (approximately $1,200,000) to the tax certiorari reserve. Another area Administration is seeking the Board’s input as to how they would like allocate remaining fund balance. Mr. Montgomery asked what the downside would be to place all monies into the tax cert reserve and if not expended, then transfer to the capital reserve fund. He believes that any funding to the capital reserve fund should be a budgeted item. Dr. Harrison believes that it is important to the district's fiscal long term health to fund the capital reserve fund for expenses that the District will face down the road. Mr. Whitney, Mr. Hanna and Ms. Palmieri expressed their preference to allocate the recommended $150,000 or $200,000 in the capital reserve fund with the remaining balance allocated to the tax cert fund. Mr. Montgomery would prefer to place all remaining money into the tax cert fund. The Board will be asked to vote on the distribution at its June 28th meeting.
