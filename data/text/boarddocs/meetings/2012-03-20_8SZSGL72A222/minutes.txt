	3. Presentations/Discussions

		Presentation:  3.1 Presentation on the District's GASB 45 (Report)- Mr. Ray Cerrone of BOCES Questar reported that he provided an overview of the GASB 45 as of July 1,2011 to the Audit Committee prior to the public meeting. The report is an actuarial valuation of post-retirement benefits. Mr. Cerrone reported that school districts are required to disclose its obligations for post retirement benefits, including pensions and health insurance, paid to district retirees. Irvington's accrued liability is $66,168,837 for approximately 406 active and retired employees. He noted that there is no budgetary impact at this time as payment is at the time benefits are due.

		Presentation:  3.2 Budget Task Force  (Presentation) by Transportation Subcommittee - Ms. Allison Gilbert, Mr. Michael Krieger and Mr. Howie Stern presented their analysis of the District's current transportation costs. It is was noted that projected transportation costs for the 2011-12 school year will be $1,831,539 (not including athletics and field trips) and that costs for the 2012-13 school year are expected to increase, in part due to the rebidding of transportation contracts. The sub-committee provided cost comparisons with the other members of the consortium, Hastings, Dobbs Ferry and Ardsley, noting that Irvington transports the greatest number of students to private and special education program (173 students).

		The sub-committee also analyzed actual ridership. The average utilization rate in 2011-12 is 43% of students eligible ride the buses. Dows Lane has greatest ridership --- 61% with lowest ridership at the high school --- 21%. The district pays for all eligible students, not actual ridership. Over last three years, there has been a decrease in actual ridership.

		The sub-committee provided several options for reducing transportation costs without requiring a referendum by voters: eliminate bus monitors ($100,000) as well as reducing one Dows Lane bus, from the current eight to seven buses for a savings of $80,000. It was noted that there would be additional time on the bus if one run is eliminated. Savings of $167,000 could be realized if both options were accepted by the Board of Education (the cost of the monitor is included in the $100,000).

		Options that would require voter approval were reviewed, such as increasing or maximizing state approved distances for transportation eligibility, but it was the opinion of the Board of Education that more time would be required to review such options, such as safety, length of runs, the impact of possible staggered start times at Dows Lane and Main Street School, etc. before any decisions would be made to present a referendum to the community. The Board will schedule meetings to discuss the matter in the fall of 2012.

		The Board thanked the sub-committee for their presentation and their time in gathering the data. It was the consensus of the Board to consider the possibility of eliminating monitors for the 2012-13 school year, but expressed concern for the safety of the students on buses without monitors. The Board asked Beverly Miller to contact the transportation coordinator, Joe Urbanowicz to get incident reports for the current school year as well as contact  the other quad districts who do not have monitors to ask about their experiences with incidents, etc. Mr. Urbanowicz will also confirm if there are cameras on each bus, as was promised. The Board also asked that Beverly Miller follow-up with Mr. Urbanowicz regarding the savings he reported would be realize by implementing cost efficiencies in private school transportation.

			The Board asked the administration to further explore the possible options further but will not hold up the budget adoption.

			Should savings be realized by implementing any of the options for the 2012-13, the Board can decide how best to utilize the savings.

		Information:  3.3 2012-13 Budget Discussion:Update of Proposed Budget (Presentation)

		Dr. Roelle provided follow up to the discussion at the March 6, 2012 meeting, i.e. class sizes for physical education, noting that high school section enrollments would remain close to the current years class sizes, and that World language class sizes would also have favorable ratios.

		Dr. Mosenthal, H.S. Principal, noted that there will be some class size increases as well as possible reductions in offerings; he noted that course selection is student driven as in the past. Dr. Mosenthal addressed the .5 guidance position. Next year the .5 would be a chairperson without a student load and the 30 students will be divided among the remaining three counselors for a student load of approximately 200. It is possible that students might not get their first choice due to scheduling conflicts or courses being closed out. The impact is not insurmountable.

		Dr. Roelle addressed new budget adjustments: addition of .4 FTE Elementary A.I.S. and .4 FTE Middle/High School "Floating" which is equivalent of two days. How the time is used will be determined as schedules are developed and would require Board approval. Ms. Miller addressed the fiscal implications of the .8 FTE ($66,400). To cover the expense in order to stay under the tax cap, it is recommended to reduce the  Asst. Supt. for Curriculum salary by $5,000 to $175,000, a delay start date for high school assistant principal along with other reductions to equipment, repairs capital projects budget lines, etc.

		The proposed budget remains at $51,156,000 with a tax rate increase of 3.01%. Ms. Miller noted that the District's assessed value as been reduced due to settled tax certioraris from $75,882.209 to $75,297,676 , a decrease of $17,139,061, which that alone requires an increase of $13.10 in the tax rate.

		Board deliberated on the topics presented and conducted a straw poll on the various items that were been discussed.

		Dr. Roelle noted that Dr. Harrison would like to have an Asst. Supt for Curriculum in place by July 2012. Dr. Roelle would like the Board's authorization to begin advertising for an anticipated position. Dr. Harrison will be involved in the interview and hiring process.

			Regarding the Tax Cap, members of the Board were aligned with presenting a budget that was under the tax cap for the 2012-13 budget, but it was noted that in the following year or two, it might not be possible without seriously impacting programs. With regard to the Assistant Superintendent for Curriculum and Personnel, Mr. Grados suggested reducing the salary an additional $15,000 to $160,000 and to use the money to address any overcrowding or sequencing issues at the high school. All members were in support of the position, with the exception of Robyne Camp who dissented, stating that although there were good reasons to have the position, i.e. APPR, Common Core Standards, elementary programs need work and is in favor of world language. It was also noted that the new superintendent, Dr. Harrison, has expressed his desire for the position and that he will be involved in the hiring. The Board was also in agreement with reinstating Grade 6 World Language as well as adding .6 floating sections at the high school/middle school.

			Dr. Roelle asked if the Board had further direction to the administration regarding the budget. All board members were in support of the proposed budget and had no further direction. The Board will vote to adopt the proposed budget at the April 3, 2012 meeting.

		Mr. Dawson thanked those who were concerned about his absence last week and noted that he was not feeling well during the last meeting and listened to a family who did not feel listened to. Mr. Dawson also noted that all members of the administration were involved in the development of the budget and was not done in isolation.

	5. Business of the Board

		Action:  5.1 Resolution for Mandate Relief

		Be it resolved by the Irvington School District that mandate relief must be addressed by the New York State Legislature as school districts implement the property tax levy limit. WHEREAS, New York State leads the nation in local property taxes, in large part because New York leads the nation in imposing unfunded state mandates upon our local municipalities and school districts; and WHEREAS, in response to New York’s property tax crisis, the Governor and members of the New York State Legislature enacted legislation imposing a limit on the annual growth of the local school tax levy; and WHEREAS, New York State school districts are under unprecedented funding shortfalls due to three years of frozen Foundation Aid, last year’s $1.2 billion aid cut, expiration of federal stimulus aid and revenue restrictions under the tax levy limit, which goes into effect for the 2012-13 school year, and WHEREAS, unfunded and underfunded state mandates continually increase the obligations and expenditures of local public school districts, and a property tax levy limit without significant mandate reform will inescapably lead to drastic cuts in essential local school district programs and services, as well as significant layoffs of school district employees, and in some cases outright insolvency, in New York State school districts; and WHEREAS, the cumulative impact of satisfying unfunded and underfunded mandates diverts local money from our school district’s top priority of student learning, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED, that we the members of the Board of Education of the Irvington School District join fellow school board members in Westchester and Putnam Counties in affirming the obligation of the Governor of the State of New York and the members of the New York State Senate and New York State Assembly, to reform mandates that are unsound, unduly burdensome or costly, and thereby lead to high property taxes in New York. BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED that, in accordance with Article 24, Executive Law §666, which establishes a Mandate Relief Council, the Irvington School District asks, by resolution of its governing body, that the Mandate Relief Council review the following mandate to determine whether the specific statute, regulation, rule or order of state government is an unfunded mandate or is otherwise unsound, unduly burdensome or costly so as to require that it be eliminated or reformed: Align NYS Special Education Laws with Federal Laws;

		BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED that a copy of this resolution be forwarded to the Governor, Senate Majority and Minority Leaders, Assembly Speaker and Minority Leader, and the members of the State Senate and Assembly representing Westchester and Putnam Counties.

		Motion by Robert Grados, second by James McCann.

		Final Resolution: Motion Carries

		Yes: Robyne Camp, John Dawson, Robert Grados, James McCann, Phil Whitney
