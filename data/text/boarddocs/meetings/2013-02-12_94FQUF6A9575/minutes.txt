	7. Presentations/Discussions

		Discussion: 7.1 Review Options for 2013-14 School Year Calendar (Presentation)

		Dr. Harrison presented a brief Powerpoint presentation regarding the calendar development process and noted that NY has interesting twists and poses some challenges. He also noted that when developing the 2013-14 calendar, administration looked to make improvements to the yearly calendar, such as establishing procedures for emergency closings, early release days, and better alignment of Superintendent Conference Days, as well as the early release days at the end of the year, adding that those days are necessary to meet the requirements of APPR, grading tests, etc. Contractural obligations must be taken into consideration as well when developing a calendar.

		The three options presented are:

		Option 1-Would bring in students on September 9th but there is concerns at the late start date and this scenario would bring in staff on the 3/4th. This option also eliminates the 1/2 day prior to the December holiday and having schools reopen January 2nd. The last official day of school is June 26th, and is designated by SED as a rating day, and is unusually late. Dr. Harrison pointed out that 180 student days are necessary and that four Superintendent Conference Days can also be used as student days. This builds in six snow days

		Option 2- Again, students start on the 9th and the major difference is that there is a full two weeks off in December, as there are concerns that there might be large student absences, noting that surrounding districts and BOCES are also recommending a two week vacation and also eliminating April 21st as provided in Option 1.This calendar provides for 184 days, with four snow days.

		Option 3 - Superintendent's preferred model, with staff coming in in August and students beginning on September 3rd, which would help students transition, two weeks in December, and allows for five built in snow days. The Board will move to approve the calendar at the second March meeting. Dr. Harrison stressed that instructionally, it is much more beneficial to bring the students back earlier and allowing for a full week of instruction when they return on the 9th.

		Mr.McCann asked about the possibility of scheduling the October 4th conference day for October 11th, making it a four day weekend. Dr. Harrison noted that administration saw value in having the conference day sooner rather than later in order to establish the teachers' focus earlier.

		The Superintendent stated that his recommendation would be Option 3.

		The Board appreciated the fact that there are eight emergency days built in and seemed to favor option 3. Mr. Whitney asked that the administration try to minimize the partial days as parents tend to find them disruptive. The three options will be posted on the District's website and the Board will be asked to approve a calendar at the second meeting in March.

		Discussion: 7.2 Budget Discussion: Revisions and Potential of Reductions (Presentations)

		Dr Harrison reviewed the scope of the work thus far done with regard to the budget beginning in December and concluding with the instructional, technology budgets as well as revenue projections.

		Questions from the February 5th meeting were addressed, such as how the district utilizes the Edith Winthrop Center which cost $17,090. Dr. Kor reported that 22 staff members received four training days then provided turnkey training to fellow teachers within their building. Dr. Harrison noted that this is cost neutral. The administration will provide a deeper presentation into the questions regarding staff development at a later meeting.

		Ms. Miller reported that the Arts in Education program which is administered through PW BOCES for programs such as the IEF's funding of the Sloop Clearwater trip are reduced but again reported that the District does not receive the revenue until the next year.

		Dr. Harrison highlighted the cost escalations that are out of the Board's control, such as pensions, health benefits and tax certioraris. The administration did revise the proposed budget and it is approximately 1% or $502,661 less, totaling $53,725,762 or a 5.02% increase. Mr. Grados commended the administration in finding 1% in reductions but questioned why bus monitors are not on the list for a savings of $100,000 which could be used elsewhere in the schools. Dr. Harrison pointed out that the revisions represented a first pass at addressing the challenges and that over the next several meetings other creative reductions will be discussed between the administration, the Board and the community.

		Mr. McCann expressed concerns about the self-funding of workers' compensation which could prove detrimental to the District in the case of a catastrophic accident. Ms. Miller reported that there is also a catastrophic policy and an additional $40,000 would be added in a reserve fund. Although a good idea, Mr. McCann would like to hear from the insurance carrier.

		Mrs. Kerner stated that she is uncomfortable with the elimination of the .4 social studies and .2 FTE French and would not want to look to the classrooms for reductions but rather must look to enhance programs, feeling strongly about the students in the classroom, the vision of where Board wants the district to be in three years along with the importance of long term planning and discussions are critical to the health of the district.
