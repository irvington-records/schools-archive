	4. Business of the Board-I

		Discussion:  4.1 Board Discussion on Draft Policies: Non-School Sponsored Student Expression and School Sponsored Student Expression

		Mr. Grados noted that it is important to remember that it is a volunteer Board and may not always roll out as best it could and it is important to remember that the Board has a fiduciary responsibility to the district and that the community would want the Board to consider what is in the best interest of District. This is the fourth meeting that the policies have been discussed, and has received both pro and con communication from the community.

		It was the consensus of the Board to not pursue the adoption of the Non-School Sponsored Student Expression and School Sponsored Student Expression policies as the Board members believe that the Code of Conduct adequately covers such matters. In addition, it was noted that the draft policies were too broadly written and vague. The Board also touched upon the role of the policy committee and it was suggested that time be set aside to discuss the role of the policy committee, especially when new policies are being considered. Mrs. Kashkin and Mr. Grados,sit on the Policy Committee and both agree that they do not see the role of the committee to decide which policies to move forward to the entire Board for consideration or reject in committee, but rather, as Mr. Grados noted, to prepare them for the entire Board to review, and he doesn't personally think that two members should be able to "kibash a policy in committee." It was also speculated by Mr. Montgomery if a policy committee is needed as it is the administration who introduces policies and it is the responsibility of the whole board to approve. Mrs. Kerner suggested that perhaps a public discussion take place before drafting a particular policy and based on initial discussion, the Board can decide to move a policy forward or not.

	11. Presentations/Discussions

		Discussion:  11.1 2013-14 District and Board of Education Goals (Presentation)

		Dr. Harrison stated that tonight's discussion was Part II of a conversation that began on September 24th to develop the 2013-14 district goals and as part of the crafting of goals, it is important to identify the types of experiences we want for the District, and to ask questions such as what are the needs of students and if our programs meet their needs? Dr. Harrison reviewed the Boards thoughts, themes and questions raised from the last meeting as well as review of last year's goals. Robyn Kerner noted that she believes it is better to have more specific goals rather than broad goals as were last years. Mr. Montgomery believes that it would be helpful to have a few goals that are specific and defined and asked what can be committed to by the end of the year.

		In discussing what the curriculum and instruction goal should include, Mr. Whitney suggested a review of the curriculum to identify gaps in the continuum and to be clear as to areas to focus on as well as areas that would require additional expense, i.e. technology, foreign language, also need to be honest with where the weaknesses are in the current curriculum. Mrs. Kashkin noted that she has a unique perspective as a mother of twins and believes that the curriculum mapping will assist in closing the gaps in the continuum of learning K-12. Dr. Harrison agreed that the curriculum mapping will address any inconsistencies of experiences within a grade and address identified gaps but it is also going to provide focus on vertical alignment of curriculum to improve instruction K-12, a process that will span a few years. Mr. Montgomery would also want to assess how students are doing beyond Irvington in order to be sure we are giving students what is needed to succeed beyond Grade 12, and asked to develop a process to address what skills and aptitudes desired and then have tool to see what can be accomplished this year. The Board asked what aspect of mapping can be assessed at the end of the year. Dr. Kor noted that curriculum mapping is time consuming and possibly that by the end of the year might be possible to have a continuum of skills identified in a particular area. Dr. Kor further explained what curriculum mapping is and suggested having the Board see the complexity of the process, noting that once the mapping is completed it can then be reviewed and revised as needed. It was suggested to identify one or two areas that are in need and target them as specific goals for the school year. Communication to provide the Board and community with regular updates on the status of curriculum mapping, short term improvements being made with areas for growth and measures of progress was also requested A review to ensure efficiency of resources, i.e. the human infrastructure and teaching assignments was also noted suggested.

		With regards to the Long-Range planning, updating last year's goal and to putting some measurable objectives into the plan was discussed which would include the creation of a long range plan.

		To address the facilities goal, it was stated that the district will need to address the fields but the goal also should encompass all facilities, including the space utilization study as well as the IMS quad area, and not be too narrowly defined. The Board should be clear with the community that the fields are a part of the facilities as well as part of any long-range goal for the District's facilities.

		Dr. Harrison's said next challenge for the Board is how will the Board's goals relate to the District's goals and to determine the Board's commitment to supporting the goals towards the betterment of all students.

		Information:  11.2 School Safety Improvements (Presentation)

		Dr. Harrison explained that school safety became a major focus after the Newtown tragedy but that he also had initial concerns about safety after first walk through with the Interim Superintendent prior to his start in the District, especially due to the open campus atmosphere at the HS/MS. A thorough safety and security assessment was performed throughout the District, resulting in building plans and districtwide safety plans being updated. Drills are conducted on a regular basis with input from the Irvington Police and Fire Dept. Dr. Harrison reviewed the steps taken this year to increase security within the District:

-
			addition of trained and experienced security greeters at all buildings;

-
			all doors are now locked and visitors must present valid ID;

-
			implemented use of two-way radios; and

-
			installed electronic buzzer system at the MSS office and Dows Lane.

		The total cost of implementation of the above is approximately $153,793.02. Dr. Harrison noted that there will be continued focus on security including further review of the open campus concept at the MS/HS.

		In reply to Mr Whitney's query if judging where we are with safety on a scale from 1 - 10, where the District would fall, Dr. Harrison noted that the elementary schools would be an 8 but there are still concerns at the HS/MS campus as it is an open campus, outside food delivery and that it is difficult to control student traffic and other open entrances aside from the main entrances that have the security greeters.

		Mrs. Kerner wants to be cognizant that the drills can be disruptive and need to be mindful of the time involved in conducting drills. Mr Montgomery would like to better understand how protocols are evaluated and how do we build in outside assessment and not get set in routines. Dr. Harrison that the firm, CLPS  developed the District's plans and will provide ongoing assessment and communication as well engaging the Irvington Police and Irvington Fire Departments in continued dialogue.
