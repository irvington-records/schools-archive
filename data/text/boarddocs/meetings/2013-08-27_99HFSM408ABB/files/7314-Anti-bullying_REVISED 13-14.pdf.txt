SUBJECT: ANTI-BULLYING, HARRASSMENT AND HAZING POLICY

The Irvington Union Free School District is committed to providing a positive, safe and
supportive learning and working environment that promotes respect, dignity and equality and is
free from bullying, hazing, harassment and discrimination. The Board recognizes that
harassmentbullying, hazing, harassment, and discrimination are detrimental to student learning
and achievement. Such conduct not only interferes with the mission of the District to educate its
students and disrupts the operation of the schools but can also be harmful to students who are the
targets of this behavior as well as those students who witness or participate in such acts.

To this end, the Board condemns and strictly prohibits all forms of bullying, harassment, hazing
and discrimination by District employees or students on school grounds, school buses and at all
school-sponsored activities, programs and events, regardless of whether these activities,
programs and events are held on District property.

Definitions:

Pursuant to the Dignity for All Students Act (“DASA”) (Education Law §10-18) harassment
means the creation of a hostile environment by conduct or by verbal threats, intimidation or
abuse that (a) has or would have the effect of unreasonably and substantially interfering with a
student’s educational performance, opportunities or benefits, or mental, emotional or physical
wellbeing; (b) reasonably causes or would reasonably be expected to cause a student to fear for
his or her physical safety; (c) reasonably causes or would reasonably be expected to cause
physical injury or emotional harm to a student; or (d) occurs off school property and creates or
would foreseeably create a risk of substantial disruption within the school environment, where it
is foreseeable that the conduct, threats, intimidation or abuse might reach school property. The
harassing behavior may be based on any characteristic, including but not limited to a person’s
actual or perceived race, color, weight, national origin, ethnic group, religion, religious practice,
disability, sexual orientation, gender (including gender identity and expression) or sex. For
purposes of this definition, the terms “threats, intimidation or abuse” shall include verbal and
non-verbal actions.
       Bullying (often characterized by an imbalance of power, unwanted teasing, touching,
       threatening, intimidating, stalking, physical violence, theft, public humiliation,
       destruction of school or personal property, social exclusion including incitement and/or
       coercion, and rumor or spreading of falsehoods), cyberbullying (harassment through any
       form of electronic communication) and hazing (harassment in connection with induction,
       initiation or membership) are forms of harassment, which are also covered by this
       definition.

Discrimination includes the use of actual or perceived race, color, weight, national origin, ethnic
group, religion, religious practice, disability, sexual orientation, gender or sex as a basis for
treating another in a negative manner.

For the purpose of this policy, the term “harassment” is defined as the creation of a hostile
environment by conduct or by verbal threats, intimidation or abuse that has or would have the
effect of unreasonably and substantially interfering with a student’s educational performance,
opportunities or benefits or mental, emotional or physical well-being; or conduct, verbal threats,
intimidation or abuse that reasonably causes or would reasonably be expected to cause a student
to fear for his or her physical safety. The harassing behavior may be based on a person’s actual
or perceived race, color, weight, national origin, ethnic group, religion, religious practice,
disability, sex, sexual orientation, or gender identity or expression.

For the purposes of this policy, bullying includes systematic and intentional infliction of physical
harm or psychological distress on an individual or group of individuals. Bullying can encompass
unwanted purposeful, written, verbal, non-verbal, social or relational, or physical behavior that
has the potential to create long-term damage, cause discomfort or humiliation, or unreasonably
interfere with the individual’s school performance. Bullying may be premeditated or a sudden
activity. It may be subtle or easy to identify, done by one person or a group. Bullying is often
characterized by:

Prevention:

The District aims to foster an educational setting wherein all students are treated with respect and
dignity. The school setting provides an opportunity to teach children, and emphasize among
staff, that cooperation with and respect for others is a key District value. The District’s goal will
not only be aimed at prevention but also to decrease further incidents of bullying harassment and
discrimination and to help students build more supportive relationships with one another by
integrating the harassment and discriminationbullying prevention program into classroom
instruction. The District will create guidelines for training and will provide training to staff
members in accordance with state law and the Regulations of the Commissioner of Education, to
raise awareness and sensitivity to acts of bullying, harassment, hazing and discrimination, to
enable staff to prevent acts of bullying, harassment, hazing and discrimination and to provide
intervention and reporting.

The District will ensure that a course of instruction is provided in grades kindergarten through
twelve, which includes a component on civility, citizenship and character education. Such
component shall instruct students on principles of honesty, tolerance, personal responsibility,
respect for others, observance of laws and rules, courtesy, dignity and other traits which will
enhance the quality of their experiences in, and contributions to, the community. For purposes of
this policy “tolerance”, “respect for others”, and “dignity” shall include awareness of and
sensitivity to bullying, harassment, hazing and, discrimination and incivility in the relations of
people of different races, weights, national origins, ethnic groups, religions, religious practices,
mental or physical abilities, sexual orientations, genders and sexes, whether perceived or actual.
The District’s efforts to prevent instances of bullying, harassment, hazing and discrimination will
also include:

      Learning about and identifying the early warning signs and precursor behaviors that may
       lead to bullying, harassment, hazing and discrimination.
      Gathering information about bullying, harassment, hazing and discrimination at school
       directly from students.
      Establishing clear school wide and classroom rules about bullying, harassment, hazing
       and discrimination.
      Training adults in the school to respond sensitively and consistently to bullying,
       harassment, hazing and discrimination.

      Providing adequate adult supervision, particularly in less structured areas such as in the
       hallways, cafeteria and playground.
      Raising parental awareness and involvement in addressing problems.

Reporting and Investigating:

A student who believes that he/she has been bullied, harassed, hazed and/or discriminated
against or other students who have knowledge of bullying, harassingharassment, hazing and/or
discriminatory behavior must are expected to report it to a staff member. All District personnel
are responsible for reportingmust report all complaints of bullying, harassment, hazing and/or
discrimination that they receive from students as well as any instances of such behavior that they
are aware of, to the designated Dignity Act Coordinator or building principal of the school where
the incident occurred. When such conduct is of a sexual nature, as defined by the District’s
Sexual Harassment Policy, it must be reported to the designated Title IX Officer. In accordance
with state law the Board shall also appoint at least one staff member at each school building to be
designated as a Dignity Act Coordinator and be trained to handle human relations in the areas of
race, color, weight, national origin, ethnic group, religion, religious practice, disability, sexual
orientation, gender and sex.

At all times, complaints will be documented, tracked and handled in accordance with the
District’s policies and Code of Conduct, as applicable. Incidents will be included in the Violent
and Disruptive Incident Reporting (VADIR) system as required by the Regulations of the
Commissioner of Education but in no case less than on an annual basis.

In order for the Board Administration to effectively enforce this policy and to take prompt and
corrective measures it is essential that all victims and persons with knowledge of bullying,
harassment, hazing and/or discrimination report such behavior immediately. The District will
promptly and fully investigate all complaints, formal or informal, verbal or written. To the
extent possible, all complaints will be treated in a confidential manner, although limited
disclosure may be necessary to complete a thorough investigation.

Disciplinary Consequences:

While the focus of this policy is on prevention, bullying, harassingharassment, hazing and
discriminatory acts may still occur. In these cases, offenders will be given the clear message that
their actions are wrong and that the behavior must not reoccur. Offenders will receive in school
guidance in making positive choices in their relationships with others and if appropriate,
disciplinary action will be taken by the administration in accordance with the district’s Code of
Conduct. If the behavior rises to the level of criminal activity, law enforcement will be
contacted.

Non Retaliation:

All complainants and those who participate in the investigation of a complaint in conformity
with state law and District policies, who have acted reasonably and in good faith, have the right
to be free from retaliation of any kind. The District expressly prohibits any retaliation against

complainants, victims, witnesses and/or any individuals, who initiate, testify, participate or assist
in the investigation of any allegation or report of bullying, harassment, hazing and/ or
discrimination. District administrators will monitor participants in investigations and victims of
bullying, harassment, hazing and/ or discrimination to ensure that the behavior has ceased, no
retaliation has occurred and support or counseling has been afforded to the individuals involved,
as needed.

Dissemination, Monitoring and Review:

This policy, or a plain language summary, shall be published in student registration materials,
student, parent and employee handbooks, and posted on the District’s website.

Each year, as part of the annual review of the Code of Conduct, this policy will be reviewed to
assess its effectiveness and compliance with state and federal law. If changes are needed,
revisions will be recommended to the Board for its consideration.

In addition, the Board will receive the annual VADIR data report for each building and for the
District as a whole, with particular attention to the trends in the incidence of bullying,
harassment, hazing and discrimination. Based on the review of the data, the Board may consider
further action, including but not limited to modification of this policy and additional training.
