Technology Planning
This document represents a plan designed to launch both short- and long-range
planning for technology for the Irvington Union Free School District. The plan is
based on a framework that was developed to guide the decisions and next steps for
the District. The plan includes the following:
   ➢ Theories of Action and Goals, which reflect the current work of the District and
     the vision for future work and expectations for teaching and learning
   ➢ The structure for group representation and creation of future technology
     planning and documentation

Irvington UFSD Technology Goals


 ➢ Technology Goal #1: All learners will have equitable access to a range of digital
   resources appropriate for instructional goals and all learning styles, using an
   anytime, anywhere, supportive computing environment.

 ➢ Technology Goal #2: All learners will gain and develop foundational technology
   proficiency, media literacy skills, and the ability to practice digital citizenship
   across all grade levels and content areas.

 ➢ Technology Goal #3: All staff members will be provided with ongoing
   differentiated professional development targeted to their individual needs,
   grade level/content area with a continual focus on emerging and innovative
   technologies.


The design of this document assumes:
   ➢ The structure of the goals will provide a consistent approach including defined
      expectations.
   ➢ The plan will be reviewed and refined as needed, while maintaining
      District-wide goals.
   ➢ Each building will use the goals and expectations to create developmentally
      appropriate curriculum and instruction.

                         Irvington UFSD Strategic Plan Goals



To achieve its mission and vision, and to provide for the future of its students, the
Irvington Union Free School District will:

   1.   Provide students with a rigorous, comprehensive, enriched and diversified
        curricula that will prepare students to achieve their personal best, and will
        integrate technology in their learning.
   2. Encourage innovation, creativity and risk taking to inspire a dynamic learning
      environment.
   3. Foster the social and emotional growth of all students and promote a culture
      where students are active participants in society.
   4. Support educators through targeted professional learning and opportunities
      for collaboration.
   5. Strengthen local connections to and ownership of our schools.
   6. Ensure the fiscal health of the District and provide for a high-quality learning
        environment.

                              Theories of Action & Goals



Theory of Action
If we provide students with rigorous, authentic learning experiences rooted in a
comprehensive curriculum, then they will acquire the knowledge, skills and
dispositions of successful 21st Century learners that will prepare them to thrive in a
rapidly evolving global society.

Goal
In order to develop successful 21st Century learners that will be prepared to thrive in
a rapidly evolving global society, the Irvington School District will:

     ➢ Provide students with rigorous authentic, learning experiences.
     ➢ Develop a comprehensive curriculum that includes:
         ○ aligned and articulated content
         ○ defined learning outcomes
         ○ a balanced and systematic approach to assessment
         ○ 21st Century skills and dispositions - problem-solver, flexible thinker,
             collaborative learner, effective communicator, empathetic citizen, and
             self-reliant, reflective, creative risk-taker.

Theory of Action
If we value and foster the professional learning and growth of all members of our
school community, then we will (increase ownership and) build our collective
capacity to support student success.

Goal
In order to (increase ownership of our school community and) build our collective
capacity to support student success, the District will:
      ➢ Engage all members of our school community in the process of continuous
          improvement through the articulation of District goals.
      ➢ Involve all members of the school community in learning and developing
          the knowledge and skills to support student success.
      ➢ Provide educators with professional learning designed to support student
          growth.
      ➢ Provide all staff with professional learning to develop the skills necessary to
          meet District needs.

Theory of Action
If we engage in a systematic approach of classroom observation, collecting data and
offering feedback to one another, then evidence-based decisions will promote
reflective practice, inform instructional design and guide professional learning in
order to continuously improve/advance student achievement.

Goal
In order to continuously improve student achievement through professional
learning, reflective practice, and instructional design, the District will:
     ➢ Engage all K-12 instructional leaders in various approaches to classroom
       visitation to collect evidence of teaching and learning.
     ➢ Utilize the Instructional Rounds protocol as a tool to assess our progress
       toward our relevant theories of action and in addressing instructional goals.
     ➢ Use the Framework for Teaching as the benchmark for instructional best
       practices.
     ➢ Use the Annual Professional Performance Review as one indicator of
       student performance and teacher effectiveness.

District-Wide Technology Planning Committee (Formerly Steering Committee)
The mission of the District-Wide Technology Planning Committee is to develop a
comprehensive technology plan to represent the current needs and expectations of
the District K-12. This team of District staff will provide oversight of the plan through
regular review and revision. The team will include administrators, teachers, and
students and will be representative of grades K-12.

Plan Development
The team will develop this framework in detail including defining the application of
standards and expectations for all students and teachers K-12 based on the New York
State Computer Science and Digital Fluency Learning standards.

          ➢ Review recommendations made by the Model Schools Instructional

                                  Technology Goal #1

All learners will have equitable access to a range of digital resources appropriate for
instructional goals and all learning styles, using an anytime, anywhere, supportive
computing environment.

Action Steps
   ➢ Provide proper training and communication about available resources (to
      support equitable access)
   ➢ Continue to provide devices to all stakeholders to ensure equitable access
      including internet, home-based services, internal connections.
          ○ This includes replacement cycles for end of life devices and broken
             devices.
   ➢ Creation of a 3-year plan to accommodate requests and ensure a replacement
      cycle is ongoing.
   ➢ Research to ensure that digital resources and devices are continuing to meet
      needs, and are aligned to state laws (ex. NYS Education Law 2-d).
   ➢ Implement appropriate innovative technology (hardware and software) that
      meet educational goals, and are aligned to state laws (ex. NYS Education Law
      2-d).
   ➢ Increase communication/collaboration with community partners (vendors,
      LHRIC, etc.).
   ➢ Assess, refine, revise appropriate innovative technology (hardware and
      software) that meet educational goals, and are aligned to state laws (ex. NYS
      Education Law 2-d).

Assessment of Progress
Utilizing surveys including BrightBytes and Google Surveys, to identify the range of
equitable access to digital resources for all learners. Checkpoints will be scheduled
over the next three years (ongoing), as evidenced in our action plan. This data will be
used to make revisions and provide additional resources aligned to NYS Education
Law 2-d. Formative data will also be collected including student project-based
learning examples, teacher-created activities, and local assessments. Further, using
data from various sources including Classlink and anecdotal survey data, we will be
able to identify if our goal has been met, and make changes to our plan moving
forward. Additionally, annual surveys of students, staff and community members will
assess the use of technology and related instruction. Further, technology use will be
reflected in teacher and Department goal-setting. When gaps are identified with
home accessibly - whether devices or internet access - the District will bridge the
gap by providing necessary resources. This data will be tracked annually to inform
planning. We will know we accomplished this goal when we see a decrease in the
number of support tickets for issues related to internet connectivity, device reliability,
and access to resources.

                                  Technology Goal #2

All learners will gain and develop foundational technology proficiency, media literacy
skills, and the ability to practice digital citizenship across all grade levels and content
areas.

Action Steps
   ➢ Identify/Establish District-wide technology planning committee.
   ➢ Identify standards to be taught/use foundational skills survey.
   ➢ Identify resources to be used that align to NYS Education Law 2-d, shared list
      created for teachers.
   ➢ Complete an updated foundational curriculum technology needs assessment.
   ➢ Develop a scope/sequence.
   ➢ Use a method that allows for consistency across grade levels/content areas
      K-12, but rolls out over a 2-year period of time in small, manageable ways.
   ➢ Assess, refine, and revise scope and sequence for program and curricular
      effectiveness.

Assessment of Progress
Utilizing surveys including BrightBytes and Google Surveys, to identify foundational
technology proficiency, media literacy skills, and the ability to practice digital
citizenship across all grade levels and content areas. Checkpoints will be scheduled
over the next three years (ongoing), as evidenced in our action plan. This data will be
used to make revisions and provide additional resources aligned to NYS Education
Law 2-d. Formative data will also be collected including student work samples,
project-based learning experiences, student and teacher focus groups,
teacher-created activities, and local assessments. Further, there will be a review of
work across grade levels and content areas to ensure alignment with NYS Computer
Science and Digital Fluency Standards. Using data from student assessments, and
teacher-created assessments, we will be able to determine whether our students
have met this goal. Additionally, teacher surveys will be administered to identify
where additional support is needed. We will know that we have met this goal by
looking at evidence from student work examples. An example of a third-grader
showing competency might be the use of Google documents,and sharing and
collaborating in and outside of the classroom. We expect to see students’
application of these skills in typing proficiency, internet safety, and digital etiquette
across all grade levels.

                                Technology Goal #3

All staff members will be provided with ongoing differentiated professional
development targeted to their individual needs, grade level/content area with a
continual focus on emerging and innovative technologies.

Action Steps
   ➢ Anticipated hiring of an instructional technology coach (ITC) in 2023-24
      budget review process.
   ➢ Using local and regional partnerships for on-demand professional
      development/scheduled webinars/coaching.
   ➢ Cohort of teachers/administrators plan to create a curriculum aligned to
      Educational Technology using previously used needs assessment. Plans will
      be developed based on results.
   ➢ Develop a strategic plan that allows for consistency across grade levels/content
      areas K-12, but rolls out over a 2-year period of time in small, manageable ways.
   ➢ Assess, refine, and revise professional development offerings, courses, and
      implementation strategies.

Assessment of Progress
The Professional Development Plan Committee (PDPC) is committed to making the
needs of instructional technology a focus of District-wide professional development.
A needs assessment has been shared with teachers to identify areas of professional
development needs and to determine the current capacity of our educators. This
data will inform the planning process over the next three years. Professional
development will be delivered in a number of ways including:

   ➢ On-demand professional development using vendor partners including LHRIC
   ➢ 1:1 coaching by our Instructional Support Team
   ➢ Instructional Technology Coach (ongoing budget request).

As part of the PDPC committee, professional development will be evaluated on an
ongoing basis through exit surveys, interviews with teachers, and classroom
observations.

Ongoing evaluation of curriculum development and implementation will occur to
determine whether an adequate goal is being attained. When such evidence is not
observed, support will be provided to further develop curricula and offer professional
learning. We will know when we met this goal based on teacher feedback based on
the type and variety of professional learning opportunities, and how it met their
overall professional needs.
