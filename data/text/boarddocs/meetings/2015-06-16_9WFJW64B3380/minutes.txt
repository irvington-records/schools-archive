	5. Presentations/Discussions

	Discussion, Presentation: 5.1 Irvington High School Presentation

	Dave Cohen and Deb Mariniello shared the work that is taking place at the high school, noting that the focus is in building rigorous educational opportunities for students. As in the other schools' presentations, Mr. Cohen reviewed how the Theories of Action are embedded within the high school with deep and high levels of learning. The presentation was structured to provide an overview of the types of experiences that are provided to students, a robust academic curriculum throughout all content areas, and the sequence of courses that are provided for students grades 9 through 12, core classes, electives, college level courses as well as selectives and electives, especially beginning in 11th grade. Ms. Mariniello noted that the next generation standards for Science which will engage students in more activities and hands-on activities. She also noted that most students in Irvington take mathematics over the four years, with offerings in AP Calculus AB, AP Statistics and AP Computer Science. Also reviewed was the physical education program as well as the Arts, which also provides for advanced placement courses, including Project Lead The Way, and many visual arts opportunities and many opportunities in the area of music, which Mr. Cohen noted was very robust for a school district the size of Irvington.

	In measuring success at the high school level includes summative assessments including quizzes, unit exams, mid terms and finals, as well as Regents and SATs, formative assessments which include portfolios (AWEs), Google Classroom, closure/exit tickets, peer review, etc. Mr. Cohen summarized the data that was received from a survey of students (108 responded out of a possible total of 119) on the new 9th grade writing program, including but not limited to, what they felt was most improved through the program as a writer, what strategies would be carried forward, and if the feedback received would lead to greater awareness as a writer. Some of the open ended questions' comments on the experiences were shared: interdisciplinary writing experiences, more opportunities to explore grammar to lead to enhancements in their writing, more teacher conferencing and outlining. The analysis of the feedback will be provided to the teachers to enhance the teaching. The Board asked if they could receive all the students' comments as well as the entire survey.

	Ms. Mariniello spoke to the Bridges Program and thanked the Board, administration and the PTSA for their support of the program which provides students with a non-traditional class beyond the classroom, including a five week internship. She noted that Mr. Lent brought the program to a level that was exceptional for its first year.

Instructional coaching has also been a focus to enhance teachers' pedigogy and practice to better serve the students' learning.

	Following the presentation, Board members had the opportunity to ask questions and comment on the information provided. There were questions and discussion regarding the survey that students took regarding the new writing program. Mr. Whitney asked how the process of new course selection was undertaken, and he expressed his concerns with the survey. Dr. Kor noted that the purpose was to inform the administration’s planning and future development of the program. Mrs. Kashkin asked about the enhanced use of the Naviance Program and it is determined that all the correct information has been put in by students. Mr. Cohen noted that it is difficult to ensure that students are updating the information and working to create opportunities for seniors to work with the counselors to input all information in order it to be of use to future students.

	Mrs. Kerner commented on the Theory of Action that deals with rigor and the deeper levels of higher thinking and how that translates into teaching. She also asked about the previous conversations about the restrictiveness of scheduling and how that might be handled to provide flexibility in student's choices as they move through the grades. She also asked about the Science Research program and if there are steps being taken to tap into community expertise to strengthen the program and if there plans to provide more technology-based courses. She noted that it is important to get the community to provide feedback as to what might be best integrated into the high school program and what kind of district they want Irvington to be. With respect to Greek III, it will not be offered next year as it is run based on capacity and student interest, which for next year was low. There was discussion as to Irvington University for parents that incorporated the special education component and that administration will be looking at how to provide that topic either as a separate class during the program or perhaps going back to a stand-alone program. Mrs. Kerner concluded that it is important to look at ways to better communicate all that is going on at the high school.

	Mr. Montgomery asked about the resource constraint that prevents Greek being offered each year, Mr. Cohen said it is often teacher capacity as there might be more sections of Latin needed but also could be student capacity. Mr. Mongtomery stated that the Board should be informed of this and to make the decision to offer it or not. Mr. Montgomery asked about how decisions are made for AP offerings vs honors offerings and why AP Physics is not an option. His feeling is that this is a topic that should be explored to offer honors courses that would give more quality to the high school offerings. Mrs. Kashkin suggested that a survey be conducted for students to respond to see if there would be interest in a Honors course in History to follow AP History.

	Discussion: 5.2 Review of Technology Plan

	Dr. Harrison noted that there have been two formal presentations related to technology and this is the next step in the evolution of the plan that must be approved by the Board as per SED in order to be eligible for the Smart Schools Grant.

	Dr. Kor noted that the framework was shared last year but that in the fall of this year there was a four-day work session among staff and administration and met for 5 day sessions to flesh out the goals and narrowed the goals to seven goals and reflect the work that is being done and the work that is felt important to be done. The plan begins with the Theories of Action, which are global and many things can fit under that umbrella and that the plan is based upon the ISTE Standards. As required for the Smart Schools Grant, the plan must go through 2018 and that it is a living document which will continue to grow and be built on changes in technology, as we would not want to predict what technology will look like in five years. Dr. Kor reviewed the components of the plan and how the plan and its goals will look at the building level.  A survey will be done to create benchmarks to help inform knowledge of what is working and what is not. Measure of student success is also indication of an effective technology and will be an ongoing process. Mr. Lubinsky spoke to survey program called Clarity, which will survey parents/students as to what technology is available in the home, how it is used, comfort level of students and teachers, etc. It will also allow to measure if we are seeing changes overtime to ensure that we are moving the program forward.

	The Board wanted to be sure that the document will be used as a tool, what roadblocks might impede the follow through, and how areas of concern will be handled. Dr. Kor again stated that it is a living/breathing document and areas of concern or considerations will be addressed. The Board can expect an update annually to address changes, etc. Mr. Grados reminded that the needs in the use of technology and the teaching of technology must be bifurcated as they are different topics. He also asked if a Board member might sit on the Technology Committee. Mrs. Kerner noted of the shared-decision making plan. Dr. Kor noted that the framework was begun during the two Technology. Concern was raised at the amount of time that teachers were away from the classroom/

	Mr. Montgomery noted that he found the document difficult to read through and is not comfortable with the plan as it is challenging to understand. Mrs. Kerner concurred with Mr. Montgomery and also noted that she agrees that teaching of technology is distinct from using technology and perhaps that there should be two distinct plans.
