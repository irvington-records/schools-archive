What is Restorative Practices?
Restorative practices are the science of relationships and community.
When applied in schools, restorative practices proactively improve
climate and culture. Restorative practices also provide responses to
wrongdoing that focus on repairing harm.

Alternative to Traditional Discipline
The Social Discipline Window shows different
types of discipline based on the amount of
CONTROL and the amount of SUPPORT involved.
 ●   When there’s very little control or support, it is
     neglectful, essentially it’s not there at all.

 ●   When our control is high but our support is low,
     discipline is something we are doing TO the students
     and it’s punitive.

 ●   In the reverse, when our support is high but our
     control is low, it’s a permissive style.

 ●   Our goal is high control and high support. An
     environment and style of discipline that holds our
     students accountable to high expectations while
     providing them the support they need to achieve it.

Affective Statements

Affective Statements
“I” statements that tell others how what they are doing makes you feel and why it
makes you feel that way. They are an effective way to model naming feelings and
teach students how to express their emotions appropriately. When used with
fidelity, they can effectively reinforce positive actions and explain feelings without
assigning blame or shame.


Affective Statements can be used at any time.
Using affective statements improves the school community, which in part further
improves communication, social skills, and academics. When the school
community uses affective statements, they gain a greater understanding of others'
emotions and their feelings.

Community Circles
The circle is a potent symbol. Its shape implies community,
connection, inclusion, fairness, equality and wholeness. Meeting
in a circle establishes a level playing field for all participants. A
class, a group of students or a group of adults can meet in a circle
to discuss, answer questions, solve problems, play a game or offer
feedback. A circle has a structure, purpose and focus. It may be
proactive or responsive.

Main Street School
               The principal, school social worker, school psychologist, school counselor and two teachers
               participated in 3 full days of training with the K-12 Team. The MSS team has met multiple times to
               develop our approach and implementation plan for MSS.

               The MSS Team has engaged the entire faculty and staff in multiple learning sessions about restorative
               practices.

               Students engage in Community Circles. They meet in a circle to discuss, answer questions, solve
               problems, play a game or offer feedback. A circle has a structure, purpose and focus. It may be
               proactive or responsive.

               Teachers and the school counselor engage in Restorative Chats with students regularly to address
               incidents (conflict) between/among students or when students are not meeting behavior
               expectations.

               We also provide the opportunity for a Community Reset. The goal of our Community Reset is to
               address an incident of harm using a restorative approach. The questions are designed to be
               answered by everyone in the circle and help students explore who has been affected and to
               understand what needs to be done to repair the harm.

               Additionally we provide Classroom Community Resets. A Classroom Community Reset is a strength
               based approach focusing on relationships among a classroom community. They help the classroom
               community understand each other’s needs helping to create a more empathetic and equitable
               learning experience.

               Our restorative approach to community building and discipline are aligned to our many layers of SEL
               work.                                                                                            13

●   “The use of Restorative Circles has allowed students to express themselves in a way that
    is personal and also allows them to feel comfortable within the classroom. The classes
    that have implemented these models have noted the student's increased engagement
    and one that is welcomed and requested in the future.” (Julianna)

●   “The Restorative Practice focuses on building communities to include all voices in an
    effort to make connections and foster positive relationships, which in turn optimizes
    student learning and success.” (Allicia)

●   “Something that stood out to me from the training is the importance of building
    community and trust within the classroom. The use of circles and affirming statements
    are tools we can use to connect with students and develop their social emotional
    learning.” (Diane W)

●   “The focus on prevention through community building is extremely positive and possible.”
    (Diane S)

●   “I think one of the highlights for me was the idea behind the language we use in our
    questioning. Rephrasing our questions when investigating a conflict to be open ended
    with a focus on an explanation being shared by the parties involved, can help to gain an
    understanding as to why the behavior may have been done. Not to excuse the behavior,
    but in terms of repairing, getting to the root of the problem is necessary. We always talk
    it out with students who may be in a conflict, but simply rephrasing the way we talk with
    them or ask them about the incident can be eye opening.” (Michelle)
