		Presentation: 3.2 Teachers' and Principals' APPR Recommended Plans (Presentation)

		Dr. Raina Kor and Mr. David Sottile provided an update on the Annual Professional Performance Review (APPR), for both teachers and principals. They provided an overview of the APPR committee's work over the past year along with the key components of the review system of teachers, i.e. observation, student performance and end of year evaluations. Irvington's APPR is based on the rubric by Charlotte Danielson. The District must submit the plans by July 1st to the Commissioner of Education, who then has until September 1, 2012 to either approve or reject. The Board of Education will be asked to approve both the Teachers' and principals' Plans at their June 19, 2012 meeting. The Committee's work also included the relationship between the APPR and the new Common Core Standards. Dr. Kor noted that both teachers and principals must be evaluated under the new APPR.

		The presentation included the methodology for evaluating teachers, i.e. the four domains  which will then yield a score and ultimately result in a composite score declaring either Highly Effective (59-60); Effective (57-58); Developing (50-56) and Ineffective (0-49). Mr. Grados asked how the District will ensure that all scoring will be done consistently. Dr. Kor noted that observations will be an important component of the APPR and that that training has not yet taken place. This is referred to as the "Local 60% or Other Measures"

		State assessments will account for 20% of the composite score. The remaining 20% will be based on local assessments, either locally developed or purchased. This area is negotiated between the District and the Teachers' Union. Mr. Sottile provided an overview of how the composite scores will be generated. Teachers must receive, in writing, their score and rating for local measures of student achievement by the last day of the school year. The plan applies to tenured teachers as well as non-tenured teachers. Dr. Kor noted that on average, the evaluation process will take approximately 10 hours per teacher per year. Mr. McCann asked how this task will be carried out, given the time constraints of administrators. Dr. Roelle noted that there are many aspects still to be worked out and it will be a monumental task placed on the administrators. He also noted that the Superintendents' association has asked the State to consider moving back the implementation date for two years. The Dis

		Professional development must be provided to teachers in order to understand the Revised Danielson Rubric as well as how to develop a Student Learning Objective (SLO). The District is responsible for developing the Student Learning Objectives, Common Core pre-assessments and post assessments and a system for scoring. Since the new APPR regulations prohibits teachers from scoring their students' exams the District must Also to be worked out is how the District will handle the scoring of assessments since teachers cannot score their own students' assessments.

		Mr.Sottile provide an overview of the principals component of the APPR which is similarly formatted to the teachers' APPR, evaluated on student growth on State Assessments or comparable measures-20%, 20% on locally selected measures of student achievement and 60% on broad assessment of principal and management actions. The principals have selected the McRel Principal Evaluation System rubric.

		Mr. Whitney asked if the impact of the data management has been assessed and its impact on Jesse Lubinski, the District's Chief Information Officer . Dr. Kor noted that a tremendous amount of work has been done in preparing our system and much of the data has been cleaned up in anticipation of the implementation of the APPR but more work is ahead to prepare the system to handle all the additional assessment requirements. Dr. Kor noted that everything that is known at this time is being put into place, but there could be changes made at the State level. Dr. Roelle said that the Board should be proud of the APPR committee's cooperative working atmosphere in putting together the plan.

		Information: 3.3 Facilities Report Card

		Ms. Beverly Miller reported that school districts are required to present a Facilities Report to the Board to ensure that the District is being good stewards of the facilities. A report by an outside engineer is required every five years and a visual assessment is required each of the other four years. The reports given to the Board contain an overview of the needs of the District facilities. The report will  be used by the Buildings & Grounds Committee for future decision-making on buildings and grounds needs.

		Information, Policy - 1st Reading: 6.9 1st Read - Bullying, Harassment and Hazing Policy

		This is a first read for this policy and is required by NY State under the Dignity for All Students Act (DASA) and must be approved by July 1, 2012. The Board will be asked to approve it at the July 10th reorganization meeting. It will also be part of the Code of Conduct.

		Mrs. Camp noted that it did not go before the policy committee and that it should have been reviewed.

		Mr. Palermo noted that the Dignity for All Students Act goes into effect on July 1, 2012. It will also require a specific policy germane to the Dignity Act but the State has not yet issued the regulations. The policy before the Board has been reviewed by the District's legal counsel.

		Mr. McCann noted that a student who has been bullied loses his concentration and it takes time to regroup and the policy does not address consequences to the bully, such as placing a report in his file, nor does it require staff to contact parents, stating only make an effort to contact parents. It also does not have any disciplinary actions against the bully. It is unacceptable in his opinion.

		Dr. Roelle responded to Mr. McCann's comments stating that the administrator's would agree but we have been waiting for regulations from the state which will address the issues that Mr. McCann has raised. A policy only sets into motion the regulations and the consequences will also be outlined in the Code of Conduct which is undergoing a major edit which will be presented to the Board at its June 19th.

		Mr. Palermo also noted that the Dignity Act requires training of staff and students and provide preventative measures that are effective.
