	Action (Consent): 7.2 Request for NYS Legislature to End Gap Elimination Adjustment (GEA)

	Resolution: WHEREAS, the New York State Gap Elimination Adjustment (GEA) was implemented in 2010 as a one-time reduction in education aid to help New York State balance its budget and the GEA has continued in every budget since then; and WHEREAS, since its inception the GEA has reduced State Aid to the Irvington Union Free School District by $2,518,173; and

	WHEREAS, the Governor’s budget proposal for the 2014-2015 school year projects another $393,793 cut in State Aid through the Gap Elimination Adjustment; and WHEREAS, this reduction in aid has resulted in a cost shift to the local property taxpayers in the Irvington Union Free School District; and

	WHEREAS, this cost shift has resulted in unsustainable measures to balance the budgets in the Irvington Union Free School District, including the reduction and elimination of school programs, personnel and services, and the reduction of school district fund reserves; and

	WHEREAS, efforts by the Irvington Union Free School District to sustain programs and services are simultaneously constrained by a property tax levy limit; unfunded and underfunded mandates; increases in pension and health care costs; and mandated implementation of Common Core Learning Standards and Teacher/Principal Performance Reviews; and

	WHEREAS, adequate State funding was court ordered, constitutionally mandated and essential to meet all mandates and obligations and to maintain the quality of education in public schools in New York State; and

	WHEREAS, New York State touts recent aid increases as generous support to schools, although overall, school districts are receiving less in State Aid now than 2008-2009.

	NOW, THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED, that the Irvington Union Free School District’s Board of Education calls upon the New York State Legislature to immediately end the Gap Elimination Adjustment and restore the money projected to be cut under the GEA in the Governor’s proposed budget.

	11. Presentations/Discussions

	2014-15 Budget Discussion (Presentation)

	Dr. Harrison reviewed the timeline where the budget has been focused on at Board Meetings and again noted that budget development is a process and shifts need to be made along the way. Dr. Harrison provided follow-up to questions that were asked at the March 4th meeting regarding historical review of district employees, a comparison of health insurance expenses for employees and retirees as well as year to year state aid comparisons and tax cap carryover.

	Dr. Harrison explained that the original State's tax cap calculation was incorrect and in fact the cap is 3.28%, which now has the proposed budget over the tax cap by $280,000. Mr. Grados thanked John Montgomery for asking why the cap was higher than previously and thanked Ms. Miller for following up with the State to gather accurate information. Mr. Montgomery noted that Irvington is seeing growth and it should be acknowledged. Given this updated information, the District must make some adjustments, but Dr. Harrison believes that it is a responsible budget, both academically and financially. Adjustments include a reduction in the cost of an outside placement of a student that will be graduating, adjustments in BOCES fees and moving the purchase of a lawn mower to this year’s budget. Other items that had been included in the Superintendent’s original proposed budget are eliminated from the adjusted budget and include the .40FTE music, the increase in the athletic trainer services as well as the elimination of classroom and high school lockers painting. These reductions total $293,292, representing a total budget of $56,152,108 and under the tax cap of $12,724. Mr. Montgomery had asked if it was possible to move some capital projects in the proposed budget to a possible bond referendum. One consideration is to replace one student bathroom in the 2014-15 budget and the second in the 2015-16 budget.

	Dr. Harrison noted that important decisions need to be made prior to the April 24th meeting where the Board must adopt the budget and requests that the budget be as close to the final recommended budget as possible on April 1. Mrs. Kerner expressed concern that community forums are scheduled to be held after April 1st and would not want to be held to any final direction by the 1st. Mr. Grados said that there will be opportunity to listen to the community and that there will be flexibility in adjusting the budget. Dr. Harrison's suggested that scheduling a meeting between the 1st and 24th is an option.

	Comments from the Board on the revised budget included:

	Mr. Grados spoke regarding the school bus monitors and noted that it had been discussed at great length last year and he feels that having bus monitors is something that has come to be expected by parents who have moved to Irvington and he continues to support finding a way to leave monitors in the budget. Mrs. Kerner agrees but also asked what might be eliminated to balance the inclusion of monitors back in the budget. Mr. Grados would want to discuss the CIO Database position ($70,000) and the improved communications ($18,000) as items that might be considered for elimination. Mrs. Kerner feels that the tech position is one to keep in order to move the District forward. Another priority for her would be to add back the behavioral teaching assistant had been discussed and feels it is very important position as it impacts all the children in the class. She would also like to get a clearer picture of our transportation budget and be sure that Irvington is receiving the oversight that is necessary. Other priorities to trade off would include: eliminating the increase of co-teaching positions as well as the .20 FTE and one stipend for flexibility in scheduling at the HS.

	Mr. Whitney would like to know why the addition of a database manager is so essential at this time given that it is a clerical position and would not necessarily move the technology forward in the District. Dr. Harrison responded with the responsibilities of such a position and that the current configuration does not allow for ample release time for focus on the technical side and to work more closely with our teachers to introduce new technologies to the teacher, new skills and new tools. The coordinator would be able to provide oversight to all that was going on if the data component of the current model was removed from his plate. Mr. Whitney said it is not clear to him as to what the shifts in responsibilities would be with the additional time and what exactly it would be next year and without specifics it is difficult to make this tradeoff with bus monitors. Mrs. Kerner noted that technology is a goal of the district and that resources must be available to fulfill the goal.

	Mrs. Kashkin feels it is shortsighted not to have a crises TA and that certain expenditures could have been avoided if the position was in place this year and she feels strongly that this position be included. Also discussed was the cost of BOCES teacher training and its intended purpose.

	Mr. Montgomery stated that he wants to be convinced that all items that are included in the proposed budget will benefit the district before he makes any decisions. He doesn't want to see salary increases, benefit increases, etc. drive the decisions to move the district forward and is cautious to eliminate the bus monitors and shouldn't be the first place to find money. Other items he is not sure he is in favor of at this time is the high school writing class proposal, the database management position, nor does he have a strong attachment to the AIS Math, improved communications and co-teaching items. He is in agreement that the .20FTE or even a .4FTE for flexibility in scheduling is worth keeping, the crises TA position, and noted that he is sympathetic to the addition of the .4FTE and would favor including it in the budget.

	Mrs. Kerner said the big question is what is most important to the children and it is spending on the classroom and teachers and should be the last place where cuts are made. If technology and guidance are her priorities, it would be remiss to not look at the communications item would not be a priority at this time. Mrs. Kashkin also agreed to maintain the database position in order to allocate the monies necessary to develop technology in the district.

	Mr. Grados summarized the following:

	The majority of the Board would to include bus monitors back in the budget, delete the data base management position in the CIO’s office, .4FTE flexibility for scheduling, and adding back the special education crises TA. He noted that this is the initial direction to administration. Dr. Harrison responded that the administrators feel that a .2 FTE for scheduling at the high school would be sufficient. With regard to the database management position, the conversation should be about the Coordinator of the Technology position and in order to have ambitious goals, more resources must be provided in this area. He is not aware of any district in the area that doesn't have a dedicated person in such a position, he also stressed that he feels strongly about the technology position. He asked the Board for clarity on adding capital projects and Mr. Montgomery said he had initially asked to have a conversation that the conversation began because of the higher tax cap that we thought we had. Mr. Grados feels that the bus monitors are ingrained in Irvington and should be maintained at this time and would like to consider a way to keep the CIO position, if there is a way. Mr. Montgomery again reiterated that the proposal for the writing program has not state strongly that he is not in favor of such a position. Also, feels that the l4 FTE Music is also important to all students.
