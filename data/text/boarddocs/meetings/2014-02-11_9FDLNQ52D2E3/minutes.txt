	5. Grades 9-12 Common Core Learning Standards

	Presentation: 5.1 Grades 9-12 Common Core Learning Standards (Presentation)

	Dr. Kor, Dave Cohen, HS Principal, Deb Mariniello, Asst. Principal presented an overview of the changes to the HS curriculum represents the shifts in the high school program and how the Common Core has started to create a more cohesive approach teaching and learning Grades K-12. Dr. Kor noted that the presentation format for tonight is the same as the previous presentations that addressed Grades K-5 and Grades 6-8 to represent the continuum of the Common Core through Grades K-12.Dr. Prior to outlining the progression of the grade-level specific standards, Kor stressed that the anchor standards at the high school level could be viewed as the “end game” whereby the expectation is that every senior will have achieved and met the learning standards upon graduation. Dr. Kor outlined the progression of the grade-level specific standards.

	Ms. Mariniello provided examples of Anchor Standard #5 which is to analyze the structure of texts and how it progresses in complexity and sophistication. The goal is for teachers to become more explicit in their instruction in order for the students to gain a deeper understanding of the materials and she provided an example of what is given to the students in order for them to gain a greater understanding of the assignment’s expectation. One of the most important aspects of the Common Core is the relationship between the reading and writing and they cannot be done in isolation. Examples of students’ writing assignments were provided to illustrate the planning necessary on the teacher’s to achieve the learning targets, which are named as they are related to the Core.

	Ms. Mariniello provided examples of Shift 2 which is the knowledge in the disciplines, and how it applies to other content areas, i.e. social studies and science and deepens their thinking in a guided reading approach.

	Mr. Cohen provided an overview of modeling across the high school courses in mathematics and how to create real life examples. Mr. Cohen provided examples of the shifts as they relate to Algebra, noting that it is the gateway to the high school level learning in the area of mathematics, and provided examples as well as assignments focused on compound locus again provide a deeper understanding of the concept being taught. He also reported that students create their own equation thus strengthening their understanding of the concepts, builds their understanding as well expressing their understanding in their solution of the problem. There are eight mathematical practices, including reason abstractly and quantitatively, constructing viable arguments, critique others arguments and to explain their reasoning.

	Dr. Kor summarized that the feedback from graduating seniors is that they are not prepared for the college reading level, especially informational texts but the Common Core will provide informational texts experiences.  She also noted that the work at the high school is very similar as it is at the middle school level. The work will continue to look at providing the common language throughout the K-12 experience, examining text choice to ensure rigor, and align informational texts in science and social studies.

	12. Presentations/Discussions

	Discussion: 12.1 Draft of proposed 2014-15 School Calendar (Presentation)

	Dr. Harrison provided an overview of the 2014-15 school calendar noting the considerations and complexities that go into the development, especially aligning with neighboring districts and BOCES as there are many shared services while minimizing an impact on student learning. Among the changes in developing the calendar are fewer Superintendent Conference Days and more learning time for the last week of school. Dr. Harrison noted that the Superintendent's Conference Days have been aligned with other districts in order to provide opportunities for collaboration among the Districts.

	The draft calendar will be posted for 30 days and the Board will vote on its adoption at the March 4, 2014 Board Meeting.

	Discussion: 12.2 Discussion to consider granting tax exemptions to Veterans who reside within the Irvington School District (Presentation)

	Mr. Grados noted that a public Hearing is needed if the Board is going to consider adopting the resolution granting tax relief to the District's veterans. The exemption is not based on income but is based on a certain percentage of the assessed value of a residence. The BOE must decide by May 1st, with a possible Public Hearing sometime in March. There are approximately 182 veterans in the Village of Irvington, resulting in a redistribution of the tax base and an increase of approximately $79 on the averaged assessed value.

	Presentation: 12.3 2014-15 Budget Presentation - Athletics, Special Education and Pupil Personnel Services (Presentation)

	Dr. Harrison stated that the District strives to provide a fiscally sound budget to the students as well as the tax payers and expressed gratitude for the support of the tax cap override last year. Mr. Scott Palermo provided projections in the areas of special education and pupil personnel services. He noted that costs can vary should student’s with high needs move into the District during the course of the year.  The 2014-15 projected budget variance is 6.92% with .80% in new initiatives and 6.12% in actual budget to budget increases. One consideration of the proposed budget is to create the position of Asst. Director of PPS and eliminate the position of CSE/CPSE chairperson.

	Other considerations in the special education budget is to create a 1.0 FTE Crises Teaching Assistant to help teachers with students who are experiencing behavioral difficulties, with a cost of approximately $42,000. Additionally, there is a request to consider an increase in behavioral intervention services to one day per week to help teachers learn skills to better support behavioral needs. The associated expense for this consideration is approximately $35,000 and is aidable through BOCES (aid is approximately 56% and is received in the following school year.) Mrs. Miller provided an overview of the individual components within the Special Ed budget. Of note, there is a 54.45% increase in the tuition line, which is a shift in placements from BOCES placements to private placements as well as an increase in students being placed out of the district, including three incoming kindergartners.

	Mr. Whitney asked if it would be possible to benchmark our special education costs against area school districts. Mr. Palermo noted that he isn’t sure how valuable the information would be as you would also need to look at the in-district programs available to students with needs. Mr. Palermo noted that the State does provide some relief in the form of aid for high cost placements to help offset the costs.

	In the area of PPS, there is a variance of approximately 7.91% with 2.85% in new considerations and the remainder due to contractual obligations and an increase in the school physician’s cost. There is a request to increase the community aide’s position to 1.0 FTE from the current .5 FTE to better support our families and children. The current community aide is a trained social worker and does complete Social Histories for the CSE. When asked to prioritize the considerations, Mr. Palermo noted that the District is in dire need of an Asst. Director to assist with the increased needs but all requests are necessary but the first two would have the greatest impact in the immediate.

	Prior to addressing the proposed Athletic Budget, Mr. McCormack announced that both the girls’ and boys’ basketball teams are #1 seed for the upcoming sectionals. He also noted that Anthony DiNardo will complete at the State level in wrestling.

	The proposed Athletic Budget has a variance of 13.18% with 4.64% in actual budget-to-budget increase and 8.54% in new considerations. New considerations include increasing the athletic trainer to full time due to the necessary assessment in concussion management and student injuries which will help athletes get treatment sooner and to provide education as to how to prevent future injuries, which is an increase of $30,000. There is also a request to consider the restoration of middle school intramural programs, at the 6th grade level, to provide an opportunity to introduce middle school programs to the students. The budget increase associated with this consideration is $4,500 to cover the cost of a person to oversee the program. The third consideration is an add back of a second modified basketball team (boys and girls) with an expense of $13,600 to cover coaches, officials, chaperones and uniforms. Also included for consideration is an additional Varsity Winter Track Assistant Coach as well as the addition of .25% clerical assistance totaling .50% to provide more focus on athletics, to help manage communication, department business as well as scheduling needs.

	Mrs. Kerner asked if any of the considerations could be paid for through fundraising or parents willing to pay the cost. Dr. Harrison would not recommend such an approach as he feels that it is the District’s obligation to pay for salaries, etc., however, there is nothing in law that would preclude the District from doing so.

	In summary, the budget-to-budget increase is 3.93% for the budget components that have been presented to date. The final budget presentation will be presented call for with the final budget presentation on February 28 will complete the budget recommendations and considerations.
