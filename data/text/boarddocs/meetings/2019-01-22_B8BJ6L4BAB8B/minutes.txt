4. Presentation/Discussion

Roundtable Discussion - Social Emotional Learning and Shared and Part time Staff

Social Emotional Learning

Dr. Kor opened the discussion on Social Emotional Learning by asking if any of the Administrators would like to share what they are doing in their buildings.

- Ms. Chapnick thanked the Board for the new K-5 guidance counselor that was asked for in the 2018-19 budget. She said that in order to support the needs of the students one K-5 guidance counselor is not enough. The Guidance Counselor is able to connect (via Tier 1 RTI) with all students via “push-in” lessons are centered around problem solving and good choices for a total of about 8 lessons for students for the year. Teachers are in the classroom during those lessons and can continue the work in lessons and other parts of the students’ day. Another counselor would give some students another level of support and help students that have social emotional issues that impede learning.

- Ms. Mariniello added that at Dows Lane they are building a proactive and responsive program for K-3. More counseling provides explicit instruction to implement coping strategies and behaviors that promote learning.

Dr. Harrison asked if there is an entry point at the secondary level.

- Mr. Cohen responded that anxiety and stress levels among teenagers has increased. Students can exhibit various negative behaviors to cope with these issues. He said a dedicated social worker helps student with their coping skills and day-to-day social emotional learning.

Mr. Graeber asked what Tier 1 RTI looks like in the schools.

- Dr. Kor explained that language on elementary report cards was changed to behaviors that promote learning and have now evolved into “habits of mind” for students.

- Ms. Mariniello stated that we don’t want to wait until a child is struggling and has stress and anxiety before we address their needs. Morning meeting format at Dows Lane is an important component for SEL.

- Mr. Sottile said that connections students make with adults are important for Middle School students. Counselors are in the classrooms helping students with transition from 5th to 6th grade, Health class and one-on-one meetings in order to connect with students.

Dr. Harrison stated that Guidance Counselors help students with day-to-day anxiety. He asked what needs a social worker can address.

- Ms. Krieger explained the four main areas that a social worker supports administrators and families.

- Dr. Harrison said the District has become a community with more diverse needs over the years. There are more families that struggle and can use help of the social worker.

- Dr. Kor stated we need a written curriculum for social emotional learning.

Ms. Kashkin agrees there is an academic connection with social emotional learning.  She would like to understand more the need of an additional Guidance Counselor.

- Dr. Kantor explained if a student is dysregulated they cannot learn. The Guidance Counselor sets up sessions to help teachers help students so they can learn.

- Ms. Chapnick added there are 39 classes and she is not accessible to all students on every day. She said we have to have the staffing to support SEL.

Ms. Kashkin asked about what Tier 2 intervention would look like and what data would you use.

- Ms. Chapnick explained they would use attendance and academic data and behaviors that promote learning.  She explained that Tier 2 interventions are short-term  targeted and have goals and are progress monitored to see if the students respond.

- Mr. Samuelson explained there are the same concerns with high school students as there are for elementary students. There are families in the District that need to access help and tools that are outside our school buildings.

Dr. Harrison asked what a Social Worker can provide vs. a Guidance Counselor.

- Mr. Samuelson responded a Social Worker has access to outside tools (that do not cost the District money) that a Guidance Counselor doesn’t. A Social Worker could help teachers understand in advance what is going on with students.

Ms. Propper asked for clarification about the additional Social Worker. Is it a District-wide position or Grades 9-12 position? She also asked about the new NYSED Mental Health standards.

- Dr. Harrison responded that if we added a second Social Worker it could be that we have one Social Worker for elementary and one for the Campus.

- Dr. Kor also responded to her question that even without NYSED standards the District is working on a Health curriculum based on needs that have already been identified.

- Ms. Daly said they have partnered with Social Worker to help families and connect them with outside agencies to get the children to go to school.

- Ms. Krieger said the resources that a Social Worker has access to reach far beyond the school day and area of expertise of educators.

- Ms. Chapnick explained that she and the Social Worker developed an attendance response system for kids that do not want to come to school and that this early intervention has resulted in a decrease in tardiness and absences.

Part Time and Shared Staff

Dr. Kor explained that part time staff are less than 1.0 employees. Shared staff are assigned to one or more buildings.

- Mr. Cohen explained how the shared position of Music teachers play a role in more than one building. There are pull out lessons for specialty music lessons. When they are doing pull out lessons students are missing class. When there are PD  days, the scheduling of shared staff members causes them to miss key pieces of information because they cannot attend all meetings in all buildings.

- Ms. Chapnick stated when there is a shared Music staff member she has to build her schedule around the campus schedule.  It puts limitations on what can be offered to students at Main Street School.

- Mr. Sottile said that shared staff also has an impact on Core academic classes when sharing with the High School. A shared teacher might not be available for extra help for Middle School students.

- Dr. Kantor said shared staff limits flexibility. If a student is absent on a day when they usually receive OT they might not be able to make it up the next day because the shared staff may not be in the building the next day.

- Ms. Mariniello  said  they instituted a “flex” period at Dows Lane so students in one grade level receive pull out services at the same time.  She said the biggest impact at Dows Lane is sharing the .6 Instructional Coach with 27 teachers

Mr. Hanna asked if there were one or two things that would make a dramatic change to the experience at school if you could change the role of that faculty member.

- Ms. Chapnick said Music and Art Main Street School.

- Dr. Harrison said the part time Social Studies teacher at the high school needs to be full time.

- Mr. Hanna stated retention of part time staff is an issue.

- Dr. Kor said every time we need to replace a teacher, hiring committee interviews take a lot of time. It's harder to find a part time teacher and they need to be replaced more often.

- Mr. Sottile mentioned it’s harder to find a part time teacher than a full time teacher

Ms. Hargraves asked Dr. Harrison for turnover data for part time staff. She also would like to see more data regarding incidents that the Social Worker is handling.

- Dr. Harrison stated there is evidence that shows turnover in part time positions were stopped once the position was made fulltime.

Ms. Propper asked about shared positions between middle school and high school and how a new proposed schedule would impact extra help for students in the middle school.

- Mr. Sottile responded to her that they are looking at the best way to share staff in a new schedule.

Considerations for the Board of Education

Dr. Kor asked if there were any school specific requests that anyone would like to share

- Dr. Harrison said that the District is developing a true K-12 system and alignment. There is a need for safety and security enhancements and a new phone system.

- Mr. McCormack said there is a need for a full time athletic trainer. A full- time trainer can be proactive with athletes.

- Mr. Cohen wants to create flexible learning spaces. Different types of seating and standing desks. There is also a need professional learning spaces.

- Mr. Sottile said there is a need for devices for students. There is need for one-to-one devices for all students rather than students using their phones in class.

- Dr. Harrison responded 7 years ago we had spotty WIFI  on the Campus. We have also introduced WIF to elementary school through SMART Schools bond. First phase of the SMART Schools bond was for WiFI and second phase is for devices.

- Ms. Chapnick said they have increased devices but there is a need to integrate teaching with devices.  She also would like to add a PE aide. The Main Street Gym teacher spends most of the day alone in the Main Street Gym because it is a separate building from the school.

- Ms. Mariniello said there is a need to support teachers with technology. Need climate control and air conditioning in the classrooms and a need an instructional coach for professional learning.

Dr. Harrison there will be more discussions throughout the budget process that will address these points and there will be more discussion and data to support the asks. He also thanked everyone for a great workshop.

Mr. Hanna thanked everyone for sharing all their stories and information.

Action (Consent):  7.3 Approval of Resolution

Resolution: WHEREAS, the Board of Education has for several years approved, by way of formal resolution duly adopted, summer work for Jesse Lubinsky, while he was serving as a teacher on special assignment, to perform necessary technology work during the summer recess period; and

WHEREAS, effective July 1, 2017, the Board of Education initially appointed Jesse Lubinsky to serve in a twelve (12) month administrative position, Director of Technology, thereby alleviating the need for the Board of Education to continue to approve summer work for Jesse Lubinsky to perform necessary technology work during the summer months; and

WHEREAS, in order to serve in this administrative position, Jesse Lubinsky requested a leave of absence from his teacher on special assignment position, effective July 1, 2017 through June 30, 2018, which request was approved by the Board of Education; and

WHEREAS, due to union posting requirements, as set forth in the Irvington Faculty Association collective bargaining agreement, the initial appointment of Jesse Lubinsky to the Director of Technology position, effective July 1, 2017 needed to be amended to August 1, 2017; and

WHEREAS, on September 12, 2017, the Board of Education amended its initial appointment of Jesse Lubinsky to the twelve (12) month Director of Technology position to commence on August 1, 2017 to be in compliance with the above referenced posting requirement; and

WHEREAS, the Board of Education did not simultaneously act to revise the time period for Jesse Lubinsky’s leave of absence; and

WHEREAS, immediately prior to his administrative appointment on August 1, 2017, Jesse Lubinsky again performed authorized summer technology work and submitted time sheets for said work, which was approved in writing by his supervisor; however, due to an inadvertent oversight, the Board of Education did not formally approve, by way of a resolution, the 2017 work for Jesse Lubinsky, prior to his administrative appointment on August 1, 2017; and

NOW THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED, by way of this corrective resolution, the Board of Education herewith revises the initial request received by Jesse Lubinsky for a leave of absence commencing on July 1, 2017 so that he could serve as the Director of Technology to a start date of August 1, 2017 through July 31, 2018; and

BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED, that the Board of Education herewith ratifies and authorizes Jesse Lubinsky to perform necessary summer technology work during June and July 2017, immediately preceding his appointment as Director of Technology; and

BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED, that the Board of Education herewith ratifies and approves the payments made to Jesse Lubinsky in connection with said work, which were calculated using his daily rate (as a teacher on special assignment) for only those hours he worked during between June 26, 2017 and July 31, 2017 ($11,191.23).

Motion by Maura Gedid, Second by  Maria Kashkin

Final Resolution: Motion carries

Yes: Michael Hanna, Maria Kashkin, Brian Friedman, Deborah Hargraves, David Graeber, Maura Gedid, Beth Propper
