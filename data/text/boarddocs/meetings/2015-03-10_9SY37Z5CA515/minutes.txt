	Information: 3.3 Update and Clarification on Proposed IHS Program Modifications

	Dr. Kor highlighted some of the shifts that will take place within the high school curriculum and apologized to the Board that they had not been included in the process prior to it being discussed in public. She shared a graphic that showed possible scenarios for high school English options, noting that  the need had been realized that more engaging electives needed to be offered as options for students and that current offerings will still be available to students. Dr. Kor noted that public speaking is an important skill and it is believed that it should be a requirement for graduating from Irvington High School. Currently, there is an elective SUPA course. Other options include journalism and creative writing, noting that Irvington has not traditionally offered many electives in English. These options would be opt-in by students.

	Mrs. Kerner asked for clarification on the word “requirement” as it pertains to taking a presentational speaking course in order to graduate. Dr. Kor reiterated that the offerings will be elective and not be required to graduate at the present time, but that the administrators do feel that it is important. Mrs. Kashkin asked if there was a difference in rigor between the SUPA course and what the District will offer to students. Mr. Cohen explained that two years of curriculum was being examined and also looking at what is valued in Irvington. The District's course will not carry college credit as does the SUPA program and the curriculum will need to be developed.

	Mr. Whitney noted this topic was not advertised and came about due to a group of parents attending an evening event where it was discussed and it raised questions among parents. Mrs. Kerner noted that students are opting in to the course now for next year so that the discussion is important to have now. Mr. Cohen confirmed that students are beginning to speak with their counselors about the courses for next year. He also noted that it is about creating opportunity for our students and a rich curriculum for them. Mr. Montgomery wanted to clarify what is changing for next year in addition to the changes in Regents courses. Mr. Cohen highlighted that AP English will continue to be offered for juniors as well as any seniors who wish to take it. A core of students go through the junior year doing SUPA class(es) then possibly an American Literature course in senior year, as well as a SUPA course in writing. The motivation and philosophy behind the changes was to create more choice and opportunities for our students, especially offering half year courses to opt-in to more courses. It was noted that the Regents will now be given in 10th grade as opposed to 11th grade which might make the 11th grade curriculum slightly outdated but more importantly, will provide more rigor for the students. Mrs. Kerner noted that parents at the meeting were frustrated that there were no descriptions available about the new course offerings. Mr. Cohen noted that students will have course descriptions before making their choices. A packet will be sent home in advance so that students and parents to review together. Dr. Kor agreed with Mrs. Kerner's suggestion to begin any new requirements for graduation as students enter 9th grade.

	Mr. Whitney asked that the Board be kept informed of the conversations that are taking place. Dr. Harrison fully acknowledged that dialogue needs to happen.

	4. Presentations/Discussions

	Discussion: 4.1 Discussion of 2015-16 School Calendar

	Mr. Whitney noted that the Board will vote to adopt the calendar at the March 24th meeting, and that there are some unusual instances such as a late Labor Day and the addition of four .5 day professional development days. The question was posed as to the value of these days for professional development. Dr. Harrison noted that the intent is to acknowledge the large amount of curriculum work that needs to be done, with opportunities for teachers to plan together but also to limit the time that teachers are out of the classroom. He also noted that as a matter of example, other surrounding district's do employ the use of professional days on a weekly/monthly basis. Mr. Grados asked if using two full days would be possible, but because of the need to have a certain amount of days of student attendance, this is not possible. Mr. Whitney asked if it would be possible to introduce two .5 days as a start. Dr. Harrison noted that it is important to have access to teachers to be able to keep up with other districts and to be competitive and that this is a step forward in meeting the District's needs. Mrs. Kerner spoke to the fact that teachers are being frequently pulled from the classroom and said it would be helpful to look at the number of substitute hours at each building for a  five year period to determine if these days help mitigate the amount of time that teachers are out of the classroom. It was also asked by an email from a community member about aligning the .5 days to weekends. Dr. Kor noted as a historical point, that parents have had negative feedback to superintendent's days attached to weekends. Another drawback is that classes that are offered only on particular days are affected by using only Mondays or Fridays. The administrators will look to see how they could pull the data on substitutes used to cover teachers being pulled for professional development.

	Mr. Montgomery expressed that he does not feel confident the resources and time being spent on professional development is providing the benefits of improving the quality of the education, but that perhaps it is an end in and of itself. Mr. Whitney noted that it is hard to truly know their worth and that perhaps by 1) inviting Board members to staff development days or 2) having teachers speak at a Board meeting as to the benefits would provide the Board with an insight into the benefits of staff development.

	Mrs. Kerner also noted that it was suggested to move the opening day of school to September 2nd and have a four day weekend. Dr. Harrison noted that it was deliberate to open on the 3rd for two reasons 1) thinking of the construction in the schools over the summer to be ready; and 2) the contract year of the 10-month aides is September 1st, and having two days is critical to prepare for the students. Mr. Montgomery noted that it is also a difficult calendar due to the Jewish holidays falling on weekdays.

	Dr. Harrison stated that the focus tonight is on the business services, debt, buildings and grounds, transportation, insurance and employee benefits as well as technology purchasing, noting that the tax cap levy, along with the lack of state aid information from the state as to Irvington’s expected state aid are among the challenges faced in developing a responsible budget for the 2015-16 school year.

The Board asked for further information on capital reserve funds in order for them to determine if they would like to move forward with presenting it to the voters in May.

	Ms. Miller reviewed the tax levy, noting that the allowable tax levy is estimated to be $51,202,475 and given that Districts have not yet been provided state aid figures, it is difficult to know exactly what we would need to levy from taxes. She also reviewed the projected revenue, including reimbursements, payments in lieu of taxes, reimbursements from John Cardinal O'Connor School, medicaid as well as state aid which total a projected $6,304,563

	Given all the above, the proposed budget is $58,143,665 which is $214,627 over the tax cap, but again reiterated that these figures could change due to state aid figures. Dr. Harrison sought the Board's thoughts and comments on the proposed budget.

	Mr. Whitney noted that he asked Dr. Harrison to summarize all of the new considerations in order for the Board to easily look at what is proposed and if members of the Board could provide the administration with guidance as to where they would like to fall with regard to the tax cap, i.e. at tax cap or below. Ms. Miller noted that there is much fluidity in figures this year with so many gray areas. Total new considerations

Mrs. Kerner noted she wanted to stay under the tax cap and that the tax levy would be higher than an approximately 3.0% tax increase. Dr. Harrison would like to work with administrators to now prioritize their asks with the goal of coming in at or under budget. Currently, there is $1.2 million in new considerations. Mrs. Kerner would like to see more items that go directly into the classroom that affect the students rather than towards. Mr. Whitney said it would be the time for the Board to be specific as to what they might want to add to the Budget. Mr. Montgomery also has the sense that we're spending a lot but it doesn't reach the core of getting more opportunities for students and improving the education in the District. He is not comfortable spending in areas that don't necessary match their goals. He also noted that the "windfall" from the pension fund is being spent and he isn't sure he wants to go to the tax cap. He also asked if the status quo is acceptable and if everything we are currently doing is really worth it. Mrs. Kerner also stated that she hasn't heard the efficacy of the new programs, and the hires from last year which makes it difficult to get a sense of how the positions are affecting our children and if money is well spent. Mr. Montgomery feels there is an opportunity to deliver a small tax increase as he hasn't heard a strong reason to expend the funds at this time.
