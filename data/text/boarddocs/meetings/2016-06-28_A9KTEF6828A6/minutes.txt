Presentation: 7.2 IHS Presentation (attached)

Prior to the presentation, Dr. Harrison acknowledged that Steve Corbett will be moving on to another District and wished him the best. Mr. Cohen spoke of the continuation of the three year journey to offer a rigorous curriculum to the students. This year's focus was about reinforcing the infastructure and giving stability to what has been the focus for three years. He highlighted the Theories of Action and how they advance the high school curriculum. The Theory of Action that was developed for the high school reads, "If we provide students with rigorous content, balanced with learning activities that foster collaboration and effective communication, then they will successfully demonstrate deeper levels of understanding and higher levels of thinking." He noted that this resonates through the culture throughout the high school experience. He spoke of the high school experience, the academic curriculum, as well as the sequences of courses with student choices growing in the 11th and 12th grade, noting the many amazing choices are available to students. He highlighted the ELA electives as well as advanced college-level courses including AP Lit, SUPA Public Speaking and SUPA Critical Reading and Intensive Writing. Courses offered to peak students' interest include Graphic Novel and Comic Book as Literature,

Mr. Corbett highlighted the courses offered in Social Studies, including Women's Studies in Sociology, Russian Studies, etc. along with a number of AP courses, and noted that courses are reintroduced as there is interest expressed by the students. He also noted that the department is also moving in to an inquiry-based focus that will increase the rigor as well as student interest and that all courses are also focusing on the writing component. He highlighted courses available in the Science curriculum, including introducing AP Physics. Mrs. Palmieri asked about thoughts on offering physics first, followed by chemistry then offer biology since much of biology is physics-based. Mr. Cohen highlighted choices in the area of Mathematics and looking at computer program as part of the math sequence, but also looking at coding as perhaps a fourth year math. Irvington offers four AP courses, including a AP Computer Science course that was added. Mr. Corbett noted that Greek I and Greek II are now offered and noted that there has been much work on the faculty-side to increase the speaking of languages and work towards aligning the 6-12 curriculum. In the area of physical education, growth can be seen in the area of health curriculum, with partnerships with iASK, the Student Assistance Counselor along with local agencies to enrich the learning experiences and to enable broader conversations that are important to students' well-being. The Arts curriculum provides students with broad array of opportunities for students in the visual arts as well as in music opportunities. Students have reached incredible performance levels.

Also highlighted were the Bridges Program which provides an alternate learning experiences for students who would benefit from a non-traditional "class beyond a classroom". He spoke of the projects that students developed and that that is an amazing aspect of the program to hear the students' speak of their experiences. It builds success for students who might not otherwise have a positive educational experience. Mr. Corbett highlighted the many academic supports also provided to students and noted that the Special Education Chair has succeeded in supporting teachers, increased collaboration with special ed teachers, psychologists, etc. about the needs of each student. Support systems have also been enhanced for those students who do not have an IEP or 504 Plan, included the IST process and the RTI program. Over the last three years, the counseling program has been defined with better transition planning for 8-9th graders, evening programs geared to the college process, curriculum night as well as fostering better relationships with college representatives. Providing consistency for all students has been a focus of the counselors so that each have similar experiences throughout high school.

Ways that student success is celebrated at the high school include student leadership roles, including Peer Leadership changes, character building, along with the Core Value Awards introduced this year to promote 21st century dispositions and recognized over 100 students as well as the creation of a National Honor Society. Other opportunities that enhance student life include many clubs such as the Investment Club, Model UN, Mock Trial, etc. One of the goals at the high school this year was to create opportunities for students to voice their ideas, concerns, and overall student well-being with administrators.

The Board asked for specific examples of information presented, such as how effective communication is being fostered as is stated in the building's Theory of Action, conversations to create an "Irvington" rigorous curriculum and not just what the College Board's dictates for course offerings. Mr. Montgomery would like to see further conversations on looking at moving away from the Regents curriculum which he believes would open up many opportunities. Mr. Cohen believes the conversations would begin the process of moving away from the Regents, and would require the support of the Board with time provided for teachers to redesign courses that would be designed outside of the Regents.

Action (Consent): 9.2 Smart Schools Bond Investment Plan Adoption

Resolution: WHEREAS, in January 2014, Governor Andrew M. Cuomo called for New York State to invest $2 billion in its schools through a Smart Schools Bond Act (“SSBA”); and WHEREAS, voters approved the SSBA in a statewide referendum held during the 2014 General Election on Tuesday, November 4, 2014; and WHEREAS, the Smart Schools Bond Act authorized the issuance of $2 billion of general obligation bonds to finance improved educational technology and infrastructure to improve learning and opportunity for students throughout the State; and WHEREAS, as a result, the Irvington Union Free School District (“School District”) was allocated to receive $277,051; and WHEREAS, aligned with the Irvington UFSD Approved 2015-2018 Instructional Technology Plan, adopted by the Board of Education on June 16, 2015 the Public School District Smart Schools Investment Plan is designed to: • Provide School Connectivity ($185,845) • Provide Classroom Technology ($91,206) WHEREAS, Public School District Smart Schools Bond Act Investment Plan is to be submitted via the Application Survey at the Business Portal of the New York State Education Department by December 2, 2015 for review and approval by the Smart Schools Review Board; NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED, that the Board of Education of the Irvington UFSD be and hereby approves the Public School District Smart Schools Bond Act Investment Plan; and, be it further RESOLVED, that the Board of Education authorizes the Superintendent of Schools or his/her designee to submit the Public School District Smart Schools Bond Act Investment Plan to the New York State Education Department by July 1st, 2016 for review and approval by the Smart Schools Review Board; and RESOLVED, that this resolution shall take effect immediately.
