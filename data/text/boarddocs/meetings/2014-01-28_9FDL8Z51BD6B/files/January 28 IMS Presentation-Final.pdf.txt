Reading more complex texts, carefully

What does it mean for students at IMS?
 Reading challenging texts as well as “just right” texts

 Learning strategies to apply to discern meaning from difficult
    texts

 More close reading of short texts (articles, short stories, etc.)

 Reading, and re-reading

 Note-taking, “coding” text, recording of thinking

The Earth's surface is covered by 71% of oceans. In those oceans there are 1000's of creatures,
one the main ones being fish. Due to higher human demands, overfishing, and a dependency,
fish are being hurt and humans are a main cause.

Our Pollution
   Sewage discharge and fertilizer runoff from farms, golf courses and lawns enter the ocean's surface.
All of these human things increase algae growth. When these algae grow, they take up oxygen and create
dead zones. Dead zones are places that there are not many fish because there isn't enough oxygen for
the fish, so they are forced to move. When algae grow, they also produce toxins that poison aquatic
organisms, which include fish. The fishs' gills then get clogged with the toxins and die from suffocation.
The fish also eat smaller fish, whom may have consumed poison. This could result in us eating a fish that
has ingested poison. That has resulted in illness and in some cases death for us (Walls 2). Our careless oil
spills destroy fish habitats, like the Bluefin Tuna's. The spill polluted their mating grounds an shifted their
migration. These polluted area will have an effect on fish for years. Heavy metals from our
industrialization and their byproducts can gather by lakes and rivers. These are harmful to marine
organisms such as fish, which will affect the rest of the food chain and come back to us. In fact, in "2003 a
scientific report estimated that industrial fishing had reduced the number of large ocean fish to just 10%
of their pre-industrial population." (National Geographic Ocean 1) With this huge loss, it is obvious that
we are doing something wrong. When are people going to see that we are running out of fish. There is a
problem and it will come back to us if we don't correct it. People have gotten really sick from eating fish
that we poisoned. We poisoned! We are hurting fish and it has hurt us. If this isn't a clear indicator that we
have to be more careful with metals, and oil, I don't know what is.

In Grade 6, instructional time should focus on
four critical areas:

 Ratios and Proportional Relationships - connecting ratio
    and rate to whole number multiplication and division and
    using concepts of ratio and rate to solve problems;

 The Number System - completing understanding of
    division of fractions and extending the notion of number to
    the system of rational numbers, which includes negative
    numbers;

 Expressions and Equations - writing, interpreting, and
    using expressions and equations; and

 Statistics and Probability - developing understanding of
    statistical thinking.

Shift 2 (Coherence): As part of the
discussion surrounding this activity,
students are exposed to various
terminology from later grades. For example,
for a proportional relationship such as #1,
students learn to identify the rate (60
minutes/day). We discuss how this value
takes on different names throughout the
years (slope, constant of variation, rate of
change, dilation factor) depending on the
specifics of the course.

Shift 4 (Deep Understanding): By
representing these two-variable
relationships in multiple forms, students
are attaching greater meaning to the more
abstract forms. A student who sees
consistent increase in a table and graph is
better able to understand the relationship
between x & y. The questioning used in
connection with our evaluation of student
work as a performance assessment is
another example. Students are not simply
asked to reproduce the correct written
work, but also to be able to explain how
and why they did so.
