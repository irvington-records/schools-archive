     1) State the specific classroom/school needs your project addresses. How does the proposed
        project build on existing programs? Please include how this project incorporates the
        curriculum and is linked to State and/or national standards.
        This grant reflects our goals as a district. We have made a commitment to exploring and
        expanding our use of technology in the classrooms from two vantage points, hardware and
        instructional design. Current technology capabilities and student individual access to technology
        has opened doors for teaching and learning that over time will increase the demand for teachers'
        use of technology as a part of daily instruction. In order to support the growth of technology in the
        classroom, we need models of instruction. This pilot will provide us with models and allow us to
        consider varied approaches to teaching and learning through different content areas. Moving
        forward we would expect to have students using their own devices as well as ours, but for this pilot
        we need to ensure that all students have access and a platform that can be integrated by our pilot
        teachers. The Google platform will do this.

     2) Describe your project in detail. Please be specific, including how students will engage in the
        activity and how you will measure whether or not this project has been successful. Indicate
        methods you will use to accomplish and publicize your project.
        Pilot Goal:
        To expand the perception and possibilities of learning environments in the Irvington School
        District through increased use of technology.

         Pilot Objectives:
         • To support teachers and students as they explore innovative practices for teaching and
             learning.
         • To create models for future learning and higher expectations of the integration of technology
             in the Irvington School District.
         • To engage students in learning environments that more accurately reflects real life and 21 st
             Century learning.

   •    To encourage students to collaborate and think "outside the box" as they learn and grow
       through unique teaching strategies such as flipped classroom.
   •    To embed the skills and dispositions we believe necessary for our students - problem solver,
       flexible thinker, collaborative learner, effective communicator, empathetic citizen, self-reliant,
       reflective, creative, risk-taker.
   •    To create classroom environments focused on increased use of technology reflected in the
       instructional design of a 1:1 student - device ratio.
   •    To evaluate Chromebooks as a viable option for our preparedness of the upcoming PARCC
       Assessments.

   Four pilot programs will be implemented from February to June 2014. Each is designed to provide
   groups of students who have a common daily learning experience in a classroom setting with a
   Chromebook. In these classrooms, teachers will be designing instruction to include the
   Chromebook as an integral element of learning. Students will be provided with a Chromebook for
   their own use 24/7. Each student will be required to agree to and sign an acceptable and
   appropriate use policy which will also require parent consent Use of the Chromebook will be
   promoted throughout the school day by students, but expectations for instructional design
   incorporating the Chromebooks will be held to pilot classrooms only. Teachers will be provided
   with initial as well as on-going training for the Chromebook and most specifically in the use of
   GoogleDocs. . The Google Apps for Education suite supports the creation of word processing
   documents, slideshow presentations, spreadsheets, and electronic forms. These documents can be
   worked on collaboratively in real-time, by both teachers and students, and they can be shared with
   the world or kept private according to specific needs. Additionally, by leveraging powerfUl Google
   Scripts that are publically available, many of these processes of creating and sharing these
   collaborative documents can be automated, allowing teachers to maximize their time and
   efficiency. All students and teachers will have Google accounts and instructional design will
   include the use of this platform to increase student collaboration and innovation.

   Although other devices may be worthy of a pilot, we chose the Chromebook for the following
   reasons:
   • The Chromebook wi/l allow us to monitor student use and provide us with a universal platform
       for email, document and media development
   • The platform allows us to push out applications across all users as needed.
   • The ease of operation and standardization supports our exploration and collaboration as we
       launch our first pilot in this area.

   As part of our pilot we will be collecting data to inform our knowledge and understanding of the
   success of the program. Surveys as well as focus groups will be designed for data collection from
   teachers, students and parents. Data collection will begin at the initial stage of the pilot so as to
   collect baseline data specifically targeted at perceptions as well as current use/application. Data
   will be collected throughout the pilot
