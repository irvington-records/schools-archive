	5. Grades 6-8 Common Core Learning Standards Presentation (Presentation)

	Presentation: 5.1 Grades 6-8 Common Core Learning Standards

	Principal David Sottile, Assistant Principal, Joyce Chapnick and Asst. Supt. of Instruction, Dr. Kor presented an overview of the shifts taking place in ELA, literacy and math at the middle school level. In the area of ELA and literacy, students will be reading at a deeper level, and be required to provide evidence to substantiate and support their thinking. Ms. Chapnick outlined the domains for reading and the progression of grade-level specific standards, noting that a common language is now used through K-12, with increased sophistication and greater expectations in the high level but the terminology remains the same. Mr. Sottile highlighted that a focus on building content knowledge, with literacy in all the core subject areas with students practicing their inference skills and providing textural evidence in their writing. Strategies are provided to use text-based evidence to discuss texts and support arguments. Assured writing experiences ensures that all students will be provided with the same experiences across the grade level. Dr. Kor illustrated how skills learned at the fifth grade level are then built upon at the middle school with increased expectations, however the standard remains the same.

	An overview of the math instruction in the middle school level was provided. Math instruction utilizes modules and continues to use some of the existing resources that align with the Common Core, with common language among all teachers providing coherence among all grade levels, with a clear progression through various topics, leading to greater student successes. Mr. Sottile provided an outline of the focus of each grade level in mathematics, again building on the previous level, with gaining a deeper level of understanding at each level Examples of students work were used to illustrate their experiences and again noted that the use of a common language aids in the deepening understanding as students move to higher levels. It was noted that middle school students are now moving towards solving algebraic problems.

	Dr. Kor highlighted one of the next steps for the middle school is to identify where there are gaps and how best to fill in those gaps. She noted that in the areas of Science and social studies the next generation of these standards are expected to be adopted by the State and will align with the Common Core Standards. The subject matter will be different in these areas so that content will need to be shifted between grade levels as necessary. At this time, only the framework has been provided and the standards have not yet been provided by the State.

	Board members thanked the administration for their informative presentation and asked questions regarding texts for both shared experiences and independent reading opportunity. Mrs. Kashkin asked that since there are no common assessments to determine a student’s reading level, how are teachers are assessing a student’s appropriate reading level.  Ms. Chapnick reported that students have experiences reading shared texts but also have opportunities to choose texts to read independently. Teachers continually assess a student's ability as well as the types of books that would be appropriate. Book logs are utilized to also guide student's reading. Currently there is not a common assessment tool used to determine a student's reading ability but that there are many ways for a teacher to assess a student's ability.

	Mr. Montgomery asked how we would evaluate to determine how the Common Core is working. Mr. Sottile noted that through classroom observations and listening to students talk about their work, he is led to believe that there is a shift taking place and that progress is being made towards meeting the standards as students gain knowledge in the content levels as well as developing a deeper understanding of the content.

	Mr. Whitney asked what methods are used to have students read explicitly and to make logical inferences (anchor #1). Dr. Kor said that each grade now has a benchmark to ensure that all students leave a grade with the same mastery. She also noted that instruction will be differentiated to ensure that students are working at their individual level.

	Mrs. Kerner expressed concerns with the many changes and asked how teachers are doing with the shifts and how do we ensure that a mediocre teacher is meeting the standards. It was noted that viś a viś the APP, the are many opportunities for administrators to be in classrooms and because of the common language of the CCLS, it is apparent on observation on how a teacher is doing. In response to a question regarding the availability of a list of books that a child might be reading at a particular grade level, Dr. Kor responded that NYS has identified a genre and then name texts that would be appropriate at a particular grade level.

	Mr. Grados stated that the CCLS has been imposed mid-cycle for students in the higher grade levels and the struggle to learn a way and acquire the language that is associated with the CCLS. Mr. Montgomery asked if there might be value in soliciting parental feedback about what they see through their children as to how the curriculum is working.

	11. Presentations/Discussions

	Discussion: 11.1 Technology Discussion

	Dr. Harrison noted that the administration acknowledges the need for communication and dialogue as there as there are shifts in the classroom and the impact of technology on teaching and learning is an area to focus on, technology in instruction as well as instruction in technology. He noted that the needs ion regarding technology  Dr. Harrison noting that the impact of technology and teaching and learning and the integration of technology has been a focused and are passionate to reimagine how it is changing in the classroom. The District must think about how it is impacting our children and be sure that there is balance and the difference between technology in teaching and teaching technology. Need to think about how the layers of technology will make their way into the classrooms, i.e. document sharing, etc. and what are our needs will be. The approach has been to be agile and to do trial runs to see how the role is in curriculum and what needs teachers might need in the area of staff development. The purchase of Chromebooks will enable the District to plant seeds and explore what opportunities there might be. The Think Tank

	Dr. Kor addressed the particular initiatives within the District and reiterated that the District wishes to embed technology in classrooms. A pilot program will begin at Dows Lane through the award of ipads. The trial is exploratory to determine the efficacy in reality. At the middle school, there is a initiative to help students who have organizational difficulties to bring their own devise and to learn how technology supports their learning. The flipped classroom is being piloted in grades 6-12 whereby the students get the content outside the school and within the classroom, the teacher provides support. The use of Google Apps is associated with the purchase of the Chrombooks. Mr. Lubinsky, Technology Coordinator, noted that the District can assign accounts within its own domain and is able to control access at the student level.

	Looking at all the different experiences of both teachers and students, the goal is to collect information to provide the direction for moving forward. The upcoming Think Tank will provide an opportunity to focus on what classrooms will look like vis a vis technology. The District will also need to develop policies around the use of technology, with student’s bringing their own devices and protocols. There will also need to be a conversation regarding technology leadership as there is currently limited leadership and need to look at how to use resources differently.

	Mr. Whitney asked for a better understanding of goals or what problems are trying to be solved for and would begin the defining of a plan and then assess whether an initiative is successful or need to move into another area.Dr. Kor said the conversation should be about utilizing technology and changing the perception of what instruction looks like and not the hardware.

	Mrs. Kerner said that a timeline would be helpful to determine what the targets are. Mr. Grados noted that technology does tie in with the long term planning. Dr. Harrison says the expectation is to begin to roll out initiatives broad scale in September. The other issues can be addressed by administration.

	Mrs. Kashkin feels that there are two different conversations, i.e, use of technology in a classroom and technology in the classrooms. She believes that there needs to be a presentation on the different ways to reach the kids and explaining what a flipped classroom looks like or the use of Google Apps. She is looking forward to having two different conversations on these topics. She would like a clearer understanding of the initiatives underway. Mr. Montgomery stated that there is a District goal concerning technology and that the conversation does not address that goal. His concern that there is an element of putting cart before the horse, making adoption of technology the goal in and of itself instead of a means to making instruction better or improved. Would like to hear how the pilot programs are evaluated, especially the flipped classroom as he has concerns about a one way lecture. He also suggested that instead of waiting for a large technology presentation, to have small portions of conversations about the subject.

	With regard to the upcoming Think Tank, Mrs. Kerner noted that she has heard comments about the time, 4-7:00pm, is difficult for parents to participate and if there is a limited turnout, there might be consideration to changing the time. She also wonders if there is a need to bring in someone with extensive technology experience to help define the needs, looking at it from a budget standpoint, and feels it is more important that having resources earmarked for public relations. She also asked about the staff development component in the process. Dr. Kor stressed that the reality is that we need to figure out how technology will be an organic thing in every classroom but it is there. It is not able to be measured as you are asking. Teachers are being asked how do they know if the students are learning from using the flipped classroom, The commitment to our students and parents is to providing the opportunities. Mr. Montgomery asked that feedback from parents be sought when evaluating the initiatives. Mr. Whitney feels that as a Board member there are budgetary implication’s and will be asked to approve expenditures without having clarity. Mr. Grados stated that there is concern among the Board and community that we need to narrow down the questions and issues, i.e. the use of technology in the classroom, the curricular aspect of teaching technology and need to keep separate to find focus. There is an inherent chicken and egg, i.e, do you know the curriculum you want to teach using Google Apps or it could be the other way around. Also stated that it must be recognized that students need to be more than users but producers and what are those skills that we should be teaching the students. It is a changing and competitive world and must figure out what we need to give the students and might it might have little or nothing to do with devices. He would also like to have information regarding age-appropriate devices which might require an informed expert to lead the discussion.

	Presentation: 11.2 2014-15 Budget Presentation-Business & Finance, Debt Service, Buildings & Grounds, Transportation, Insurance and Interfund Transfers (Presentation)

	The presentation focused on the budget areas of buildings and grounds, transportation, debt service, insurance, and interfund transfers and highlighted the variances between the current budget and the proposed budget in these areas. Mrs. Beverly Miller and Mr. Gary Knowles provided an overview of the operations and maintenance proposed budget, which calls for a 5.53% increase over the 2013-14 school year. Mr. Knowles reviewed the districtwide building repairs  that have been identified and total $217,648. He also reviewed proposed capital building repairs which includes but are not limited to upgrades to security cameras at the campus, replacement of millwork at Dows Lane as well as sanding, restripping and resealing the campus gym floor.

	The transportation budget has an reduction of -2.06% for next year as a result of a reduction due to the shared services and increased efficiency of the Quad Village Consortium. Of note, the District no longer needs to contract with BOCES for any transportation and results in a savings of $50,000.

	The debt service also shows a reduction of -1.02% for next year’s proposed budget due to the bond refinancing that was done earlier in the year, etc. Other budget codes include Board of Education, administration, District Clerk, election expenses and other special items. Dr. Harrison noted that the increases are due to the anticipated bond referendum as well as an increase of meetings to videotaping. Mr. Montgomery questioned the use of a professional communications service and would like to have further discussions on the matter. Of note, the State now mandates that districts pay 34%of tuition paid to state schools, such as NY School for the deaf.

	In summary, this segment of the budget reflects a 1.44%, half due to salaries and half to other factors.

	Questions from the Board included capital expenses, need to know what judgment calls have been made by administration in order to understand the thought process behind the development of the proposals. The Board would like to see a wish list in order to weigh choices. Mr. Grados disagreed that the administration was tasked to deliver a budget under cap, and to ask for items that would be over the tax cap is a waste of time. Mrs, Kerner stated that she does not have a level of comfort and wants to understand the numbers behind items and in the end, it is the Board’s judgment call as to final budget that is put before the voters.

	Mrs. Kerner noted that the increase in transportation appears to be lower than the cost of living and that usually transportation increases by at least the CPI. Mrs. Miller noted that there have been savings due to efficiencies found. The consortium also agreed to extend at CPI.

	Mr. Montgomery asked that it would be helpful for comparison sake to provide current FTE's if proposed budget calls for an increase in FTE. He also made a general comment that maintenance and capital spending needs to be looked at as a whole and to examine what we should be spending each year to maintain and to determine if we are maintaining the facilities as we should be. He would like to better understand projects that might be included in any referendum and would not like to get into a situation where the District has to bond for items that might be included in the operating budget.

	The Board had a conversation about bonding projects vs.incorporating items in the operating budget as there is a cost associated with bonding. The point was made that decisions shouldn’t be made based on budget target or bond target but what is the best thing to do for facilities. Mr. Montgomery would like to get more information on how many students use the transportation to private schools, including which schools. As for transportation, is there any mechanism in place to see if the students are actually using transportation (to private schools), however, the District is legally obligated to provide transportation if a parent has filed an application and it is on file.
