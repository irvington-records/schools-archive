5. Discussions/Presentations

Information:  5.1 Discussion on School Safety (Presentation)

Dr. Harrison welcomed Village Trustee, Janice Silverberg, and Irvington Police Chief Cerrone to the meeting and noted that Mayor Smith was unable to attend tonight, but they have had conversations regarding school safety. He noted that the communication between the District and the Irvington Police Department is on going and they are good partners in keeping the school safe, including having a youth safety officer and officers are interacting in our schools with students and staff.  Dr. Harrison provided an overview of what has been done in the District to ensure safe learning environments, i.e. comprehensive emergency plans, regular emergency drills in each school, addition of five security guards, one per school and one roving guard, staff members wear ID badges, addition of buzzer-system at Dows Lane and MSS. In the coming days and weeks there will be increased police patrols, additional security guard, additional learning for staff with Irvington Police Department,emergency classroom door securty measures, enahnced guest protocols, complete a security audit and gather feedback to inform next steps. Dr. Harrison addressed large-scale enhancements that will require planning and design, engagement with consultants, a capital bond referendum for funding. Next steps include but not limited to conducting a security audit.

Mr. Hanna noted that the Board is very appreciative of all that is being done in the District to ensure everyone's safety including the partnership with the Irvington Police. He thanked Chief Cerrone and Janice Silverberg for attending.

Community Members Comments:

Nine parents expressed their opinions and/or their children's experiences as it pertains to school safety, and, in particular, the recent lockout due to an incident involving a shooting in Tarrytown where the suspect was being sought.  Several expressed their feelings that communications around the subject must be improved to provide more detailed information as to measures that are being taken, that there are situations where there is lack of security measures, such as unlocked doors at the campus, students do not feel safe, what measures are being taken to assist students who have been identified as troubled and that it is important to act quickly to recitify any safety concerns that have been identified. Several parents felt that their children are safe in the schools  and that there have been improvements over the last several years but that there is always room for improvement. A parent responded to the comment for greater transparency in safety protocols, stating that safety plans must be kept confidential. She also assured parents that the schools are proactive when there is a student who is in need of support and do communicate with paretns. A student spoke about her fear of attending school and thanked the Board for having this discussion and offered suggestions as to ways to better secure classrooms and to also include students in the discussion and perhaps to have students reach out to other students.  Dr. Harrison noted that several of her suggestions have been discussed at the administrative level already and thanked her for sharing her feelings.

Presentation:  5.2 Presentation of Proposed 2018-19 School Calendar (Presentation)

Dr. Harrison provided an overview of the components and complexities in developing the school calendar and reiterated that SED requires 184 school days which are inclusive of up to four Superintendent's Conference Days. The first day for students will be September 4th and the last day will be June 25th. The 2018-19 calendar will now have two (2), 1 hour early release days per month from October-May on the first and third Wednesdays of the month to provide for professional learning and allows for four snow days. The proposed calendar will be posted on the district's website and the Board will vote to approve the calendar at its March 20th meeting.

Presentation:  5.3 2018-19 Budget Presentation: Curriculum & Instruction Components (Presentation)

Beginning with an overview of the budget development process and the alignment of the budget with the District's strategic objectves, the curriculum and instruction components of the proposed budget were presented. Components include: instruction (teachers, teacher aides, textbooks, supplies, etc.), Library/Media Center (including resource materials, online reference databases), Guidance, Instructional Technology, Curriculum Development/professional development, supervision, co-curricular and interscholastic athletics, special education, including services to John Cardinal O'Connor School (reimbursed by students' home districts), and health services, psychologists, social worker.general (and special education, education support, instructional technology, extra curricular/athletics, benefits and other. The proposed budget for these areas is currently  $47,742,012. New curriculum considerations include:

- .5 Art teacher at Middle/High School ($37,500 + benefits);

- K-2 Project Lead The Way (PLTW) STEM Teacher ($75,000 + benefits);

- 7th/8th Grade Special Education Teacher ($75,000 + benefits);

- K-5 Guidance Counselor ($75,000 + benefits)

- Assistant coaches for athletic teams (38,000)

- Flexible classroom furniture ($33,390)

The other components of the budget were reviewed, and results in a total for the instructional proposed budget of $34,034,645 or a $1,583,469 (4.9%) increase over the 2017-18 approved instructional budget of $32,451,176.

A recap of all budget components presented during the budget development process results in the following:

Total proposed budget = $61,499,383 ($2,398,889 or a 4.1%) variance;

Allowable Tax Levy Cap Within Tax Cap = 4.24%;

Projected Revenue = $61,246,535 (2,146,041 or 3.6%) variance;

Appropriation from Fund Balance = $422,500

A review of budget trends since 2011-12 was reviewed and currently, the proposed 2018-19 budget of $61,499,383 represents a 4.06% budget increase and an estimated increase of 1.9% or tax rate of $19.50 per M which is baased on assessed valuation as of January 23, 2018.

Action (Consent):  7.1 School Safety Resolution

Resolution: Whereas, the safety of all children in our schools is of paramount importance; and Whereas the proposed 5% reduction to the current Federal education budget would include $425 million in cuts to mental health and school safety programs, such as the School Emergency Response to Violence Program and Project Prevention Grants; and Whereas seventeen students and staff members were killed and many others were injured as a result of a school shooting in Parkland, Florida on February 14, 2018; and Whereas in the past five years, 439 students and staff members have been shot in more than 239 school shootings across our Country ; and Whereas a need for greater access to mental health services for students and families in our schools and communities was ranked as a top funding priority by the majority of Superintendents in New York State ; and Whereas we believe that every child has the right to a safe school environment that has an appropriate level of security as determined by our community; and Whereas a reasoned and rational approach to gun control and increased access to mental health services for students and families in our schools and communities are essential elements of a safe school environment; and Whereas the Irvington School District fosters civic discourse and promotes student understanding of government and current issues; and Whereas a coordinated effort at the Federal and State levels, boosted by law enforcement and community engagement, is needed to change the paradigm and protect our communities and our schools; NOW THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED that: The Board of Education of the Irvington School District calls upon the New York State Assembly and New York State Senate to implement a reasoned and rational approach to address gun control and background checks, and prioritize funding and resources for increased access to mental health services for students.

Motion by Maria Kashkin, second by Brian Friedman.

Final Resolution: Motion Carries

Yes: Michael Hanna, Maria Kashkin, Catherine Palmieri, Deborah Hargraves, Maura Gedid, Brian Friedman
