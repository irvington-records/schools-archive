	4. Presentations/Discussions

	Discussion: 4.1 2015-16 Budget Discussion and Revisions (Presentation)

	Dr. Harrison reviewed the budget development objectives and the process of developing the proposed budget. Adjustments have been made to the recommended budget such as reductions in special education tuitions, greater savings in bond refunding, etc. The original recommended budget was $23,542 under the tax cap, or a 2.95% budget to budget increase, an a tax rate increase of 4.36%, partly due to a shift in the assessments which then increases the tax rate.

	At the March 24th meeting, Board members expressed their concern about the tax rate and asked for the budget to be revisited, with looking at priorities and adjustments to lower the tax rate.

	To achieve a less than 4% tax rate (3.95% tax rate increase) it was necessary to reduce the budget by $200,000. Reductions were categorized by Tier I, II and II. To arrive at a 3.95% increase, Tier I includes adjustment to the technology lease, bond refunding and a reduction in ENL of .5FTE. The additional state aid of $110,000 was also used to meet the objective.

	Tier II provides for a 3.75% tax rate increase and includes delaying a 5th grade World Language program, reduction in the substitute budget line, as well repairing the IMS parking lot as opposed to repaving it, which all result in a total reduction of $317,072 in Tier I and II reductions. Dr. Harrison noted that the 5th grade world language program will be looked at over the next year and possibly included in the 2016-17 budget.

	Tier III would further reduce the budget by $116,000, resulting in a 3.5% tax rate increase, and includes the elimination of the request to add a 1.0 FTE for the MS special education and a reduction in unpaid leave salaries. All reductions/eliminations total $433,072 from the original recommended budget ($58,143,665) with a new budget total of $57,954,435

	Potential tax rates were reviewed as they relate to the three tiers of reductions, 3.94%, 3.71% and 3.48% respectively. Ms. Miller addressed the changes to the tax cap, most notably the changes in the amount of bond refunding, etc. allowing for a 3.00% allowable tax levy increase.

	Dr. Kor spoke to the possibility of adding a 1.0 FTE ELA coach which the Board asked administration to provide what that might look like at the last meeting. Should this proposal go forward, further assessment of such a position would be addressed as to where the greatest impact of such a position would . She also noted that the loss of the teaching/learning facilitators from a past budget, has had a negative impact on the District and such a position would greatly benefit the ELA program. Dr. Harrison noted that this position has not been included in the budget, and would be approximately $96,000.

	Discussion: 4.2 Discussion of the Role & Responsibilities of the Policy Committee

	Mr. Montgomery addressed the question as to how, as a Board, they wish to address the District’s policy review. He noted that NYSSBA provides a book of recommended policies, reviews what policies we do not have, compares our existing policies and suggests language to ensure that they are compliant with current laws and/or regulations. They will also provide draft policies when needed. Draft policies that are provided also indicate if it is a required policy but Mr. Montgomery would like to know why it is required and he asks if it is conceived that the Board uses policies to attempt  impose their values and the communities on the District or a way for the Board to have an influence and help set goals and direction of the District through the process or is it more thought of as a legal process. A separate question he has is if there are existing regulations or laws that schools must follow, should the regulation and/or law be codified in policy or is it enough to have the District follow the regulations. Mrs. Kashkin added that she personally looks at a policy manual as a reference book that administrators, parents, and teachers can refer to when determining what the rules and regulations are and she sees it as an abbreviated version of NYS law, which is often difficult to locate the information as to requirements. Mr. Whitney agrees with Mrs. Kerner that the manual is a public document and provides for transparency. Mr. Whitney sees it as a benefit and not a detriment since we must follow the regulations. Mr. Montgomery stated again that he is not sure how detailed the policies should be.

	Mrs. Kashkin believes that the charge of the policy committee is to take 18 months to review and update the policy manual to comply with laws and regulations. Mr. Montgomery agrees, but the question is to what degree certain language needs to be inserted into a particular policy. Dr. Harrison stated that it is a two-pronged process, one to address needed policies and be sure our policies are in compliance and 2) looking to what policies Irvington would want included in the policy manual, noting that a commitment must be made to this arduous task.

	Mr. Montgomery asked if the Board would want to make, as a matter of course, to be provided that the reasoning behind the proposed policy, i.e. regulations, what the current practice is and what changes are required as that would be helpful to have that information available when discussing a policy. As an example, he provided the current draft of the homebound instruction policy.

	Mrs. Kashkin feels that it is a policy by policy review and that there is not one clear way to handle the review, where some will be simple and others will require discussion on processes and practice in a particular area. She also noted that there will be circumstances where there won’t be agreement among the members but that it will be up to the Board as a whole to adopt or not.
