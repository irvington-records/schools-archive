Dear Parents,

In Irvington, we continue to strive for a balance that considers all aspects of a
child’s learning. Our classrooms provide opportunities for students to learn
and grow in all academic areas, the arts, as well as health and wellness. A
priority for us has, and will continue to be the social emotional well-being of
each child. Our goal is consistent in that we want success for all students, and
know that our partnership with parents is one of our greatest strengths as a
school community.

This guide was developed to serve as a reference for parents. It provides an
overview of the Standards, as well as the detailed information for each of the
Standards for your child’s grade level. In addition, a set of Frequently Asked
Questions (FAQ’s) are provided as a quick way to learn about the changes to
the report card. We suggest that as you review your child’s report card, that
you turn to page 13 of this guide where you will find a detailed explanation of
each item on the report card for ELA and math.

We hope you find this guide helpful and welcome your feedback and ideas
for making this a more effective tool.
                             Frequently Asked Questions


Q: How do I know what the number (4, 3, 2, 1) grade means?

A: A standards-based report card’s scaled approach (4, 3, 2, 1) provides information
about student achievement based on daily work and classroom assessments. The
scaled descriptions can be found on the report card and reflect expectations, as well as
standards of work defined at each of the levels. Throughout the year, your child’s
assessments, as well as classwork, will vary based on time of year and the curriculum.

Q: Can a student perform at a level 3 and then move to a lower level in the next
marking period?

A: Expectations, as well as curriculum, will change from one marking period to the next
as students move through the year. This means:
A student may meet the grade-level expectation during the first marking period, but as
the expectations increase or the curriculum content changes, the student may not
demonstrate the same level of proficiency during the next marking period. Therefore, a
student might receive a 3 in the first marking period and then receive a 2 in the second
marking period.

Q: Why are some criterion marked NA

A: Based on the standards, some curriculum areas will reflect certain sets of skills to be
applied throughout the year. Other curriculum units are based on specific topics and
content and as a result, are taught in set timeframes and only for a portion of the school
year. In these cases NA is used to indicate this work is not being assessed in that
marking period.
