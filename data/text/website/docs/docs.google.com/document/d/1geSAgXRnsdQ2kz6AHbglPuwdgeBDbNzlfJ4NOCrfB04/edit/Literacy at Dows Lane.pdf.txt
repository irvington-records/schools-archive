Literacy at Dows Lane
                 New York State English Language Arts Learning Standards




At Dows Lane, we have a structured literacy philosophy for teaching reading and writing. Structured
Literacy encompasses a variety of instructional approaches such as Explicit instruction, Guided
Reading, Shared Reading/Writing, Read Aloud, and Interactive Reading/Writing workshops.
Instruction is explicit, direct, and focused on the structure of language. Our literacy instruction
includes the Big 6 in literacy: oral language, phonological awareness, phonics, fluency,
comprehension, and vocabulary. We believe that reading and writing are directly connected, and
students develop their skills through an integrated approach to literacy- reading, writing, listening and
speaking.
At the heart of our literacy instruction is the overarching goal of fostering a love of reading and writing.
Within Arts and Letters, our literacy curriculum, students are immersed in opportunities as readers
and writers to develop fluency and comprehension as they build knowledge and engage with texts.
Each module is NYS standards based, and includes reading and writing experiences.
Students in Kindergarten-1st grade, have explicit instruction using multisensory, structured literacy
instruction, using programs: Heggerty and Wilson Fundations, “... a research-based approach and
extensive structured reading, spelling, and handwriting curriculum using engaging, multisensory
techniques.” Fundations and Heggerty are both aligned with the science of reading. In 2nd grade,
students continue with a multisensory approach, using Wilson Fundations. In 3rd grade, teachers use
Wilson Fundations FunHub in small groups, as needed.

Teachers and Reading Specialists use many resources to support literacy instruction, such as:
Heggerty, Orton-Gillingham, Wilson Fluency, The Writing Revolution, Hochman Method, UFLI
(University of Florida Literacy Institute), Shifting the Balance, by Katie Cunningham, and Reading/
Writing Strategies with Jen Serravallo. There are decodable texts and high interest books in all
classrooms. Cursive writing is valued and taught in 3rd grade using Wilson Fundations Cursive
program. Classroom teachers use the Arts and Letters assessments, Dibels, Daffodil Hill Press for
word level assessments and decodable texts to support students as they progress. All students are
assessed for growth in reading throughout the school year.

Reading is a highly complex process. Readers must build a system of strategic actions for processing
texts that begins with early reading behaviors and becomes a consistent network.
