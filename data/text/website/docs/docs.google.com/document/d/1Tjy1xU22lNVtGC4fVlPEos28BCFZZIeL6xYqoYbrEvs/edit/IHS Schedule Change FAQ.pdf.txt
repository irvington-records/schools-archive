IHS Schedule Change FAQ
            What are we doing and why are we doing it?

What is the purpose of changing IHS's schedule?
The schedule change aims to better support the Portrait of a Graduate by addressing
student burnout, reducing stress, and creating more meaningful learning
experiences. Current data shows that 1 in 4 teens experience burnout, and IHS
students report feeling overwhelmed with seven classes requiring 45 minutes of
homework each night.

Why is this change necessary now?
National trends show alarming rates of anxiety and depression among teenagers,
with 81% reporting significant pressure. IHS students specifically report feeling
disengaged, unknown by teachers, and constantly stressed. Nearly 10% of students
forgo lunch periods, indicating an unsustainable academic load.

How does the current schedule impact student learning?
The current 43-minute periods limit opportunities for meaningful, rigorous work.
Students report monotonous experiences, fear of talking to teachers, and excessive
focus on grades rather than learning. The compressed timeframe makes
project-based learning and deeper engagement challenging.

Schedule Options
What is the 4X4 Block Schedule (Pure Block)?
  ₋​ Format: 80-minute classes that meet every other day
  ₋​ Structure: Students take 4 classes per semester instead of 8 classes year-round
  ₋​ Benefits: Longer periods allow for deeper learning, project-based work, and
     reduced daily homework load

What is the Rotating Drop Schedule (Snake Model)?
  ₋​ Format: Students have 8 classes, but only 6 meet per day on a rotating basis
  ₋​ Structure: Class periods are approximately 57 minutes
  ₋​ Benefits: Maintains access to all courses while providing longer class periods
     than current system

How do these options compare to college and work environments?
Both options better prepare students for post-secondary success. College schedules
typically feature longer class periods with breaks between courses, similar to the
proposed models. This structure aligns with the Portrait of a Graduate goals and
real-world expectations.

Academic Concerns
How many courses will my students take?
Both schedules will allow students to take up to eight courses in addition to music.

How will this affect college preparation?
Both proposed schedules better align with college structures and expectations. The
longer periods allow for more in-depth learning, critical thinking, and project-based
work that colleges value. The reduced daily stress may actually improve academic
performance and college readiness.

Our neighbors (including Byram Hills, Chappaqua, Hastings, and Bronxville) who run
this schedule have excellent post high school outcomes.

How will this affect students with IEPs or 504s?
Supporting students with diverse learning needs is central to our planning. In block
or rotating schedules, students often benefit from fewer transitions, a reduced daily
workload, and more opportunities to complete work in class with the support of
teachers and peers. This structure can be especially helpful for learners with
executive functioning challenges. Longer periods also allow teachers to differentiate
instruction, conference individually with students, and provide targeted support.

What challenges might come with a new schedule?
Change always requires adjustment, and moving to longer class periods will be no
exception. Students and teachers may need time to adapt to a new rhythm of
learning, pacing, and homework routines. To support this transition, the district will
provide a clear rollout plan that includes training, coaching, and collaborative
planning time. Extensive professional development will help teachers refine their
instructional practices to make the most of longer periods, ensuring that the change
strengthens learning rather than disrupts it.


Student Wellbeing
How will this address student stress and burnout?
  ₋​ Reduced daily homework load across fewer concurrent classes
  ₋​ Longer periods allow for more in-class work completion
  ₋​ Better work-life balance with more sustainable academic expectations
  ₋​ Increased opportunities for meaningful teacher-student relationships

Will students have adequate lunch time?
Yes, both proposed schedules prioritize student wellbeing, including proper lunch
periods. The current system has nearly 10% of students skipping lunch, which the
new schedules will eliminate.

How will this support student engagement?
Longer class periods enable project-based learning, deeper discussions, and varied
instructional methods that combat the monotony students currently report.
Teachers will have more time to build relationships and create engaging learning
experiences.
