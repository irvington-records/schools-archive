                                               ENGLISH
English 9: Introduction to Literary Study *​     ​        ​       ​       ​        ​       ​        ​
(Full year, 1 credit)

English 9 transitions readers and writers from middle school to high school literary study. Emphasis is
placed on close reading of increasingly complex texts from diverse perspectives; literary analysis of key
ideas, craft, and structure; and the processes for developing strong written and verbal expression.

English 9: Introduction to Literary Study - Honors*
(Full year, 1 credit)
Placement in English 9 Honors will be determined by final average for 8th grade English.

Students with a final average of 93 or higher in English 8 will be invited to enroll in English 9
Honors.


English 9 Honors requires students to engage in a rigorous reading and writing curriculum. The class will
read texts in addition to the English 9 curriculum, many of which are representative of diverse styles and
perspectives, and students will be asked to apply sophisticated comprehension and inferential skills to
these texts. Students will engage in both in‐class and out‐of class writing, and work on formal lengthy
pieces as well as shorter, informal pieces.

9th Grade Writing Studio
(1/2 credit)

Much like an advanced art class, the Writer’s Studio will provide an opportunity for students to explore
and engage in the creative process of writing. Throughout the course, students will learn techniques,
approaches, and processes to generate ideas and express them through the written word. Using the work
of other writers as models for their own work, students will explore and compose a variety of genres for
multiple, authentic purposes and audiences. They will also, through ongoing reflection and feedback
from their teachers and peers, make purposeful revisions to improve their pieces.


Newspaper
(full year, 1 credit, ½ year)
For elective credit only

The course will provide instruction and hands‐on experience in reporting and writing, editing, layout and
production of school media, as well as the opportunity to be published in the student‐operated newspaper,
The Paw Print, and website, The Paw Print Online. Students will also explore various facets of broadcast
journalism, including video and podcasting.

Global Studies I *
(full year, 1 credit)
This course is part of a two year sequence which studies the sweep of Global history from Ancient
Times until the present. Global Studies I, in 9th grade, studies the period from Ancient Times until
the first Global Age of the eighteenth century. The focus is on understanding historical thinking
skills, other cultures, anthropology, varying perspectives, and political, economic, and social issues,
especially in a comparative way.


Global Studies I Honors *
(full year, 1 credit)
Prerequisite: Students will be required to fulfill entrance criteria for Honors selection.
This course is much like Global Studies I in respect to content. Reading requirements are more
demanding in quantity and likewise necessitate more sophisticated reading skills. Assignments are
designed to challenge the more capable student. Assessments are more challenging than in Global
Studies I. Admission into the course is based upon student grades in previous coursework in Social
Studies.
                                        MATHEMATICS


Algebra I (full year, 1 credit)*
Prerequisite: Math 8 with a minimum final grade of C and/or departmental permission.

This course for freshmen examines the fundamentals of algebra using an integrated curriculum with
real‐world applications as it correlates to the NYS Next Generation Curriculum. The topics include:
pre-algebra foundations, solving linear equations & inequalities, graphing linear equations &
inequalities, solving systems of linear equations & inequalities, operations with polynomials,
factoring polynomials, solving & interpreting quadratic equations, relations & functions and
statistics.. Students take the Algebra I Regents exam in June.


Algebra IA (full year, 1 credit) *
Prerequisite: Math 8

This course for freshmen is the first half of a two-year program in Algebra that examines the
fundamentals of algebra using an integrated curriculum with real‐world applications as it correlates
to the NYS Next Generation Curriculum. The topics include: pre-algebra foundations, solving linear
equations & inequalities, graphing linear equations & inequalities, solving systems of linear
equations & inequalities, and operations with polynomials.


Geometry NG (full year, 1 credit) *
Prerequisite: Algebra I and pass the Algebra I Regents Exam.

This course for sophomores and accelerated freshmen examines the fundamentals of geometry using
an integrated curriculum with real‐world applications and it correlates to the NYS Next Generation
Curriculum. The geometry topics include: direct Euclidean proofs of triangles, quadrilaterals and
circles, coordinate proofs, and three‐ dimensional figures, right triangle trigonometry, similar
figures, area, volume, constructions, and transformational geometry.

Geometry Honors (full year, 1 credit) *
Prerequisite: Algebra I with a minimum final grade of an 85 and departmental permission.

This course is composed of ninth graders who completed Algebra in 8th grade. Honors Geometry
examines the fundamentals of geometry using an integrated curriculum with real ‐world applications
as it correlates to the Next Generation Curriculum. The geometry topics include: congruence, proof
and constructions, similarity, proof and trigonometry, three dimensional geometry, coordinate
geometry, circle geometry and probability. Coursework and exams are designed with adequate
opportunities for challenge and rigor.
                              MATHEMATICS ELECTIVES


Computer Programming I (½ year, ½ credit)
Grades 9-12
Prerequisite: None

This course introduces students to computer programming with a focus on topics such as: program
planning and design, programming concepts of statements, control structures, functions, objects and
classes, events, iterations, and arrays. The language used in the course is Alice, which has a similar style
to Java. The course places an emphasis on problem solving. No prior experience with computer
programming is required. This course is the prerequisite course for Computer Programming II.

Computer Programming II (½ year, ½ credit)
Grades 9-12
Prerequisite: Successful completion of Computer Programming I

The course is for students who demonstrate both an interest and competence in computer programming,
and is a continuation of Computer Programming I. Students will utilize programming techniques
previously learned and implement the corresponding Java syntax. Topics include creation of methods,
importing classes, loops, mathematical functions, arrays, conditional statements, creation of classes,
strings, and input/output. This course serves as a precursor to Advanced Placement Computer Science.
                                                  SCIENCE

Regents Living Environment/Biology (full year, 1 credit) *
Students will develop skills such as critical thinking, developing and using models, interpreting and
analyzing data and constructing scientific explanations to study the structures, functions, and interactions
between living organisms and their roles in the environment. Topics include ecology, genetics, evolution,
and biochemistry. Students will take the New York State Living Environment Regents Exam provided
they have fulfilled the 1200 minute lab requirement

Honors Living Environment/Biology (full year, 1 credit) *
Prerequisites: Students will be required to fulfill entrance criteria for Honors selection.
Mandatory summer work must be completed by any student wishing to take the course.
Highly motivated students are expected to use skills such as critical thinking, developing and using
models, interpreting and analyzing data and constructing scientific explanations to study the
structures, functions, and interactions between living organisms and their roles in the environment.
This course is fast paced and rigorous where students will study topics in detail that include ecology,
genetics, evolution, and biochemistry. Students will take the New York State Living Environment
Regents Exam provided they have fulfilled the 1200 minute lab requirement.

AIS Biology (meets every other day for a full year) *
This course supplements the Regents Biology curriculum and should be taken along with Regents
Biology. Instruction will be tailored to students' individual needs and time will be spent developing the
scientific skills and content knowledge required to be successful in biology. Students will also spend time
preparing for the New York State Living Environment Regents Exam.

French/Spanish Novice (full year, 1 credit)
At the end of French/Spanish Novice, this is what you will be able to do...

in a conversation:
You can be part of a conversation about yourself and your life. Much of the time you can also use your
language to express your own thoughts and get the things you need. You tend to speak in phrases and
some sentences. You can often ask and answer simple questions. You can do all of this in a way that your
teacher and others who are used to language learners can understand what you are saying most of the
time.

when you read or listen to something:
You likely understand the main idea in short passages on very familiar topics but rarely the supporting
facts. You usually need to read/hear the passage more than once. You may need visual cues such as
pictures, your own knowledge of a topic, familiarity with parts of words or words that are similar to
English in order to understand what you read/hear.

when you write something that you have prepared:
You can write about yourself and your life. Much of the time you write well enough to express your own
thoughts and accomplish what you need. You write in phrases and some sentences. You can do all of this
in a way that most of the time your teacher and others who are used to language learners can understand
what you are writing most of the time.


French/Spanish Intermediate II (full year, 1 credit)
At the end of Intermediate II, this is what you will be able to do...

in a conversation:
You can maintain a conversation about yourself and your life. You also use your language to
express your own thoughts and get the things you need. You can use more than one sentence at a
time. You ask and answer questions. You do all of this in a way that your teacher and others who
are used to language learners easily understand what you are saying.

when you read or listen to something:
You understand main ideas and supporting facts in short passages on familiar topics. You may
need to read/hear the passage more than once. You may need visual cues, context clues, and prior
knowledge to help you understand what you read/hear.

when you write something that you have prepared:
You write about yourself and your life. You write well enough to accomplish what you need and
pose a variety of questions. You can write some longer sentences and connect some sentences
together. You do all of this in a way that your teacher and others who are used to language
learners readily understand your writing.

Latin 1 (full year, 1 credit)*

Latin 1 is a course which promotes literacy and cultural awareness. In addition to learning the
fundamentals of the Latin language, the student will be introduced to the Roman way of life,
Greco-Roman mythology and Roman history. Students develop an ability to handle the language in four
skill areas: listening, speaking, reading and writing. This course provides a firm foundation for the study
of modern foreign languages. In addition, students will gain a greater understanding of the English
language through the study of derivatives and English grammar as it relates to Latin.




                             WORLD LANGUAGE ELECTIVE

Ancient Greek I H - An Introduction to Ancient Greek (full year, 1 credit)*
This course will present the students with an introduction to the Ancient Greek alphabet and language,
with an emphasis on English derivatives, Greek culture and readings of Greek texts. A special emphasis
is placed on learning about the Ancient World through reading authentic passages of Ancient Greek as
well as through English readings giving an overview of Ancient Greek Civilization. This course aims to
give students an appreciation not only for the richness of the Ancient Greek language, but also the culture
and civilization of the Ancient Greeks and their tremendous impact on Western Civilization.

World Mythology (half year, 1/2 credit)
This course introduces students to a multidisciplinary approach to world mythology, providing students
with an overview of mythology and its relationship to ancient cultures and culture today. In this course,
students will explore the mythology of the cultures and religions of Egypt, Mesopotamia, Greece, Rome,
Northern Europe, Asia, the Americas and Pacific Islands. This course covers the purposes and types of
myths; development of myths and mythological characters and the common elements of mythological
structures. Literary elements such as symbols, themes, and plots of myths are analyzed, enabling the
student to identify common characteristics and patterns in myths originating in various cultures and
ancient religions throughout human history. Students will explore other mythologies through
research-based projects, analyze these stories as works of literature and of history, and examine how these
stories of mythology have helped to develop other forms of literature, such as legends, fables, and fairy
tales. (This course will be taught in English)

All students must receive one credit in the Arts to satisfy the New York State Diploma requirement
Whether or not electives are run will depend upon student enrollment and scheduling.


Studio in Art (full year, 1 credit)

This course focuses on composition through the use of the elements and principles of art and design.
Techniques are taught as tools of expression in the visual arts and will be explored through drawing,
painting, ceramic work, sculpture and technical drawing. Students are required to keep a visual journal.


Drawing & Painting 1 (full year, 1 credit)
Prerequisite: Studio in Art

Students will create and experiment with a variety of drawing and painting techniques and materials.
These materials include graphite, charcoal, watercolors, acrylics, oil paint, and some non-traditional
media. Projects focus on realism in the first half of the year moving towards conceptualism,
expressionism and abstraction in the second half.


Photography (full year, ½ credit)
Prerequisite: Studio in Art

This course will introduce students to traditional black & white darkroom photography and basic digital
photography. Students will learn how to expose, develop and print their own photos as well as the basics
of digital photography and Photoshop. Lessons focus on the technical, compositional and creative
elements of the photographic medium. It is helpful that a student owns their own digital camera, but it
isn’t necessary.


Mixed Media and Sculpture (full year, 1 credit)
Prerequisite: Studio in Art

This course uses mixed media as a form of visual communication. Students will combine both
two-dimensional and three dimensional elements to create their work based on design principles. Students
will explore additive and subtractive sculpture techniques, printmaking techniques, ceramics, wire
armature, modeling, collage, mosaics and assemblage..


Graphic Design (½ year, ½ credit)
Prerequisite: Studio in Art

Graphic Art & Design is an introduction to exploring drawing and photo manipulation on the computer.
Industry standard software will be used and includes Adobe Photoshop, Illustrator, and InDesign.
Experiences will include print advertisement, logo design, product development, and web design.
Students will be expected to have a digital storage location and purchase specialty inkjet printer paper.
                                                MUSIC
 All students must receive one credit in the Arts to satisfy the New York State Diploma requirement

Concert Band, full year (1 credit) and Marching Band (full year, ¼ credit)

Students selecting band will participate in the Concert Band and the Marching Band. Members of the
Concert Band learn proper playing technique on their instrument, sight‐reading and basic musicianship
skills. A wide variety of standard band literature will be studied from the great classics to the
contemporary composers at various levels. Students will receive lessons on a rotating basis. The Concert
Band performs at two evening concerts a year. Attendance is required at the evening performances. The
Marching Band performs for home football games as well as school spirit events. The band/marching
band contributes musically to the school and often participates in various out of school performances such
as professional baseball, hockey, and basketball events.

Jazz Band (One per week, ¼ credit) (By selection and Audition only)

The Jazz Band meets once a week as part of the lesson group rotation. A wide variety of jazz literature
will be studied. Students are required to perform in the Concert Band unless playing an instrument not
offered in the concert band. Students will have the opportunity to perform for a variety of events. Students
perform at evening school concerts as well as possible out‐of‐school performances. Attendance is
required at all rehearsals and performances.

String Orchestra (full year, 1 credit)

Orchestra is open to students who have an interest in playing a string instrument. Music from a wide
variety of styles is studied and performed. The class meets everyday throughout the year. Group lessons
are offered on a rotating basis as part of the curriculum. The orchestra performs at home semi-annually.
Attendance is required at all dress rehearsals and performances. Students enrolled in orchestra may elect
to prepare and perform a solo at the annual NYSSMA Solo Festival.


Wind Ensemble (full year, ¼ credit) (By Selection and Audition Only)

The Wind Ensemble meets during a rotated lesson schedule as well as a few evening rehearsals as
performances get closer. Students are either selected or auditioned in the spring for membership the
following fall. Students are required to perform in the Concert Band (or Orchestra for string members).
Students will perform at school concerts as well as out of school performances. Attendance is required at
all performances.


Concert Choir (full year, 1 credit)
Concert Choir is open to students who wish to develop their potential as vocal musicians. Music from a
wide variety of styles is studied and performed. The class meets every day. Students who are in band or
orchestra can join, alternating days of chorus with their instrumental ensemble (½ credit). Group lessons
are offered on a rotating basis as part of the curriculum. Concert Choir performs winter and spring
concerts, as well as at assemblies and occasional other off-campus events. Attendance is required at all
dress rehearsals and performances. Students enrolled in Concert Choir may elect to prepare and perform a
solo at the annual NYSSMA Solo Festival. Advanced members of Concert Choir may audition for the
small, auditioned a cappella vocal ensemble, Chorale.

Chorale (full year, 1/4 credit)
By audition only. For more advanced singers. Students perform a cappella repertoire (typically NYSSMA
levels IV and V) of various genres, performing both on and off campus. Only members of Concert Choir
may audition for Chorale. Rehearsals are scheduled on a rotating pull-out basis.


Introduction to Music Theory (1 Semester, ½ credit)
Students in this class will learn the basics of music understanding, notation, analysis, and aural
comprehension In this half year course, students will also learn an introduction to a DJ experience.
Students will have hands-on experience in using both the hardware and software a DJ would use to create
a unique and musical listening experience. This course is geared toward the person who likes music and
wants to understand more about it. The class will meet daily for one semester.

AP Theory I (full year, 1 credit)
In preparation for the AP Music Theory exam, students will learn music terminology, notation and
compositional skills, score analysis, and aural skills. Students enrolling in this class must either take the
Introduction to Music Theory course or connect with Mr. DiGiovanni for placement into this course.

Introduction to Engineering Design (full year, 1 credit)
*Satisfies New York State Requirement for Art

Students are introduced to the engineering design process, applying math, science, and engineering
standards to identify and design solutions to a variety of real problems. They work both individually and
in collaborative teams to develop and document design solutions using engineering notebooks and 3D
modeling software.

Introduction to Engineering Design (IED) is a high school level foundation course in the PLTW
Engineering Program. In IED students are introduced to the engineering profession and a common
approach to the solution of engineering problems, an engineering design process. Utilizing the activity
project-problem-based (APB) teaching and learning pedagogy, students will progress from completing
structured activities to solving open-ended projects and problems that require them to develop planning,
documentation, communication, and other professional skills.

Through both individual and collaborative team activities, projects, and problems, students will solve
problems as they practice common engineering design and development protocols such as project
management and peer review. Students will develop skill in technical representation and documentation
of design solutions according to accepted technical standards, and they will use current 3D design and
modeling software to represent and communicate solutions. In addition the development of computational
methods that are commonly used in engineering problem solving, including statistical analysis and
mathematical modeling, are emphasized. Ethical issues related to professional practice and product
development are also presented.
                          PHYSICAL EDUCATION & HEALTH

All students must receive two credits over four years of physical education to satisfy the New York State
Diploma requirement. Also, a half credit must be received in Health Education prior to graduation.

Physical Education

Upon commencement from Irvington High School, all students must earn two credits over four years of
Physical Education to satisfy the New York State mandated requirement. Physical Education emphasizes
health-related fitness and developing the skills and habits necessary for a lifetime of activity. These
courses provide students with opportunities to achieve and maintain a health-enhancing level of physical
fitness and increase their knowledge of fitness concepts. The program includes skill development and the
application of rules and strategies of complex difficulty in the following different movement forms:
health-related components fitness (cardiovascular endurance, muscular strength, muscular endurance, and
flexibility), aerobic exercise, team sports, individual and dual sports, Adventure Education, and
recreational games. Ongoing assessment includes both written and performance-based skill evaluations.



Health Education (½ credit)
*This course is required for graduation*.

This is a mandated course. The students in Health Education are introduced to the physical, emotional,
mental and social aspects of wellness. The curriculum involves a wide range of topics such as
communication skills, decision‐making, substance abuse, human sexuality and CPR.



Instructional Lab

Instructional Lab Class at Irvington High School is a building level support class that is a scheduled
period within a student’s day. It can be offered once daily or once every other day. Students in this class
may require moderate support with organization, study skills and/or other executive functioning deficits.
In this setting, students may:
        ●​ Receive assistance with time management and long term planning of assignments
        ●​ Work on building organizational skills/systems
        ●​ Focus on developing self-advocacy skills
        ●​ Receive test taking strategies and/or study skill instruction



The Instructional Lab Class teacher, in addition to providing instruction, will serve as a liaison between
the student and his or her teachers. Working as an advocate, the teacher will facilitate communication
between core content teachers and students. The teacher will also communicate with the student’s parent
as necessary to support his or her success and independence.
