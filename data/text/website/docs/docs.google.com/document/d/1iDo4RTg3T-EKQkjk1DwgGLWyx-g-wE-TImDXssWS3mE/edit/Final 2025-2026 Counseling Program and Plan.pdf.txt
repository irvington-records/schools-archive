BACKGROUND
A school counselor’s role is central to the success of students. School counselors support
students through individual and group counseling, college and career exploration
instruction, assistance in crisis situations, and referrals to school social workers, school
psychologists or other professionals when specialized interventions are required for student
success in school (Rider, January 2018).

After more than twenty years, NYSSCA and NYACES leadership have realized their goal
which is to move preparation, practice, and credentialing into the 21st century. Since the
release of the ASCA National Standards (Campbell & Dahir, 1997), state school counselor
associations and state departments of education across the nation, coupled with the
Transforming School Counseling Initiative (EdTrust), have progressed school counseling
from a traditional support service for some students to an essential program that is focused
on academic, college and career readiness, and social-emotional development for all
students. This movement specifically targets our underserved and underrepresented
student populations.

Beginning in July of 2019, New York State legislated the most comprehensive and cutting
edge expectations that impacted K-12 school settings, as institutions of higher education.
These amendments to Commissioner’s Regulations related to school counseling were
adopted by the Board of Regents effective July 1, 2017. The amended regulations impacted
the comprehensive developmental school counseling program beginning with the
2019-2020 school year. The amended regulations can be accessed at the following link:
http://www.regents.nysed.gov/common/regents/files/517brca14.pdf.

In June, 2018, the New York State Education Department(NYSED) released a guidance
document intended to provide additional direction for school districts as they implement
the amended regulations. The introduction to the Guidance Programs and Comprehensive
Developmental School Counseling/Guidance Programs Commissioner’s Regulation
§100.2(j) states the following:

    “The New York State Education Department (“The Department”) amended the
    school counseling regulations in an effort to help increase opportunities for all
    students to be successful. It is important for students to have access to a
    certified or licensed school counselor in the early years to help inspire young
    students to strive for success and consider college and career opportunities.
    Access does not necessarily mean that every elementary school must hire a
    full-time counselor; but requirements such as core curriculum instruction do
    require that the time of a certified school counselor be included in the
    elementary program. Every school district will need to make personnel
    decisions based on student needs and current staffing configurations. It is

K-12 COUNSELING PROGRAM OVERVIEW
The school counseling department in the Irvington UFSD is committed to providing every
K-12 student with a comprehensive program that delivers academic, social-emotional and
college and career development. Student centered, goal focused, individualized and
personalized services describe the commitment of the school counselors.


As part of the District Strategic Plan, the Portrait of a Graduate has been created as a
framework to guide thinking around curriculum development, assessments and student
experiences. The tenets of the Portrait of a Graduate are listed below and are supported by
the work of our school counselors in their daily interactions and on-going support of
students.

ELEMENTARY SCHOOL PROGRAM
Link to Dows Lane Elementary K-3 Counseling Program
Link to Main Street School 4-5 Counseling Program

The elementary years are a time when students begin to develop their sense of self and
belonging in their school environment. These are vital, formative years as students are
developing a variety of skills that include decision-making, communication and life skills, as
well as character values. It is also a time when students shape and acquire attitudes toward
school, academic learning, self, peers, social groups and family.

The elementary K-5 school counselors deliver a comprehensive, developmental, and
systematic program of services based on national standards. All students have access to our
school counselors through ongoing classroom lessons, and our Multi-Tiered System of
Support (MTSS) program.

As the journey begins, starting in kindergarten at Dows Lane, the school counselor
supports students as they begin to become a part of their new school community with a
first exposure to social awareness, self-awareness, self-management, and relationship skills.
In first grade, we build upon foundational skills and a common language with students
becoming actively engaged in the classroom by using responsible decision-making, growth
mindset, and coping strategies to fully engage in self exploration to optimize academic
learning. In second grade, at Dows Lane, our students learn to make positive choices that
foster a strong sense of self within the class and school community as they become more
self-aware, socially aware and feel empowered to make positive choices. In third grade,
students culminate their Dows Lane experience by developing the important connection
between sense of self and the acceptance of others that fosters a positive classroom and
school community. This is when social-emotional learning, school-wide habits of mind and
character values come together.


                                                                          4
Elementary students transition to Main Street School for fourth and fifth grade. The school
counselor delivers classroom lessons to all students, focusing on conflict resolution,
leadership, organization, goal-setting, positive self-talk, and stress management. Fourth
and fifth graders develop their self-awareness and self-management skills by learning
about the importance of managing strong emotions. Students learn skills to improve and
navigate relationships, increase their social awareness, and make responsible decisions.
They discuss the different ways strong emotions can influence their thoughts and actions
and practice rethinking situations so they can feel calm and think more clearly. Fifth
graders consider their transition to middle school with the support of the school counselor.
Additionally, the school counselor collaborates with teachers and staff, administration and
members of the SEL team to support students' social and emotional needs through small
group and individual counseling and coaching.

The counselors facilitate a smooth transition for students from third to fourth grade and
then again from fifth to sixth grade to support their social-emotional needs, and ongoing
support, as they move between buildings in the District.

The Elementary School Counselors:

      ●​ Teach learning strategies, self-management, and social skills through classroom
         lessons
      ●​ Facilitate short-term, small group counseling
      ●​ Provide immediate, short-term individual, and crisis counseling
      ●​ Work collaboratively with teachers, staff, school psychologists, school social
         worker, and administrators
      ●​ Consult with families
      ●​ Participate in District-wide initiatives i.e. Social-Emotional Development, Lead
         Learners (DEI-Diversity, Equity and Inclusion), Restorative Practices, etc.

In addition to student based activities, the counselors engage in professional development
monthly (i.e. PLRD, Superintendent Conference Days). They participate in professional
learning communities: character building committee, emergency response team (ERT),
social-emotional learning team, and multi-tier system of support team.

THE MIDDLE SCHOOL PROGRAM
Link to Middle School Counseling Website

Each child is assigned a certified school counselor who is there to support the whole child
emotionally, socially and academically throughout their three years of middle school. The
school counselors fill multiple roles for parents, students and staff, as sources of
information, resolvers of conflicts, integral team members and as the primary point of
contact between home and school. The school counselors exemplify the school's
commitment to maintaining a vital and human connection between adults and young

                                                                          5
adolescents. They guide, counsel, redirect and set limits for behavior, while helping
students to channel their energies in productive and positive directions.

The School Counselors understand that each year of middle school is unique, and that the
changes from sixth to seventh to eighth grade are profound and must guide the nature of
adult interactions with students. They meet with students on an ongoing basis, individually,
in small groups, and through classroom visits. Relevant topics such as transitioning to
middle school, scheduling, stress management, study strategies, career planning and
interest exploration, and managing the world of smartphones/social media are explored.
The counselors work closely with both the elementary and high schools to ensure a smooth
transition for students from 5th to 6th grade and 8th to 9th grade.

The counselors meet with every student individually each year (6th through 8th grade) to
have an IPR (Individual Progress Review) discussion/check in. Students complete a
questionnaire that assesses their career interests and academic/social-emotional progress.
This questionnaire helps guide the session with the counselors.

THE HIGH SCHOOL PROGRAM
Link to School Counseling Website

9th GRADE YEAR:
The ninth grade year is an important time of transition into the final four years of schooling
in the District. High School Counselors work closely with students as they transition from
middle school to high school. This transitional support begins while students are still in 8th
grade when they meet with their counselor in preparation for 9th grade. Once the school
year begins, the school counselor will touch base with each student discussing classes and
continuing to build their relationship with each student. Throughout the year counselors
visit classrooms to support the strategies needed to help students develop coping skills to
communicate, take action, and make intentional choices.

10th GRADE YEAR:
School counselors launch the tenth grade year with classroom discussions in preparation
for Career Day, which is held in November. Students are exposed to academic and career
options based on personal strengths collected through a career survey. Students will also
be given the opportunity to take the PSAT and PreACT. In early spring, counselors meet
with the students to continue their post-secondary planning conversation. These
discussions offer an opportunity to plan for the latter two years of high school including
topics of graduation requirements, SAT and ACT testing and how students will continue to
adapt knowledge, skills, and interests to engage in the school community and beyond as
an important component of each student’s personal development.

11th GRADE YEAR:
The eleventh grade year is when it all comes together and the Postsecondary
“conversation” becomes the “plan”. Students take the PSAT/NMSQT in the fall and engage
in classroom discussion. The results of which are reviewed during the Individualized Post
Secondary Planning meetings. This meeting with the school counselor includes a
personalized conversation regarding interests, skills and needs that may inform future
decisions. Concurrently, the school counseling department hosts multiple evening
programs open to families with plenty of opportunities for exposure to the latest college
trends and information. At the end of the year, counselors meet with each student to
develop summer goals. All of the connections this year work toward the goal of identifying
individual student strengths as well as needs necessary for the development of a plan for
the future.

12th GRADE YEAR:
The twelfth grade year begins with an individual meeting for each student to review the
completion of postsecondary goals, and progress towards graduation. The counselors host
an annual student event, From Admissions to Aid, that presents an overview of the post
secondary process and financial aid. Students are provided with continuous support based
on their individual needs throughout the academic year. In May, the counselors host the
annual Senior Send-Off, a celebration of student success, where students declare their plan.

Section 1​   INTRODUCTION TO COMPREHENSIVE SCHOOL COUNSELING

Define
School counselors create comprehensive school counseling programs that focus on
student learner outcomes, based on national standards and data driven decisions. The
mission and vision statements guide the development of an effective comprehensive
school counseling program while the national standards and student competencies guide
and support student development in the academic, college and career, and
social-emotional domains. The foundation provides and serves as the solid ground on
which the rest of the program is built on. The foundation is considered the what of the
program which defines the student knowledge, attitudes, and skills that are learned
because of the school counseling program” (ASCA, 2019).

School Counseling Department Mission
The mission of the Irvington School Counseling Program is to develop and deliver a
comprehensive school counseling program that cultivates in students the knowledge and
skills necessary to experience success in academic pursuits, social–emotional development,
and career and post-secondary aspirations. We are committed to building strong
relationships with students, families, teachers, and the Irvington community as we foster
student self-awareness and self-reliance through collaboration, support, advocacy, and
education.


School Counseling Department Vision for Students’ Success
It is the vision of the Irvington School Counseling Department that students from Irvington
will become independent, socially conscious, civically minded members of society. With
the help of school, family, and community, students will strive to be the best version of
themselves having developed the skills needed to maximize their academic success and
social-emotional development.

Deliver: Tier 1, 2, and 3
The Delivery system shows how school counselors deliver a balanced and effective
program. The scope of the program may differ from the elementary level to middle school
through high school levels as it is adjusted to meet students’ developmental needs. For
example, in elementary school, the classroom curriculum and group counseling activities
are utilized more frequently than at the high school level. Irvington school counselors aim
to deliver a program that is balanced and focused on academic and social-emotional
development and career and college readiness.

Services are delivered at the three different tier levels:
       ●​    Tier 1 - reflects the school counselor’s approach to providing a quality
             grade-by-grade school counseling curriculum to every student.

                                                                                           12
      ●​   Tier 2 - targets students in need of additional support and focuses on specific
           needs. Tier 2 interventions are often delivered to small groups of students.
      ●​   Tier 3 - is focused support for students who will best benefit from individual
           support in addition to Tier 1 and 2 activities.

Direct Services: Direct services are in-person interactions between school counselors and
our students. These activities include: Individual Counseling, Group Counseling, Classroom
Curriculum, Crisis Intervention and Crisis Management, Student-Family Conferences and
Meetings, Career development activities, Educational Planning, IPR, goal setting, and
college/post-secondary readiness.


Indirect Services: Indirect services are a result of the school counselors’ interactions with
others including consultation and collaboration with families, teachers, social workers,
school psychologists, resources for additional assistance, and working with other educators
and community organizations. These services also include participation on school
committees and student support teams, collaborating with college admissions counselors,
MTSS meetings, organizing evening workshops for parents and family members, and
community outreach.

Manage
Effective programs require strong organization and effective management. The
management component of the IUFSD Comprehensive School Counseling program
addresses the when (timelines, building and department calendars), the why (use of data),
the how (program activities, mapping, and gap analyses, the who (is involved), and on
whose authority (advisory council) the school counseling program is delivered.

The school counseling department in the Irvington UFSD prides itself on delivering a
program that maximizes student benefits and has a developmental scope and sequence.
The program has a strong framework and is based on collective observations of identified
areas of student need. Activities are on a calendar that is available in each school building.

The school counselors annually participate in a program review to identify Tier 1, 2 and 3
activities, review indirect and non-counseling activities and identify program gaps that
need to be addressed. The management system also addresses community feedback
through the Advisory Council

Assess
Accountability provides evidence of program accomplishments or student gains as a result
of intentional efforts by the school counselors by using data to identify program outcomes
and effectiveness. School counselor accountability answers the question of what is the
result of these efforts.

and civic readiness.

Annual Individual Progress Review (IPR)
Each student, in grades 6 through 12 meets with their school counselor annually to
participate in an Individual Progress Review (IPR). This may include student records,
social-emotional development, academic skills, and college/career readiness. Topics
included or emphasized will vary depending on the developmental stage and individual
needs of each student.
