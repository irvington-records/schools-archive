Dear Parents/Students:

The Irvington High School Course Catalog is a comprehensive document outlining and
explaining all of our course offerings. This document is a tremendous tool for students and
families and can be used to help align students towards their post-secondary goals. By
considering courses throughout your entire high school experience, you will be better prepared to
make strong selections for next year. I encourage you to keep in mind the following guidelines
as you make your selections:


    1.​ Please discuss future goals. Colleges and future employers will look carefully at each
        student’s transcript and ask two questions. The first one is obvious – how well did
        the student do in their courses? But the second one is equally important – did this
        student challenge her/himself by making the most of what Irvington High School
        had to offer?

    2.​ Become familiar with the courses and various State mandates regarding graduation
        requirements. There are various requirements for different classes, and it’s
        important for the student and their parents to know those requirements.

    3.​ Electives will be scheduled based on availability and student interest. Every effort
        will be made to provide Requested Courses; in some cases changes may be required.

                                                ENGLISH

English 10: Literary Study - Honors *
(Full year, 1 credit)

Placement in English 10 Honors will be determined by final average for 9th grade English.

Students with a final average of 90 or higher in English 9 Regents, or 80 or higher in English 9
Honors will be invited to enroll in English 10 Honors or AP Seminar.

Students will study and utilize literary techniques to critically read novels, short stories, plays, poetry, and
nonfiction texts, many of which represent diverse perspectives. Students will study and discuss language,
content, and structure and how they develop meaning. In addition to writing longer pieces, 10th grade
students will share core writing experiences focused on argument paper and literary essays. Grammar and
usage are also addressed.

Honors students will read additional or different texts and will be required to apply sophisticated
comprehension and inferential skills to these texts. Students in English 10 Honors are expected to
independently apply the writing process to various assignments.

In June, all tenth grade students will take the Common Core English Regents Exam as a final.

English 10: AP Seminar (anticipated 2025-2026 school year)
(Full year, 1 credit)

Placement in English 10 Honors will be determined by final average for 9th grade English.

Students with a final average of 90 or higher in English 9 Regents, or 80 or higher in English 9
Honors will be invited to enroll in English 10 Honors or AP Seminar.

Step into the world of discovery with AP Seminar, a dynamic course designed to sharpen your
research skills and spark meaningful conversations about the complex questions shaping our world.
Together, we will dive into diverse and engaging materials which range from thought-provoking
literary classics to cutting-edge news articles, personal stories, scholarly studies, and evocative
media.

In this course, you won’t just learn, you’ll explore, question, and create. You'll develop your own
viewpoints and engage in scholarly research practices to support them. You’ll bring your ideas to life
through compelling essays, presentations, and team projects. Whether you're working solo or
collaborating with peers, you'll practice the art of critical thinking and hone effective oral and written
communication skills that will set you apart in college and beyond.

AP Seminar isn’t just about learning facts; it’s about mastering how to think, argue, and lead with
confidence.

In June, all tenth grade students will take the Common Core English Regents Exam as a final. AP
Seminar concludes with an AP exam as well.

                                        SOCIAL STUDIES

Global Studies II (full year, 1 credit) *
This course is part of a two year sequence which studies the sweep of Global history from Ancient Times
until the present. Global Studies II, in 10th grade, studies the period from the Age of Revolutions in the
mid-eighteenth century through present-day global connections and interaction. The focus is on
understanding historical thinking skills, other cultures, anthropology, varying perspectives, and political,
economic, and social issues, especially in a comparative way. Students will take the Regents exam in
Global History and Geography at the end of the year.

AP World History (full year, 1 credit) *
Prerequisite: Students should have a minimum of a 90 in Global I Honors and a 92 if in Global I
The course highlights the nature of changes in global frameworks and their causes and consequences, as
well as comparisons among major societies, beginning with the foundations of civilization and continuing
to the present. It emphasizes relevant factual knowledge, leading interpretive issues, and skills in
analyzing types of historical evidence. Periodization, explicitly discussed, forms an organizing principle
to address change and continuity throughout the course. Acceptance into this course is based on a
student's grades in previous Social Studies classes. In this course must take the New York State Regents
Examination in Global History and Geography.



                               SOCIAL STUDIES ELECTIVES

Sports History and Marketing (Semester Course, ½ credit)
This course includes a dual focus on the history of sports and sports marketing. There will be a review
and historical perspective on sports and sports culture in the United States. Students will study these
facets via the media, readings and research activities. In addition, students will gain a basic introduction
of marketing concepts. Marketing concepts and theories that apply to sports will be developed and
exercised by the students. Sponsorship, management, marketing plans, and promotion will be covered in
this course. This course will also delve into the application of this knowledge in marketing strategies.This
course is open to seniors, juniors and selected sophomores.

Current History and Politics (1950’s to the Present) (½ year, ½ credit) *
This course gives the student the opportunity to explore political, economic and social issues that have
occurred within the last half of the 20th century and the first part of the 21st century. Although the focus
will be on the United States, there will be considerable attention to world events. Students will be
expected to use and respond to a variety of sources. Participation will be a key requirement. Students will
also be expected to have knowledge of the daily news. This course is open to seniors, juniors and selected
sophomores.

Regents Earth Science (full year, 1 credit) (Lab)*
Prerequisite: It is recommended, but not required that students have taken and passed the Living
Environment course & Regents exam.
Students will develop skills such as critical thinking, developing and using models, interpreting and
analyzing data and constructing scientific explanations to study the earth and its place in the universe.
Topics include geology, meteorology, astronomy, and Earth history. Students will take the New York
State Physical Setting Earth Science Regents Exam provided they have fulfilled the 1200 minute lab
requirement

Honors Earth Science (full year, 1 credit) (Lab)*
Prerequisites: Students will be required to fulfill entrance criteria for Honors selection.
Mandatory summer work must be completed by any student wishing to take the course by the first
day of the new school year.​      ​       ​       ​        ​       ​        ​       ​       ​
Highly motivated students are expected to use skills such as critical thinking, developing and using
models, interpreting and analyzing data and constructing scientific explanations to study the earth
and its place in the universe. Various topics will include geology, meteorology, astronomy, as well
as Earth’s history. Students will leave the course well prepared to take AP Environmental Science, if
they choose. Students will take the New York State Physical Setting Earth Science Regents Exam
provided they have fulfilled the 1200 minute lab requirement

Regents Chemistry (full year, 1 credit) (Lab)*
Prerequisites: Students must have taken and passed the Living Environment course & Regents
exam and should have taken or be enrolled in Geometry. It is also recommended, but not required
that students have taken Earth Science.
Students will develop skills such as critical thinking, developing and using models, interpreting and
analyzing data and constructing scientific explanations to study the composition and behavior of matter
with a focus on relationships, processes, mechanisms and applications. Topics include matter and change,
atomic structure, chemical bonding, chemical reactions, and behavior of solutions. Students will take the
New York State Physical Setting Chemistry Regents Exam provided they have fulfilled the 1200 minute
lab requirement.

Honors Chemistry (full year, 1 credit) (Lab)*
Prerequisites: Students must have taken and passed the Living Environment course & Regents
exam and should have taken or be enrolled in Geometry, and fulfilled the entrance criteria for
Honors selection. It is also recommended, but not required that students have taken Earth Science.
Mandatory summer work must be completed by any student wishing to take the course by the first day
of the new school year.
Highly motivated students are expected to use skills such as critical thinking, developing and using
models, interpreting and analyzing data and constructing scientific explanations to study the major
concepts of matter and the changes that matter undergoes, atomic structure, chemical bonding, chemical
reactions, and behavior of solutions. This course is a faced paced, rigorous, and mathematically oriented
study of chemistry with technology infused throughout the curriculum. Students will take the New York
State Physical Setting Chemistry Regents Exam provided they have fulfilled the 1200 minute lab
requirement.

Science Research (Full year; 1 credit)*
Science Research is a three year science elective designed for those motivated students who have a strong
interest in the sciences and wish to research a topic of their choice in STEM. Starting sophomore year and
culminating in their senior year, students have the opportunity to engage in independent research that they
design and conduct under the supervision of their mentor. This research is often conducted during the
summer. Students in the program present their research at numerous competitions, both at the local and
state level. They will also present their research to the community at the annual Irvington High School
Science Research Symposium.

Algebra 1B (full year,1credit)*

This course for sophomores is the second half of the two‐year program in Algebra. The topics include:
factoring polynomials, solving and interpreting quadratic equations, relations & functions, and statistics.

Geometry NG (full year, 1 credit)*
Prerequisite: Algebra I and > 65 on the Algebra I Regents Exam.
This course for sophomores and accelerated freshmen examines the fundamentals of geometry using
an integrated curriculum with real‐world applications and it correlates to the NYS Next Generation
Curriculum. The geometry topics include: direct and indirect Euclidean proofs of triangles,
quadrilaterals and circles, coordinate proofs, logic proofs, and three‐ dimensional figures, right
triangle trigonometry, similar figures, area, volume, locus, constructions, and transformational
geometry.


Geometry (full year, 1 credit)*
Prerequisite: Algebra I or Algebra 1B
This course for sophomores and juniors examines the fundamentals of geometry using an integrated
curriculum with real ‐world applications as it correlates to the Next Generation Curriculum. The
geometry topics include: congruence and similarity (including constructions), trigonometry, three
dimensional geometry, coordinate geometry and circle geometry. The course does not culminate in a
regents exam.Students will be properly prepared for the SAT I.

Computer Programming I (½ year, ½ credit)
Grades 9-12
Prerequisite: None
This course introduces students to computer programming with a focus on topics such as: program
planning and design, programming concepts of statements, control structures, functions, objects and
classes, events, iterations, and arrays. The language used in the course is Alice, which has a similar style
to Java. The course places an emphasis on problem solving. No prior experience with computer
programming is required. This course is the prerequisite course for Computer Programming II.


Computer Programming II (½ year, ½ credit)
Grades 9-12
Prerequisite: Successful completion of Computer Programming I
The course is for students who demonstrate both an interest and competence in computer programming,
and is a continuation of Computer Programming I. Students will utilize programming techniques
previously learned and implement the corresponding Java syntax. Topics include creation of methods,
importing classes, loops, mathematical functions, arrays, conditional statements, creation of classes,
strings, and input/output. This course serves as a precursor to Advanced Placement Computer Science.

Advanced Placement (AP) Computer Science Principles (full year, 1 credit)
Grades 10-12 This course runs every other year and will run in 2026-27.

Prerequisite: Successful completion of Computer Programming I/II with averages of 75 or better in
each class and successful completion of full Algebra I program OR Successful completion of
Algebra with an average of 75 or higher
The AP Computer Science Principles course is designed to be equivalent to a first-semester introductory
college computing course. In this course, students will develop computational thinking skills vital for
success across all disciplines, such as using computational tools to analyze and study data and working
with large data sets to analyze, visualize, and draw conclusions from trends. The course engages students
in the creative aspects of the field by allowing them to develop computational artifacts based on their
interests. Students will also develop effective communication and collaboration skills by working
individually and collaboratively to solve problems, and will discuss and write about the impacts these
solutions could have on their community, society, and the world. AP Computer Science Principles builds
on a foundation of mathematical and computational reasoning that will be applied throughout the study of
the course. Students are required to take the AP exam in May.

in a conversation:
You can be part of a conversation about yourself and your life. Much of the time you can also use your

some sentences. You can often ask and answer simple questions. You can do all of this in a way that your

when you read or listen to something:
You likely understand the main idea in short passages on very familiar topics but rarely the supporting
facts. You usually need to read/hear the passage more than once. You may need visual cues such as

when you write something that you have prepared:
You can write about yourself and your life. Much of the time you write well enough to express your own

Latin 1 (full year, 1 credit)*
Latin 1 is a course which promotes literacy and cultural awareness. In addition to learning the
fundamentals of the Latin language, the student will be introduced to the Roman way of life,
Greco-Roman mythology and Roman history. Students develop an ability to handle the language in four
skill areas: listening, speaking, reading and writing. This course provides a firm foundation for the study
of modern foreign languages. In addition, students will gain a greater understanding of the English
language through the study of derivatives and English grammar as it relates to Latin.


Latin 2 (full year, 1 credit)*
Prerequisite: Successful completion of Latin 1.
After a thorough review of first year grammar, students will continue their study of Latin grammar,
culture and mythology, while further developing the four skills areas of listening, speaking, reading and
writing through the study of Latin syntax and literary style. Students will compare and contrast ancient
cultures with their own culture and learn the ideals that guided men and women in the past.

                             WORLD LANGUAGE ELECTIVE

Ancient Greek I H - An Introduction to Ancient Greek (full year, 1 credit)*
This course will present the students with an introduction to the Ancient Greek alphabet and language,
with an emphasis on English derivatives, Greek culture and readings of Greek texts. A special emphasis
is placed on learning about the Ancient World through reading authentic passages of Ancient Greek as
well as through English readings giving an overview of Ancient Greek Civilization. This course aims to
give students an appreciation not only for the richness of the Ancient Greek language, but also the culture
and civilization of the Ancient Greeks and their tremendous impact on Western Civilization.

Ancient Greek II H- Ancient Greek Prose (full year, 1 credit)
Prerequisite: Successful completion of Greek I H
This course is a continuation of the topics and themes covered in Ancient Greek I- An Introduction to
Ancient Greek. After a brief review of the grammar and vocabulary of Greek I, students will continue
their study of the Ancient Greek language and culture. The centerpiece of the course will be learning
about the culture, history and mythology of the Ancient Athenians through the reading of real passages of
Greek authors such as Plato, Herodotus, Apollodorus, and Demosthenes. Through this students will gain
a richer understanding of how sophisticated and beautiful Greek culture and literature is, and be able to
compare the society and literary styles of the past with the greatest works of Western Civilization.

World Mythology (half year, 1/2 credit)
This course introduces students to a multidisciplinary approach to world mythology, providing students
with an overview of mythology and its relationship to ancient cultures and culture today. In this course,
students will explore the mythology of the cultures and religions of Egypt, Mesopotamia, Greece, Rome,
Northern Europe, Asia, the Americas and Pacific Islands. This course covers the purposes and types of
myths; development of myths and mythological characters and the common elements of mythological
structures. Literary elements such as symbols, themes, and plots of myths are analyzed, enabling the
student to identify common characteristics and patterns in myths originating in various cultures and
ancient religions throughout human history. Students will explore other mythologies through
research-based projects, analyze these stories as works of literature and of history, and examine how these
stories of mythology have helped to develop other forms of literature, such as legends, fables, and fairy
tales. (This course will be taught in English)

All students must receive one credit in the Arts to satisfy the New York State Diploma requirement
Whether or not electives are run will depend upon student enrollment and scheduling.

Studio in Art (full year, 1 credit)
This course focuses on composition through the use of the elements and principles of art and design.
Techniques are taught as tools of expression in the visual arts and will be explored through drawing,
painting, ceramic work, sculpture and technical drawing. Students are required to keep a visual journal.


Drawing & Painting 1 (full year, 1 credit)
Prerequisite: Studio in Art
Students will create and experiment with a variety of drawing and painting techniques and materials.
These materials include graphite, charcoal, watercolors, acrylics, oil paint, and some non-traditional
media. Projects focus on realism in the first half of the year moving towards conceptualism,
expressionism and abstraction in the second half.

Drawing and Painting II (full year, 1 credit)
Prerequisite studio art & Drawing and Painting & instructor’s permission:
This class is ONLY for exceptionally motivated students that can work independently alongside the
regular drawing and painting class. Students need to choose their assignment from a provided
independent curriculum or create their own course sequence. No one can register for this class without
the instructor's permission.


Photography (full year, ½ credit)
Prerequisite: Studio in Art
This course will introduce students to traditional black & white darkroom photography and basic digital

                           ARCHITECTURAL TECHNOLOGY

Architectural technology is a collaboration of courses offered in design and drawing with
emphasis on modern technology such as CAD (computer aided drawing) and scale model
building, entwined with Project Lead the Way (a program for engineering design and S.T.E.M.
education), the successful completion of this program sequence is comparable to a first year
college architectural student.

Design and Drawing for Production* (full year, 1 credit)
*Satisfies New York State Requirement for Art.
Design & Drawing for Production is a foundation course on the high school architecture pathway. This
course exposes students to some of the authentic concepts and techniques used in drafting and
architectural design. Starting with the design process, students will develop style and technique through a
series of design problems that will introduce a variety of materials, working methods and tools. Students
will engage in the complete design process, conceptualize their ideas through technical drawings,
maintain a design journal, and apply mathematical concepts in the design and building of their pieces.
Students will work with a balance of hand drafted drawings, CAD modeling, and architectural and
structural modeling projects.

Students will learn basic drafting ranging from orthographic projection, multiview drawings, isometric
and axonometric perspective, and residential construction details. Students will explore a variety of
materials and techniques and learn the appropriate and safe techniques to alter these materials using hand
tools. They will learn to choose the best technique and materials to create ideas they envision. An
incorporation of basic design knowledge, spatial visualization and modeling projects will give the student
an excellent base for more advanced technology study.

Architectural Modeling I (full year, 1 credit)
*Satisfies New York State Requirement for Art.
Architectural Modeling is a specialization course on the high school architecture pathway. This course
exposes students to some of the authentic concepts and techniques used in architectural design and
woodworking. Starting with the design process, students will develop style and technique through a series
of design problems that will introduce a variety of materials, working methods and tools. Students will
engage in the complete design process, conceptualize their ideas through drawings, maintain a design
journal, and apply mathematical concepts in the design and building of their pieces. Students will work
with a balance of architecture model projects, hand crafted decorative and functional objects, and CAD
modeling.

Students will explore a variety of materials including balsa wood, chipboard, foamboard, plexiglass, wood
and even concrete and plaster! Students will then learn the appropriate and safe techniques to alter these
materials using both hand tools and power tools. They will learn to choose the best technique and
materials to create ideas they envision.

Project Lead the Way is pre‐engineering curricula that help schools prepare a new generation of

The program is designed to help all students explore math, science, and technology. Project Lead the Way

Introduction to Engineering Design (full year, 1 credit)
*Satisfies New York State Requirement for Art
Introduction to Engineering Design (IED) is a high school level foundation course in the PLTW
Engineering Program. In IED students are introduced to the engineering profession and a common
approach to the solution of engineering problems, an engineering design process. Utilizing the activity
project-problem-based (APB) teaching and learning pedagogy, students will progress from completing
structured activities to solving open-ended projects and problems that require them to develop planning,
documentation, communication, and other professional skills. Students are introduced to the engineering
design process, applying math, science, and engineering standards to identify and design solutions to a
variety of real problems. They work both individually and in collaborative teams to develop and
document design solutions using engineering notebooks and 3D modeling software.

Through both individual and collaborative team activities, projects, and problems, students will solve
problems as they practice common engineering design and development protocols such as project
management and peer review. Students will develop skill in technical representation and documentation
of design solutions according to accepted technical standards, and they will use current 3D design and
modeling software to represent and communicate solutions. In addition the development of computational
methods that are commonly used in engineering problem solving, including statistical analysis and
mathematical modeling, are emphasized. Ethical issues related to professional practice and product
development are also presented.

Prerequisite: PLTW Introduction to Engineering Design
Through problems that engage and challenge, students explore a broad range of engineering topics,
including mechanisms, the strength of materials and structures, automation, and motion. Students develop
skills in problem solving, research, and design while learning strategies for design process documentation,
collaboration, and presentation.

Principles of Engineering (POE) is a foundation course of the high school engineering pathway. This

Physical Education
Upon commencement from Irvington High School, all students must earn two credits over four years of
Physical Education to satisfy the New York State mandated requirement. Physical Education emphasizes
health-related fitness and developing the skills and habits necessary for a lifetime of activity. These
courses provide students with opportunities to achieve and maintain a health-enhancing level of physical
fitness and increase their knowledge of fitness concepts. The program includes skill development and the
application of rules and strategies of complex difficulty in the following different movement forms:
health-related components fitness (cardiovascular endurance, muscular strength, muscular endurance, and
flexibility), aerobic exercise, team sports, individual and dual sports, Adventure Education, and
recreational games. Ongoing assessment includes both written and performance-based skill evaluations.

Instructional Lab

Instructional Lab Class at Irvington High School is a building level support class that is a scheduled
period within a student’s day. It can be offered once daily or once every other day. Students in this class
may require moderate support with organization, study skills and/or other executive functioning deficits;.
In this setting, students may:

      ●​   Receive assistance with time management and long term planning of assignments
      ●​   Work on building organizational skills/systems
      ●​   Focus on developing self-advocacy skills
      ●​   Receive test taking strategies and/or study skill instruction


The Instructional Lab Class teacher, in addition to providing instruction, will serve as a liaison between
the student and his or her teachers. Working as an advocate, the teacher will facilitate communication
between core content teachers and students. The teacher will also communicate with the student’s parent
as necessary to support his or her success and independence.
