              The Maker Mindset
Maker Mindset and Maker Education:
● Allow for creativity
● Provide problem-based learning opportunities that make use of hands-on
  collaborative learning as an approach to authentic problem solving
● Fall in line with a growth mindset
● Prepare students for jobs of the future, many of which don’t even yet exist!

Elective: Sustainable School
The 9th grade Foundations of Research classes recently
designed a Sustainable School!

In a ﬁrst phase, students built their ideal school with a focus
on sustainability and guided by the UN Sustainable
Development Goals. Students conducted research, used
NoodleTools, and created a brochure that laid out details and
images with a Works Cited.

For the design portion, which was new this year, students built
their school using Floorplanner.com.

 Elementary Library: Interactive Fairy Tales
Students in Library Enrichment are either retelling and
summarizing or writing their own fairy tale!

They are breaking the story down into four components:
First, Next, Then, Last

For each of those sections, they are writing about two
sentences, then recording each section using the
MakeyMakey Audio Sampler app.

They are drawing a scene from the story and adding
conductive components using copper tape, conductive
ink, or paper clips.

 Weather and Climate                                 curriculum unit, add a creative,

 Green Screen Videos
3rd graders created their own green screen
videos using WeVideo where they became
meteorologist travel agents! Students
researched information about a particular
location, and then created their video where
they shared news about patterns in
average monthly temperature and
precipitation data, the best activity and
month of year to visit for that activity, and
things someone would need to prepare for
the temperature, precipitation, and the
activity. This project took the last three
lessons of this Science21 unit, and created
a fun and engaging way for students to
share their research and ﬁndings!

   About Stephanie                                                     for more!



Stephanie Peborde Burke has been an educator for over 17 years with experience teaching middle school, high school, and coaching teachers. She
previously taught 6th Grade Science at Fox Lane Middle School, and Science Research at Fox Lane and Yorktown High Schools. In 2021-2022, she was
the K-12 DICE (Design, Innovation, Creation, and Expression) Facilitator at the Blind Brook-Rye Union Free School District where she ran the high school
and middle school’s FabLab and supported K-12 teachers with experiential, hands-on learning in all content areas. Last year, she returned to
Bedford/Fox Lane as the EdTech Specialist and STEAM Coach. As a member of the leadership team, she supported K-12 teachers with STEAM and
EdTech through coaching, co-teaching, active learning, interdisciplinary experiences, enrichment, and the NYS Computer Science and Digital Fluency
Standards. Currently, she serves as the Instructional Innovation Coach and Specialist in the Irvington Union Free School District. Her role can support,
but is not limited to:
- Instructional Technology
- NYS Computer Science and Digital Fluency Standards
- Creative and innovative teaching & learning
- K-12 experiential, hands-on, interdisciplinary experiences in all content areas

Dr. Burke has been involved with the Westchester and Science and Engineering Fair (WESEF) since its inception in 2001 and currently serves on its
executive board as treasurer. She has also served on the leadership committee for the Westchester-Rockland Junior Science and Humanities
Symposium (WR-JSHS) since 2014.
In May 2023, she graduated with her Ed.D. in Educational Technology Leadership from New Jersey City University. Her dissertation is titled, The Impact
of a High School Maker Education Program on Student Interest in STEM Careers: A Mixed Methods Study. She has also presented about Maker
Education at SITE, NYSCATE 2018, NYSCATE 2022, and LHRIC TLI Tech Expo 2023 (same presentation for both events).

Dr. Burke is a nature lover, birder, Geocacher, foodie, geek, and enjoys spending time with her husband and two young children. As a maker herself, she
enjoys being creative through arts and crafts and a variety of media, including 3D designing and printing.

    Moving from Static to
     Dynamic Learning
⊹    Static learning is learning that lacks movement, action, or change.

⊹    Dynamic blended learning happens through constant change and
     activity, growing and evolving over time
       ×   It can take place anywhere, anytime, and is personalized
           and differentiated
       ×   Learner-centered, giving students choice, embedding
           communication, collaboration, critical thinking, and
           creativity skills where they align with learning goals
       ×   Digital tools are used for more than substitution, to do and
           create things previously unconceived
