Shapers of the World HS English Elective:
Found Sound Instruments
In the “What is music?” unit, students created their own instruments.

Teams of students developed unique instruments using materials available
from home and school. After building, groups shared with the class the
name of their instrument, explained which of the 3 categories of
instruments it fell under (aerophone, membranophone, idiophone), and
then demonstrated how it worked.

After all groups spoke, the entire class engaged in a jam session playing
along to music of all different genres. The teachers asked students to play
along to the rhythm or the beat requiring students to demonstrate their
understanding of the difference.

This activity took place in the classroom, but in the future could collaborate
with other teacher(s), or work in a makerspace where the class could take
advantage of additional equipment, supplies, and space.                          2

Foundations of Research HS English Elective:
Sustainable School
The 9th grade Foundations of Research classes recently
designed a Sustainable School!

In a ﬁrst phase, students built their ideal school with a focus
on sustainability and guided by the UN Sustainable
Development Goals. Students conducted research, used
NoodleTools, and created a brochure that laid out details and
images with a Works Cited.

For the design portion, which was new this year, students built
their school using Floorplanner.com.

In a ﬁnal stage, students recorded a walk-through video of
their school and discussed the connections between the
objects they chose and the sustainability goals.                  4

      HS English: TED Talk
One of the high school English classes recently created TED
Talk speeches and wanted their ﬁnal presentation to look like

                                  MS: Independent Project
An 8th grade student, stopped by a makerspace to test his 3D
printed design using the air compressor. His design wasn’t for
any classes, it was a personal project based on something his
tech teacher mentioned. He was talking about how he wanted to
ﬁll balloons faster and mentioned something called a venturi.

After that conversation, the student began reading up and
understanding what a venturi is actually used for. They have a
range of utilities that range from anything like a paintball gun to
an airplane engine. The way it works is that it's just a gas
chamber which transfers potential energy, in the form of pressure,
into kinetic energy via funneling the high speed air through a
wind chamber. A by product of the process happens to be
creating a vacuum which can be used to suck up gases and
liquids into the air stream.

The student tested his design multiple times, and created a
vacuum to suck up water and even lift a tissue from a ﬂat surface!
His ﬁrst design had cracked after testing, but the following week,
the student came back with a newly printed version to test.           20

   8th Grade Math: 3D Models of Washington, DC
8th graders took on the roles of architects to create a three-dimensional map of
Washington, D.C., complete with 35 proportional models of real-life buildings in
the nation’s capital.

Under the guidance of math teacher Gregg Pernick, Bernard Keating, Diana
Tomaselli, instructional coach Nancy Fitzpatrick and innovation coach Dr.
Stephanie Peborde Burke, the students used the actual dimensions of the
iconic landmarks to scale them down, with a scale of 20 feet in real life to 1 inch
on the models. Using cardboard boxes and minimal art supplies, the eighth
graders worked in groups to create the models of the buildings. This connected
to the students’ study of geometric transformations, speciﬁcally dilations and
scale factor.

Throughout the hands-on, minds-on process, the students worked in small
groups to design and create their buildings, which fostered their creativity and
problem-solving skills. Once the students built their models, they painted them
white in their art classes with assistance from art teacher Nina Rossi.

The experience helped prepare the students for their annual eighth grade
social studies trip to Washington, D.C., from March 20-22, when they visited the
buildings whose models they constructed.

6th Grade Study Skills: Home Study Environment
6th Graders in Alison Meiseles’ Student Skills classes recently took a deep dive
into optimizing their home study environments through a hands-on, reﬂective
learning activity.

The project began with a self-assessment, where the students evaluated their
typical homework spaces based on key factors, such as lighting, organization,
privacy, noise levels, distractions and work habits – including the routine of
charging their Chromebooks each night. After identifying strengths and areas
for improvement, the students captured photos of their study spaces to serve as
models for the next phase of the project.

With guidance from Instructional Innovation Coach Dr. Stephanie Peborde
Burke, the students used Canva to create detailed ﬂoor plans of their home
study environment. They designed the layouts, selecting wall colors, furniture
and storage to mirror their actual spaces. Upon completing their designs, the
students labeled and described each element, and used their plans to assess,
evaluate and adjust their home study environment. The ﬁnal step involved
presenting their ﬂoor plans and reﬂecting on changes they could implement to
enhance their productivity.

As a result of the project, the students reﬂected and made physical changes to
their workspaces at home.                                                          38
