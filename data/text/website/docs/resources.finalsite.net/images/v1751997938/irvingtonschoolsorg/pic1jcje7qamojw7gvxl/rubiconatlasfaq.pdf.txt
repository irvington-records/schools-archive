                                         Rubicon Atlas FAQ’s

What is Rubicon Atlas?
A website designed to support the creation of school curriculum maps and to communicate
grade-by-grade, course specific curriculum objectives.

What is a curriculum map?
A curriculum map is a tool for providing a sequence of learning expectations for the school year. Our
curriculum maps identify what we expect our students to know and be able to do at the completion of
each course or grade level, reflected through individual units of study.

What can I expect to find on a curriculum map?
Named units of study that include:

        ●    Enduring Understandings - Enduring Understandings (EU) are statements that
             encompass the “big ideas”. They are concepts that are interdisciplinary and have long
             lasting value.*
        ●    Essential Questions – Essential Questions (EQ) lie at the heart of a subject or a
             curriculum. A good essential question promotes inquiry, is debatable and doesn’t have
             one “right” answer. The EQ guides the unit of study and serves as a way to promote
             “uncoverage” of important ideas and concepts. *
        ●    Content – Content refers to the subject matter itself, key concepts, facts, topics, and
             important information.
        ●    Skills – Skills are targeting proficiencies, actions and strategies for demonstrating
             understanding of the content.

* In some units of study you may find both District and Unit EU’s and EQ’s - this is reflective of some
curriculum areas where we have worked K-12 to develop overarching “big ideas” that frame the
Unit EU’s and EQ’s at the different levels.

Are the New York State Standards part of the units of study?
Yes, all units of study are based on the New York State or other acceptable set of standards. Due to
the length of the standards we have not included them in each map, but you can click below for a
direct link to Engageny where all standards can be found.
https://www.engageny.org/
