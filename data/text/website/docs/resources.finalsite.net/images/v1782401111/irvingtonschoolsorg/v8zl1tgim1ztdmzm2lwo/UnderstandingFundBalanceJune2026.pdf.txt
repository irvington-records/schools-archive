Categories of Fund Balance
Fund Balance
• Unassigned Fund Balance
   o   Recommended and maximum amount is 4% of next year’s budget

• Appropriated Fund Balance
   o Funds designated for revenue for next year’s budget
   o Approved in budget process


• Reserve for Encumbrances
   o Incomplete orders at year-end that carry over to next year’s budget
   o Amounts fluctuate from year-to-year


• Reserve Funds
   o Restricted for specific purposes
   o Funds assist with long range financial planning, managing liabilities, and future
     budgetary “surprises”

Why Have Reserves?

• Used for long range planning – known costs
   o   Worker’s Comp tail claim payments
   o   Tax Certiorari refund payments
   o   Legal settlements
   o   Contractual payments to retirees


• Used for long range planning – future costs
   o   Long range facilities plan


• Used to manage economic fluctuations and reduce budgetary swings
   o   TRS/ERS rates increasing can result in severe budgetary pressure
   o   Inflation effects - example utilities, health insurance
   o   Costs that may be uninsured or within deductible limits

Why Have Unassigned Fund Balance?

• Office of State Comptroller/SED allows up to 4% of the next year’s budget as
   unassigned funds and recommends 4% as a measure of fiscal health to avoid fiscal
   stress

• Previously larger levels were allowed – municipalities are not restricted to 4%

• Cash flow – avoid borrowing for Tax Anticipation Notes (T.A.N.) and resulting interest
   costs to fund operations until tax funds arrive in October and February

• Strong financial health leads to a better credit rating typically resulting in a lower
   borrowing rate for future capital projects

• Ability to weather any potential storms and manage fluctuations in key drivers such as
   pension match rates or insurance premium increases

Do We Gain Interest on Our Reserves?

YES!
• Our reserve funds are invested throughout the year
  o   Investments must follow NY General Municipal Law, Article 5-G and 3-A,
      which requires safety and liquidity
  o   District primarily invests through a Local Government Investment Pool
      managed by NYCLASS (merged with NYLAF), NY Cooperative Liquid Assets
      Securities System

• Some years, interest rates are low and thus cannot be expected to add
  much to our reserves

• Current year, we expect approximately $515,000 in interest revenue with
  rates approximately 3.5-4.0% much of this year. While interest rates were
  lower than our experience in 2024-25, our increased balances kept
  interest revenue strong in addition to utilizing higher yield Treasuries.

Example: Long Range Facility Planning Aided by Capital Reserve

• The District maintains a long range facilities plan. This includes work that
  cannot be covered in an annual budget resulting from our building
  conditions survey and from improvement ideas. Some projects contained
  in our long range facility plan include:
       o   Roofing and ceilings
       o   HVAC and plumbing fixtures
       o   Electrical upgrades
       o   Windows and door replacements
       o   Paving and concrete work
       o   Repurposing of space requiring renovations for academic needs

• When identified areas of concern become more urgently needed or
  desired, District seeks to initiate a capital project with voters.

• Funding is typically through a voter approved bond but amount may be
  offset with funds in a capital reserve. Use of the reserve reduces annual
  borrowing costs that must be included with the general fund budget

Example: Long Range Financial Planning/Budgeting Aided by
Retirement Reserve
• Having a retirement reserve will minimize the budgetary impact of any large swings in
   retirement system contribution rates




• In this example, if the contribution rate increased to 10% from the 8.24% match, the
   budget would need to include an additional $528,000 which could consume much of the
   tax levy increase. A rate match of 11% might necessitate program/staff cuts if funds in a
   reserve were not available. The contribution rate is largely dependent on the stock market
   and investment performance by NYS TRS as investments account for 85% of the TRS
   income, and 15% coming from employers and employees matches.

Example of Reserve Use: Tax Certiorari Reserve

• Began year with $4,819,607
• Money is added and subtracted each year
• Transferred $1,683,544 to General Fund to pay out on settlements for current fiscal year
   compared with $458,516 the previous year

• Total exposure is ~$1,241,408,606 based on actual filings as provided from the Assessor’s
   office. This represents the difference between the assessed value and requested
   reduction. A more realistic exposure is closer to $7.5 - $12 million through settlements
   and court proceedings.

• In 2012-13, the District borrowed $3,575,000 to augment reserve. This borrowing has
   been paid and the goal is not to have to borrow again as interest payments create a
   budget burden.

• Recommend maintaining current level by adding to tax cert reserve with $1,500,000
   allocation of surplus funds available to avoid future borrowing

• Supplement with a $300,000 transfer from the Workers Comp reserve which is now
   overfunded
