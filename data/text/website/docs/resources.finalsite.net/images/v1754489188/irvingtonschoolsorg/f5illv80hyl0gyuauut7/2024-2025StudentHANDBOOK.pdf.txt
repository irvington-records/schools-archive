      Irvington High School Environment
The Irvington team works to create a safe and orderly environment that supports student
success, both in and out of the classroom. In this environment we strive to help you build
strong habits in the areas of Respect, Responsibility, Empathy, and Kindness, in school, at
school related events, and in the community, we will support you as you:

    ❖ Work to the best of your ability by being on time to all classes, paying attention,
      and completing all work by clearly established deadlines.
    ❖ Treat others respectfully at all times, through both your actions and the way in
      which you speak to and about others in all forms including social media. Use of
      profanity, language that is vulgar or offensive to others, or words that are
      derogatory or make fun of others are unacceptable at IUFSD.
    ❖ Are truthful at all times, and encourage your friends to be truthful as well.
    ❖ Accept responsibility for your actions at all times.
    ❖ Respect school property, i.e. lockers, textbooks, Chromebooks, uniforms, etc.
    ❖ Ensure that fellow students feel safe and comfortable at IUFSD, and actively
      work to help those who feel uncomfortable or threatened in any way (physically,
      emotionally, etc.) by confiding in an adult who can intervene appropriately.
    ❖ Inform an adult in school if an incident begins outside of school and you believe
      it may continue in school.
    ❖ Use technology responsibly and for academic pursuits; you may only record,
      video/audio, or take pictures if given specific permission to do so.
    ❖ Understand and follow all the rules set forth in this handbook, as well as those
      established by your teachers.

             Student Rights & Responsibilities
You Have the Right to:
● Address the Board of Education on the same terms as any other resident of the
    district;
● Take part in all district activities on an equal basis regardless of race, gender, sexual
    orientation, or national origin; and
● Present your version of the facts and the circumstances in all disciplinary concerns
    and to be suspended from instruction only after your rights, pursuant to Education
    Law, have been observed.
● In cases where there is ongoing threat of danger and/or disruption, suspension may
    be immediate, students’ rights pursuant to Education Law will be observed.

You Are Responsible to:
   ● Accept responsibility for your actions;
   ● Work to the best of your ability and attend class regularly and on time;
   ● Conduct yourself in an appropriate manner at school and at all school-sponsored
       events;
   ● Participate appropriately in all Fire Drills/Emergency Drills:
               Classroom teachers provide supervision and take attendance during
               emergency drills. Stay with your class and follow your teachers’
               directions. The use of cell phones during emergency drills is
               prohibited.
   ● Contribute to an environment conducive to learning;
   ● Show respect to other people and to property;
   ● Report fairly and truthfully, the circumstances of school-related issues;
   ● Be familiar with and abide by all rules of conduct;
   ● Encourage others to do the right thing; and
   ● Take care of school property and equipment.

School-Issued Resources:
Our residents/taxpayers pay for school property. Please take care of it. Students will have
the opportunity to hand in school-issued books, technology, and other resources at the end
of the school year. Students must keep their receipt as a confirmation that they have
returned a book. We will not issue additional school resources, transcripts, and/or
diplomas until all previously issued resources have been returned and all fines or
obligations have been satisfied.

                            The School Day
Busing
The school day begins at the bus stop. Students who live 1½ miles or further from the
high school are eligible for school bus transportation. Because safety is the first priority
on the bus, students can enter or exit the bus only when it has come to a complete stop.
All passengers must be in their seats when the bus is moving. The driver has authority on
the bus and will report any unacceptable behavior, which can result in a suspension of bus
privileges.

Senior Parking
Parking is a privilege for Seniors only. To be eligible for parking privileges seniors are
to complete a Parking Permit Registration form, and obtain a parking permit, which must
be displayed. Seniors may only park in the upper gravel lot. Seniors disregarding the
parking policies will have the following consequences

    ●    The signed registration is the first warning/notice
    ●    The 1st violation will result in 1 detention and a hold on the IC account
    ●    The 2nd violation will result in 2 detentions and a hold on the IC account
    ●    The 3rd violation will result in the suspension of parking privileges, the car will
         be booted, the IC account will be put on hold and a guardian will be contacted to
         take possession of the vehicle

UNDERCLASSMAN ARE NOT PERMITTED TO PARK ON CAMPUS. IF AN
UNDERCLASSMAN IS FOUND TO BE PARKING ANYWHERE ON CAMPUS
DURING THE SCHOOL DAY THEY WILL BE FINED PER FINE SCHEDULE
AND WILL HAVE THEIR IC ACCOUNT LOCKED UNTIL PAYMENT AND
WILL JEOPARDIZE THEIR SENIOR PARKING PERMIT PRIVILEGES.

Access for Students with Handicaps
Irvington High School is accessible to all people.
Lockers
We encourage students to use their assigned locker to store their personal items.
Lockers (Gym)
Gym lockers will be assigned at the beginning of the school year by the Physical
Education Department. Students will be provided with a school issued combination lock.
It is very important for students to store their belongings in a locker secured with a lock
during PE classes and after- school practices and games.

Inclement Weather
We encourage you to register for automated email or phone messages regarding changes
to the school day schedule. In the event of school cancellation or delay due to snow or
for another emergency. Please contact Leann Serao in the high school office at
leann.serao@irvingtonschools.org to register for Constant Contact.

Lost and Found
If you’ve lost something or found something you should check the lost and found bins
located in the hallway on the second floor, in the cafeteria or in the library. It is suggested
that any electronic devices, such as cell phones, should be brought to the main office.

Working Papers
Students between the ages of 14 and 18 need working papers in order to obtain an
after-school job. Applications are available in the Counseling Office or on the School
Counseling website. A Parent or Guardian must sign Part I of the form for consent. If the
nurse has a copy on file of a physical exam that has been given within 12 months prior to
the issuance of the employment certificate, the student can have the nurse sign the
Physical Fitness Certification page. If the nurse does not have a current copy on file, the
form must be completed by the student's physician.

                                 Attendance
School attendance is both a right and a responsibility. Irvington High School is an active
partner with students and parents in the task of ensuring that all students meet or exceed
the expectations of the New York State Learning Standards. As regular school attendance
directly correlates to academic success and contributes significantly towards attaining a
high school diploma, the high school has developed, and will revise when necessary, the
following comprehensive Student Attendance Policy.

      9th & 10th graders are not allowed to leave campus during the school day.

   11th & 12th graders are allowed to leave campus only during their free periods.

Absences

Parents/guardians must contact the Attendance Office, 914-269-5483 or email Ms.
Adinaro at venus.adinaro@irvingtonschools.org to inform the school of their child’s
absence with details of the absence before or the morning of the child’s absence. Any
calls after the fact or the school day is over will not change the unexcused absence unless
there was a family emergency. Calls or emails from the student themselves are not
allowed.

You will not receive a confirmation call back or email when you leave a message;
however, all parents are encouraged to check their own child’s attendance on IC Portal
throughout the day. Parents will automatically receive a daily email attendance report at
the end of each school day if and when there is a coded unexcused absence. Parents must
have a registered IC account in order to receive attendance emails.

Medical/Personal Appointments

Medical and personal appointments should be made before or after school hours.
However, when a student must miss school due to a medical/personal appointment, a
verification note/receipt must accompany the student when they return to school. When
arriving late due to an appointment, the student needs to sign in at the main office with
their note/receipt. When a student needs to leave school early for an appointment, the
parent/guardian needs to call the attendance office and the student needs to sign out at the
main office and bring in their note/receipt the following school day.




                                            14
Lates

If a student will be arriving to school late, the parent will need to call the Attendance
office and inform the attendance office of the time their child is expected to arrive with
the reason for their lateness; when the student arrives at school he/she must sign in at the
attendance office. Students MUST sign in for verification and upon their arrival their
attendance will be updated.

Road Tests

Students are allowed up to 4 consecutive periods to take his/her ROAD TEST - not their
learner’s permit. Attendance will be coded as Road Test. Classes missed over and
beyond the given 4 periods will be marked as Unexcused.


College Visits:
Up to 3 days per year are excused for college visitations for Juniors and Seniors only– and
only upon prior knowledge and approval by the parent/guardian to the attendance office.
If a sibling will be going along on the trip, their absence will be marked as
Vacation/Personal.


Students That Become Sick While at School

Students who feel sick while at school need to inform their teacher and go to the nurse’s
office. The nurse will make her assessment and if necessary call the parents to come pick
up the student. Students who leave school because they do not feel well without checking
out through the nurse will be considered unexcused for any missed classes and will be
considered cutting their classes. A letter, phone call or email from a parent after the fact
will not excuse the absence.

If you are injured:
If you are hurt in any way, even if it seems like a minor injury, report to the nurse to file
an accident report.


Extended Illness:
During the first week of an extended illness, a parent or guardian must contact the
attendance office. Parents must provide a doctor’s note, detailing the nature of the illness
as well as the potential length of absence.



                                             15
After School Activities:
Students that participate in after-school activities such as sports, plays, concerts, dances,
exhibitions, field trips, etc. need to be in school the day of the activity. If a student will be
late for any reason, they need to be signed in at the attendance desk by the end of 2nd
period (10:05) and attend the remainder of the day’s classes to get credit for attending
school that day and to be allowed to participate in their after school event. Otherwise, they
will not be permitted to participate in their after school activity. Likewise, if a student goes
home early for whatever reason, they will not be permitted to participate in an after school
activity.

Field Trips:
Signed parental permission forms are required for all field trips. There are no refunds for
missed trips or change of personal plans. Students are responsible to communicate with
all of their teachers in advance of the trip and must make up for all missed work.

 The IHS School Counseling Department
The School Counseling Department in Irvington UFSD is committed to
providing every high school student with a comprehensive program that delivers
academic, social-emotional and college and career development. Student
centered, goal focused, individualized and personalized services describe the
commitment of the school counselors.

Mission
The mission of the Irvington School Counseling Program is to develop and
deliver a comprehensive school counseling program that cultivates in students the
knowledge and skills necessary to experience success in academic pursuits, social
–emotional development, and career and post-secondary aspirations. We are
committed to building strong relationships with students, families, teachers, and
the Irvington Community as we foster student self-awareness and self-reliance
through collaboration, support, advocacy, and education.

Vision for our Students’ Success
It is the vision of the Irvington School Counseling Department that students from
Irvington will become independent, socially conscious, civically minded
members of society. With the help of school, family, and community students will
strive for their best selves having developed the skills needed to maximize their
academic success and social/emotional development.




                                        17
Counselor Assignments
The IHS Counselors follow Irvington's Comprehensive School Counseling
program to assist all students. Each ninth grade student works with one
counselor who is assigned to them based on their last name and they will work
together throughout all four years of high school.

Direct Services provided by the School Counselors include in-person
interactions between School Counselors and students. These activities include
and are not limited to: short-term individual counseling, group counseling,
classroom curriculum, crisis intervention and management, prevention activities,
student-family conferences and meetings, assemblies, career development
activities, educational planning, goal setting, and college and career exploration.

Each student meets with their school counselor annually to participate in an
Individual Progress Review (IPR). This may include student records, social/
emotional development, academic skills, and post-secondary planning. Topics
included or emphasized will vary depending on the developmental stage and
individual needs of each student.

Indirect Services are a result of the school counselors’ interactions with others
including consultation and collaboration with parents, teachers, social workers,
school psychologists, referrals for additional assistance, and working with other
educators and community organizations. These services also include participation
on school committees and student support teams, collaborating with college
admissions counselors, organizing evening workshops for parents and family
members, and community outreach.

Grades
Course grades reflect a student’s class participation, completion of daily
assignments, unit tests, quiz marks, class work, projects, and final or Regents
exam grades. Local Midterm, Final and Regents Exams count for a percentage of
the final course grade. Grades are updated regularly and may be reviewed
through the IC Portal. All courses conclude with a final assessment.

There is a grade floor of 50% for every quarter EXCEPT the last quarter in the
course, for which there is no grade floor.

High Academic Achievement
To earn Honor Roll distinction, students must achieve a GPA from 3.25-3.63. To
earn High Honor Roll distinction, students must achieve a GPA at or above 3.64.
The student must be enrolled in at least three courses and must not have a grade
below 70 for any course.

Extra Help
Teachers are available for extra help. Some teachers have scheduled weekly
office hours while others schedule individual student meetings. Please consult
with your individual teacher regarding their policy.

Earning College Credit
There are two ways to earn college credit as an IHS student.

We offer Advanced Placement (AP) courses as part of a national program
sponsored by the College Board. Depending on your score on the AP exam and
the colleges to which you are applying, you may earn varying amounts of college
credit. Specific information is available via www.collegeboard.com. See your
school counselor for details. Please note that students are expected to take the AP
exam associated with the course. Students who do not fulfill this requirement
will have their course changed from an AP designation to an Honors designation
on their transcript.

We offer other courses (SUPA, PLTW, etc.) that may also allow students to earn
credit. Depending on your final grade some colleges may grant credit. Specific
information is available via the individual college registrar. Please see your
counselor with any related questions.




Schedule Changes
Please discuss any changes you are considering with your parents, teachers and
counselor. Until you have met with your counselor, completed the necessary
forms, and been issued a new schedule from your counselor, please follow your
current schedule. Students may add new courses to their schedule within the first
two weeks of school, schedule and class size permitting.

We will consider course-level change requests in consultation with the student,
counselor, parents, and both the sending and receiving teacher. (An example of a
course-level change would be moving from Geometry Honors to Geometry
Regents.) There is no “boost” added to student grades when changing a
course-level.

    ●   Students may only drop Full-Year courses until one week following the
        release of Midyear grades.
    ●   Students may only drop Semester 1 courses until one week following
        the release of Quarter 1 grades.
    ●   Students may only drop Semester 2 courses until one week following
        the release of Quarter 3 grades.




Ranking
We do not rank our students. We do, however, designate a Valedictorian and
Salutatorian to the two students with the first and second highest weighted,
cumulative grade point averages (GPA) respectively.

These grade point averages will be calculated at the conclusion of the first
semester of the senior year.

Full classes taken in the senior year will be included in this calculation with the
average of the first and second quarter being factored in.

School Records
Every student has a permanent transcript and a permanent file. This file includes
the results of any testing in the IUFSD, as well as from any previous school
system attended. The nurse also maintains a health

record. Students under 18 years of age need parent permission to review their

No part of a student’s record may be divulged to any person, organization, or

         immediately in writing of the information which has been subpoenaed
         or which is the subject of the court order.

                                 Academic Integrity:
Honesty is the ability to tell the truth, even under pressure, and integrity is the dedication to do what is
right. It is expected that all members of the Irvington High School community will act in an honest
and ethical manner, displaying academic integrity at all times.

Plagiarism:
Plagiarism is using someone else’s words or ideas and presenting them as your own. This is a form of
academic fraud. Failing to give credit to others by indicating the source of the ideas or work in a
proper citation is an example of being dishonest and cheating. This also may violate the legal rights
of others. Plagiarism is more than simply copying compositions or papers, it also includes using,
referencing, or copying parts or all of another person’s work into your own assignment without
crediting the source.


Plagiarism includes:
     ● copying and pasting from the Internet or any other source without citing the source and
         including quotation marks around these borrowed words. This can include, among other
         things, the following: text, music, drawings/pictures, video clips, maps, ideas, Power Points,
         tables/charts/figures, and interviews.       Possible sources include books, magazines,
         newspapers, movies, web pages, letters, advertisements, TV programs, blogs, other people,
         or any other medium or source;
     ● copying and editing someone else’s words by reordering the words in the sentence or by
         using synonyms to express the other person’s ideas/words. To paraphrase a source, we must
         present the ideas entirely in our own words, not simply change a few words;
     ● copying or taking from another person’s homework or assignment, either in part or in its
         entirety.
     ● copying someone else’s ideas without crediting the source (this includes copying from Spark
         Notes, Cliffs Notes, or other study guides)
     ● submitting papers written by others (this includes siblings, friends, etc., with or without their
         knowledge)
     ● submitting the same paper for more than one class
     ● downloading entire or parts of research or term papers from the Internet
All work or ideas taken from other sources, even if you paraphrase the words, must be properly cited
in accordance with your teacher’s requirements.
If you are not sure how to do this, it is your responsibility to ask your teacher to explain how he/she
wants work cited. The school librarian can also assist you with this.




                                             36
Other Examples of Academic Dishonesty include:
     ● copying from other people during exams;
     ● obtaining examination materials prior to test distribution;
     ● using or possessing cheat sheets or other written resources;
     ● using electronic equipment, including graphing calculators, cell phones, iPods, smart
         watches, MP3 players, recorders, devices, copy of unauthorized information, etc., to obtain,
         store, transmit or copy information or material to assist you or another person during a quiz,
         test, exam, or any other assessment; or
     ● allowing others to copy your work, give others answers to use as their own, or obtain
         answers from a tutor, sibling, family member, etc. on a graded essay or assignment.
All instances of academic dishonesty will be reported to school administration.
Consequences for academic fraud may include:
     ● A grade of “0” on the assignment or exam
     ● Completing the “How NOT to Plagiarize Workshop” with the librarian
     ● In-school suspension
     ● Community service
     ● Restriction from participation in extracurricular activities and organizations
     ● Dismissal from an Honor Society

Repeat offenders of academic dishonesty will face more serious consequences.

The Board of Education of the Irvington Union Free School District (7310.vi) believes that learners
should dress with the intention to promote and engage in a positive learning environment. The primary
responsibility for this resides with the student and parents or guardians. Student attire must ensure the
health and safety of all students, and it should support a student’s ability to access their education

students, parents and staff.

hostile or intimidating environment by targeting groups based on race, ethnicity,
gender, sexual orientation, gender identity, religious affiliation, weight, or any other
protected groups.

other students or staff.

Students who do not adhere to these requirements will not be allowed to attend class.
Administrative personnel have the authority to require a student to change their attire if it violates this
dress code. Parents will be called if appropriate clothing is not available or the student refuses
dress-code appropriate clothing.

                                Purpose

The Irvington High School’s philosophy of discipline is based on the belief that
teaching students to be responsible is an ongoing process that will help them to
internalize values and help them make sound ethical and moral judgments.

Irvington High School is a community of individuals who are expected to
demonstrate respect, sensitivity and openness to others. These attitudes lead to an
active concern for ourselves, our community and the world around us. Our goal
for school discipline is to provide a structure that promotes this sort of empathy,
learning and academic success.

The Irvington High School community strives to help our students develop:
   ● A positive and accepting attitude towards self and others;
   ● Respect for the rights and welfare of others;
   ● A capacity for self-motivation and self-discipline;
   ● A high level of self-esteem and self-respect.

understanding of our corrective disciplinary procedures which are designed to
prevent disruptive actions and foster constructive, pro-social behaviors that are
conducive to a high-functioning learning environment.

                 Enforcement of Rules & Regulations
        The principal is responsible for deciding if a rule has been broken. Students who break
        rules may be suspended for up to five days and referred to the Superintendent of Schools,
        which can result in a long-term suspension from school. Hearings before the
        Superintendent are closed to the general public and all records of them are kept
        confidential. At Superintendent’s Hearings, persons charged with violating the rules
        described on the preceding pages may be represented by an attorney, may be
        accompanied by a parent or others, have the right to present witnesses and other
        evidence, and may cross examine witnesses who testify against them. Administrators,
        faculty, staff, or visitors who break any of the aforementioned rules or violate the IUFSD
        Code of Conduct in any way may be disciplined under the appropriate Education Law or
        Civil Service Law or in appropriate criminal or civil proceedings.
