Dows Lane Second Graders Put Counting Strategies into Action
Estimating, organizing and problem solving gave second grade students in Ms. Falcon’s class a chance to take the lead in their mathematical thinking during hands-on counting lesson at Dows Lane Elementary School.
Working with partners, students received collections of objects and were challenged to determine how many they had using tools and strategies of their choice. They began by estimating the number of objects in their collection before developing a plan for how they would organize and count them.
As students worked toward a total, they explored how grouping objects can make counting more efficient and applied their understanding of place value to the process. Partners then recorded their strategies on recording sheets, creating a visual representation of how they arrived at their answers.
The student-driven lesson extended beyond finding the correct total. Using their recordings, students shared and compared their approaches with classmates and reflected on how different strategies could be used to solve the same mathematical problem.
Through questions such as “How does grouping help us count more efficiently?” and “How did you use place value to help you count?” students were encouraged to explain the reasoning behind their work and make connections among the strategies used throughout the class.
The experience reflected the Irvington Way as students were engaged in their own learning and collaborative as they planned, problem-solved and shared their mathematical thinking with their partners and classmates.
