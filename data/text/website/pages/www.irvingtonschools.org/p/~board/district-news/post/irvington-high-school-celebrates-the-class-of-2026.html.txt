Irvington High School Celebrates the Class of 2026
Surrounded by family members, friends, faculty and staff, members of Irvington High School’s Class of 2026 marked a significant milestone in their educational journey. They celebrated the completion of their high school careers during a commencement ceremony at Matthiessen Park on June 20.
Principal Jon Hirsch opened the ceremony by welcoming the graduates and guests. In his speech, he reflected on the bright future that lies ahead of the students, with so many possibilities.
“You are leaving Irvington High School, but you are not leaving it all behind,” Hirsch said. “Everything we have tried to teach you is already in there. The lessons, the friendships, the habits, the values. You will carry them with you as you move forward.”
Superintendent of Schools Dr. Mara Ratesic offered heartfelt advice as the students prepared to embark on their next chapter. In her remarks, Dr. Ratesic reflected on how life has changed since the 1990s but the core things that shape people – relationships and community support – continue to be important. She highlighted the importance of empathy, connection and having each other’s back.
“My reflections on the 1990s bring me great joy, but looking at all of you fills me with even more hope for what lies ahead,” Dr. Ratesic said. “We take immense pride in your achievements and ask that you keep us in your hearts. Know that we will always be your biggest supporters, cheering you on every step of the way.”
The students were also addressed by Board of Education President Susan Brunenavs, class president Julia Welter, senior Will Richman, salutatorian Lucy Acharekar and valedictorian Joshua Greenbarg – each offering their unique reflections on their time in Irvington and the road ahead.
Brunenavs encouraged the students to leave room for surprise when life doesn’t go as planned.
“Create a little space between what happens and how you respond,” she said. “Some of the best decisions you will ever make emerge from that space.”
Acharekar referred to people holding onto past experiences and plans like a “paper trail,” and encouraged her classmates to embrace the changes.
“As much as we like to hold tightly to our plans and our histories, our surroundings, our friends, almost every aspect of our lives are about to change, and we’ll change, too,” she said. “Not everything has to be scripted, and life doesn’t have to follow the outline. It’s been incredible seeing how we’ve changed these past 4 years, and I’m so excited to see who we become.”
In his speech, Greenbarg offered heartfelt encouragement to love openly and generously. He urged his fellow classmates to go beyond simply liking things or people and to embrace the power of expressing their love out loud.
“Don’t be scared to dispense your love generously,” he said. “Let everyone feel it. Love, love, and then love some more while you’re at it. The world really, really needs it.”
Signifying the conclusion of one chapter and the beginning of another, the students received their diplomas to the applause of family and friends. After moving their tassels to indicate their graduation, they tossed their caps in celebration.
