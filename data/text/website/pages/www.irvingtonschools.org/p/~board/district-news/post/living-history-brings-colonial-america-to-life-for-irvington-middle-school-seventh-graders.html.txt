Living History Brings Colonial America to Life for Irvington Middle School Seventh Graders
Hands-on activities transported Irvington Middle School seventh graders to colonial America during the school’s Living History event on Oct. 23.
Throughout the morning and early afternoon, students rotated through a series of interactive stations designed to give them firsthand experiences with activities and traditions from the colonial era. Members of the seventh grade team facilitated the activities while dressed in period costumes, helping immerse students in the experience.
Students pressed apples to make cider, churned their own butter, tried weaving and made candles. Other stations introduced students to blacksmithing, English country dancing and colonial games such as Jacob’s ladder, cup and ball, Graces, and hoop and stick. Students also had an opportunity to create a whirligig, a traditional spinning toy.
The event was planned and facilitated by the seventh grade team under the guidance of team leader Christina Mitchell. By taking concepts beyond the classroom and giving students opportunities to learn through participation, Living History offered an engaging way to explore aspects of daily life during the colonial period.
Supplies for the stations and presenters who participated in the event were funded through a grant from the Irvington PTSA, whose support helped make the hands-on learning experiences possible.
Living History also put the Irvington Way into action by encouraging students to Be Engaged as they participated in new experiences, asked questions and explored history through hands-on learning.
