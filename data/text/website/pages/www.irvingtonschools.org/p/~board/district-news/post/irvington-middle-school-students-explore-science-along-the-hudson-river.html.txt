Irvington Middle School Students Explore Science Along the Hudson River
Testing water chemistry, examining aquatic life and observing the changing tides turned the Hudson River into a living laboratory for middle school students.  
During the annual A Day in the Life of the Hudson River experience, all sixth graders traveled to Matthiessen Park for a full day of scientific investigation alongside eighth grade Earth and Space Science students, who took on dual roles as field scientists and mentors to their younger peers. The experience was planned by sixth grade science teacher Amy Panitz, Science Department Chairperson and Earth and Space Science teacher Karen Murphy Bodnar, and sixth grade science co-teacher and team leader Jessica Doherty.  
Throughout the day, sixth graders rotated through seven hands-on workshops focused on the Hudson River ecosystem. Students measured dissolved oxygen and salinity, observed tidal currents, seined for fish and macroinvertebrates, and identified organisms found in the Hudson River Estuary. Additional workshops included STEM, Nature Quest, Delicate Dilemma and Mindful Movement activities. 
Sixth grade science teacher Amy Panitz said the experience connects directly to students' Thinking Like a Scientist and Populations and Resources units while giving them an opportunity to develop a deeper connection to an important natural resource in their own community.  
"It is our hope that students participating in Day in the Life of the Hudson and Harbor 2026 will develop a deeper connection to this precious natural resource and a greater appreciation for the unique and biodiverse estuary ecosystem right here in Irvington," Panitz said.  
"We hope students gain a better understanding of the Hudson River as a living system and how scientists use observations and evidence to understand environmental change," Bodnar said. "For my 8th-grade Earth & Space Science students, it’s an opportunity to apply what they’ve learned in class to a real-world environment through field observations and investigation. 
Bodnar said seeing the eighth graders balance their roles as leaders and scientists was a highlight of the experience. 
"They were responsible for helping younger students learn while also making their own observations and connecting their findings to Earth & Space Science," Bodnar said. "That combination of leadership and authentic, hands-on learning makes this experience especially meaningful." 
For Panitz, the students' reactions throughout the day captured the value of the experience. 
"The expressions on their faces reflect their curiosity and engagement, and the fact that they are all focused on the same phenomenon captures what makes this day so meaningful—students sharing an experience, asking questions, and discovering the world around them together," Panitz said. 
Through exploration, collaboration and mentorship, A Day in the Life of the Hudson River gave students an opportunity to experience science in their own community while embodying the Irvington Way as curious learners, thoughtful collaborators and leaders who support one another.
