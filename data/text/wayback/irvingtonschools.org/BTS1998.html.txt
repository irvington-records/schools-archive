Back to School 1998
The Irvington Schools will begin the 98-99 school year with two new principals, a director of technology and many new faculty members in all three schools. Our new Superintendent, Dr. Vincent T. Beni will replace me on January 1, 1999. All three buildings in the District will begin implementing Phase 1 of the New Technology Plan and curriculum modifications are slated for all levels as we prepare for the new state assessments.
Dows Lane Elementary School will begin the year with its main focus in the following areas:
- Continuing the preparation of staff and students for the change in learning standards as developed by New York State.
- Implementing the beginning stages of the New Technology Plan.
- Beginning the Multi-Sensory first grade class.
- Engaging in a collaborative effort with the Dows Lane Site Committee, the faculty of Dows Lane School, and the Parent Teachers Association to create school-wide activities to promote respect and civic mindedness.
Beginning four years ago, Dows Lane Elementary School embarked upon major curriculum revisions. The frameworks for science and mathematics have been completed, and the first draft of the Language Arts curriculum was presented to our Board on June 1998. These documents serve several purposes: first, to help coordinate and align the curriculum; second, to allow teachers to examine their teaching techniques, methods, and materials to determine what is successful, what needs to be improved or enhanced, and to develop a plan to do so; third, to insure that we prepare for the new standards and assessments from the State Education Department. Working hand-in-hand with outside curriculum experts and in-district staff, a vision was created, goals were identified, as well as outcomes for all the grade levels. Curriculum work is an ongoing process. We will continue to develop hands-on assessments in science. Extended task assessments will be developed this year to be included in math portfolios. In Language Arts, the staff will examine the draft of the Language Arts Curriculum, and make appropriate changes. An assessment for Language Arts K-3 will be developed using the State guide. Students will be prepared for the new state assessments in Mathematics and Language Arts at the fourth grade level. The totality of this work reflects a change in pedagogy that is more in line with the type of instruction that has been taking place at Dows Lane over the years. At all first grade levels we will offer a class that has a multi-sensory Language Arts component. Based on the Orton-Gillingham Model, this will enable students who may struggle with various aspects of language development to learn in an integrated manner with a great deal of reinforcement, using all the modalities. The key factor is that each student will be taught in the smallest teachable units and thus experience a high rate of success. Dows Lane School will develop a series of school-wide activities to reinforce the idea of respect and responsibility. Among the activities that are planned are rallies in which particular positive behaviors are highlighted throughout the whole school, i.e., good manners. Children will be taught conflict resolution skills. There will be Good Citizen Awards for children who have shown themselves to be considerate of others. International Week will highlight the varied cultures that are part of our ancestry at Dows Lane. Technology at Dows Lane, already an important part of the instructional program, will take a giant step forward. All first grade teachers will come "online" to the school’s network. Teachers using outdated computers in grades two, three, four, and five (second grades especially) will have the computers replaced so that they may be able to use the multi-media software that is now available on the network.
Irvington Middle School looks forward to an exciting year as we welcome the largest student body in twenty years, new faculty members and our new Principal, Kathleen Fernald. We will be maintaining our traditions and making a few changes to strengthen our fine existing programs. We are thrilled with the technology initiative which will bring computers into every classroom. Teachers will be able to make presentations or have a student work independently conducting research or working on a project. This supplements our full computer lab which enables each student to work at a computer. Our goal is to integrate technology instruction throughout the curriculum and to help students see the interdisciplinary connections in the world around them. The Math Department has restructured their program to better accommodate the students’ needs and meet the new state standards. By the end of eighth grade, two thirds of the students will have completed Course I, the first level of high school math. In addition, all math students will be preparing for the new eighth grade State Assessments which match the more rigorous State curriculum. In science and social studies, we will continue our eighth grade science unit connected with the Jason Project. Funded by the Irvington Educational Foundation, the program allow students to interact directly with research scientists via various technologies. Through the generosity of the Suzanne Dorff Memorial Fund, seventh grade students will participate in an environmental education experience at Camp Herrlich. This hands-on interdisciplinary experience will be an integral part of the science and social studies curriculum and tie into our character development program. To help parents negotiate the Middle School years, we have expanded our parent education. In October, George Cohen, a consultant on character education, will return to work with seventh grade students and parents. To help students with organization and study skills, all of our parents will be receiving, "Roadmaps for Parents," a parent guide of suggested strategies which augments our student "Roadmaps,". We look forward to an exciting year at IMS, as we welcome new students and teachers, and explore new programs and technologies.
Irvington High School students returning this fall will be greeted by Dr. Scott Mosenthal, our new Principal and new teachers in English, Math, Science, Special Education and Spanish. In addition, they can expect to see a refurbished cafeteria, a revised dress code, and the repeat of the 9th grade orientation. Curriculum-wide, a new science research course will be offered, in addition to a new intensive resource course. The inclusion program, begun last year and which puts special education teachers in English and Social Studies classrooms, will be expanded to include seniors as well as the underclassmen. A tutoring workshop for interested juniors and seniors will be given at the end of the summer; their names will be distributed to parents interested in utilizing their talents as peer tutors for younger students. As part of the technology plan adopted by the District this spring, a new technology coordinator will be offering in-service courses for teachers; also, the purchase of laptop computers and some networking are slated for the High School in the first year of the District’s technology plan. The new dress code was modified by the faculty this spring and adopted by the High School Site Committee. Students will no longer be permitted to wear hats/headgear and overcoats in class, and midriffs must be covered. The cafeteria is undergoing a facelift. New flooring will be installed, round tables will replace the long rectangular ones, and a jukebox will be available for student use. The Orientation for all 9th graders will be September 3rd at 10:30 a.m. in the High School Auditorium. Schedules will be distributed, extracurricular offerings will be discussed, and 9th graders will be given a tour of the High School by their Peer Leaders. Other innovations will include luncheons for high honors students every ten weeks, the distribution of plan books to all 9th graders, and a barbecue the first day of school for all students and staff members.
Richard A. Hajek
