Our students are heterogeneously mixed in self-contained classrooms for all subjects
except mathematics, where they are grouped by ability. Differentiated instruction is
used to meet the varied needs of all children within their regular classrooms. A balanced
approach is used to teaching literacy across all curriculum areas, especially in English
language arts. Science and social studies are “brought to life,” using many hands-on
interactive activities. Technology instruction is integrated into all aspects of the
curriculum. In addition to English language arts, mathematics, science, and social
studies, students participate in physical education, art, music, and theatre. Fifth-grade
students have the opportunity to participate in Spanish and French instruction every
other day for 20 minutes.

Schools. Transportation is the responsibility of the parents. For further information

Student absences on days for religious observance are excused absences. For major

assign new work or tests. If your child is to be absent for religious reasons, please
inform the school office.

DISTRICT POLICIES AND PROCEDURES

Student Directory Information
A parent or eligible student may refuse to permit release of certain data: student’s
name, image, address, phone number, birth date and place, field of study, etc.
The complete policy is available in the Office of the Superintendent. If you do not
wish such information released, please notify the Superintendent, in writing, by
Sept.15 of each year.

Student Records: Access, Amendment, Release
A parent or eligible pupil may inspect or copy the student’s records of academic
achievement, results of standardized aptitude and achievement tests, teacher or
psychological evaluations, college recommendations, health, attendance, guidance
or census records. Application to inspect or copy records should be made, in writing,
to the principal of the school attended.
The parent or eligible pupil who believes that information in the records is inaccurate
may request that the Irvington School District amend the record or records.
As students in the district, your children may be acknowledged for their achievements
during the course of their academic career, whether in scholastics or sports. The dis-
trict often spotlights students and their accomplishments in school district publications
including the newsletter, this calendar, and in brochures and other publications.
Unless the building principal is informed in writing by Sept. 15 of each school year
that this is unacceptable, it is understood that permission is granted by parents for
the use of student images and names in materials published by the district for this
purpose, as well as on the district’s website. This permission remains in force through-
out a child’s time in the district, unless notification is provided to the contrary.

TRANSPORTATION SERVICES

Eligibility
District children who attend Irvington public or parochial schools will be provided
transportation as follows:
    1. All Kindergarten through 5th graders who live more than 1/4 mile from the
       school they legally attend.
    2. All children grades 6 through 8 who live more than 1 1/4 miles from the
       school they legally attend.
    3. All students in grades 9 through 12 who live more than 1 1/2 from the school
       they legally attend.

In-District Transportation
Inquiries about in-district transportation should be directed to Douglas Carter, Quad
Village Transportation Coordinator, at 591-3052.

Out-of-District Transportation
Parents who wish the district to provide transportation for their children to private
or parochial schools outside the district must file a written request for this service
before April 1 each school year. Letters should be addressed to:

    Board of Education
    Irvington High School
    40 North Broadway
    Irvington, NY 10533-1328
Included should be the name, age and grade of the student and the school he/she
is to attend. To qualify, a student must reside no more than 15 miles from the school
in which he/she is legally enrolled. Inquiries about transportation for students with
disabilities who do not attend BOCES classes may be made anytime by calling

These efforts take numerous forms. The PTSA supports students through programs
that coordinate with and enrich the curriculum. At Dows Lane and the Main Street           Sincerely,

International Week, Earth Day and Author/Illustrator Days for each grade.

helps Middle School parents stay informed through a monthly newsletter prepared
in conjunction with Middle School staff. At the High School, the PTSA funds
PSAT/SAT preparatory courses, Regents study courses and, among other things,
scholarships for graduating seniors.

The PTSA continues to hold a Book Fair at each school. At Dows Lane, the fair is
held in the fall; at the Main Street, Middle and High schools, it is held in the spring.
The spring book fair provides one-stop shopping for fulfilling summer reading lists.

The PTSA also provides directories of students (to its members) at each of the
schools. It works closely with the school administration and school board throughout
the year to ensure that parents’ and students’ input is considered when planning cur-
riculum and programming. It also sponsors educational workshops to assist parents
in developing the skills they need to raise and protect their children.

Parents and other community members are urged to attend school board meetings.
The PTSA facilitates parental involvement through its many activities and fund-raisers,
as well as through community events such as the Winter Festival, Pre-School
Carnival, and Spring Luncheon. It also continues to work with other community
groups such as the Irvington Education Foundation, the Community Advisory
Board, the O’Hara foundation, the Irvington Chamber of Commerce, the Irvington
Athletic Association and DARE.
