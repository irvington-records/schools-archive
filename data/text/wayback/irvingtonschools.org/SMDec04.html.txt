School Matters, December 3, 2004
By Superintendent Dr. Kathleen Matusiak
The Irvington School District is committed to providing a well rounded educational experience for every student in grades K-12. To that end, we focus on the whole child, offering quality academic opportunities as well as extra-curricular activities. Character education is also an important component of our education program. A district-wide committee has been meeting with the Superintendent of Schools to study the district's conduct code as well as the merit of these character education programs to determine what should be established K-12 in the future. Two schools in our system are piloting the following new character education programs this year.
The first, which is 50 days in duration, is at Main Street School and is called "No Put Downs". Teachers deliver classroom lessons that focus on five important skills. They are entitled to: 1) Think About Why, 2) Stay Cool, 3) Shield Myself, 4) Choose a Response, and 5) Build Up. Teachers and children engage in discussion about the harmful effects of bullying and how to treat one another with respect. In the school cafeteria, a bulletin board features students whose behaviors reflect the skills taught in the No Put Downs program. Feedback from the pilot program, which is being funded by the Irvington Education Foundation, has been very positive thus far.
The other new character education programs being implemented in our district this year are at the Middle School. One is "Social Decision Making and Problem Solving" and the other is "Facing History and Ourselves". Unlike the No Put Down program, children learn and apply concepts, as opposed to having structured lessons. The programs are based on the enduring understandings that: communities exist and have rules; learning is a life-long process that defines who we are; communication and tolerance of alternative perspectives is essential to a healthy community; and individual existence equals contribution. The essential questions considered are:
- What is fair?
- What is ownership?
- What makes a good community?
- How is inaction action?
- What defines who we are?
Both schools' programs are expected to produce some similar outcomes including: the creation of a common vocabulary and students' metacognition; development of students' problem solving and process building skills; strengthening of students' inter/intra personal skills, communication skills, and decision making skills; assistance in helping students learn how to self advocate; help in creating students' self-awareness; and facilitation of students' shared experiences and reflection.
