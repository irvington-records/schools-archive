SOURCE http://irvingtonschools.org:80/OBMath2.html | DATE 2005-02-14 | TITLE The View On Board: Math 2

For more than two hours on the evening of Tuesday March 9, our community engaged in an intense, thoughtful, civil discussion on the proposed changes to our math program - with a focus on the issue of tracking students in the early grades. The Board and administration appreciate both the content and tenor of this interchange.
The overflow crowd represented three distinct groups: those who are convinced that tracking should be retained, those who feel tracking is not appropriate in the early grades, and others who came to get information or ask for clarification. What we all shared was a commitment to excellence; our common goal is to ensure that the teaching and learning of math in our school district reflects best educational practice and offers every student the opportunity to develop his or her full potential.
There were several specific points raised. We need to continue to provide appropriate extra help for special education students and slower learners. Higher achieving students need the opportunity for enrichment and challenge, both in the classroom and in extracurricular activities. Our teachers need thorough training and ongoing support. As we will have a new Superintendent of Schools and new Assistant Superintendent for Instruction next year, we need to plan for continuity and ongoing program evaluation.
For many people, it was difficult to imagine what a homogeneous classroom would look like. Certainly, the differentiated instruction model is very different from a "talk and chalk" approach. We were fortunate to have Debbie Rogan, a 2nd grade teacher and district resident on hand. Debbie, a passionate advocate of not tracking young children, described her classroom for us: a vital, energetic environment with several small groups (whose composition might shift with the task), peer learning, and a range of activities, manipulatives and print materials. In this type of classroom, the teacher can move among groups and activities, facilitating learning for all and engaging individual students as needed. Lisa Urban, our Math Department Chair for grades 6-12, offered a different perspective. As a teacher of Middle School students, who have been and will continue to be grouped for more formal classes, Lisa acknowledged being skeptical of de-tracking at first. During the course of the Math Curriculum Committee's work, however, she changed her mind. Lisa, too, now advocates eliminating ability grouping in the elementary grades. Significantly, the Math Committee reported that not one high performing district in our region tracks in the elementary grades. Finally, Board members noted that we discussed this issue with six candidates we interviewed for the Superintendent's position, all seasoned educators, representing districts in New York, New Jersey and Connecticut - not one favored tracking in the elementary grades.
Where does this leave us? Our educators, including our Superintendent and Assistant Superintendent, Math Chair, building principals and teachers, have, after a comprehensive two year process, recommended that we move to eliminate tracks in the elementary grades as we implement the new Trailblazers program. They are our experts; they are the ones who are teaching our children, and they are unquestionably committed to our goal of excellence. The Board of Education very much appreciates the thorough, reflective work of our faculty and administration. We particularly thank our Assistant Superintendent, Dr. Jennifer Dolan-Waldman, for her professionalism and patience in explaining the recommendations and answering questions in numerous presentations and meetings over the past months.
This is a serious task. After much deliberation, we feel that we can best assure the successful implementation of our math curriculum by phasing in the Trailblazers program in detracked classrooms, beginning with 3rd grade next year, and moving on to 4th grade the following year. This plan will first establish a consistent program at Dows Lane, with all K-3 students remaining with their classroom teachers for math. As next year's 3rd graders move to the Main Street School in 2005-06, we will have had a year to evaluate the program, continue to train teachers, fill in gaps as necessary, create enrichment and support programs and involve our new administrative team in the process. This plan assures continuity in that the students who have been grouped in this year's 3rd and 4th grades will continue in their groups through 4th and 5th grades. As has been our practice, and as the math committee recommends, all students will be phased in Middle School, beginning in 6th grade.
A word about communication. There has been a great deal of it on this issue - two extended Board discussions, two presentations for parents, many meetings with small groups and individuals and countless telephone conversations. The Board has had an extensive dialogue with the parent group opposed to de-tracking. An "FAQ" sheet went out to our e-mail list in preparation for this meeting, inviting all parents to attend, and we believe that every one of you who e-mailed us received a response. Please note, however, that our policy is to reserve the weblist for district use; it is not an open forum. We continue to invite your input and respond to all comments and questions.
We can all be deservedly proud of the level of public discourse that took place at Tuesday's meeting. We look forward to an ongoing dialogue as we begin implementing our exciting new math program. Thank you to all the parents, including several whose children will not be directly affected, for being part of an extraordinary process. As Board Trustee George Huzinec said at the end of the meeting: "More knowledge will surface as we move forward. I hope that all those involved continue to be active learners about this subject. I know I shall".
As always, the Board of Education welcomes your feedback: contact us at board@irvingtonschools.org.
