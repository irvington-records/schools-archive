Artwork submitted by Melissa Tierney, a fifth-grader at the Main Street School in Irvington, was selected to be part of an exhibit at the New York State Disabilities Awareness Art Contest. Melissa's poster, which promotes a "sharing community," will join others on display at the Empire State Plaza in Albany from June 7-13.
The artwork was selected by a judging panel from thousands of entries submitted by students across the state. Melissa's original poster depicts a wheelchair-bound student playing basketball, while being cheered on by a crowd of students. "It is our hope that visitors to the exhibit will have an opportunity to learn from the students' art how the inclusion of people with disabilities is taking place in your community," said Catharine McHugh, a spokesperson for the Advocacy Services Bureau in Albany.
Melissa said she was inspired to enter the contest after she took part in "Project Reach," a program run by Southern Westchester BOCES, that aims to enlighten and sensitize students to the feelings of those with handicapping conditions. A few weeks before Melissa drew the poster, she joined Main Street and Dows Lane students in experiencing first-hand how it feels to have a disability, by being exposed to "Project Reach's" simulations of conditions such as dyslexia, hearing impairment and blindness.
"Project Reach made me realize the kind of obstacles and frustrations handicapped students face everyday," said Melissa, who is hopeful her poster --- along with the artwork of others in the show --- can help promote tolerance for all students.
In Irvington, "Project Reach" is funded by a generous grant from the Irvington Education Foundation.
