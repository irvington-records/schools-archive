IRVINGTON HIGH SCHOOL SENIOR INTERNSHIP PROGRAM

Description

The Senior Internship Program is an opportunity for Irvington High School seniors to experience practical and meaningful internships for the purpose of learning more about a particular career. The internship might be based on an area of study or a future job. Eligible seniors will take their senior exams the week of May 10 (the same week as the AP Exams) and then spend the next four weeks at their internship.

Rationale

The Senior Internship Program will enable students to learn professional and technical skills and concepts in a variety of career areas, including:

Knowledge of basic principles of organization and administration, as well as general information about the demands of work
Enhancement of interpersonal and communication skills
Intellectual skills developed through experiences involving research and/or individual meetings with professional supervisors.

Student Responsibilities

The student intern will receive credit for his/her internship providing he/she fulfills the following requirements:

Reporting to the site of the internship for at least four days for an average of five hours
Reporting to the high school twice during the internship in order to meet with his/her mentor 
E-mailing or turning in his/her weekly journal 
Making a 5-minute presentation about his/her internship experience during the week of June 14 to an audience consisting of his/her mentor, parent, and peers
Work with the sponsor to develop an understanding of specific responsibilities and assignments
Follow the attendance policies of the school as well as the sponsor’s policy regarding hours worked, personal appearance, behavior, and office practices. 
*    See attachments for examples

Sponsor Responsibilities

Provide an orientation of the agency/organization to the intern
Introduce the intern to key members of the staff
Meet with the intern regularly to review progress and performance
Speak with the teacher liaison weekly to address issues and concerns and report on the intern’s progress
Sign the intern’s weekly timesheet/complete the evaluation at end of the internship*

Mentor Responsibilities

Help the intern arrange for his/her internship
Phone the internship sponsor once a week to address issues and concerns
Collect and monitor the weekly timesheets
Read and respond to the weekly journal reflections
Meet with your intern on two occasions to review his/her experience
Make one site visit if possible

Timeline

January: evening assembly for parents and seniors to introduce the program 

February: letter to school parent community asking for internship possibilities

March: seniors are assigned by lottery to mentors; placements are selected

April: sponsors are notified of senior intern placements*; expectations shared*

May: final exams the week of May 10. Internships begin May 17. Seniors come to school twice during internship for reflections/processing with mentor 

June: presentations week of June 14.
 
Notes

Students must complete the internship to receive full academic credit for the school year since this internship is in lieu of classes. Grading will be done on a pass/fail basis.
Students who are in danger of not graduating will be required to attend school in supervised workshops in addition to their internships (example: a student who is not passing English will attend an English workshop in the morning before heading off to his/her internship; consequently, the required internship hours will be reduced to 12 hours instead of 20). Workshops will be handled by the senior teachers.

Placements must be approved by the principal.

January, 2004

Dear Community Member,
It is with enthusiasm and anticipation that we write to you about a program at Irvington High School—The Senior Internship. The purpose of the program is to merge the energy and talents of our students with the experience and expertise of the workforce in our community. Our hope is that by combining careful, systematic organization from us with a willingness to provide an opportunity for meaningful work experience from the community, we will create positive, memorable learning experiences for both parties.
If you, or somebody you know, would be willing to sponsor a senior in this venture, you would be saying yes to giving one of our students a valuable opportunity—one they could possibly build on for the rest of their lives.
To be more specific:

Seniors will take their final and/or Advanced Placement exams the week of May 10. They will intern from May 17  to June 10, working a minimum of four days per week (Monday-Thursday) and a minimum of five hours a day
Seniors will keep a daily timesheet (signed by the sponsor) and a daily journal to reflect on this experience
Seniors will be assigned a teacher mentor at the high school who will monitor their progress and serve as liaisons with the sponsors
Seniors will meet two Fridays at the high school to meet with their teacher mentors and share their experiences with other seniors
The mentors will contact their students’ sponsors weekly and conduct one site visit if possible

The internship possibilities are endless. Obviously, we don’t want students to simply do busy work; we’re hopeful that the sponsors will make the internships as meaningful as possible given the short time frame. 
If you are willing to sponsor an intern, please return this sheet (see back); if you have any questions, please feel free to give me a call. (And since we hope to continue this program, please feel free to return this sheet even if your area isn’t listed and you’d like an intern; perhaps we can use your offer next year!)
Thanks for your interest in this collaboration between community and school.

Gratefully,
Scott Mosenthal, Principal

IRVINGTON HIGH SCHOOL SENIOR INTERNSHIP PROGRAM:

STUDENT ASSIGNMENTS

Student Assignments: to be e-mailed to mentors.

May 21`

Describe why you chose this internship and what you hope to gain from it personally and career-wise
What have been your first impressions at your internship?
Describe a problem that you face at your internship (feeling ignored, boredom, feeling overwhelmed, etc.). Discuss possible solutions to the problem

May 28

Describe a day at your internship. What is your job like? What are the people like? What is most satisfying/frustrating about your internship?

June 4

In preparation for this reflection, interview a person who works at your site. To consider: why did this person choose this job? How long has she/he been doing it? What initially inspired the person to choose this job? What is his/her educational background? How does it fit into this person’s career plans? What are the frustrations, disappointments, challenges, triumphs of the job for this person? What is your impression of this person? How successful is she/he at what she/he does? Do you think this person is fulfilled by her/his job? 

June 11

For this session, please return with your sponsor’s evaluation. What have you learned from this internship? What have you enjoyed most/least? Would you recommend this placement for someone next year? Why/why not? What would you have done differently the past three and ½ weeks if you had the chance to do it again? Why?

June 14

Please evaluate the internship experience from the school’s perspective. Is this a program we should continue? Why/why not? How can it be improved? What are it’s strengths and weaknesses?
