Master Plan Update: December 20, 1999
At the Board of Education meeting on Tuesday, December 14 the Board formally adopted the resolution, recommended by Dr. Vin Beni, our superintendent of schools, to submit a bond in the amount of $47million to the community.
Each of us on the Board recognizes that we have taken a momentous step. Each of us realizes that this bond proposition offers a unique opportunity for our school district to combine facility design and educational programming to achieve academic excellence. Each of us wholeheartedly supports this bond.
Much information has already been made available about the proposed master plan that would be funded through the bond, and much more will follow as we approach the February 3 vote date. In brief, the bond would authorize the district to borrow $47million for a major construction and renovation project. The significant and continuing growth of student enrollment in the district necessitates a response of this magnitude. However, the district has developed a comprehensive financial plan that includes substantial NY State building aid and BOCES rental for use of space, which will minimize the tax impact on district residents.
The specific proposal follows two years of intensive study and deliberation, with ongoing community participation. The major components of the proposal include: construction of a new middle school, as well as a field house containing a theater and a gymnasium, at the high school campus; extensive renovation of the existing high school; repair and maintenance of the Main St. and Dows Lane buildings; and refurbishment of our athletic fields. The plan effectively addresses the challenges posed by the growth in student population by reconfiguring Dows Lane as a K-3 school, moving grades 4 and 5 to the Main St. building, and maintaining a distinct middle school program in a new facility.
In the course of developing this proposal, members of our community expressed interest in other possible solutions, or in additional features to this master plan. These options were studied in depth.
First of all, the possibility of acquiring land for a new school site was explored. The ultimate cost, including land acquisition, site preparation and construction, would have added $16million to the bond proposal. Balancing this cost increase against the enhanced opportunities for middle school students and the ongoing administrative savings of having the middle and high schools share a campus clearly came out in favor of building at the high school campus.
Concerns about possible disadvantages of housing grades 6 through 12 on one campus have also been thoroughly addressed. In fact, many districts in Westchester already have this campus configuration. Conversations with staff in these districts indicate that the advantages far outweigh negative considerations. Again, the joint campus model also allows for much more programmatic flexibility, while maintaining the physical and educational integrity of the two schools.
Many residents requested that the inclusion of a pool be considered as part of the master plan. This issue, too, was investigated with great diligence. Our architect and construction manager worked with a prominent consultant to determine how a pool could be incorporated into the proposal. The only possibility would have been an indoor pool under the newly planned gymnasium/auditorium. In terms of cost, the pool would have added $5.6 million to the basic bond - 12% over the proposed $47 million. The estimate is high because the proposed field house would need to be substantially enlarged, with major structural revisions, in order to accommodate a pool. Alternative sites are simply not available given the space constraints of the district's campuses.
Finally, the Board recognizes its mandate as providing for the educational needs of the students of the school district. We can only consider a pool as one aspect of a physical education program. In this context, inclusion of the pool option, even as a ballot choice, would be inappropriate since the cost of the only possible pool option is indefensible in light of other district needs. There is a further consideration: the Board, administration, consultants and community have developed a strong, sound, prudent master plan. We need to focus on how this one plan addresses our facilities needs and enhances our educational programs, rather than become distracted by debate over a costly option which offers real, but limited benefits.
We wished to share our thinking with residents at this time, as we prepare for a vote on the bond proposition scheduled for Feb. 3, 2000. This proposal is, we firmly believe, simply the best option available. As well as addressing our space needs and refurbishing field space, the bond provides significant enhancements of our science labs, library and research facilities, art and music rooms and technology program; and adds a cafeteria, gymnasium and 800-seat auditorium to our facilities.
Thanks to all of you who have participated in the development of this proposal. We members of the Board of Education are privileged to present this proposition to the community and respectfully request your serious consideration of the bond proposition on Feb. 3. In January you will receive detailed information on the scope of the project at each school. A separate mailing will also list dates when you can tour our facilities and gain even more information about the master plan.
