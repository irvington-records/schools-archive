Bond Facts Continued
A: The majority of districts (over 35 at last count) in our area have already addressed or are currently in the process of addressing enrollment growth through bond referenda. Although Irvington is frequently compared to our neighboring districts, our anticipated growth is far higher than that of Hastings or Dobbs Ferry. Within a few years, our school District will be similar in size to Byram Hills, Rye and Tarrytown. Irvington's situation is clearly unique: no other district in our region has seen the combination of growth to date and projected growth at such a rapid pace. Our student population will have doubled from its low to its projected high in 2007-08 of 2286 students.
A: In addition to maintaining the integrity of the Irvington School District, passage of the bond would be beneficial to our community in many ways. Creation of a separate Middle School will ensure Irvington's outstanding educational system. The proposed gymnasium/theater complex at the Middle School/High School campus will be available for the enjoyment of all residents. Additionally, the reputation of a school district has a direct effect on a community's property values. Investing in our schools maintains a high demand for local real estate and will continue to increase property values. Most importantly, Irvington will continue its tradition of excellence in education.
A: Without voter approval, the District would be unable to deal with the projected increase in its student enrollment. Increased class sizes throughout the District, reduced programs, split sessions, and other emergency measures, which would negatively impact our excellent educational program, would result. Our reputation as one of Westchester's premier school districts would be harmed, negatively affecting our property values.
By passing this Bond Referendum before June 30, 2000, the District will be able to maximize our State Building aid. After June, this aid will be lowered to only 10% of costs over the life of the bond. The District would then lose more than $7 million in state aid.
Finally, this bond is an opportunity for our District to provide a safe learning environment for our students. As the result of past financial difficulties, the District could not address all of its facility needs. It is important to remember that just as homeowners must maintain their homes, the District must maintain its facilities that inevitably wear out, such as boilers, roofs and windows. These large projects would be difficult to complete using our yearly budget. This Bond will enable the District to address all of these critical needs while gaining the 18% State Building Aid, which would generally not be available for maintenance projects in a yearly budget.
A: The Board of Education is determined to have our facilities ready for our burgeoning student population by the 2003-04 school year. Due to a very lengthy State Education Department review process, it is essential that we vote at the earliest possible opportunity. February is the earliest the District can vote, due to requirements for submission of documents to the State Education Department. It is important to emphasize, however, that the Board has not in any way rushed in fashioning this Master Plan proposal. The process, described in detail above, is the result of nearly 2 years of research, hours of community volunteer effort and many public meetings.
