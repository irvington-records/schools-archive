Long Range Planning
**Reprinted here with the kind permission of the Irvington Viewpoint, first published in the September 1999 issue as a "School Matters" column, by Janice Silverberg.
Long range planning remains the focus of Irvington’s Board of Education, administration, and the many volunteers participating in the regularly scheduled LRP committee meetings. Exploratory conversations are in progress regarding every possible site for a facility expansion or a new building. Our existing schools locations have been thoroughly scrutinized as to expansion potential, alternative use of space and/or program changes.
There is on-going dialogue with the governments of the three communities (Irvington, Greenburgh and Tarrytown) which comprise our district. It is important to note that our school district encompasses an area of almost four square miles – twice the area of our immediate neighbors, Hastings, Dobbs Ferry and Ardsley to which we are often compared; this, and the fact that it includes three different communities make the Irvington district unique.
Various expansion options have been studied in depth. Each has been evaluated first of all in terms of contribution to the district’s educational program. The likely long and short-term effects of reconfiguring grades and relocating schools have been studied. The pros and cons of land acquisition vs expansion of existing sites have been compared. We have taken a hard look at how best to configure and utilize special areas such as science and computer labs, cafeterias, auditorium and gymnasium spaces and libraries to achieve maximum efficiency. As we balance educational with fiscal concerns, we have also considered the staffing needs of each option and assessed the likely continuing impact on future annual operating budgets as well as one-time renovation and construction costs. In every case, we have examined how well the particular plan would accommodate the projected enrollment numbers over the coming decade. Please refer to the minutes of these meetings, which are posted on the district website (www.irvingtonschools.org) or contact the Superintendent’s office (591-8501) to obtain copies.
In the course of these deliberations, the district received two very welcome pieces of news. First of all, after many requests, the State Department of Transportation has approved the placement of a traffic light at the intersection of High School Drive and Broadway. This will greatly alleviate the existing traffic flow problems at that point, and should ensure a much safer access to the campus. The second boon to our community is incorporated in the recently passed state budget, which includes an additional 10 per cent for building aid for local districts.
As we begin the new school year, we expect with the help of the Long Range Planning Committee to have narrowed the number of potential options to two or three. Each will be thoroughly scrutinized by the Board and the community as we move to a master plan that will best meet the current and future needs of our district. Please plan on attending the Board of Education meetings on September 14, when the Board will receive preliminary reports from the committees engaged in the long range planning process, and most particularly on September 28, which will be a full Board work session on the issue.
