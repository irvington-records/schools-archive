Bond Facts Continued
- The State Education Department has increased required classroom size by 50%.
- New State Standards require students to take more classes.
- Computer labs did not exist back then. Additional classroom space is needed as well for technology to be integrated into our daily curriculum.
- Full day kindergarten did not exist in the 1970's.
- Special education students are increasingly educated in-district.
- A new 6-8 Grade Middle School will be a freestanding structure to the east of the High School. A wing for specialized educational spaces (such as science, music and art) and a new cafeteria will complete a quadrangle formed by the new complex and will allow for significant cost-savings by enabling the Middle School and High School to share some facilities.
- A new gymnasium/theater complex will be constructed to the east of the new Middle School.
- The High School will be completely renovated, resulting in modern classroom, technology and library spaces.
- Dows Lane will become a K-3 grade school and the Main Street School building will be converted to a 4-5 grade school. (Dows Lane is currently a K-5 school. Main Street is currently a 6-8 school.)
- Districtwide improvements will include major field renovations, new tennis courts, additional parking, and ADA (American with Disabilities Act) compliance.
- In addition, the Master Plan will enable the District to complete important renovations and infrastructure repairs to provide our children with safe and appropriate educational facilities.
A: The Long Range Planning Committee carefully studied the possibility of temporary measures as well as permanent solutions available to our District in light of our rapidly growing enrollment including reconfiguring grades, relocating administrative offices, scheduling changes, expansion/renovation, and construction of a new school on a separate site. The Committee and the Board of Education, after much analysis, concluded that the only option that met our long-term enrollment and educational program needs was the construction of a new school. The Board and Committee then analyzed a variety of proposals before concluding that this Master Plan was both the most educationally sound and most cost-effective proposal that would meet our space needs for many years to come.
