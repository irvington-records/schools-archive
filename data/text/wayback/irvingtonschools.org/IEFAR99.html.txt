Annual Report ~ April 1999
Through the overwhelming generosity of the Irvington community, 1998 was an extremely successful year for the Irvington Education Foundation. IEF fiscal 1998-1999 brought more than $100,000 in funds to support enrichment programs in technology, science and math, visual and performing arts, humanities and multi-cultural curriculum, special education and physical education. This extraordinary response was elicited from our June direct mail solicitation and from our magnificent "Life is A Cabaret" auction and gala fund-raiser held in the fall.
Founded in 1996 to enhance the quality of our public school education, the IEF has raised more than $165,000 since its inception. We are proud to be a part of this community and are thankful for its investment in its children.
As a result of the superb response from our community, IEF was able to fund 23 significant enrichment projects, spread between Dows Lane, Irvington Middle School and Irvington High School for the 1998-1999 school year. The funded projects were selected from grants submitted by the faculties of the district and include:
Humanities and Multi-Cultural Programs
- 150 new books for IMS library will be purchased to modernize and supplement current biography section.
- Acclaimed African American poet, Joanna Featherstone, will perform works of noted African American poets and writers as part of an enrichment of social studies and English curricula for IMS students.
- Performance of an interactive, magical, musical adventure story from Haiti will enrich student’s study of different cultures and rhythm, for grades 2-5.
- Civil War re-enactment will be funded again this year for Dows Lane 5th graders as part of their study of this period of American history.
Math, Science and the Environment
- Calculator Based Laboratory system will be purchased for IHS Science Department, enabling the accumulation, storage and analysis of data collected by students during science experiments.
- The Jason Project , an interactive exploration of the tropical rainforest, in its second year for IMS 8th graders, will be enhanced through procurement of slides, artifacts and anecdotes educating students about the delicate balance between indigenous people and the rainforest.
- Study of live birds of prey, mammals and reptiles will dovetail with Animalia theme; 6th and 7th graders will discuss animals in literature and legends.
- Purchase of math manipulatives for 6th and 7th graders will help them conceptualize more effectively.
- Customized environmental program focusing on team building at the Herrlich Environmental Center was provided for 7th grade students. This generous grant was funded by the Suzanne Dorff Memorial Fund.
- Deep Sea Magic Show will return to Dows Lane to teach kindergartners about the fragile ocean environment.
Physical Education
- New offerings at IHS supplement traditional physical education programming by exposing students to alternatives such as yoga, karate and weight lifting.
Special Education
- Funding of an assistive technology consultant to evaluate the communication needs of special education students in the middle and high schools and make specific recommendations.
Technology
- Purchase of two networked computers for IHS will expand the students electronic search tools enabling students to more quickly access relevant Internet links.
- Two computers were purchased to further IEF funded course Architectural Design and Drawing, building upon the vision at IHS to establish a complete Computer Aided Design (CAD) laboratory.
Visual and Performing Arts
- Funded High School play, "Little Shop of Horrors", to be presented April 23-24, 1999 to the community. This represents the third year of IEF funding.
- Purchase of "black scrim" to enhance theatre productions in High School auditorium.
- Special assembly will be held to expose students to modern and jazz dance, an art form not currently offered at IHS.
- Purchased acoustical band/choral shells to enhance performances of musical groups in K-12, both indoors and outdoors.
- Funded special tour of Madame Walker’s residence focusing on African Art and African American artists for Middle School and High School art students.
- Animalia, project at the Middle School enabling art students to study and create animals, focusing on our changing environment. The art produced will be used in a musical performance scheduled for the school term.
- Westchester Council of the Arts Artist in Residence will introduce Dows Lane first graders to the art of papermaking.
- Purchased laminating machine for Dows Lane which will preserve existing and newly donated art prints.
The Community Rises to The Challenge
The IEF’s second annual fundraising event was held at the magnificent home of Nancy and Bruce McGaw, and provided the perfect backdrop for the talents of Irvington’s own magical performers. It also brought together our entire community; local restaurants, businesses, teachers, administrators, students and community members in a wonderful concerted effort. Thanks to all. We look forward to building upon our success and continuing to provide innovative, quality programs for the Irvington public schools.
