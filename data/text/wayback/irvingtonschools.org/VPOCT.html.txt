Long Range Planning
**Reprinted here with the kind permission of the Irvington Viewpoint, first published in the November 1999 issue as a "School Matters" column, by Janice Silverberg.
Many months of meetings, deliberations and debate came to fruition with the presentation of the proposed Master Plan for expansion of our school facilities, which took place at the Sept. 28 Board of Education meeting. The district’s architect, Peter Gisolfi, first compared several possible grade configuration/building options. His analysis was based on factors including the relative square footage necessary (based on State Education Department guidelines), costs for any land acquisition or site preparation, construction costs and programmatic suitability. He also described the timeline for bringing a specific proposal to the community for a vote, as well as the process of developing the design and obtaining the necessary approvals for a construction/renovation project.
Mr. Gisolfi then described the proposed plan developed by the Long-Range Planning Committee and the Board of Education committees. This proposal would add a distinct Middle School (grades 6,7,8) wing to the existing High School, and include a fourth wing housing science labs, an expanded cafeteria, computer areas and music rooms. The existing gymnasium wing would be refurbished, with greatly enhanced library space and art rooms. A separate field house, including a second gym and a theater, would complete the proposed facilities expansion, and have the additional advantage of offering accessibility to the larger community. Key elements of the concept include widening and rerouting High School Drive, adding parking, and refurbishing the East Field.
Renovation and repair of our existing buildings are critical to the proposed master plan. Michael Orifici, of JMOA Associates, the construction manager for the proposed project, outlined the extensive work necessary at all three schools. Refurbishment of the lower Dows Lane field and construction of tennis courts where the old basketball court is located would enhance our overall athletic program.
As the plan has not been finalized, there is not yet a specific dollar figure for the project. Mr. Gisolfi estimates a cost of $34 million for new construction under this proposal. An additional $8-$11 million would be necessary for renovations at the three existing schools. These renovations will be prioritized, with some funded through the annual budget. Based on these estimated costs, the district’s financial advisor Bob Kerr was able to spell out a scenario for financing the project assuming a 30 year bond at current interest rates of 51/2%. Assistant Superintendent Don Marra then presented the outline of a financial plan for the district. This plan factors in State Aid (18% for projects approved before July), BOCES capital and rental contributions and the reduction of our CERT line to come to an anticipated annual increase of between $165 and $215 for most homeowners.
At breakout tables after the presentation, residents had the opportunity to ask questions and discuss the project with the consultants, members of the Board of Education and the administrators. A great deal of verbal, written, and E-mail feedback has been received since the initial presentation, and discussions continued in the October meetings. Overall, the community response is strongly supportive of this proposal. Many have indicated the wish to include a pool, which would add to our athletic program as well as provide a real benefit to the community. This, as well as other suggestions, is subject for careful consideration at upcoming Board meetings.
The process of crafting the optimal master plan for the district continues through November. Please make your views known. A special hot line has been set up to receive your comments: 591-4239. As always, you may contact any member of the Board, the administration, or the Long-Range Planning committee directly.
