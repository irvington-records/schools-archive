Executive Summary
The Long Range Planning Committee (LRPC), comprised of board, administration and community members, was established by the Board of Education during the 1997-98 school year to analyze the challenges facing the District as a result of its rapid student population growth. Presented to the Board of Education and the public on January 26, 1999, this report represents the beginning of a process to inform the Irvington school community of the need to address our space and facilities requirements for the next 5 to 10 years.
In the fall of 1998, the committee recommended, and the Board approved, the appointment of a new locally based and well-respected demographer. The demographic and capacity report indicates that:
- The challenges presented by our rapidly growing student population will continue for the next 5 to 10 years.
- Student population growth is expected to be the most rapid in the Middle School and High School. Enrollment growth is likely to be 37% in the Middle School (from 346 to 474 students) and 68% in the High School (from 372 to 626 students) from1998-99 to 2006-07.
- Total District student enrollment is likely to surpass the all-time District enrollment high of 1844 as early as the 2001 school year. This all-time high enrollment takes place against the backdrop of a changing educational environment which requires additional space.
- All three of our schools are close to or at capacity , as they are currently utilized.
- Total District student population by the 2003-04 school year is likely to be 1937 but could be as high as 2111, respectively a 21% and 32% increase from our current population of 1597.
- Total District student population by the 2006-07 school year is likely to be 2029 but could be as high as 2319, respectively a 27% and 45% increase from our current population of 1597.
In addition, the committee has determined that Dows Lane, with a student population approaching 900, is the largest elementary school in suburban Westchester.
The committee has reviewed a variety of measures which might address our District’s facilities needs, analyzing the pros and cons of each. These options include: grade reconfiguration, temporary measures (such as buying more modulars or modifying our educational program), relocation of our administrative offices, scheduling options, school building expansion/renovation and construction of a school. As a result of its deliberations, the LRPC recommends that the Board of Education begin the process of meeting our space and facilities challenges by:
- Retaining an architect.
- Expanding the LRPC.
- Developing a process to elicit community input.
- Examining funding options including state aid, BOCES aid and financing.
- Developing short term solutions to address the District’s challenges until a long range solution is in place.
Finally, the LRPC Vision Statement asserts that in fashioning any long term solution, the Board and community must:
- Meet our needs for the next 5 to 10 years.
- Provide a solution which addresses our needs from a district-wide perspective.
- Take into consideration our changing educational environment.
- Provide our students with a safe and healthy learning environment.
- Provide for growth in a cost-effective way.
- View the maintenance and enhancement of the Irvington School District’s educational excellence as our most important concern
