The following are the minutes of the Public Work Session of the Board of Education of the Irvington Union Free School District, held in the Campus Presentation Room, January 24, 2006, with the following present:

Mr. Paul Mandel, Mrs. Diane Mundree, Mrs. Janice Silverberg, Mrs. Carol Gutman, Mrs. Robyn Kerner.  Also present:  Dr. Kathleen Matusiak - Superintendent of Schools, Mr. James Reese - Assistant Superintendent for Business, Dr. Scott Mosenthal – High School Principal, Mr. Scott Fried – Middle School Principal, Mr. Donald Kuhn – Main Street School Interim Principal, Mr. Joe DeGennaro-High School Assistant Principal, Ms. Raina Kor, Middle School Assistant Principal, Mr. Joseph Rodriguez-Dows Lane Pricipal, Ms. Eileen McGuire – Dows Lane Elementary School Assistant Principal, Dr. Joel Klein-Interim Director of Special Education, Ms. Madee Stern-Assistant Director of Special Education, members of the community. 

The Public Work Session was called to order by Mr. Mandel at 7:40 p.m. 
      
Comments from Residents
Several Parents expressed concern that some math students are not being adequately challenged and requested an increase of 2-3 teaching positions to meet the needs of students for the 2006-07 school year.
Deb Weintraub, PTSA Co-President, announced that the PTSA, along with the Westchester Medical Center and the Health and Safety Committee, is sponsoring an inexperienced driver program on February 1, 2006 at 7:30 p.m. in the CPR for parents and on February 8th for students. On February 7th, a 50th birthday party for Dows Lane Elementary is scheduled for students.  Tickets for the Harlem Magic Masters on February 10th, went on sale on Friday, January 20th and sold out immediately.

2.     Information Items
         Dr. Matusiak reported on the following:
She attended the Chamber of Commerce Networking meeting on Tuesday, January 24th and had the opportunity to explain the 2006-07 budget process and how it will be presented to the community. The meeting will be broadcast on the cable station.
The Special Education Consortium has finalized an agreement that allows member districts to pool their resources to provide special education programs to its students. The Consortium is going to look into the possibility of developing summer programs.
Dows Lane has begun their indoor recess program and kicked off the No Put Downs program.
She has visited several 5th grade classes, sharing poetry and speaking to them about what a “budget” is.
The IEF has provided $15,000 for theatre lighting that will be installed for the HS musical. The resolution will be on the February 7th agenda for Board approval.
High School Senior, Danielle Frimer, has been named an Intel Semi-finalist and will be recognized at the February 7th Board meeting.

3.     SWBOCES Services Presentation
 Dr. Jeffrey Hanson, Assistant Superintendent of SWBOCES, provided an overview of the programs and services BOCES offers to component school districts as well as newly created programs for the 2006-07 school year. SWBOCES is seeking feedback from school districts as to what other programs and services they’d like to see offered. Mr. Paul Mandel noted that Irvington enjoys a positive relationship with SWBOCES and values the work that they do for districts.

4.    2006-07 School Calendar-Supplemental Log – Appendix A
The draft 2006-07 was presented to the Board for review. The Board will vote for adoption at the March 7th Board of Education Meeting. 

2006-07 Budget – Enrollment Presentation-Supplemental Log-Appendix B

Dr. Matusiak explained that the budget process and presentations to the Board and community has started earlier this year and that presentations on the major components that comprise the total budget will be presented at each Board meeting. The presentations will be factual in nature and ask “what if” questions only. Final Recommendations will be made at the formal budget adoption at the April 18th Board of Education meeting.                  By presenting the information in this way, it is hoped that the Board of Education and the public will have ample time to digest the information being presented and provide feedback to the Administration.

Dr. Matusiak reviewed the projections provided by the demographer. The trend, through 2009-10, is for lower enrollment numbers at the elementary level and an increased enrollment at the secondary level.

Mr. Reese reviewed the 2002-2006 school year enrollment figures. For the 2005-2006 school year, the average class size at the K-5 level is 19.8, with seven sections in grades K-3 and eight sections in grades 3-5. For illustrative purposes only, Mr. Reese, provided the average class sizes K-5 if the District were to reduce each grade by one section. For instance, the average class size at the K-5 level would be 22.3.  
Mrs. Diane Mundree and several community members voiced concern about reducing the number of sections for grades K-5. Mrs. Mundree stated that the community feels very strongly about maintaining lower class sizes. Dr. Matusiak again emphasized that this information was factual in nature and that no recommendations were being made at this time, but that it is important for Administration to provide the information necessary for people to make informed decisions when looking at the total budget.

The average class size for grades 6-8 is projected to increase slightly, to 19 students. For grades 9-12, the projections also show a slight increase.

The projected enrollment for special education shows a slight increase, from 182 to 188.  Out-of-district placements are expected to remain the same at 17 students.  

Mrs. Janice Silverberg asked Dr. Matusiak and Mr. Reese if it was possible to get a more substantive look at number of actual students in academic instructions given that there are students pulled out for special education instruction, AIS as well as enrichment. This information would be helpful to get a picture of the activity in classrooms and the actual numbers at any given point in a day.
