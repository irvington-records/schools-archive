  II	INFORMATION ITEMS - None

             The Agenda was changed as follows:

Dr. Matusiak reported that September 17 has been designated “National Constitution Day.” Dr. Ralph Napolitano visited classes at the Main Street School and Dows Lane to speak with students about the U.S. Constitution. Dr. Matusiak welcomed Jena Goldman, Samantha Lee, Yoshiyo Funakoshi, Kira Murpy and Alaina Steinberg, Main Street School students from Ms. Nancy Woeckner and Ms. Joan Snell’s classes. The students performed a skit they wrote on the birth of the U.S. Constitution.  Mitchell Toolan and Katie O’Donnell, students from Ms. Mary Lynn Child’s 3rd grade class, read their class’ Constitution  that was written by the class.

Mr. Mandel thanked the students for their presentation. 

III	COMMENTS FROM RESIDENTS

Deb Weintraub announced that the 1st general membership meeting of the PTSA will be held on 
October 19th at 9:30 a.m. in the Campus Presentation Room.  Dr. Matusiak will speak to the members.  Tickets for the Sock Hop at Dows Lane on October 21st will be on sale October 15th at 9:00 a.m. at Dows Lane.  The PTSA is sponsoring a Walk-A-Thon and Bake Sale on Thursday, October 13th at Dows Lane to raise money for the new playground.

A resident asked why 9th grade Spanish and Math workbooks were not yet available and when they’d be distributed. Dr. Mosenthal said that half the books had been delivered and the other half were being shipped shortly. 

Residents questioned rumors that the District was considering eliminating the 5th grade Language Arts program next year. Dr. Napolitano responded that the District has no plans to eliminate the program, however, it is reviewing the program in order to determine how to better integrate the program into the curriculum.
 
Several residents asked about other language courses being eliminated.  Mr. Mandel responded that the Board of Education has supported the world language program, even in difficult fiscal times and is committed to offering programs that have strong student interest. Dr. Mosenthal stated that because of the numerous AP course offerings, students are forced to choose between courses and that offerings are subject to change from year to year depending on interest.

VI	PROGRAM AND POLICIES

Superintendent’s Report – None

B.  High School Report and Action Plan –(Supplemental Log-Appendix A)
Dr. Scott Mosenthal and Mr. Joe DeGennaro presented the Irvington High School Report and Action Plan. Dr. Mosenthal reported that there are 597 students at the High School.  Dr. Mosenthal presented an overview of the Class of 2005, reporting that 98% of the class graduated with Regent Diplomas, and 47 students graduated with High Honors, a “tremendous accomplishment” for a school the size of Irvington. There were 5 National Merit students. Regent’s test scores rose, nearing the District’s goal of 50% of the population scoring in the 85-100% range.  The number of AP courses offered has increased and high scores were maintained.  

Dr. Mosenthal proudly pointed out that 114 out of 136 students are National Latin Exam award winners. The Academic Challenge Team were in the top 15 in the nation and that Westchester Magazine ranked Irvington 8 out of 47 of Westchester high schools. Also noteworthy, 53 students achieved Advanced Placement Scholars status.

Mr. Joe DeGennaro reported on the Academic Eligibility Policy and Student Management. Dr. Mosenthal reviewed the wide range of Academic/Extracurricular Highlights for 2004-2005. He pointed out that without the support of the PTSA and the IEF, these programs would be greatly diminished.

New programs for the 2005-2006 school year include the Alternative Education Program and the Therapeutic Support Class along with a skills labs and greater choices of English electives.
  
Dr. Mosenthal reviewed the I.H.S. Building Action Plan Initiatives that were developed via the Tri-State Consortium and guided by the 3-year Strategic Plan. The funding for the initiatives under the Strategic Plan will be budgeted within the school year that they are to be implemented.

Discussion of Artificial Turfed Fields – (Supplemental Log-Appendix B)
Mr. Ron Tetelman of Eberlain and Eberlain presented proposed alternative placements for the proposed tennis courts and East Field, which provides greater distance between the District and Fieldpoint property lines. The proposal is contingent on negotiations with Deerman Park.  The surveyor is compiling the necessary deeds, easements, etc. before staking the property line with Fieldpoint.

Mr. McCormack reviewed a table showing how the fields are utilized and a grid showing when proposed lighting will be needed.  He pointed out that without lighting, the District will be unable accommodate all the teams that it supports.

A baseline budget will be developed breaking out the individual costs of the items contained in the proposal. Mr. Mandel noted that the Board was to vote on the proposal at this meeting but has decided to postpone the approval and Bond Referendum.  No alternate dates have been set.

I.  Change to Superintendent’s Conference Days
 
Dr. Ralph Napolitano asked the Board of Education to consider extending the half day Superintendent’s Conference days scheduled for January 12 and March 16 to full days, and rescheduled for January 30th and March 27th  respectively.  Dr. Napolitano reported that this is necessary in order to provide teachers with appropriate scoring time to meet the deadlines required by the New York State Education Department to score the ELA assessments.
