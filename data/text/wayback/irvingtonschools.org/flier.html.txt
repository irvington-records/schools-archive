Announcement of 9/28/99 Board Meeting
OF THE PROPOSED MASTER PLAN
FOR THE IRVINGTON SCHOOL DISTRICT
MEETING THE CHALLENGE: SPACE FOR STUDENTS
Our student population is growing rapidly. We can anticipate an increase of between 28% and 45% within the next 5 years; enrollment will almost certainly exceed 2000.
This increase is making a significant impact on all three district schools:
- Dows Lane, with 950 children enrolled this year, is the largest elementary school in suburban Westchester;
- The Main St. building, which has housed our grades 6 through 8 Middle School for 25 years, will soon only be able to accommodate two grades;
- The High School building requires extensive structural renovations, as well as improvement of inadequate and outmoded science lab and library facilities;
A long-range planning process has been underway for a year and a half, culminating in a summer of intensive study and deliberation by our Board of Education, administration, and 28 member community Long Rang Planning Committee. These groups have worked with the district’s architect, Peter Gisolfi, and construction manager, JMOA Associates, and consulted traffic and financial planning specialists as well.
The goal: to develop a masterplan for the district that will meet our immediate educational needs, provide for future growth and ensure continued academic excellence
MASTER PLAN RECOMMENDATION
The various groups involved in long range planning carefully considered the realities of the anticipated increase in student population, time constraints, limitations of existing sites and buildings, construction feasibility, programmatic impact and cost of land. As a result, several building and grade configuration options were eliminated. There is growing consensus on the broad outline of a master plan for the community to consider:
- K-3 at Dows Lane
- Grades 4 and 5 in the Main St. building
- Construct a new 6-8 Middle School on the High School campus, with a separate field house for a gymnasium/auditorium
There are a number of clear advantages to this plan:
- additional science labs and other facilities
- increased course options for middle school students
- separate wings would maintain physical distinction between the two schools
- expanded cafeteria could offer lunch throughout the district
- immediate and on-going staffing and administrative savings
- entire community would benefit from the field house
- traffic analysis indicates that improvements to HS Drive, including the addition of a traffic signal at the intersection with Rt. 9, will result in safe traffic flow at acceptable levels.
The possibility of acquiring land remains under consideration. Our district is "land poor", and constructing a new school on a new campus is an attractive option for many reasons. Ordinarily, a fourth campus would be most consistent with our anticipated student population, would best position the district for future growth, and would add to our limited field space, thus enhancing our athletic programs. However, the costs involved in the purchase of property and site preparation are high. A new campus would add considerably to the cost of the project as well as extending the time necessary for completion by at least a year.
TUES. SEPT. 28, 7:30PM IN THE MIDDLE SCHOOL AUDITORIUM
The Board of Education will conduct a work session, reviewing the long range planning process to date, explaining the reasons for elimination of certain options from consideration, and exploring the specifics of the master plan recommendation in more detail.
The district’s consultants will report, and be available at breakout tables after the presentation to answer questions. The community will hear from our:
- architect
- construction manager
- traffic analyst
- financial advisor
The discussions about the potential enhancement of our district have been very exciting. At this time, the community will have the opportunity to review the details of the proposal and offer input to the Board prior to the adoption of a specific plan. Without doubt, our community will be asked to consider a project of great significance: in scope, in effect on education and in cost. We invite and urge all residents to attend what will surely be one of the most important school board meetings of the year.
