New Buildings! How will they impact instruction?  Although this has been the worst winter in at least five years, the new buildings are ahead of schedule.  It is anticipated that the Superintendent’s Conference Day planned for June will be used to help with the move into the new buildings.  In addition, there are two Superintendent’s Conference Days planned for September in order to provide additional time for teachers to settle into their new spaces.  Administrators shared ways the new buildings will impact instruction.
High School: The science classes will no longer drive the schedule because teachers will not be sharing labs.  The art department will have three rooms and a photo lab.  There will be several computer labs each designed for different purposes.  The library will have additional access to computers in order for all students to have an opportunity to use computers.  The music department will have a larger space to accommodate a large band.  There will be an additional meeting room designed for presentations.  Finally the HS administration offices will be reconfigured.
Middle School: Science and Home & Careers will have a larger space with new equipment.  The floor plan allows for a team structure, which will provide teachers with the opportunity to talk.  Student movement throughout the building will also be easier.
Main Street School (4-5): As the 4-5 school moves to Main Street, they are considering new and old traditions.  They are determining the most appropriate layout while considering the pros and cons of teaming.  There are three areas of focus that the Transition Committee is currently working on: a playground, parking, and storage.
Dows Lane (K-3): K-3 will have additional space, even without the modulars. This will give specialists classrooms and will ease transitions. They are considering changes in schedules.

Discussion of New Programs Under Consideration/Budget for 2003-2004 - Recent articles in the Journal News and The New York Times outlined Gov. Pataki’s budget cuts. It is still unclear how the reduction in aid will impact schools although there are major concerns about how the budget will affect Irvington directly.  Despite the budget cuts, several new programs are still being considered. Principals and Department Chairs outlined several proposed programs.
Full Day Kindergarten: In order to enhance the ELA and literacy program, create a less fragmented schedule and have less pressure, a full day Kindergarten program is under consideration. The proposal is to continue to have half days in September in order to provide time for screenings and parent meetings, and then to begin the full day program in October.
Science Research: The science department is proposing a three year-three credit program for students who want to take additional science classes. A teacher would be trained for two weeks during the summer. Then students would work with a mentor to further examine an area of interest.  Students would receive college credit from SUNY Albany. 
High School Electives: The High School is also proposing several additional electives including Statistics, AP Computer, Music in Our Lives, Music Theory and college credit for a fourth year of foreign language. 


Grants - The IEF and PTA are looking for significant grants. The grants should enhance programs and are not used for salaries.

Updates on Curriculum Work - Principals and Chairs shared the work that has been completed.
Scott Mosenthal said that Business Department has been gathering information from various sources, making visitations and developing course proposals.
Joe DeGennaro reported that the PE/Health Department has been following the model of the ELA committee. They have been aligning the current curriculum with the state standards. They have set a timeline, which includes visitations, staffing, considering new programs, and have begun drafting a written document.
John LaMassa said that the Science Department has continued to work with a consultant in order to outline “where we are” and “where we want to go.” They are examining the curriculum, courses, sequence, delivery, and inquiry activities. In addition, they are working with the Math Department to coordinate math skills with science curriculum. The technology component is being examined including Pasco Probes and SmartBoards. 
Raina Kor reported that the Library committee is looking at the needs in grades K-5 and the research component. The new library will have a lab as well as individual access to computers.  This is in addition to the two labs in the Middle School and High School. 
The World Language Department is finalizing their board presentation. They are exploring the possibility of starting foreign language in 5th grade. They have been examining current research to make this decision. 
Mrinalini Rajwar told the group that technology has been increased at the Middle School. There are several classes with clusters of computers in order to integrate technology and differentiate instruction. The teachers have been given a lot of training in order to integrate technology effectively into their classes.  It is anticipated that additional classes in the Middle School and classes in the High School will have clusters of computers next year, depending on the budget availability. 
Nadine McDermott reported that the Art Department has completed several school visits where they discussed several targeted issues with the Department Chairs.  They are examining the collected data and plan to begin writing in March. 
Lisa Urban and Jennifer Dolan-Waldman told the Committee that the Math Department is examining several new textbook series that will incorporate State and national standards.  The three series that were considered were Everyday Math, Investigations/TERC, and Trailblazers.  It was determined that Everyday Math did not meet the needs of the district.  The remaining two series will be piloted to determine the appropriate curriculum.  With both programs it will be crucial to incorporate staff development.  In addition, the issue of phasing is being examined in order to ensure that students are appropriately prepared for 4th and 8th grade tests as well as the Math A exam that is required for graduation in NY State.  The goal of the curriculum changes are to make sure that students are challenged and supported at all levels, and that students have more opportunity to engage in math at all levels.  We would also like to see more students complete math through the pre-calculus level.
