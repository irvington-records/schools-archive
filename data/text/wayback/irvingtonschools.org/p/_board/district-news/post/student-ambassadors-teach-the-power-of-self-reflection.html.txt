Student Ambassadors Teach the Power of Self-Reflection
As part of the Student Ambassadors: Bringing Self-Aware Individuals to IMS program, Irvington High School sophomores recently partnered with Irvington Middle School sixth graders to lead a lesson focused on decision-making and self-awareness.
Building on a prior reading of “The Good Egg,” a book that explores themes such as managing expectations, emotional regulation and being kind to oneself and others, the high school students continued to guide the sixth graders in developing self-awareness skills. During the Remote-Control lesson, the students engaged with a series of real-life scenarios and reflected on their thoughts and reactions.
“They were asked to ‘pause’ and check in with their feelings, ‘rewind’ and think about why they felt that way and ‘fast forward’ to discuss how they responded,” said Irvington High School teacher Carleen Julian, who initiated the program. “The lesson closed with the sixth graders writing in journals the Ambassadors provided about what they learned and how they would use what they learned in the future.”
The scenarios addressed several experiences, including navigating group work when a peer is left out; balancing multiple commitments such as sports, academics and family obligations; coping with unexpected academic outcomes and managing feelings when excluded from social plans.
“The sixth graders were overwhelmingly involved, voicing their thoughts confidently and excitedly,” Julian said. “It was a pleasure to see them in the classroom setting, the Ambassadors serving in the capacity of teacher in such a natural way and the sixth graders expressing their enthusiasm for their time together.”
Sixth grader Joey Guzman said that he learned that life is full of challenges and sometimes you need to press the pause button.
“There are good times to press the pause button which will help you stop and think about what you are doing,” sixth grader Olivia DiCarlo said.
The new peer initiative was designed to strengthen connections between students and deepen understanding of the district’s Portrait of a Graduate attributes. It also provides younger students with mentorship from older peers while allowing high school students to develop leadership skills grounded in reflection, empathy and personal growth.
