Bond Facts
A: Since 1989, Irvington School District enrollment has risen by over 600 students (55%). Between now and the year 2007-08, enrollment is projected to rise by an additional 600 students. Overall, this would represent a 102% increase District-wide since 1989. This enrollment increase has also made Dows Lane one of the largest elementary schools in Westchester. This surge in the lower grades is now impacting our Middle and High Schools. In order to provide sufficient instructional space, we need to undertake significant expansion throughout our District. In addition to providing additional classrooms, we need to prepare our students for the new, more stringent New York State educational standards. Our plan accomplishes this by renovating existing space and providing for such specialized school programs as science and technology, special education classes, art and music instruction, and interscholastic athletics. Finally, we must provide our students with a safe and stimulating learning environment by updating and repairing our aging facilities. Our District's long-term educational and facility needs are addressed in this efficient, cost-effective and comprehensive Bond Referendum that will be presented to voters on February 3, 2000.
A: The decision to add space to our facilities was the result of a long and meticulous process of study and discussion that began in December 1997. At that time, the Board of Education formed the Long Range Planning Committee (LRPC), composed of Board trustees, community members and administrators, to study enrollment trends and present a range of options for Board and community consideration. After many meetings and much research, the LRPC presented its work in January 1999. The LRPC was then expanded to 28, including additional non-parent community members, teachers, parents, students and administrators. Its new charge was to advise the Board of Education in the process of narrowing options and designing a proposed Master Plan. The LRPC and the Buildings and Grounds Committee met throughout the spring and summer of 1999 with the District's architectural and construction manager consultants to study ideas, review necessary renovation and repair projects and provide feedback to the Board. A series of public meetings was held by the Board of Education to solicit community input on the proposed Master Plan. These meetings were widely advertised by the Board and were attended by several hundred community residents. After considerable public discussion, the Board held a series of public work sessions to finalize the Master Plan proposal.
