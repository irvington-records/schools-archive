While our community takes great pride in the new facilities on the high school/middle school campus, the cold winter weather has made it all too apparent that there are problems with the heating system which must be addressed.
Aware that several classrooms and public areas of the building are very cold, or uncomfortably warm, we have been working daily to identify and correct the problems with the HVAC (Heating, Ventilation and Air Conditioning) system, and to get the system operating as it was designed to. While we have continued our attempts to resolve these problems with the contractor responsible for this aspect of the building project, several hundred thousand dollars in payments is being withheld pending a satisfactory resolution of the outstanding issues in the finish work - the final phase of the HVAC installation.
In an effort to finish the job properly we have put together a new team that includes the architect, a mechanical engineer, a "controls" sub-contractor, the construction manager and District employees. We will be adding a new mechanical contractor, electrician and general contractor as necessary to finish the work. Funds withheld from the original contract will be redirected to get the HVAC system working as designed. To date, we have evaluated what problems exist with equipment, controls, and software and are developing a plan to systematically address each one. The entire HVAC system will be reevaluated once all work has been completed.

While the HVAC problems are obviously serious, this has been the only major "glitch" in the completion of the district's master plan for facility construction and rehabilitation. As this process began over five years ago, and certain changes have occurred, this is a good time to review the scope and progress of the master plan projects. The funding for the Master Plan, estimated at $57 million in total cost, is provided through a $47 million bond approved by the community in 2000, a $3.8 million bond also voted by the community in 2003 and an appropriation in the General Fund budget, which as planned began in 2000 and will continue until 2007, which is annually transferred to the Capital Fund. This combination of bonds and capital transfers was designed to take advantage of historically low interest rates in borrowing, minimize the year-to-year tax impact on district residents, and allow for some flexibility in the sequencing of the final aspects of the master plan.
All of the new construction, including the Middle School, Cafeteria/Music/Science wing, campus Library and art studios, and the new theater/gymnasium building are complete and, other than the HVAC problems, fully functioning. The existing high school building has been extensively renovated, and technology infrastructure integrated into the facilities. The original schedule was, however, revised in order to take advantage of some cost saving opportunities. For example, it made sense to bid all the Master Plan windows (High School replacement windows, windows associated with the new construction, Dows Lane windows and windows at the Main Street School) at one time. The Construction Manager believes that the District saved about $600,000-$700,000 by "bundling" the window bid in this manner. The Dows Lane and Main Street window project, originally scheduled for later in the project, was therefore moved up to this past summer.
The original schedule was also adjusted to save money on the High School renovation project. The original plan was to phase in the renovation of this facility over two years. Rather then incur contractor start up and mobilization costs of doing this work as two separate projects in two separate years, we decided that it made good sense to bid the work as a single project. Here, too, the district realized significant savings - over $100,000 as a result of this change.
A final driving force behind the adjustment of the original schedule was the fire at Dows Lane in 2000. The District was able to gain some advantage from this very unfortunate incident by combining District funds with the insurance payments to rebuild the north end of the Dows Lane facility, renovate the main office, add a third floor over the office area, put in a new kitchen and install an elevator.
These decisions benefit the district in many ways; notably, the new windows are considerably more energy efficient, and we thus realize savings in our heat and electricity bills. We anticipate completing the remaining Master Plan projects by the end of the 2006/2007 school year, as was originally planned. However, as certain projects were moved forward in the master plan, others had to be moved back in the schedule. Most of these are in the site work area: completion of high school drive and refurbishment of the playing fields. Nevertheless, during the five years since the inception of the master plan, it has become evident that the intense use and resulting further deterioration of the district's field space requires a more extensive rehabilitation than originally anticipated. We are presently exploring the possibility of putting down artificial turf on one or more fields, and adding lighting. Important factors in this discussion are the vastly improved usability and durability of turf fields and the greatly expanded field use time which lighting would provide. This discussion will continue at the February 15 Board of Education meeting; as always, community members are encouraged to attend the meeting, or to communicate your suggestions to us via email at board@irvingtonschools.org.
