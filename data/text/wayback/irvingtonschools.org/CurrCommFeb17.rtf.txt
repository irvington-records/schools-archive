Review of Feedback from First Meeting:

The meeting opened with introductions of the group.  Jennifer Dolan-Waldman, Chair of the group, then shared with the group the responses to the questions which had been developed at the October meeting.  The questions and their responses had been drawn from faculty from all 4 buildings in the district, as well as parent representatives.  The responses in order of importance according to the respondents were:

In what areas do you think this district does well?
Hire and maintain enthusiastic and supportive staff
Prepare students for higher education
Have parents and a community who are very supportive of the program as well as the students (through the PTSA and IEF, for example).
 
What do you think we could better?
Improve K-12 articulation between grades and schools
Provide more staff development
Support teacher collaboration

What are your concerns about beginning this process? (of a district-wide curriculum review committee)
How will the teachers find time to do this?
Will there be teacher training to support this effort?
How will all teachers be given a voice in the process?

What would you like to see included in all curriculum documents?
A checklist of things to be covered at each grade
A curriculum map and timeline
Teacher training and professional development plan
A list of provisions for students who don’t meet assessment standards

What’s appropriate feedback for this overarching committee to give to the content area committees? 
Questions about the K-12 progression of instruction
Questions about how the curriculum prepares students for NY State assessments
Questions about the how assessment is part of the curriculum
Questions about the correct balance between content areas and programs
Questions about how the curriculum supports different learning styles

What feedback should we give the general public about district progress?
Updates at school Board meetings
Newsletters with a synopsis of committee activities
Website reports of curriculum committee activities and accomplishments

Jennifer then discussed the presentation which she made to the Board in January when she outlined the various professional development activities that had taken place between last summer and the first semester.  A full presentation on the year’s activities will be presented at the May meeting of this group.  Professional activities have been linked to the 5-year curriculum cycle which was accepted by the Board at the beginning of the school year.  Additionally, training sessions to help teachers prepare for new or ongoing State assessments have been strongly supported.  

A revision to the curriculum cycle was then discussed.  Physical Education and Athletics had been scheduled for review in 2003-04.  However, because of the rapid growth of the numbers of students in the District, and because of the building project, the Athletics program will be reviewed sooner.  

Presentations by Content Area Committees
Several curriculum areas are currently at implementation or review stages.  A member of each of those committees made a brief presentation on the status of their work. 
 
ATHLETICS – The Athletic Director, Artie McCormack, discussed the process he will follow as he begins the needs assessment and review of the Athletic programs.  He is currently assembling a group that will consider a wide variety of topics related to modified, JV and varsity sports.  Members of the committee will be selected from the student athletes, coaches, school administrators, Village recreation and parents.  Artie expects to have the first meeting before the end of March.

COMPUTER TECHNOLOGY – This area is at the implementation stage.  The District’s technology plan was developed over two years ago and many of the recommendations are in place or phasing in.
           Scott Mosenthal,  High School Principal, talked about the Technology Committee which is chaired by Mrinalini Rajwar, Director of Technology.  Other members of the Committee include teachers and administrators from each building.  The group brings concerns from each building to the district group, and helps to determine plans for staff training and targets for professional growth.  One goal of the group this year is to set specific student outcome expectations and benchmarks. 
        Scott Andrasko, 5th grade teacher, discussed the in-house multi-session course which had been developed in response to teacher requests and needs, and has been offered in after-school sessions since the beginning of this semester.  He distributed the description of the course offerings which include basic application software such as Word and Excel, but also offer other software applications for use with students in different content areas.  
        There was also discussion about the impact of technology on the daily lives of teachers and how increased access to email and other technology might impact the way communication is carried out.  This topic will be considered at each of the schools during the next few months as the staff works to determine appropriate used of technology in the communication process. 

ENGLISH LANGUAGE ARTS –  Initial comments about the work of this Committee were made by Jennifer Dolan-Waldman, Assistant Superintendent for Instruction.  This Committee began its work in early November, and is comprised of one representative of each grade, Kindergarten through Grade 12, as well as other staff including reading and special education teachers.  In addition, the Program Chairs for Grades 6-12 also attend some of the meetings.  
Kathy Forde, English Language Arts Program Chair, described the initial efforts of the Committee members to review the State core curriculum, and to determine how instruction in Irvington aligns with the expectations of the State.  The State curriculum is very detailed and comprehensive in the number of skills included, but those skills are assigned to a group of grades, such as Grade 2-4.  The teachers have reviewed the curriculum and have had discussions within grade level teams or departments to determine where specific content should be introduced, and where it should be expected to be accomplished by the students.  In addition, she said the group is working on developing a philosophy statement about Language Arts instruction, which will guide the efforts of the group and their ultimate recommendations. 
Other members of the Committee, several of whom were participants in this meeting, shared their experiences working with colleagues.  The group is in the initial stages, but appears to be making good headway in emphasizing the need to review this vitally important content area from a full district-wide view.  Recommendations from this group are expected in December 2001 in order to be included in the budget plans for 2003-03.  There is likelihood that some of the work of this committee will be continued through work during the summer of 2001 so that those recommendations can be developed during the Fall in preparation for the December presentation. 

MUSIC – The Chair of this Committee is Lola Garcia, Principal of Dows Lane 4-5.  This group has recently had their first meeting at which all of the music teachers in the district came together to review the State standards in music.  The initial work of this group was to review the elementary, intermediate and commencement level standards outlined, and to compare those expectations with the current program.  The committee is currently collecting and reviewing samples of other districts music curricula.  These documents will be reviewed at future meetings which will take place through the school year, and possibly into the summer.

PUPIL PERSONNEL SERVICES – This group has been working for over a year on a review of special services provided to students.  
                        Susan Levey, Director of Pupil Personnel Services, has been leading a group of district staff through a series of visitations to other schools in the area to review their Language Arts programs for special education students.  Schools that have been selected for visitations are those whose students have scored particularly well in State testing.  The focus of the visitations has been in the English Language Arts area. 

TECHNOLOGY -  This content area is addressed largely through the Middle School program.  Therefore, Lauren Allan, Principal of the Middle School, is serving as Chair for this review. New York State will be testing students in this area in May/June of this year.  In order to assure that our students are properly prepared the entire Middle School staff has reviewed the limited sample questions that have been distributed, and determined in which subject area classes the content can be addressed.  There was some discussion among the participants in this meeting about the value of technology instruction in a wide range of areas, and the availability of appropriate space to continue that instruction when the new  Middle School is built.  
                        Penny Delany, Board Member, shared information about the plans for the new buildings, which include a science building with 12 new labs, one of which will possibly be available for use in this area for Middle School students.  
                        Rena Speisman, Parent, emphasized a concern about providing life skills for students and encouraged the committee to keep the unique needs of middle school students in mind.

After the committee reports, the group had a discussion about the impact of the New York State testing program on the amount of time available for instruction.  While acknowledging the pressures and impact of the State tests, Jennifer emphasized that while we must prepare students for those assessments, teachers are also working on other ways to gauge student performance.  Curriculum that is developed within the district should reflect the concerns stated in the feedback developed since the first meeting, the state core curricula for the content, and Irvington standards and expectations.
