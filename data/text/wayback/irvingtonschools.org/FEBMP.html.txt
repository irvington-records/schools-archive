Master Plan Update: February 1, 1999
This Thursday, Feb. 3, our community will vote on a proposed $47 million bond referendum. This is certain to be one of the most important decisions on education that we will make. The Board of Education wishes to ensure that district residents have the main points of the proposal at hand.
STUDENT ENROLLMENT
The increasing number of students in the Irvington School District is the single major factor leading to the proposed bond referendum.
- Current enrollment of 1,708 will grow to nearly 2,300 by 2007/08.
- Last June, 86 students graduated from Irvington High School; this September, 173 children entered Kindergarten, pushing Dows Lane enrollment to over 950.
- Irvington’s high school population will grow 90% within the next 7 years. Middle school enrollment will grow by 62% in the next 7 years. (National statistics show high school enrollment growth to be only 13% in the same period). OUR NUMBERS FAR SURPASS NATIONAL, STATE, AND LOCAL ENROLLMENT PROJECTIONS.
FINANCIAL PLAN
The bond would authorize the district to borrow a TOTAL OF $47 MILLION.
- State aid of 18% and BOCES participation will directly reduce taxes.
- Reduction of debt payments and of the annual certs line will further mitigate the financial impact.
- The majority of homeowners would pay approximately $281 a year.
- Energy efficient renovations will actually reduce maintenance costs.
- Sharing certain facilities will result in additional efficiencies.
- Regardless of where students are housed, increasing enrollment will require additional staff.
BENEFITS
This bond offers a comprehensive, cost-effective and creative solution to our growing student enrollment and building needs.
- The district will gain a new middle school; a gymnasium/theater available to the community; upgraded library, research, technology, music, art, and science lab facilities; and refurbished playing fields.
- Much needed renovations to our aging high school (replacement of 35 year old roof, windows, heating and electrical systems), will receive the 18% state aid, as will new construction.
- The High School will be made accessible to the physically disabled with ramps, an elevator, appropriate bathrooms and wider doors.
- Our investment in education will benefit our present and future students, and our community as a whole.
Questions about the bond may be directed to Dr. Vincent Beni, Superintendent of Schools, or Paul Mandel, President of the Board of Education.
