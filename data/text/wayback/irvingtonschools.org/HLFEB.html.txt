HEADLINES FEBRUARY, 1998
The budget process: Teacher staffing needs for next year was the focus of discussion at the Feb. 3 Board of Education meeting. Mr. Hajek listed six factors which affect staffing levels. These include increasing enrollments (demographic analysis predicts a 6% increase next year), the number of sections taught by each teacher (defined contractually), new programs and scheduling formats, the impact of the new State standards, and mandated limits on class size in Special Education.
For Dows Lane, Mr. Hajek anticipates budgeting for additional sections in grades 2, 4 and 5 next year, and a reduction of one section in third grade, for an overall addition of two classroom teachers. This plan would maintain class sizes at a maximum of 22 per section for grades 1 through 5. Kindergarten would be maintained at 6 sections with 24/25 students in each. Part time teachers would be added in Art, Music and Physical Education to ensure that all children receive one period of Art and Music and two periods of gym each week. A part time string program is also planned to enhance the music program.
Additional part time positions in Music, Art and Foreign Language may also be required in the Middle School. Increased enrollment may result in additional English and Social Studies sections for 7th grade. At the High School, staff increases are also proposed for Social Studies and Foreign Language. One new position, a half-time science teacher, is projected. An increase in the number of Special Ed. students entering High School next September mandates an additional teacher, which will result in one Special Ed teacher at each grade level.
Each year, school principals present requests for program and curriculum supports. These fall into two categories: Level I includes routine requests for replacement of worn-out texts and equipment. At the Feb. 24 Board meeting, the principals made their Level II requests, which are for field trips and other enrichment activities. This year, the relatively small number of proposals in theLevel II category included staff training and replacement of Middle School math texts which had became outdated.
Transportation: The Board resolved, on Feb. 24, to put our transportation contract out for bid. The main reason for this decision is that our current contract with Advance Transit is ten years old, and does not include some major provisions which are now standard. Don Marra, the Assistant Superintendent for Business and Finance, has prepared a thorough revision. The bid specifications would ensure the best service for the district, whichever company provides the service, including the district’s right to specify the types of buses we need, and the qualifications of bus monitors. We are also supporting a proposal for a new BOCES managed Quad Transportation Consortium, which would be based out of an office in Irvington High School. The proposed consortium would enable BOCES to be more directly and visibly involved in day to day transportation issues.
Capital projects: In accordance with our five year plan and current needs, Mr. Marra recommended that we consider at Dows Lane gymnasium floor repairs, a new canopy from the rear entrance to the modulars and a handicap access ramp; at the High School repair of the athletic track, and beginning the extensive work necessary to repair the courtyard, steps, retaining walls and sidewalks which have become badly deteriorated. The Board resolved to approve Mr.. Marra’s proposal that we move forward with this project by obtaining plans and specifications so that we may put it out to bid.
Other points: A new Internet Use policy has been approved, emphasizing responsible use and safety. The Board has interviewed several firms to conduct the Superintendent search, and will be announcing the appointment next meeting. Appointments to the IMS Principal Search Committee will also be on the next agenda.
