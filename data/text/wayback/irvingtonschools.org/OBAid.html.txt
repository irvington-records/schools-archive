Needless to say, we are living in difficult financial times. We all recognize that slowing economic growth and declining stock market values place a burden on individuals while creating large national and state government deficits. It is of critical importance that you, as taxpayers, understand the impact of these trends and other state actions on public education, the most critical service provided on the local level.
The NY State Constitution requires the state to fund public education and the Governor to submit a budget to the legislature for approval by April 1. School districts are required to submit their budgets for voter approval, this year on May 20 (Districts are the only municipal entities required to gain voter approval of their budgets). That's the theory. Here is the sad reality and how it directly impacts you and our children:
New York does not even approach its responsibility to fund public education. On average, the State pays only about 40% of public education costs across the state. In Irvington the situation is much worse: the State pays only 5-6% of our costs while paying as much as 80% to other districts. This is due to an archaic state funding formula that penalizes communities such as ours, and results in an unfair burden on our local taxpayer.
This year, the Governor has proposed a budget that cuts public education by over $1.2 billion (8.5%). The Governor continues to attack BOCES (Board of Cooperative Educational Services), which provide cost-effective services to local districts. However, the Governor proposes more funds for experimental charter schools. The result: a massive shift in the burden of funding public education from the state to local communities.
Irvington Schools, the fastest growing district south of Albany (51% or 658 students over the last 7 years), is only receiving $618,000 more aid than we did 7 years ago. This minimal aid increase does not cover increased costs (i.e., teachers and staff, costs of benefits, technology, utilities, and facilities construction) and results in a decrease in state aid per pupil of 16% to only $816. This is much lower than most districts in the area.
The April 1 State budget deadline is a fiction. For the past 18 years - and in all probability this year as well - the Legislature has not enacted a budget on time, adopting one budget as late as September. Since districts must prepare budgets by April and receive voter approval in May, we are forced to guess how much aid will be received or to cut programs that might otherwise have been funded. This year, the Governor's budget results in a projected loss of $237,000 in direct State aid to Irvington. In actuality, this cut could be much higher depending on other proposals (cuts to BOCES and building aid).
At the same time, the State and Federal governments are increasing regulations and bureaucratic paperwork, adding to the cost of education without measurable benefit. An increasing number of "un-funded mandates", requirements to provide services without additional aid, are another example of how the State and Federal governments shift the burden of education to local taxpayers.
The Irvington School District needs you to speak out against this untenable situation. We ask you to contact the Governor and our state representatives to protest the Governor's budget and to support our legislators in opposing his unfair proposals. We are attaching sample letters to send to the Governor, our representatives and legislative leaders. (It is extremely important that the leadership in the Senate and Assembly receive the message.) You are welcome to download these samples or to send your own. Letters and calls are much more effective than emails. You may wish to add some of your own views or personal experiences, e.g.:
- smaller class size has made a difference for my child
- teacher training is important to help our staff remain current
- additional funds for technology will insure that my child is prepared for the world outside of school
- improved facilities are important for creating an environment conducive to learning
Please take the time to make your voice heard: from past efforts we know that, together, our community can make a significant difference. Thank you in advance for your efforts! As always, the Board of Education welcomes your feedback: email us at board@irvingtonschools.org.
