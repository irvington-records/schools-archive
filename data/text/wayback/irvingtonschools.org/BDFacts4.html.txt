Q:  How much will this bond proposal cost?
A:  The bond referendum would authorize the District to borrow $47 million.  This amount includes approximately $34 million for new construction, $10 million for the extensive renovation of the High School, and $4 million for needed work at the other two schools.  
Two points need to be stressed.  First, the District could borrow no more than the $47 million approved by the voters.  Careful planning and management are critical, as there can be no "cost overruns" passed on to the community.  Second, the District's comprehensive financial plan includes a capital line in the annual budget which is essential to ensure appropriate facilities maintenance in the future.
Q:  How does the Board plan to minimize the impact of the proposal on taxes?
A:  The Master Plan Proposal is both innovative and cost-effective in providing for our current and anticipated educational needs.  As part of this proposal, the District has formulated a Financial Plan to minimize the impact of this project on our taxpayers.
    - By locating the Middle School next to the High School, the District will realize significant savings by sharing some facilities.  We will also be able to enhance our educational program by enabling Middle School students to have access to advanced High School courses.  Additionally, by locating the Middle School next to the High School, the District will realize ongoing cost-savings due to increased opportunities to share staff.
    - By passing the Bond Referendum by June 2000, the District will be able to maximize state building aid at the rate of 18%.  After June, the District will be eligible to receive only 10% aid.
    - By folding urgently needed repair and renovation projects into our total proposal, the District qualifies for the 18% state building aid on these projects as well.  (Repairs and renovations done through the regular operating budget generally do not qualify for this aid.)
    - The District will take full advantage of reduced debt obligations and plans to eliminate $500,000 certiorari line from the annual budget.
    - By maximizing BOCES participation in our project, the District will be able to construct facilities that will be needed in the future at no additional cost.  While BOCES is using these spaces, we will receive significant rental income.
    - We will be building only what we really need.
The estimated net impact of all of these factors on an average Irvington School District homeowner would be approximately $285 per year over the life of the bond.
