The following are the minutes of the Public Meeting of the Board of Education of the Irvington Union Free School District, held in the Campus Presentation Room, September 7, 2004, with the following present:

Mrs. Penny Delany, Mrs. Diane Mundree, Mr. Paul Mandel, Mrs. Janice Silverberg, Mrs. Carol Gutman.  Also present:  Dr. Kathleen Matusiak - Superintendent of Schools, Mr. James Reese - Assistant Superintendent for Business, Dr. Ralph Napolitano - Assistant Superintendent for Instruction, Dr. Scott Mosenthal – High School Principal, Mr. Scott Fried – Middle School Principal, Mr. Donald Kuhn – Main Street School Interim Principal, Mr. Joseph Rodriguez – Dows Lane Elementary Principal, members of the community. 

A reception for teachers new to the District this year was held prior to the start of the meeting.

I.  	CALL TO ORDER

The Public Meeting was called to order by Mrs. Penny Delany at 7:35 p.m., the pledge of allegiance was given.

The Agenda was changed as follows:

II	INTRODUCTION OF NEW FACULTY AND STAFF

Mrs. Delany described the new teachers as a group of very talented, experienced and enthusiastic additions to our staff and that we are happy to have them.  Dr. Matusiak asked each of the building principals to formally introduce their new staff to the Board and community.  

III	MINUTES OF MEETINGS

A motion was made by Mrs. Janice Silverberg, seconded by Mr. Paul Mandel, to approve the minutes of the Public Meeting of August 24, 2004.  
Vote:  5-0 Approved

IV	INFORMATION ITEMS  

	Dr. Matusiak reported on the following:
A very good opening day of school with few “glitches”.   Administrators met the buses to greet the students.
Compliments to the custodial staff for the preparation of the buildings and to the clerical staff for their support.  
Positive feedback from the staff relating to the Superintendent’s Conference Days.  
On-going dialogue and examination of the parking conditions at the Campus.

V	COMMENTS FROM RESIDENTS

A resident commented on the very early start at the High School.  The schedule change has also resulted in some students having to wait 40 minutes for the extra-curricular and sports activities to begin at the end of the day.  

 VI	PROGRAM AND POLICY ITEMS

  Superintendent’s Report - 
1. Opening of School Reports

Irvington High School:  Dr. Mosenthal reported a “great day”.  The morning drop-off and afternoon pick-up went fairly smoothly but did result in a back-up on the hill.  Administrators will monitor the conditions and also restate the times when students should be arriving at the Middle School and/or High School.  Discussions will encourage everyone, parents and students, to keep the drop-off and pick-up process as efficient as possible.  
 



Irvington Middle School:  Mr. Fried reported a “fantastic” day.  Arrival was fairly smooth with the buses and everyone found their way around.  1st period was extended and used for a brief 6th grade orientation and 7th and 8th grade “town meetings”.  Lunch is proceeding more smoothly with a minor change in procedure.    

Main Street School:  Mr. Kuhn reported an opening enrollment of 321 students, Grade 4-5. The 4th graders seemed to make an excellent adjustment to the Main Street School.  All students met in the Auditorium and then accompanied their teacher to the classroom.  AM and PM buses were fairly smooth with a few exceptions.  Window installation has been completed in all classrooms with stairwells and hallways being completed.  Everyone seemed to pick-up where they left off in June.

Dows Lane Elementary:  Mr. Rodriguez reported that the school year is off to a very smooth start at Dows Lane.  Buses were on-time in the morning and afternoon with one exception.  Mr. Rodriguez said that the two Superintendent’s Conference Days were very productive for teachers.  He also thanked the secretarial and custodial staff as well as the aides.  The Kindergarten started with an enrollment of 143. 

Mrs. Delany thanked the principals for their reports.

Substitute Teacher Orientation
A Substitute Orientation meeting is planned for next week.  The subs will be given a handbook with district information, protocols and procedures.  Substitutes are important to school districts to cover for teacher illness as well as other duties and mandated responsibilities that require the teacher to be out of the classroom. Support for substitutes guarantees the continuity of education.  Drs. Matusiak and Napolitano are looking forward to the meeting.
