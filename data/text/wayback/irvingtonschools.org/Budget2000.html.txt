Several factors have influenced the development of the Superintendent's recommended budget:
- Enrollment Increase
- New York State Standards
- Superintendent's Entry Plan Results
- Facilities Needs
- Technology Plan
- State Aid
The recommended budget reflects the impact of these issues.
Enrollment Increase - Projections indicate that we will have over 1800 students next year, an increase of 5.5%. Sixteen new teachers and support staff will be hired to insure that class sizes remains equal to 1999-2000 levels and so that we can enhance our support services and course offerings primarily in the Middle School and High School.
New York State Standards - The district will increase its focus on Academic Intervention Services and Staff and Curriculum Devlopment to insure that all of our students can meet the more difficult New York State Standards. The District will implement the recommendations of the Pupil Personnel Services study in order to increase its focus on meeting student support needs. Summer Academies for students who need extra support will be continued and expanded for identified students in grades 3-12. New high school courses will be offered (including 2 additional Advanced Placement courses), and the multi-sensory program will be expanded to include 1st through 3rd grades.
Technology - In the High School a network will be installed, each classroom will receive a workstation and a new computer mini-lab will be supplied. A mini-lab will be outfitted in Dows Lane, the new classroom in Dows Lane will receive a cluster of workstations and older computers at the Middle School will be replaced. Our emphasis on staff development will continue its focus on incorporating technology into our daily curriculum.
Superintendent's Entry Plan Results - An important District need identified by the Entry plan was for increased K-12 Curriculum Coordination. The 2000-2001 begins to address this need with the creation of the position of Assistant Superintendent for Instruction.
Facilities - The District will begin implementing its Master Plan by increasing the line in the budget for capital expenditures. Smaller capital projects will be initiated including: tile replacement, gym floor repair and window replacement at Dows Lane and drainage work and auditorium roof repair at the Middle School. Additional modulars will be placed at Dows Lane to accommodate increased enrollment at that school.
State Aid - Irvington continues to receive a disproportionately low share of aid from New York State.  In fact, we receive less than 7% of our budget from the State.  The average district receives 43% of its budget from State funds. In 2000-01 we may receive only $871 in state aid per pupil, much lower than neighboring districts.
The budget focuses on instructional needs and actually reduces the amount of money spent on non-instructional items. 91% of the budget increase is devoted to instructional items. Although the tax rate increases by 4.95%, any homeowner who applied for the STAR program will see a reduction in taxes. For example, an owner of a home assessed at $18,000 will see a school tax reduction of $221.
The Board will continue to discuss the budget at its meeting on April 25 and will adopt a budget on that date which is the last date for budget changes. A hearing will be conducted on May 2, with the public budget vote and Board election on May 16.
