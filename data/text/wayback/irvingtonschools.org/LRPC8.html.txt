Recommendations
The Long Range Planning Committee unanimously believes that our District faces a serious and ongoing space problem as the result of rapid student population growth. As a result of this study and report, the LRPC believes that the Board of Education and the District must immediately embark on a process to add to our educational facilities. In order to accomplish this goal, the LRPC recommends that the following next steps be pursued by the Board of Education:
- Retain an architect to further develop District options.
- Expand the LRPC to include staff (administrators and teachers) and non-parents.
- Develop a process to inform the public while seeking community input.
- Examine funding options including state aid, BOCES aid and financing.
- Establish a timeline for the project.
- Further assess program needs given new standards/technology requirements.
- Maintain and update demographic data.
- Inform the Village Planning Board regarding the potential for the construction of significant additional classroom space on High School hill.
- Develop short term solutions to our District’s challenges as a part of the budget formulation process.
The recommendations of the LRPC represent an excellent opportunity for the Irvington School District. Several factors make the present the most propitious of times to begin the process of addressing our long term space and facility needs:
- The District now has a clear and definitive demographic report, which underscores the serious nature of our space needs. Two factors stand out as clarion calls to action: 1) that Dows Lane is one of the largest elementary schools in southern Westchester, and 2) that total District enrollment will exceed the all-time high of 1844 students either by the year 2001 or 2002.
- Long term interest rates, and therefore borrowing costs, are near an all-time low.
- New York State is currently providing districts with an additional 10% in aid to undertake school construction projects (therefore, Irvington would receive 18% reimbursement rather than 8% towards any construction/renovation project).
- The financial condition of the Irvington School District is the best it has been in many years.
- The District has proactively addressed normal maintenance concerns in recent years. However, several larger maintenance items will need to be addressed soon. This type of major facilities update could be accomplished in the context of a construction project and may therefore garner state aid which is unavailable for normal maintenance projects.
- Changing educational needs, especially with regard to technology and special education, can now be addressed properly in the context of a major construction project.
It is the belief of the LRPC that by embarking on this course, the Irvington School District will be able to respond to our space requirements for years to come while maintaining and enhancing the educational excellence of the Irvington Schools, which our community so values
