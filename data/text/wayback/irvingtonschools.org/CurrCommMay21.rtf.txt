The meeting began with a brief commentary from Dr. Louise Feroe, a Committee member from Mercy College.  As a follow-up to our February discussion on the impact of standards and assessments on students at the high school level, Dr. Feroe presented another view of the standards movement.  In general, she said, colleges have welcomed some assurance that students who enter their programs will have the skills and knowledge that the State tests attempt to measure.  The various State tests which students complete while in the local school setting, she indicated, provide some reassurance to the colleges that students are adequately prepared for the college level work they will encounter.  


PROFESSIONAL DEVELOPMENT ACTIVITIES
The discussion then moved on to a review of the professional development activities which have taken place during the 2000-01 school year.  Jennifer shared some information with the group about the number of days and the types of activities in which staff have participated.  Of the 414 days used this year (for a staff of 155), 101 days were for High School staff; 74 for Middle School; 96 for Dows Lane K-4; 99 for Dows Lane 4-5; and 44 for staff shared throughout the district.  While some of the sessions attended were on State assessments and training requirements, many more were focused on the areas of our curriculum focus and review.  Many sessions were through local service providers, while other sessions were held in-house with the use of a consultant to work with our staff.  
After attending a session, staff members are asked to submit a feedback form to provide information to be shared with colleagues as well as their supervisor.  This information has been and will be useful in determining which activities are deemed most useful by those who attend them.  Some of the sessions included: Training for Advanced Placement courses in European History, Calculus, and Economics; Balanced Literacy in Kindergarten; Team Training for Technology Support; Using Document Based Questions in Elementary and Middle School Social Studies Classrooms, and Content Area Reading & Writing.   There were also frequent sessions provided for grade level teachers through in-house sessions working hands on with consultants in the area of reading and in science.  In addition, several groups met through the year to review curriculum and prepare for summer curriculum writing sessions.


CURRICULUM REVIEW COMMITTEES
After hearing about the professional development activities, the curriculum committees currently under review gave brief updates on their activities: 

COMPUTER TECHNOLOGY – Mrinalini Rajwar, Director of Technology, said the Technology Committee has been working on the implementation of the technology plan which was adopted a few years ago.  The plan has been the guide for the technology program which has been progressing significantly, and will next year provide an in-house facilitator/trainer to expand the efforts to support teachers as they integrate technology into the classroom.  The Committee has submitted a proposal for summer work during which time they will develop the standards and expectations for student use of technology at each grade level.  
 
ENGLISH LANGUAGE ARTS – Jennifer Dolan-Waldman, Chair of the ELA Committee, gave a brief overview of the work accomplished; Kathy Forde (for Grades 6-12) and Kathy Burke (for Grades K-5) discussed the projects they will lead this summer to get the curriculum into written form.  The purpose of this document will be for teacher use. Lauren Allan discussed the plans for a Middle School group to develop a writing rubric which can be used in all content areas to help students learn common expectations for written work.  
There was also discussion from the whole group about how they would like to receive the information from the ELA group for our fall meeting.  We agreed that we will divide up the review process in advance, getting the full document to all committee members, but asking different groups to focus on different areas so that a full review of the written material can be accomplished, and feedback can be provided in a useful and constructive manner.

MUSIC – Lola Garcia, Chair of the Music Committee, discussed the process for review of curriculum and program that has taken place this year, and the plans for the group to visit other schools next year to learn more about other programs.  This information should provide useful guidance as the music curriculum is written next year. 

PUPIL PERSONNEL SERVICES – Susan Levey, Director of Pupil Personnel Services, described the study group process she has led which has focused on the performance of our special education students which has not met our expectations.  In an effort to explore other programs, several visits to similar, high achieving schools were made during the course of the year.  Erika Trautman, one of the teachers involved in the study, shared the results of the visits and some recommendations from the study.   

MIDDLE SCHOOL TECHNOLOGY - Lauren Allan, Middle School Principal, discussed the new State exam in this area, and the limited amount of information which has been available.  Our students will participate in the first test in this content area this year.  The results of this test are designed only to help guide the program, and will not be used for student evaluation.    


SUMMER CURRICULUM REQUESTS
In order to use the summertime to plan for next year and to do work that is difficult to complete during the school year, staff members were asked to submit proposals for summer curriculum writing projects.  Members of this Committee were asked to serve on a sub-committee to review the proposals and determine which projects to fund.  In response to the request for proposals, 17 proposals were received, for a total request of 225 days of summer work.  The sub-committee will meet to review these proposals on May 24, and will notify applicants of their response. Given the large number of days requested, the sub-committee will have to determine which proposals align best with the areas under review within the curriculum cycle, and which projects will best support district needs.  Because the English Language Arts area is the largest area under review this year, proposals in that area will be given priority, although other projects will also be supported if they meet an identified need.


S.A.V.E.  LEGISLATION
Another area that has taken a significant amount of time this year is the S.A.V.E. (Safe Schools Against Violence in Education Act) legislation, which requires every district to develop several different plans to safeguard the students and staff.  Included in the requirements of plans to be developed are:  a district and a building safety plan; a code of conduct for students at all grade levels; training for school staff in violence prevention; training for students in character education; and plans for fingerprinting all new employees.  All of these requirements have involved many staff members in developing the plans, and will require additional time for staff training in subsequent years.
