A group of Irvington High School officials and a student traveled to Albany last month to the "2002 Education Technology Conference" to give a presentation on the district's use of the new "Smart Board" high-tech teaching setup which the district is piloting in the school's social studies classes.
The group met with members of the New York State Assembly, who sponsored the conference, to demonstrate how teachers are integrating technology into the teaching of history. Irvington social studies teachers Dr. Erik Weiselberg and Amy Devlin, accompanied by Irvington H.S. student Seth Feinstein, showed how the "Smart Board" allows teachers to display power-point presentations, images, movies and web sites. The setup includes a laptop computer, interactive white board, projector, color laser printer, DVD-VCR player and sound system.
The "Smart Board" can interact with the displayed material via an electronic pen/pointer and capture any notes written on the board to an electronic file. Any files saved can be uploaded to a web set and later accessed by students.
The "Smart Board's" sound system can present audio and video resources and amplify the teachers' voice for students with hearing impairments.
"This gives teachers a new and exciting tool with which to expand what is going on in the classroom," said Irvington's Director of Technology Mrinalini Rajwar, who also attended the conference. "Gone are the days when a teacher relied on textbooks and printed handouts to supplement the curriculum.
At the conference the group had the opportunity to meet and share ideas with other educators, along with Assemblywoman Naomi Matusow, chair of the Libraries and Education Technology Committee and Assemblyman Richard Brodsky, chair of the Corporations, Authorities and Commissions Committee.
