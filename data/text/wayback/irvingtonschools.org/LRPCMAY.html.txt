Long Range Planning - Report to the Community
May, 1999
The district is now entering Phase Two of the process of planning for greatly increasing numbers of students in our schools.
The long range planning process has hinged on careful assessment of the demographic data prepared for the district. Clearly, there is an ongoing influx of students into all our schools. This increase is primarily driven by single family home re-sales; in a 30 month period, almost 300 additional school age children moved into the district. While some of the demographic report deals with projected increases due to new housing construction and general population trends, it cannot be too strongly emphasized that our district must plan for the education of children who are already here. Next year, for the first time there will be eight sections of one grade level at Dows Lane. With almost 900 students, Dows Lane is already the largest suburban elementary school in southern Westchester.
The Long Range Planning Committee’s report, presented in January to the Board and over 250 residents, outlined six options for dealing with the space and facilities expansion necessary to accommodate the anticipated enrollment. These were explored at Board of Education meetings, including a special meeting on April 20 which was also attended by more than 250 members of the community. In addition, Superintendent Dr. Vin Beni held a series of meetings with various community groups.
On May 4 Board conducted a work session dedicated to the further evaluation of the district’s options. As a result of this process, the Board eliminated options that were inconsistent with our purpose of defining a solution which would meet the needs of the entire district for the next five to ten years. Thus, various plans to simply move grades around within our existing facilities were discarded. The Board also struck options that did not maintain academic excellence in our schools. Larger class sizes throughout the district, elimination of programs, and split sessions were among the options cut on this basis.
This process of evaluating a range of possible solutions led to the conclusion that the only viable option will be to significantly increase our classroom space by adding to our existing facilities. This solution responds to State guidelines for larger classrooms, and the space requirements for science labs and technology instruction as well as to our increasing enrollment.
The Board has engaged an architectural firm, Peter Gisolfi Associates, to assist the district in developing a Master Plan. Mr. Gisolfi’s charge is to present to the Board and the community all possibilities for construction of new facilities on our existing sites, as well as all possible renovation/reconfiguration of existing facilities to glean additional classroom space. In order to ensure that we have the necessary expertise to assess the costs and benefits of each of these possibilities, the Board will also retain the services of a construction manager and a financial planner experienced in school construction projects.
Mr. Gisolfi, the Board and the Administration will be working closely with an expanded
Long Range Planning Committee. The committee will now include the Assistant Superintendent for Business and Finance, the principals of each school, members of the teaching and support staff, representatives of the Site Committees and the PTSA, students from the Middle and High Schools, non-parent community members as well as two Board of Education members, Dr. Beni, and parents who comprised the original committee. This committee will represent the interests and ideas of the community during the course of developing an educationally and financially sound Master Plan which will serve the district’s present and future needs.
As always, members of the Board of Education, and our administrators welcome your comments and questions. Our goal is to ensure that the planning process continues to be inclusive, thorough and responsive to community concerns.
