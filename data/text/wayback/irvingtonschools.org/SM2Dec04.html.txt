School Matters, December 22, 2004
By Janice Silverberg
With a K-3 population of 600, Dows Lane School is a thriving facility. The program at Dows Lane includes English/Language Arts, Science, Social Studies, Specials and Support Services as well as Mathematics. At a recent Board of Education meeting, principal Joe Rodriguez and Assistant Principal Eileen McGuire reported on the Dows Lane program, and the successful initiation of the Trailblazers math curriculum. The Trailblazers program was developed at the University of Illinois-Chicago with National Science Foundation (NSF) funding, and meets the rigorous standards of the National Council of Teachers of Mathematics (NCTM). Trailblazers conforms to New York State standards for the teaching and learning of math, is grounded in real life problem solving and combines high expectations for student performance with an equitable focus on the active involvement of students with diverse learning styles.
Of particular value is connection of Trailblazers to Science and Language Arts. Using the scientific method as a basis for analysis and problem solving, the program encourages authentic scientific practice as the students identify variables and patterns, organize and analyze data. Links to Language Arts include an increased stress on word problems and discussion, and the use of literature to portray mathematics in the real world.
The Trailblazers program balances whole class, small group and individual instruction with an overall emphasis on conceptual development through both "paper and pencil" and hands-on activities. Continuous assessment, both formal and informal, guides instruction and keeps students, parents and educators informed of individual and group progress. Staff development is critical to the success of Trailblazers. There was significant preliminary training and preparation; now the faculty continues to work with a consultant on the implementation of the program and devotes monthly faculty and grade level meetings to ongoing planning.
Enrichment activities are provided for first through fifth grade to ensure that all students have the opportunity to develop their maximum potential. In first grade, enrichment comprises whole class instruction, and focuses on extension activities and the reinforcement of new and unfamiliar teaching techniques. Parallel teaching at varying levels of difficulty, small group as well as whole class enrichment instruction offer second and third graders the opportunity to explore math topics in depth and to use construction projects to transform the abstract to the concrete. In fourth grade, a math enrichment class, aligned with the NYS standards, focuses on finding multiple solutions to a single problem by sharing strategies and writing responses. The fifth grade enrichment consists of a twice-weekly recess time math club, which explores the ways in which math can be found in art and constructs mathematically based art projects.
The Instructional Support Team serves our youngest learners by bringing together parents, administrators, classroom and special education teachers, psychologist, social worker, reading specialists, speech pathologists, occupational and physical therapists to develop instructional strategies for students who are having difficulties that affect their ability to learn successfully. The outcomes of a team assessment are shared with everyone involved with the particular student, may include referrals for special services or academic intervention services (AIS) supports, or the development of a behavior management plan, and provide for monitoring and follow up.
It is clear that Dows Lane is providing a solid foundation for the academic excellence our district values and supports.
