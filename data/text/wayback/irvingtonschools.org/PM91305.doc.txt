The Public Meeting was called to order by Mr. Paul Mandel at 7:37 p.m. and the Pledge of Allegiance was said.  

II	INFORMATION ITEMS  

Proposed NYSSBA By-Law Amendments 
The Board reviewed Article 2 – Schedule of Membership Dues as well as Items 1-6.  Mr. Paul Mandel stated that the increase in dues is not unreasonable. Currently dues for Irvington School District are $6,400 and the proposal is as follows: SY 2005-06: $6,700; SY 2006-07: $7,100. He also stated that NYSBBA is “critical resource to school districts” and he is proud that Irvington School District is a member.

A motion was made by Mrs. Carol Gutman and seconded by Mrs. Diane Mundree, to adopt By-Law Amendment A and Items 1-6 of the NYSSBA 
Vote: 5-0 Approved.

Vote for Trustee on SW BOCES Board 
Members of the Board of Education were not familiar with the two candidates running for the vacancy on the SW BOCES Board, therefore, the vote was tabled.

C.   Superintendent’s Report:  
Dr. Matusiak reported that the feedback she received on opening day was positive. No bus problems were reported and she thanked the Administration for their planning and efforts.
The two students from New Orleans have arrived and are living with their grandmother.

 
III         COMMENTS FROM RESIDENTS

Deb Weintraub announced that the PTSA and the District will be adopting  Slidell, a town in Louisiana, devastated by Hurricane Katrina.  The PTSA will facilitate fundraising events with each school and help coordinate donations of needed items.  More information will follow.
A resident requested that the Board of Education revisit building a pool on District property.
Residents from Fieldpoint expressed concerns regarding the District’s plan for adding tennis courts and artificial turfed fields on the east side of the Middle School/High School property which shares a property line with some Fieldpoint homes.  Drainage, lighting, proximity to property lines and visibility from their property were among their chief concerns.  These concerns were addressed by Mr. Ron Tetelman of Eberlin & Eberlin, consultants to the District during the formal presentation of the Report to the Irvington Board of Education by the Ad Hoc Committee for the Enhancement of Irvington Schools Athletic Fields.
 
IV	PROGRAM AND POLICY ITEMS

A.   Opening of School Reports

Dows Lane Elementary: Mr. Rodriguez reported that it was the “smoothest” opening that he recalls. 568 students are enrolled with 99 staff members. Average class size is 20-21 with 22 being the highest in a 1st grade class.   Among the new and continuing programs at Dows Lane is the “No Put Down” program, the Literacy Profile, and the Trailblazers program in Math.  The 3rd graders will be starting their own newspaper with parent volunteers helping out. IEF is funding the Junior Achievement program for 1st and 2nd graders. Dows Lane is celebrating its 50th birthday this year and various events will be scheduled throughout the year and every child will participate in some way. 

Main Street School: Mr. Kuhn reported that it was also a very smooth opening day.  There are 156 4th graders and 158 5th graders, with an average class size of 20.  There are 55 staff members, 10 of whom are new. The Smart Boards projectors have been mounted and all the wiring for computers completed. Among new initiatives will be the Odessay of the Mind, a program for creative problem solving strategies, and the Jason Project where 5th graders participate in group lessons utilizing 15 mobile laptop computers. Both programs are funded through an IEF grant. 

Irvington Middle School:  Mr. Scott Fried reported a “very smooth” opening day.  There are a total of 469 students enrolled and 60 staff members. The schedule for welcoming 6th graders included an extension to 1st period allowing teachers more time to review schedules, lockers, etc. The early dismissal of 7th and 8th grade allowed the 6th graders to work cooperatively in small groups which helped reduce the “overwhelmingness” of their 1st day.

Irvington High School:  Dr. Scott Mosenthal welcomed 597 students and 99 BOCES students.  With the Middle School now on the same campus, the 8th graders are familiar with the campus, making for a smoother transition to high school.  Due to high interest, many of the AP courses are approaching the maximum of 30 students. Currently, only one class per course is offered, but Dr. Mosenthal expects as enrollment grows, multiples of certain AP courses will need to be offered, providing some flexibility in scheduling classes.

Mr. Mandel thanked the Principals for their reports.

V        PROPOSAL FOR ARTIFICIAL TURFED FIELDS AND OTHER ENHANCEMENTS

Presentation of the Report to the Irvington Board of Education by the Ad Hoc Committee for the Enhancement of the Irvington Schools Athletic Fields-Supplemental Log – Appendix A

Mr. Paul Mandel, spokesperson for the Ad Hoc Committee, thanked the 27-member Committee for a “wonderful effort” in preparing the report.  The Committee met four times during the summer and 5 members made site-visits to schools that have enhanced fields. 

Last Spring, the Board of Education charged the Committee to investigate the District’s options for enhancement of varsity athletic fields and to make recommendations to Board of Education in September, 2005.

The presentation included information on the current athletic programs offered by the District as well as data on student participation, assessment of the needs, options, recommendations and benefits. The presentation provided background information on the Master Plan of 2000 and the 2003 Bond Referendum. Cost estimates, funding through a bond referendum, as well as alternative funding sources were also presented.

Mr. Mandel noted that the District enjoys a cooperative relationship with the Village of Irvington and utilizes Village facilities such as Scenic Hudson, Memorial Field and tennis courts at Pennybridge and Fieldpoint.  The Irvington Recreation uses District fields for their AYSO and Pop Warner teams.  All facilities, both District and Village, are stretched to their maximum.

Mr. Ron Tetelman, President of Eberlin & Eberlin, consultants to the District, presented the schematic drawings for the development of the East Field and proposed tennis courts. The plans provide for water retention, drainage and landscaping, thereby minimizing the impact to the District’s neighbors at Fieldpoint.  Mr. Tetelman reiterated that the schematics were still in the development phase and that as questions or concerns arise, they will be addressed and changes made accordingly.

Cost estimates for the proposal are $5,675,250. The impact on the tax rate is projected at 1.53% or $6.90 increase per 1,000 assessed value. The Board of Education is committed to identifying alternative sources of funding for the proposed project. Fundraising will be ongoing, before and after a bond referendum, thereby decreasing taxpayers’ contribution to the project. 

There was discussion following the presentations regarding the funds that were earmarked for field enhancements through the 2000 and 2003 Bond Referendums. It was explained that during construction or renovation projects, priorities shift, items are added, deleted, pushed forward or postponed based on on-going assessment of needs. The 2000 funding focused on the rapid construction of academic facilities and renovations. The 2003 Bond referendum was in response to an emergency situation concerning the roof at the Main Street School and some items that were scheduled for a later date, were bundled together so the District could take advantage of economy of scale. Also, the $696,600 earmarked for East Field renovations in 2006-07, in the 2003 bond, is still available.
