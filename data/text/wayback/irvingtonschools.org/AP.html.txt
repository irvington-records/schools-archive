52 IHS Students Named AP Scholar
Fifty-two students at Irvington High School have earned the designation of AP Scholar by the College Board in recognition of their exceptional achievement on the college-level Advanced Placement Program® (AP®) Exams.
The College Board's Advanced Placement Program offers students the opportunity to take challenging college-level courses while still in high school, and to receive college credit, advanced placement, or both for successful performance on the AP Exams. About 17 percent of the more than one million high school students in almost 15,000 secondary schools worldwide who took AP Exams performed at a sufficiently high level to merit the recognition of AP Scholar.
Students took AP Exams in May 2004 after completing challenging college-level courses at their high schools. The College Board recognizes several levels of achievement based on student's performance on AP Exams.
At Irvington High School, two students qualified for the National AP Scholar Award by earning an average grade of 4 or higher on a 5-point scale on all AP Exams taken, and grades of 4 or higher on eight or more of these exams. These students are Bernard Bluhm and Christine Wang.
Fourteen students qualified for the AP Scholar with Distinction Award by earning an average grade of at least 3.5 on all AP Exams taken, and grades of 3 or higher on five or more of these exams. These students are Anees Benferhat, Bernard Bluhm, Miriam Farber, Seth Feinstein, Samuel Galgano, Frederica Lipmann, Richard Liu, Christina Milo, Eugene Petracca, Aurelien Thompson, Diaphel Thompson, Christine Wang, Alex Weisberg, and Jason Woods.
Ten students qualified for the AP Scholar with Honor Award by earning an average grade of at least 3.25 on all AP Exams taken, and grades of 3 or higher on four or more of these exams. These students are Ariana Boffey, Lara Boffey, Caitlin Bucci, Christina Ciraco, Erica Cooper, Joseph Kamien, David Latov, Jenna Lazar, Tristan Naumann, and Hayley Skousen.
Twenty-eight students qualified for the AP Scholar Award by completing three or more AP Examinations, with grades of 3 or higher. The AP Scholars are Dinah Alobeid, Nouf Bazaz, Romit Bhattacharya, Bjorn Briese, Adam Brodsky, Clara Cooper-Mullin, Sruthi Devarinti, Joseph Florio, Robert Forster, Dana Gordon, Sidney Goudie, Kuna Hamad, Claire Holland, Julie Iodice, Carla Kalvin, Kaitlyn Litcofsky, Christopher Macies, Amanda McCarthy, Daniel McElligott, Martin Pepeljugoski, Gregory Roth, James Ryan, Eric Saar, Jesse Samberg, Madeline Shepherd, Margaret Silverberg, Jordan Suchow, and John Tavolacci.
Most of the nation's colleges and universities award credit, advanced placement, or both based on successful performance on the AP Exams. More than 1,400 institutions award a full year's credit (sophomore standing) to students presenting a sufficient number of qualifying grades. Thirty-four AP Exams are offered in a wide variety of subject areas, each consisting of multiple-choice and free-response (essay or problem-solving) questions (except for the Studio Art exam which evaluates students' original artwork).
The College Board's mission is to connect students to college success and opportunity. We are a not-for-profit membership organization committed to excellence and equity in education. Founded in 1900, the association is composed of more than 4,500 schools, colleges, universities, and other education organizations. Each year, the College Board serves over three million students and their parents, 23,000 high schools, and 3,500 colleges through major programs and services in college admissions, guidance, assessment, financial aid, enrollment, and teaching and learning. Among its best-known programs are the SAT®, the PSAT/NMSQT®, and the Advanced Placement Program® (AP®). The College Board is committed to the principles of excellence and equity, and that commitment is embodied in all of its programs, services, activities, and concerns.
