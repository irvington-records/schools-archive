Back to School 1999
**Reprinted here with the kind permission of the Rivertowns Enterprise, first published in the August 27, 1999 issue as a contribution to the "Back to School" Issue.
As the end of summer rapidly approaches, the faculty and staff of the Irvington School District are preparing for the arrival of more than 1680 students, a 5% increase from last year’s student enrollment. That enrollment is the largest in the school district since 1976. We are rapidly approaching and will soon surpass our all time high enrollment of 1844 students which occurred in 1970.
To assist in identifying the solution to the space needs for this expanded enrollment, the Board of Education has established a 28 member Long-Range Planning Committee comprised of board members, administrators, teachers, parents, students and residents. The Committee is reviewing a variety of options, using the Board’s Long-Range Planning Committee vision statement as its guide. That statement provides that our solution must:
- Meet our needs for the next 5 to 10 years
- Provide a response which addresses our needs from a district-wide prospective
- Take into consideration our changing educational environment
- Provide our students with a safe and healthy learning environment
- View the maintenance and enhancement of the Irvington School District’s educational excellence as our most important concern
The district has been working with its architects, construction manager, and traffic consultant to assess the current status of all district properties to determine their capacity to be expanded. Beginning in September, the Board will conduct several meetings and work sessions to discuss these options and to get significant input prior to making a decision to be presented for community consideration. It is clear, however, that significant additional space will be needed to address the enrollment needs and to help prepare students to face the challenging new State standards. It is also clear that the high school building, built in 1965, needs some improvements.
Many new faces will be here to greet the students when they arrive. The Board has appointed 29 teachers to fill vacancies throughout the district since last spring. These vacancies were the result of the retirement of several veteran teachers, the enrollment increase, the resignation of some teachers, and the demand for new programs and services due to the new State standards. The new teachers have undertaken a significant orientation program this summer, including training in technology to insure their comfort with and knowledge of our technology plan’s goals.
We were also fortunate to hire Mrinalini Rajwar as the new Director of Technology. She will spearhead the implementation of year 2 of the district’s Technology Plan. The technology program will have two major focal areas this year. The first is a significantly expanded staff development program for all teachers and staff. Many of our teachers were involved in a variety of staff development opportunities over the summer. The district is committed to the ongoing development of its staff.
The installation of a network and computer lab at the high school will be the second priority area.
Elementary classrooms now have multiple stations. The Middle School has a 24-station computer lab, a 14-student mini-lab and a computer in every class. Teachers and students use the labs to conduct on-line research of primary and secondary sources, utilize WebQuests, create reports and presentations, and manipulate mathematical and scientific data on spreadsheets. We continue to emphasize the integration of technology into all areas of the curriculum.
New program opportunities will be available for students at each school. The district has supplemented the reading program at Dows Lane and the Middle School with the hiring of additional staff at both schools. New special education staff have also been hired to continue with our goal of providing services to students with special needs in their own schools, rather than sending them to special programs out of district. The Middle School support staff has also been supplemented with the addition of a part-time psychologist and part-time student assistance counselor.
At the High School, there are significantly expanded opportunities for students. A new environmental science course as well as a Graphic Design course will be offered in addition to new Advanced Placement courses in European Studies and Art. The High School staff will also be focusing on intervention services to assist students who will need support to meet the new State graduation requirements.
Both Middle and High Schools will have new starting times: 8:35 a.m. for Middle School and 8:15 a.m. for the High School.
An exciting addition to our extra-curricular program will be a Friday night (September 17) football game. This should be a terrific community event.
We are committed to ongoing communication with all of our community. In addition to our regular mailings to all residents, we will be making use of our website to provide you with valuable updates on a variety of school issues. Visit us at
www.irvingtonschools.org. Sign up there to receive regular e-mail with announcements and important information.
We also encourage you to call us and to attend board meetings to provide us with your views on the important issues we face this year.
Dr. Vincent T. Beni
Superintendent
