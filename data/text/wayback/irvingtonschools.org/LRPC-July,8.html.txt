Long Range Planning Committee
Meeting No. 2: Minutes
July 8, 1999
- Don Marra opened the meeting and gave an overview of the agenda:
- Traffic Study Report
- SEQR Process
- Building Assessment Report
- Site Analysis Report
- Review of Options
- Agenda for next meeting
- Mr. Marra explained the Traffic Study Report which was prepared by Frederick Clark Associates under the supervision of Michael Galante:
- The purpose of the study was to understand traffic access issues and evaluate vehicular circulation;
- Traffic volumes were analyzed at Dows Lane, the Middle School and the High School by performing traffic counts and analyzing the data to determine existing conditions. These counts were then correlated with the projected population increases at all three schools.
- Mr. Marra explained the rating system that is used to classify signalized and unsignalized intersection approach capacities:
- Level of service for signalized intersections is defined on a scale from A to F, based on the average stopped delay per vehicle during a 15 minute interval of a peak one-hour period, with A being less than 5 seconds and F being more than 60 seconds.
- Level of service for STOP-controlled intersections is also defined on a scale of A to F with A being less than 5 seconds and F being more than 45 seconds.
- The study concluded that:
- Dows Lane cannot accommodate additional traffic;
- The Middle School cannot accommodate additional traffic;
- The High School can accommodate additional traffic, but manned traffic control or signalization is desirable.
- Sandra Mintzes from Peter Gisolfi Associates outlined the procedures that are required by the State Education Department for filing State Environmental Quality Review (SEQR) information. She explained the differences in process if and when draft and/or final environmental impact studies are required.
- J. Michael Orifici from JMOA Engineering, P.C. reported on the progress to date for surveying the buildings and sites to identify potential capital improvement projects that might be included with the current project or in a 5-year capital plan. He explained that it is beneficial to group projects together for cost efficiency and to maximize building aid. Building aid to Irvington is currently 8%. The State was giving 10% additional aid last year and might be extending that subsidy, which would make Irvington eligible for 18% for capital projects. Aid is not available for repair projects. However, projects which are included with renovations are aidable.
- Bob Reiter from Peter Gisolfi Associates talked about the work that is underway for analyzing the school sites. He presented a drawing which showed the schools in the larger context of the entire district with neighboring communities. It was noted that Irvington differs distinctly from its immediate neighbors, Dobbs Ferry and Hastings, in size, character, and amount of land which has the potential for development. In addition, he presented an analysis drawing for the High School site which diagrammatically showed buildings, open space, recreation space, and green edges. An additional drawing analyzed the topography of the site and indicated where there are buildable areas and areas which are too steep for development.
- Dr. Vincent Beni reviewed a matrix of options which were based upon the space requirements presented to the LRPC at the previous meeting.
- Two of the options shown had been eliminated because they included building two new schools and do not include using the existing Middle School.
- A third option of a K-4, 5-6, 7-12 configuration was discussed by the committee and considered the least favorable.
- The options still under consideration include:
- K-3, 4-5 at Main Street, 6-8/9-12 at the High School
- K-3, 4-5 at Main Street, 6-8 at a new site, 9-12
- A new option was introduced: K-3, 4-6, 7-8 at Main Street, 9-12. A new 4-6 school could be located on the east field of the High School site or at a new site. A 4-6 would be smaller than a 6-8 middle school and would require less space for play fields.
- The LRPC members expressed interest in the 4-6 school option.
- Dr. Beni also reported that several potential new sites are being researched for potential acquisition.
The next LRPC meeting is July 29, 1999 at 8:00 a.m. at the High School.
