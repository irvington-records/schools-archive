CURRICULUM WORK

Jennifer opened the meeting with a review of the curriculum work that has been done in the last four years.  In 2000, there were no written K-12 documents.  The various committees which have been at work since then have done a significant amount of needs assessment, writing and review, bringing the curriculum cycle around to the point where English Language Arts, the first area undertaken, will be reviewed again in 2005.  As a result of the work already completed, the next round of reviews will be significantly different in that there will be existing documents to work from as well as some years of experience in implementing the work.  The second review round will, therefore, focus more on evidence of student success with the curriculum, and will address modifications necessary.  

There was then a review of the status of projects currently in the writing phase.  Reports were made by the chair of each department involved.

World Language – The final report is in typing, ready to go to print shortly.  There are a few logistical questions to be answered.    
Business – Also in typing.  There is a draft that has been reviewed and has been returned for final copy.  It should be “good to go” in September.
Science – Just about done, ready to minor editing.   The introduction and mission statement have also been written.
Social Studies - This area just began their work this year, and they have focused on incorporating the “Understanding by Design” work under the leadership of Raina Kor.  This reflects the implementation of training all staff in this approach to curriculum design, and based on the success of the work this year, will be continued in future needs assessments.  The committee’s work so far has focused on identifying enduring understandings that students should have as a result of their social studies courses.  Next year’s work will be to do the actual curriculum writing.    



Math – The work has been finished for some time, but we have needed to make edits based on changes in the Math A course as described by New York State.  The print document should get to the printer by September.
Music – Finishing touches are in the works involving editing and recommendations.
Art – By June, there will be a complete draft. 
Health and Physical Education – The document is being typed with refinements still being added.  Both documents will be completed by the end of this school year.  Implementation will begin in September 2004.



TRI-STATES 

The Visitation Committee will be here for 3 days, October 20-22.   The Committee will interview teachers, parents and others.  There will be a survey sent out shortly before the visit to get feedback prior to the visitation and to help guide the questions.  Kerry Moscato reported on the work that has been done so far, and the emphasis on collecting evidence of student work and performance.  Kerry asked that any teachers on the committee focus on collecting additional work samples now because the full cycle of the year is complete and the evidence over time should be more available now. 
With Kerry’s departure, Eileen McGuire and Scott Mosenthal will take on leadership of this effort.  Eileen and Scott will meet during the summer to review the materials submitted and lay the groundwork for the visit while working with the new Assistant Superintendent.	



RECOMMENDATIONS FOR THE CURRICULUM COMMITTEE 04-05

The group divided into smaller subgroups to address the question of how this committee might be restructured to serve the needs of the district better.  There were many suggestions about revisions to the existing structure, including those below.  This information will be shared with the new Assistant Superintendent and implemented, where possible, when the committee reconvenes. 

To function more efficiently:
Time during school works better. 
Committee reports should be distributed prior to the committee meeting so that the committee can then discuss the content of the report.
Consider sub-committee meetings targeted at a specific discussion so that not everyone attends every meeting, but additional people will be included based on the topic,
their level of concern, and
their information on the topic.
Include student work to illustrate what’s been done specifically with students – emphasize how curriculum translates into student learning - more sharing, more evidence.
Prioritize issues on the committee according to the most important first.
Have some meetings in the morning, some in the afternoon.
Set up an agenda for the year and presenters from different buildings to develop a better understanding of content areas across buildings and between curricular areas.



To focus on group dynamics:
Role and responsibilities – define what each role is expected to do and how they can contribute to the work of the committee.
Have more active curriculum committee in which we have active, heated discussions and debate issues.
Improve communication among Board members, committees, parents and teachers.
Allow time for global discussion related to curriculum issues such as new items and ideas.  
Increase discussion, reduce reporting.  
Consider establishing a message board or newsletter to convey information to the committee members.  
Break into smaller groups, have specific jobs for groups.  If there is a new course, for example, a smaller group could review that proposal.   Students should have a role in the creation of new courses through their contact with department chairs who will represent their interest and comments.    

To improve communication:
Define expectations for the committee members after a meeting.
Discuss how to communicate with parents.  The group should be the first place to discuss a topic, then move out to the broader community for discussion.

To enhance coordination of efforts:
Allow for more parent input re programs that have been supported by PTSA and after-school programs, e.g.
Consider grants that were not funded and those that what, what people’s dreams are for future programming.
