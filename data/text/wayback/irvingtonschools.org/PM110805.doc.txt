                      1.  Middle School Report and Action Plan
Mr. Scott Fried and Ms. Raina Kor presented an overview of the Middle School program. Mr. Fried reviewed the enrollment figures and stated that enrollment is flattening out. With regard to state assessments, Mr. Fried stated that the school has done very well overall, with scores in the mid to high 80’s. 6th grade ELA and math scores show students are moving into the standard base 3 and 4 level but that the focus now needs to be on moving more students from level 3 to 4, thereby focusing on providing more challenges to the higher-level achieving students.  Biology, French and Spanish assessments show that all students across the board meet the highest level. Ms. Raina Kor spoke about developing the “whole child” and the importance of preparing a student academically for high school, but also of understanding the developmental, physical and mental changes taking place. 

 In the 7th grade, English, social studies and science continue to be teamed, enabling the teachers to build close relationships with a core group of students as well as allowing the team of teachers to discuss the child in depth, not only with regard to curriculum but also teaching strategies found to be successful for a particular child. 

In 8th grade, a double block of math is offered every other day. Staff development is incorporated into the program affording teachers the opportunity to design and deliver differentiated instruction to students.  The model helps to extend children from where they are to where they can possibly be. Social Studies, Science and ELA instruction continue to be delivered in a team model. The Middle School has added a Drama Club, Student Council and other initiatives that enhance the middle school students’ experiences.

Mr. Fried stated that the Middle School benefits by the funding provided the PTSA and IEF. The funding provides opportunities that might not be available within the confines of their budget, thereby enriching the learning environment. 

Mr. Fried reviewed the special education programs at the Middle School. The new multi-grade Theraputic Support Program focuses on academics and behavioral concerns with opportunities for mainstreaming.  
In the Collaborative Education class, a special education teacher works with general education teachers to provide differentiated instruction to students.  

As part of the districtwide Three-Year Strategic Plan, the five goals of the Middle School’s Action Plan were reviewed. Cognos, a data warehousing tool, provides information which shows specifically where students need to work and where teachers and administration need look to address what is necessary to bring students to the next level. The Action Plan addresses the goals to shift students achieving the standards, to students who can achieve much higher.

Other initiatives that are part of the Action Plan include problem solving/social decision making programs to increase the opportunities for leadership and proactive strategies to improve good character development amongst students. Edline, a web-based portal, is now operational and  provides communication between teachers, students and parents. Teachers can post assignments, projects, grades and attendance. Edline also serves as an informational resource tool for parents. 

     Discussion of Scope of Artificial Turfed Field Proposal and Adoption of SEQRA Review-Lead Agency Resolution

Mr. Paul Mandel explained that the purpose of the discussion is to prepare for the submission of the Environmental Assessment Form for the SEQRA review. Mr. James Reese summarized the process and noted that the resolution that the Board would be voting on tonight is to permit the construction architects to circulate the EAF. After the 30-day period, the District can then declare itself as Lead Agency for the project. The final scope and costs of the project will be defined for the bond resolution and is not part of this resolution.  

 Mr. Paul Mandel introduced Mr. Ron Tetelman, President of Eberlin & Eberlin, consultants to the District, who reviewed the revised schematics of the turfed field proposal and other enhancements to the fields, the proposed tennis courts as well as lighting of the fields. Revisions include repositioning East Field to push it back from Field Point’s property line, additional landscaping and screening, and positioning the tennis courts closer to proposed parking area, thereby reducing the impact to the residents of Field Point.  

Dr. Matusiak stated that the District’s legal council advises that the Board consider adopting a broader, rather than a narrower, scope of the project for SEQRA Review. This does not bind the District to its individual components but rather allows for flexibility when the Board determines the final scope of the project and its associated costs. 
Schematic drawings of the proposal are not a part of this review process and the District would be able to tweak the placement of the fields, tennis courts and parking area prior to finalizing the project’s scope.

Mr. Mandel stated that by artificially turfing and lighting Meszaros Field, renovating and turfing East Field, constructing tennis courts and a parking area, the campus complex would be complete and offer the facilities necessary for its physical education and athletic programs. Mr. Mandel also stated that he feels that the lighting of East Field, at this time, might be too costly to be a part this project and might further delay finalizing the proposal due to possible legal action against the District by some residents of Field Point.

Mrs. Diane Mundree stated that at this time the District does not have the proper facilities to provide for its physical education and athletics program and that in order to educate the whole child, it is the District’s responsibility to offer students opportunities for success both academically as well as athletically.  She acknowledged that some residents are concerned about budget constraints and how it might impact the funding of academic programs in the future. 

Mrs. Janice Silverberg stated the athletic programs are very important to the students and to the community, but that education is the core mission of the District. As trustees, it is their obligation to ensure that nothing will jeopardize academic programs and to balance the needs of students with the concerns of the community. 

Mrs. Robyn Kerner stated that once the Board decides on the scope of the project and its costs, the Fundraising Committee will than seek possible corporate and private contributions as well as other creative means to fundraise money to offset the costs to the community.

Residents expressed concerns regarding the lighting of East Field and its environmental impact, the costs associated with such a project and drainage issues. Some residents questioned whether the District would realize the maximum benefits of East Field should the Board decide not to install lighting at East Field. Ron Tetelman noted that electrical conduits could be installed at the time that the field is renovated and turfed for possible future lighting.

After discussing the concerns expressed by Board members and members of the community, the Board decided to submit the full scope of the proposed projects for the SEQRA review.
 
A motion was made by Mrs. Robyn Kerner, seconded by Mrs. Carol Gutman to adopt the following Lead Agency Resolution.

“WHEREAS, the Board of Education of the Irvington Union Free School District (the “District”) is planning to undertake a proposed Field Restoration Project (the “Project”) which will include:  enhancement of the District’s varsity fields by replacing the current natural grass with artificial turf on the high school football field (Meszaros Field), on the baseball field (East Field), and on Dow’s Lane upper field, drainage for the fields, installation of field lighting, four (4) tennis courts on the High School campus, parking, and reconstruction of the 400 meter running track and the surfaces for field events.

WHEREAS, the District is the Agency principally responsible for undertaking the Project, and it has had a Long Environmental Assessment Form (EAF) prepared for the proposed action, and has consulted with its design and environmental consultant, Eberlin & Eberlin, with respect to the environmental impacts of the proposed action,  

WHEREAS, all of the activities of the proposed action with the exception of the construction of four (4) tennis courts fall within the perameters of a Type II action as described in 6 NYCRR 617.5(c), and 

WHEREAS, the construction of four (4) new tennis courts on the High School campus does not meet or exceed the thresholds of a Type I action, and is not contained on the Type II list,

NOW THEREFORE, IT IS HEREBY RESOLVED, by the Board of Education of the District as follows:

		1. The District designates the action as an Unlisted Action.

2.  The District, as the involved agency principally responsible for undertaking the Project, authorizes its   design and environmental consultant, Eberlin and Eberlin, to circulate the EAF and consent for lead agency status to all appropriate agencies.”
Vote: 5-0 Approved.
