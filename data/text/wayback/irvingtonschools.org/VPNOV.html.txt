Long Range Planning
**Reprinted here with the kind permission of the Irvington Viewpoint, first published in the December 1999 issue as a "School Matters" column, by Janice Silverberg.
This month's Board of Education meetings and work sessions have resulted in the development of a comprehensive proposed master plan for the district. The proposal includes extensive renovations to our three existing buildings as well as construction of a new 6 - 8 middle school at the High School campus.
The proposed Middle School would occupy a separate building opposite the present High School classroom wing, with two floors of clustered classrooms. Both the middle and high school programs would have dedicated science labs in a new fourth wing. The present L-shaped building would thus become a rectangle with a large interior green quadrangle space. The current auditorium would be reconfigured as a two-story state of the art library and research area. A new music area would be located in the science wing, which would also house a cafeteria for both schools. A separate field house would include a two-story, 800-seat auditorium as well as a full size gym, practice and weight rooms to supplement the space of the present gym. The district's architect and construction manager have created an alternate plan, which may include a pool; this plan is being studied closely.
The construction costs for the new middle school wing, science/cafeteria wing and field house (125,000 sq. ft.) would be $33,150,000. An additional $2,225,000 in sitework would be necessary, and would include renovation of the East Field as well as provision of additional parking between the new field house and the existing practice field. The existing high school requires a new roof, windows and exterior masonry repair. Reconfiguration and renovation of the interior (classrooms, hallways, offices, common areas) is necessary to accommodate student population growth, program needs, access for the disabled, and State regulations. These improvements bring the total cost for this campus to $45,324,500.
Renovations to Dows Lane would include sitework (including refurbishment of lower Dows field and the addition of tennis courts); new windows, floors and lighting in the old wing; and upgrading the mechanical, and electrical systems. These improvements total $2,162,550. The largest portion of the $1,479,500 proposed for the Main St. school would entail replacement of the retaining wall, drainage control, and new windows.
The total project cost of the proposed plan is $48,966,550. A comprehensive financial plan has been developed, in consultation with bond and financial counsel, and with our independent auditor. If the district were to assume a $47 million bond, at 5.5% interest, over a 27-year period, the actual new costs to the great majority of district homeowners would fall between $234 and $312 based on assessed values. These numbers take into account several mitigating factors that will reduce the impact on the taxpayer. Chief among these is state building aid of 18% (conditional on a district vote by June 30). BOCES construction and rental payments, elimination of the district's Certiorari line, and prior debt reduction would also significantly decrease the annual impact of the bond. These figures do not factor in tax relief to homeowners of the STAR program, which will further reduce the actual annual tax impact. The Board of Education intends to adopt the proposal in December, with a community referendum tentatively scheduled for early February.
We thank the many members of the community who have offered their input, expertise and advice on this master plan. This proposal best accomplishes all of our long range planning goals, and will ensure the best educational programs and facilities for Irvington's children.
