School Matters, January 19, 2005
By Superintendent Dr. Kathleen Matusiak
An organization comprised of high achieving schools in the New York, New Jersey and Connecticut area sent a team of eighteen "critical friends" on October 20, 2004 to review the Irvington English Language Arts (ELA) program. These teachers and administrators spent three days poring over various types of information (student work, policy documents, test results, etc.) which had been gathered for over a year by a committee of very dedicated K-12 Irvington staff members. This committee was led by administrators, Dr. Scott Mosenthal and Ms. Eileen McGuire.
In addition to reviewing massive amounts of students' work aligned to fifteen different indicators, the team interviewed parents, students, teachers and administrators and visited classrooms throughout the district. Each student at the High School and Middle School was surveyed, as were the ELA teachers in the district. A parent survey was sent home to each household in the district. The team centered its investigation on three questions:
- To what degree is the K-12 English Language Arts curriculum articulated and aligned?
- To what degree does our analysis of student performance from multiple sources effect our curriculum and instruction?
- To what extent do our schools balance high standards of academic achievement with the educational needs of diverse learners?
A general overview of the finished report, which contains a number of commendations as well as recommendations for the district to consider in its upcoming Strategic Planning process, was presented at a special Board of Education meeting on November 22, 2004. Overall the committee was impressed with the dedication, motivation and persistence of the staff. Visiting team leader, Dr. Michael Furentino, stated, "Irvington School District has created a warm, student-centered environment that provides a rich setting for students to learn."
On Thursday, December 16, 2004, the Irvington Tri-State Committee convened to identify priority recommendations in preparation for the district's Strategic Planning. Among the highlighted considerations were:
- mapping and articulation of the K-12 ELA curriculum data warehousing, analysis and articulation of student performance
- district-wide emphasis on differentiated instruction and tiered lessons
- development of portfolio assessments and literacy profiles
- ongoing program evaluation
- systematic student reflections of their work and progress
- evaluation and implementation of a systematized writing program
- systematic and sustained professional development
- increased staffing for ELL program
- identification and implementation of cooperative learning models
- implementation of research protocol 1-12
The Committee will develop a PowerPoint presentation with a salient overview for the staff at the Superintendent's Conference Day on Thursday, February 10th. At that time, Dr. Matusiak will explain the next steps for these and other recommendations that will emerge from her entry plan. Dr. Matusiak was happy to note that the Tri-State Report will provide a structured approach for ELA growth and improvement over the next three years. Community members can download the report by accessing the school district's web site: www.irvingtonschools.org under "News" or by clicking http://www.irvingtonschools.org/news.html.
