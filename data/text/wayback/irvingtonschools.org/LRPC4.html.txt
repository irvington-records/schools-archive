Review of Committee Activities
The LRPC began meeting on February 25, 1998. It concluded that its task should be broken into three steps:
- Data gathering
- Data analysis
- Development of Options
To initiate this process, the LRPC began gathering information, which it would need to analyze District space needs. First, the committee collected and reviewed previous space and facilities studies including the Space and Facilities Studies of January 1989, June 1990 and January 1991. The studies reviewed demographic projections and proposed various options for consideration of the Board of Education including changing grade configurations, purchasing modulars and construction. These studies resulted eventually in the design and construction of the Dows Lane extension, which was completed in 1996.
Throughout the time that the LRPC deliberated, the committee collected and analyzed information on how other districts in the Westchester/Putnam region were being impacted by student population growth and how they were dealing with the situation. Newspaper clippings from this compilation are available in Appendix A. Many districts in the nearby area have also been faced with increasing enrollments and have responded by contemplating or undertaking major construction/renovation projects. These districts include: Dobbs Ferry, Ardsley, Blind Brook, Bronxville, Byram Hills, Chappaqua, Croton-Harmon, Garrison, Harrison, Lakeland, Nanuet, New Rochelle, North Rockland, Pearl River, Pleasantville, Port Chester, Putnam Valley, Ramapo Central, Rye, Scarsdale, Valhalla, White Plains, and Yonkers. In all, 25 of 54 Westchester, Putnam and Rockland school districts are planning projects. ("School Construction is Booming", The Journal News, 12/8/98 pg. 1B). Irvington, having the reputation as an excellent school district, has attracted many younger families resulting in a dramatic increase in our student population.
On April 20, 1998, the LRPC visited Dows Lane and the Middle School, escorted by Principals Joseph Rodriguez and Andrew Kerfut, respectively. On May 11, 1998 the LRPC visited the High School, escorted by Principal and Superintendent Richard Hajek and Principal-designate Dr. Scott Mosenthal. The LRPC went through each building, literally from basement to attic, discussing with the respective Principals the usage of the various school spaces and the issues facing each school.
On May 11, 1998 the LRPC met with all of the building Principals in a round table discussion. This discussion reviewed the current and future challenges facing each school, as well as the Principals’ vision for dealing with the district-wide problem of our growing student population.
Throughout the process of reviewing the challenges of Irvington’s student population growth, the LRPC identified the quality of our demographic data as a key source of concern. At the beginning of the 1998-99 school year, it became clear that the district’s demographic projections had underestimated the extent of our student population growth. The actual student enrollment in September 1998 was 1604 as opposed to an average forecast in 1997 of 1522 and a high forecast of 1541. The LRPC decided to interview a highly recommended local demographer, Dr. Bruce Bothwell, to replace our previous demographer. Dr. Bothwell’s demographic projections use the generally accepted demographic methods including cohort survival ratio and tabulation of live births. In addition, Dr. Bothwell would contact Village planning boards and realtors. Dr. Bothwell’s analysis would also include reviews of housing turnover and building construction, which would be used to fine-tune his projections. As the result of these discussions, the LRPC recommended to the Board of Education that the District appoint Dr. Bruce Bothwell as its new demographer. This recommendation was subsequently approved by the Board of Education on October 6, 1998.
The LRPC also recommended to the Board of Education that the District retain the additional services of Dr. Bothwell’s Focus Consulting Associates, including Dr. George Port and Mr. Richmond Young, for the purpose of analyzing the District’s building capacities for each of our schools. This recommendation was approved by the Board of Education on October 20, 1998.
Throughout the fall of 1998, the LRPC also met with various architectural consultants in order to validate the potential of several options for possible renovation and/or school additions or construction.
