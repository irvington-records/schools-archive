School Matters, October 25, 2004
By Superintendent Dr. Kathleen Matusiak
The No Child Left Behind legislation has created a new phenomenon in education - Data-Driven Decision Making. As a result, a paradigm shift is sweeping many school districts across the country as they focus on data more than ever before. Educators have always collected information about different aspects of the educational program, but now they are using the data to make decisions. Test results and other student performance data are not just being reported. They are being analyzed on an ongoing basis to inform instructional practices.
Data has become an essential ingredient in the school improvement process. As Paul Houston, Ph.D., the Executive Director of the American Association School Administrators (AASA) states in "Using Data to Improve Schools: What's Working", the effective use of data can measure students' progress, evaluate programs and instructional effectiveness, guide curriculum development and resource allocation, promote accountability, and most importantly, ensure that every child learns."
In the Irvington Schools, we are just beginning to embark on this important initiative called Data Warehousing or Data-driven Decision Making. Systematically, we shall help staff, students and parents understand the concept and the rationale for embracing it, visit other districts where it is established and working well, provide initial training to key staff, and then develop a long range plan for getting all other members of our learning community on board. We shall begin to build what Houston calls "a culture of inquiry that values the data for sound decision making." The plan to introduce and train individuals in data warehousing will directly align with the district's Strategic Planning process that will begin in the second semester of the 2004-05 school year.
What Exactly is Data-Driven Decision Making? In Using Data to Improve Schools: What's Working, it is described as:
- Collecting data
- Analyzing data
- Reporting data
- Using data for school improvement
- Communicating through data
To ensure effective implementation, these key steps are critical:
- Develop a shared vision and decide what we want all our students to know and be able to do
- Decide what data might be helpful and where it can be obtained
- Access what data currently exists in the district
- Access how to get "perception data" as well
- Develop a systematic plan to provide training and ongoing support to staff, while continually building trust and providing encouragement and ongoing opportunities for practice and dialogue.
As the Irvington Schools move forward with data-driven decision making, this communication and several others will be used to keep you informed of our progress.
