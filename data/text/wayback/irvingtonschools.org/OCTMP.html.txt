Master Plan Update: October
Over 250 residents attended the Sept. 28 Board of Education meeting at which our consultants presented the proposed Master Plan for the Irvington School District.
What does the proposed Master Plan entail?
Substantial increase of classroom space; enhancement of facilities and educational programs for all three schools; expansion of athletic programs; potential for community use. Key proposals:
- K-3 at Dows Lane; Grades 4 and 5 at Main St.
- New Middle School (6,7,8) built on High School campus
- More science labs, music & art rooms, expanded library and cafeteria
- Field house for additional gym and theater
- Extensive renovation of existing High School
- Renovation and repair of Dows Lane and Main St. buildings
- Additional parking, rerouting and widening of HS Drive, traffic light
- Refurbishment of East Field, and lower Dows; tennis courts at Dows Lane
How much will this cost?
As the plan is not finalized, there is not yet a specific dollar figure. The general proposal for the new construction outlined above would cost approximately $34 million; the cost of the renovations at all three schools could be between $8 and $11 million. The final figure would depend on prioritization and providing for certain projects in the annual budget. Working with our Financial Advisor and Auditor, we have developed a possible scenario for financing a $40 million bond, as this will help us to understand the likely financial impact on our community. Several factors would mitigate the potential cost of financing: favorable bond ratings and interest rates, State building aid (18%), BOCES capital and rental contribution, and reduction of our CERT line. Assuming that these factors are all in place, we would anticipate an annual cost of approximately $200 per household.
How long will this take?
The district’s architect Peter Gisolfi, and construction manager, Michael Orifici of JMOA Associates, presented the timeline for bringing a specific proposal to the community for a vote, which we anticipate can occur at the earliest in January 2000. He then outlined the phases of the project: design, State Education Department approval (which alone will take at least six months), bidding (in accordance with WICKS Law requirements), and construction. While some infra- structure renovations could begin earlier, the new construction elements would realistically begin in the fall of 2001, with certain projects slated for 2003.
What happens in the meantime?
The district will maintain its commitment to academic excellence while ensuring a range of temporary solutions to the space needs of the students.
What’s the next step?
The verbal and written comments made at the Sept. 28 meeting, and the continuing communications to the Board and administration since, show a strongly favorable initial response. Public meetings throughout Oct. and Nov. will continue the process of answering the questions and concerns posed by the community, and eliciting ideas and input. We expect to adopt a plan by early December.
Please make your views known.
Please make an effort to contact Board of Education members and/or administration with comments about this proposal.
