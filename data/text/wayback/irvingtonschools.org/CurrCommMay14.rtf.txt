The Committee, composed of teachers, parents, administrators and Board members and chaired by Jennifer Dolan-Waldman, Assistant Superintendent for Curriculum, met for the third and final time for this year to review the progress of the many curriculum projects which are currently underway.  

T
he meeting began with a brief review of the work being done in the curriculum areas currently in the second year of their work, the Decision Making phase of the Curriculum Cycle:  The person leading that area of review is noted in italics.

English Language Arts - J. Dolan-Waldman
The written work on the curriculum document is complete and at the printer.  All staff members will be given a copy of the full document both for reference on the ELA content as well as for an example of how we are formatting curriculum work.   
On June 24th there will be a meeting to plan the implementation of the ELA work, focusing on professional development, as well as classroom practice and support. The meeting will be chaired by Lauren Allan and Jennifer and will be attended by the members of the ELA Committee as well as English teachers 6-12. 

Music - Nadine McDermott 
The work of the Music Department has been extended a year in order to accommodate the new staff in this area.  They have completed much of the work on General Music, and are continuing to work on writing the instrumental and vocal curriculum areas.  The rest of the work should be completed after the upcoming conference days when the department meets again.
  	
Family & Consumer Science  - Raina Kor
The NY State standards in this area are linked to those in Health and in Physical Education, areas that will begin their reviews next year.  Because the Family and Consumer Science content is addressed at the middle school level, Raina has been working with staff at that level who are involved in the areas directly related to Family and Consumer Science (FCS was formerly known as Home & Careers Skills) as well as Guidance, PE and Health teachers who address the standards in their area of instruction also.  The work of the FCS group has focused on linking all the content areas through the NY State standards, and in developing a curriculum that begins the linkage between FCS, Health and PE.  As a result of the work done this year, there will be stronger linkages among will these areas next year.  That linkage will continue to develop as the other areas enter the Needs Assessment and Decision Making phases.  
 

T
he Committee then heard reports from staff involved in areas at the end of the first year of their work, those in the Needs Assessment phase:

Library – Mrinalini Rajwar. 
The focus of this review has been to use the curriculum developed by Irvington librarians in conjunction with other librarians in the region, and to incorporate that work into district planning for the new libraries that will exist when the schools move into their new buildings in September 2003.
Through the work done both by the ELA and Library committees, it has become apparent that the approach to research needs to be more clearly delineated by grade so that students’ skills will develop in a logical and sequential order.  An outgrowth of this discussion is that the Social Studies department will work with the English Department to determine which skills students need for research, and determine at what point they will be taught.  This work will be begun on the Superintendent’s Conference Days this year, and will continue with summer work. 

World Languages – L. Boyce & L. Allan
Linda told the committee that the language teachers have been working throughout the year to develop a written curriculum, and will use additional conference days and summer work to complete the work for the 7-12 instructional program.  Additional work of this committee involves the review of the possibility of an elementary language component.  That topic is currently under discussion with a sub-committee and the recommendations in that area will be developed next year when this area moves into the Decision Making phase. 

Mathematics – L. Urban & J. Dolan-Waldman
Lisa reviewed the committee’s work which has  included a review of current district practice and  visitations to other school districts.  Jennifer distributed a copy of the beginnings of the math curriculum document, including a vision statement for math instruction in the Irvington School District, and a sample of the grade level curriculum format alignment with the seven NY State key concepts in mathematics.     
Both Lisa and Jennifer emphasized that no decisions have been made yet on any area, nor are they expected to be made until next year when this committee moves into the Decision Making phase of the review.  A comment from a Committee member focused on encouraging the math group to select materials at the elementary level that will be exciting and challenging for students at all ability levels.   

Art – Nadine McDermott & Raina Kor   
This group has met several times during the year, and will continue their work on the upcoming conference days.  


S
ummer Curriculum work was the next item on the agenda.  On April 19, there was a meeting of a subgroup of the Curriculum Committee to review teacher Requests for Proposals for summer work (RFPs).  One teacher from each project made a brief presentation to the subgroup so that they could learn more about each proposal. 
The subcommittee then determined how to allocate funds based on specific districtwide goals and how each project linked to district planning and needs.  By using the Superintendent’s Conference Days on June 24th and 25th, the subcommittee was able to provide time for more days than had been requested, because more staff will be available on the June days.  There will also be funding for 200 days of summer work above and beyond the time provided during the conference days.  
The subcommittee will meet again in September to review the work submitted, and to approve funding for all work which meets the goals agreed upon at the April meeting.   

 
T
he final activity for the meeting was a reflection on this committee’s work this year.  The group broke up into smaller groups for the purposes of discussion, responding to specific questions about how the committee had functioned this year, and how it might work better next year.  Specific comments and suggestions included:  

Provide more opportunity for small group discussion on the specific curriculum areas under review  
Ask presenters to provide print copies of their reports prior to the meeting so that committee members could read the material in advance 
Redefine the purpose of being on this committee 
Clarify the obligation of committee members in disseminating information to other members of the school community
Determine how this group act as “critical friends” to those working on curriculum projects
Review how information be shared with parents and students more directly and widely

These suggestions will be reviewed when this committee reconvenes for the 2002-03 school year.
