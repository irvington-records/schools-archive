Long Range Planning Committee
Meeting No. 3: Minutes
July 29, 1999
- Don Marra opened the meeting and Dr. Vincent Beni reviewed the LRPC mission statement which stresses that the Irvington School District must:
- Meet our District’s needs, for a projected 5-10 years.
- Provide a solution to our space problems from a district-wide perspective.
- Provide our students with a safe and healthy learning environment.
- Provide for growth in a cost-effective way.
- View the maintenance and enhancement of the Irvington School District’s educational excellence as our most important concern.
- Peter Gisolfi Associates (PGA) presented a summary of the preliminary list of building assessments. The list will ultimately have a cost attached to each project and a priority assigned. The school district will then decide on projects to be included in a bond and projects to be completed within the school budget as part of a 5-year plan.
- State building aid has been increased by 10% to a total of 18%. Projects included in the bond would have 18% of their cost covered by state aid.
- PGA reviewed the status of the options. The following options have been considered:
- Option A: K-3 (Dows Lane); 4-5 (Main Street); 6-8 (New); 9-12 (HS)
- Option B: K-3 (Dows Lane); 4-5 (Main Street); 6-8 & 9-12 (at HS)
- Option C: K-4 (Dows Lane); 5-6 (Main Street); 7-12 (at HS)
- Option D: K-2 (Dows Lane); 3-5 (New); 6-8 (New); 9-12 (HS)
- Option E: K-5 (Dows Lane); K-5 (New); 6-8 (New); 9-12 (HS)
- Option F: K-3 (Dows Lane); 4-6 (New); 7-8 (Main Street); 9-12 (HS)
- PGA presented two diagrams for Option ‘B’ and one for Option ‘F’.
- Dr. Vincent Beni reviewed the assumptions to date:
- Building sizes and space requirements for all the options are based on the high-end demographic projections.
- The current enrollment (at the end of July) meets the middle projections. By the time school starts, the enrollment number may move somewhere between the middle and high projections. The demographic projections will be updated after the beginning of school.
- There is an obligation to build space for BOCES.
- The demographic projections do not account for any new development on major parcels that may eventually be on the market.
- The possibility of building on the existing football field was discussed. The benefit of having the field at the HS site and the cost of building a new field elsewhere outweighed the opportunity for building on this area.
- The following issues were discussed by the Committee:
- Sufficient parking at the MS/HS campus is needed.
- There is efficiency in building shared spaces in Option ‘B’.
- There is efficiency in total staff requirements in Option ‘B’.
- The scheduling of shared spaces, cafeteria, and gym needs further study.
- The separation and independence of MS students from HS students and the benefits of advancing into HS courses needs to be thoroughly discussed.
- Additional playing fields might be necessary.
- The following building alternatives were discussed:
- building an enclosed competition swimming pool for school and community use;
- building a separate gym and auditorium to be shared by the community;
- building a new small theater and keeping the existing auditorium;
- renovating the existing auditorium into a new library;
- building the MS building in three clusters;
- building a cafeteria with a kitchen to serve as a satellite producing hot lunch for the three schools in the District;
- constructing new science rooms to be shared by both MS and HS; 6th grade might have a dedicated science room
- The construction schedule needs to be developed and the potential disruption of school time needs to be understood.
- The MS concept survives in Option ‘B’.
- The HS and MS buildings should have separate entrances.
- Additional land for a new MS or for new fields may be necessary.
- MS children would be in school at lunch time instead of being out on Main Street.
- The possibility of buying land from Westwood for parking was mentioned.
- The playing fields would be preserved with Option ‘B’.
- Building a separate new middle school means adding 40,000 sf of new construction more than the MS/HS combination of Option ‘B’. In addition to the increase in construction cost, a new site must be acquired and the site developed.
- Adding parking at the lower property in front of the HS towards Broadway should be considered.
- The cost of repairs to the HS, MS and DL must be added to the cost of construction.
- The Committee will continue to provide feedback on the options discussed at the next meeting.
- Next meeting will be on August 24th at 6:00 p.m. in the faculty room at the High School. The construction manager shall provide preliminary construction and renovation costs.
- At the next meeting, the following items shall be discussed: Pros and cons of options, land purchase, and building assessments.
- Don Marra will organize a visit to all three schools, scheduled to take place on one day.
Options ‘D’ and ‘E’ were previously eliminated. Option ‘C’ was also eliminated. Option ‘A’ depends on finding additional property. Option ‘F’ proposes a new intermediate (4-6) school at the east field of the high school campus. This option eliminates the east field. Option ‘B’ attaches a new separate Middle School to the High School with some shared facilities, and retains the east field.
