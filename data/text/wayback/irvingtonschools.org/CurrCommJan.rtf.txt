The Committee, composed of teachers, parents, administrators and Board members and chaired by Jennifer Dolan-Waldman, Assistant Superintendent for Curriculum, met for the second time this year to review the progress of the many curriculum projects which are currently underway.  The meeting began with introductions and a review of some current status of a number of different issues.

Implementation of the English Language Arts (ELA) curriculum:  At the October meeting, the Committee reviewed a draft of the K-12 ELA curriculum.   ELA was in the “Needs Assessment” phase of the curriculum review cycle last year.  At that time teachers from grades K-12 met regularly to develop the specifics of the curriculum which will be finalized during the second year of review when that area moves into the “Decision Making” phase of the cycle.  At this meeting the discussion focused on the implementation that had taken place so far based on the work teachers have done and the plans that have been made to revise the curriculum to focus more on a balanced literacy approach.  

Teacher goals:  Jennifer said that the district had set 3 goals for professional development for all staff which reflected the need to focus on enhancing students’ reading and writing skills, as well as on providing a range of opportunities for students at all performance level.  Teachers have developed specific goals for this year based on those recommendations.  Many teachers also now have the option of selecting specific professional activities to focus on, either alone or in groups, to further their professional development on topics of particular relevance and interest to them. Chuck Johnson described a project that he and several other science teachers at the Middle School have selected to continue on the work they began last summer.  In order to provide consistency in supervision of teachers, the administrative staff and the program chairs have also been participating in session on improving their supervisory skills.  

Building plans: Jim Reese, Assistant Superintendent for Business, spoke with the group about the status of the building project.  He indicated that the project was awaiting State approval for a long time but the approval finally was received and the project has been put out to bid.  Once the bids are awarded the work will begin and there will be a great deal of activity at the HS/ new MS site.  The anticipated start date for that is early March.

Budget for 2002-03:  The budget for the next school year is currently in development.  The recommendations of the various curriculum committees that began their work last year and made their recommendations this year will be reviewed and considered for next year’s budget.  

PTSA and IEF Grants:  The grants awarded to the district by the PTSA and the IEF have helped to fund many different projects and programs.  Several curriculum projects have been expedited because of the contributions from these two sources.

Sub-committee to review requests for summer curriculum projects: Jennifer asked for participation from some members of the group on the sub-committee that will review requests from teachers to do curriculum work during the summer.  Last year there were more requests than could be funded.  The role of the sub-committee is to review the request and make decisions either to fund the project as requested, modify it somewhat, or deny it.  Decisions are made primarily on the basis of the connection of the project to the areas of focus of the Curriculum Cycle.  Last year the district funded over 250 days of teacher work during the summer.  
Requests for Proposals (RFP) for summer work will go to staff by March 12, to be returned by April 5.   They will be reviewed on April 19 with notification to staff by April 26.   At this meeting, Lauren Allan, Jill Rembar, Mrinalini Rajwar, and Nadine McDermott offered to serve on the review committee.  2-3 other participants will be added, if possible.  [Note: Susan Dillon has also agreed to be on the review committee.]
 

Updates on specific areas

Curriculum work begun in 2001-02.  These committees began their work during September/October of this year.  They are in the “Needs Assessment” phase of their review. This phase generally entails doing research of state and national standards for the content area; in visitations to other schools or programs that are of interest; and, in beginning to plan for curriculum writing work, much of which takes place in the summertime.  The committees beginning their work this year are listed below, as are the staff members chairing or co-chairing the review.

Library – Mrinalini Rajwar.  The review of this area has been greatly facilitated by the fact that a year ago the Irvington librarians worked with others in the region to develop a K-12 library curriculum.  The work of this committee will be to integrate that curriculum into classroom practice and to make recommendations for how to use the new library spaces available in September 2003. 

World Languages – Lauren Allan and Linda Boyce.  All language teachers in the district are participating in this review which is focusing on: 
committing the current curriculum to similar written form as that used for other areas 
reviewing practice concerning student assessments such as the Regents exam and in other formal and informal means
considering the development of a Foreign Language in the Elementary School (FLES) program.  

Because this is the “Needs Assessment” phase of the review process, Lauren emphasized, there will be no significant program changes considered for implementation until September 2003.  Jill Rembar commented that she was glad to see that the district was taking a long and thoughtful look at the questions of the elementary program and allowing sufficient time to study the question and select a good program, if the decision is made to do that. 

Mathematics – Jennifer Dolan-Waldman, Lisa Urban.  A summary of the standards from the National Council of Teachers of Mathematics was distributed.  Jennifer added that their standards, along with those of New York State, are providing the basis for the review of the content of the district’s math program.  
The focus of the committee, as has been the case with every area under review, is to consider all facets of the program and open them up to discussion.  For math, this includes:

a review of the program at all levels
a consideration of what books and materials to use to support the program
a discussion of the delivery of instruction including grouping practices
staff development 
possibilities for additional courses to be considered for the HS as the population increases.   

Both Lisa and Jennifer emphasized that no decisions have been made yet on any area, nor are they expected to be made until next year when this committee will have completed the “Needs Assessment” phase of the review.   

Art – Nadine McDermott.   To date the Art Committee has met 3 times. The first meeting focused on reading and discussing what an art program should be and developing guidelines for the curriculum work.  At the second meeting the group refined their belief statements and worked on philosophy statements.  At the third  meeting, 3 teachers shared what they had learned by visiting each other’s classes. The group then began to delineate the skills and projects that are currently being done at each grade level and for each course at the high school.  At the next meeting, the committee will continue with this work and align each skill/project with the Standards.     


Curriculum work begun in 2000-01.  Some areas began their review last year and are now in the “Decision-Making” phase of the review cycle.  This phase typically includes completing the written documents for the area; making recommendations for program changes; and, planning for professional development to support the changes.  

Music - Nadine McDermott.  The music department to date has had 4 meetings. The curriculum writing sessions have included discussions on developing a philosophy of music education, an analysis of the NYS Learning Standards in the Arts as well as the National Standards in Music.  They are approaching the work standard by standard, and grade level by grade level.  They currently have a draft of a philosophy of music education as well as a draft of General Music, kindergarten through third grade, complete for Standard 1. The benefit of this work will be a clearly articulated curriculum that supports the course offerings and will provide students a wide range of opportunities to learn through music by performing, creating, reflecting and participating. 

Athletics – Art McCormack.  The report from the Athletics Committee was presented to the Board in the fall.  It contained many recommendations that will be reflected in this year’s budget. The Committee reviewed student participation in sports with an awareness of levels of participation by gender, by season, and by level. Among other areas.  They also reviewed the number of teams that have been added in the past 2 years to reflect the growing student population.  Comparative data from other districts was secured through Artie’s connections to other area Athletic Directors (AD) with whom he has frequent contact through regular meetings.   His report will be posted to the district web site.   

Middle School Home & Careers – Raina Kor.  As part of the “Needs Assessment” phase there have been several visitations to area schools to explore how other schools deliver the Home & Careers curriculum.  Chappaqua, Katonah, and Mamaroneck Middle Schools have all welcomed visits.  Observations of their programs indicate that each school has a very different approach to instruction in this area. The State standards are met with involvement of various departments such as guidance, health, and technology.  The facilities were reviewed to help in planning for our new Middle School.   

English Language Arts – Jennifer Dolan-Waldman.  This committee has been working since October 2000 on the K-12 curriculum.  The written document is largely completed, and will be available for distribution in the springtime.  At this time teachers are working on developing supporting materials to help deliver the curriculum at each grade level.  
The discussions which have taken place over the year have focused on assuring continuity of instruction, and on gaining agreement about the philosophical approach to be used.  Balanced literacy, an approach that supports students’ reading for specific skill instruction as well as for a development of appreciation for literacy, is the overall philosophical approach districtwide.  In addition, the importance of frequent assessment of student progress should be linked to flexible groupings that allow students opportunities to work with different groups over the course of instruction.  Developing classroom and school libraries that have a wide range of levels of materials has been supported through grants from the IEF and PTSA.   


Discussion on improving communications.  The final topic of discussion was on how to get information such as that shared at this meeting out to other members of the school community. The group agreed that the website was a good place for dissemination of material.  The minutes of our meetings will also be posted there, and we will ask to have a reference to that included in the weekly email of district events.
