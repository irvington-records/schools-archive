Irvington Union Free School District
                      Reopening Plan Addendum

                                 April 2021



Overview:

The introduction of effective COVID-19 vaccines coupled with the availability
of Rapid Antigen Tests have equipped the Irvington UFSD to be able to safely
expand daily student attendance. Following a thorough process in which
there was engagement from stakeholders, with support of the Board of
Education, the Irvington UFSD planned to phase in a return to full, daily
student attendance.



Community Engagement:

The Irvington UFSD is committed to engaging its school community in
critical decisions that affect its students. Initiating the consideration of
returning students to full, daily attendance (all students having the
opportunity to attend school five (5) days per week), the District facilitated
two surveys to the parent community to assess the school communities risk
tolerance associated with shifting classroom-based social distancing from six
(6) to three (3) feet and installing NYSED-approved barriers on all student
desks or tables. These surveys provided critical data that informed decision
making that was spearheaded by the Superintendent of Schools and the
Reopening Steering Committee.            The survey results indicated that
approximately 95% of the parent community was supportive of such an
initiative.

Mitigation Efforts:

In an effort to continue to mitigate the spread of COVID-19 within the
Irvington UFSD, the District will continue to employ all strategies outlined in
the District’s Reopening Plan. However, the following modifications will be
implemented:

   ● Social distancing in classrooms reduced from six (6) to three (3) feet;
   ● All student desks and tables will have NYSED approved barriers
     installed;



                                                                                   4
   ● Seating in the cafeteria will be continue to be six (6) feet without
     barriers;
   ● Supplemental dining spaces will be provided at each school as follows:
        ○ Dows Lane Elementary School: Gymnasium,
        ○ Main Street School: Auditorium, outdoors with the use of tents,
           and
        ○ Irvington Middle School and Irvington High School: Outdoors
           with the use of tents;
   ● Mask breaks will be provided outdoors only with appropriate social
     distancing;
   ● Parents will be encouraged to drive their children to school or have
     them walk rather than use school bus transportation to maintain low
     bus ridership; and
   ● Surveillance testing will be conducted on a semi-monthly basis.

The District will evaluate COVID-19 surveillance testing data on an on-going
basis in accordance with the plan below. Additionally, the Superintendent of
Schools will remain in communication with the Westchester County
Department of Health, which as the local Health Department shall advise as
to whether the District should take specific measures to modify this plan to
provide for healthy school communities. As the Westchester County
superintendents of schools meet with the Westchester County Department
of Health on a weekly basis, the regularly scheduled meeting shall serve as an
official vehicle for communications and decision making.

Should there be an increase in the prevalence of COVID-19 cases with the
Irvington schools, the District is prepared to modify student attendance and
either resume a hybrid model or transition to 100% to remote instruction if
necessary. All students, teachers and staff are encouraged to be equipped for
such a transition.

Surveillance Testing Plan:

To understand the COVID-19 prevalence in the Irvington UFSD schools, the
District will facilitate a surveillance testing program. The plan outlined below
was developed in consultation with the District Medical Director and the
Westchester County Department of Health.




                                                                               5
Licensure, COVID-19 Test Selection and Administration
The District will utilize the Westchester County Department of Health’s
(WCDOH) Limited Services Laboratory License # PFI: U631 CLIA# 33D0723201
to administer the tests.
The Abbot BinaxNOW Rapid Antigen Test will be utilized and will be sourced
through the New York State Department of Health (NYSDOH) through
WCDOH distribution. These test kits will be provided free of charge to the
Irvington UFSD.
To avoid disruptions to the surveillance testing program, the District has
purchased 1,000 BinaxNOW test kits as a precautionary measure. Should
these test be utilized, they will be administered by Mohawk Day Camp
Laboratory License # PFI: Z031 CLIA# 33D21287344.
The tests will be administered by District staff members and the nursing staff
of the Mohawk Day Camp Laboratory with whom the District has contracted.
Individuals administering the BinaxNOW test have completed all necessary
training.


Testing Methodology
The District will facilitate surveillance testing semi-monthly. Every two weeks,
5% of the students and staff members who are physically present on school
premises will be tested. This means that a total of 10% will be tested monthly.


Random Sampling
Those tested will be required to have a consent form on file with the Irvington
UFSD Office of the Superintendent. Utilizing excel, those for whom consent
was received will be randomly selected and scheduled to attend a mandatory
surveillance testing clinic. In all cases, individuals being tested will be
provided with a testing appointment a minimum of 48 hours prior to the
respective date.


Evaluation of Surveillance Testing Data

The Superintendent of Schools and the District Medical Director will review
surveillance testing results on a semi-monthly basis to evaluate the
prevalence rates in the District and in each school. All testing data will be
reported to the WCDOH and NYSDOH on the specific date testing is
completed.



                                                                               6
The District rate of prevalence will be compared to the Westchester County
rate or the Village of Irvington rate, whichever is higher. If the Irvington rate is
higher by 1% or more, the model of student attendance will be re-evaluated.



    Illustration:

    If the prevalence rate for Westchester County is 4% and the Village of
    Irvington is 5%, the rate for Irvington will be applied. If the rate within
    the District or a single school was 6%, the District would implement
    measures to mitigate potential viral spread.



In all such cases, the Medical Director and the WCDOH shall be consulted.

Depending on the level of prevalence, following consultation with the Medical
Director and the WCDOH, the District may take the following steps to
mitigate further spread of COVID-19:

   ● Direct specific classes of students to participate in 100% remote
     learning for 10 days;
   ● Direct specific grade levels of students to participate in 100% remote
     learning for 10 days;
   ● Direct specific schools to participate in 100% remote learning for 10
     days;
   ● Direct Direct the entire district to participate in 100% remote learning
     for 10 days; or
   ● Direct the entire District to attend in the District’s hybrid model.



Reporting of COVID-19 Prevalence in the Irvington Schools

The District will report all cases tested to the NYSDOH on the date of
administration via the ECLRS. The district will also report all positive cases to
the Westchester County Department of Health on the day of the positive test
and include a copy of the Laboratory Reporting Form. Prevalence levels,
therefore, will be reflected in the New York State COVID-19 Dashboard.
Additionally, the District will report all relevant data to its school community
via the weekly District newsletter and will post it on the District website,
www.IrvingtonSchools.org, on a semi-monthly basis.

 Surveillance for SARS-CoV-2 includes ongoing systematic activities, including collection, analysis, and
interpretation of health-related data that are essential to planning, implementing, and evaluating
public health practice. Surveillance testing is generally used to monitor for a community- or
population-level occurrence, such as an infectious disease outbreak, or to characterize the occurrence
once detected, such as looking at the incidence and prevalence of the occurrence. Surveillance testing
is used to gain information at a population level, rather than an individual level, and results of
surveillance testing can be returned in aggregate to the requesting institution. Surveillance testing may
sample a certain percentage of a specific population to monitor for increasing or decreasing prevalence
and to determine the population effect from community interventions, such as social distancing.
