Irvington Leader Shares SEL Best Practices at Regional Conference
Assistant Superintendent for Instruction and Human Resources Dr. Gail Duffy presented at the Putnam-Northern Westchester BOCES’ Supporting the Whole Child Regional Conference on Dec. 2.
In her workshop, “How To Be an SEL Champion for Your Students,” Dr. Duffy took a deeper dive into the state’s social-emotional learning benchmarks and CASEL competencies, provided insight into K-12 instructional strategies and mental health practices, and shared hands-on strategies and activities that teachers could utilize in the classroom to support the SEL needs of their students.
The state’s SEL benchmarks include assisting students in developing self-awareness and self-management skills that are essential to success in school and in life; using social awareness and interpersonal skills to establish and maintain positive relationships; and demonstrating ethical decision-making skills and responsible behaviors in personal, school and community contexts. Dr. Duffy also provided insight into how Irvington educators have been acting as SEL champions and providing thoughtful, inclusive and caring learning environments for all students. Using the five competencies – self-awareness, self-management, social awareness, relationship skills and responsible decision-making – teachers are planning and delivering lessons that incorporate these competencies into teaching and learning.
“SEL is an integral part of the educational experience at IUFSD,” Dr. Duffy said. “We can all be SEL champions in daily interactions with students, families, staff and our school community by modeling these competencies and providing space for our students to continue to grow and become the very best version of themselves.”
As a conclusion to her presentation, Dr. Duffy encouraged educators to share examples of how they teach using the social-emotional learning competencies and discuss how they will make SEL a focus in their classrooms going forward.
The conference brought together educators from across the region and provided them with opportunities to explore emerging and promising practices in instructional strategies, SEL and mental health practices, and school and districtwide support models that support the growth of all learners. They also reflected upon the successes and challenges of educators and engaged in professional development opportunities.
