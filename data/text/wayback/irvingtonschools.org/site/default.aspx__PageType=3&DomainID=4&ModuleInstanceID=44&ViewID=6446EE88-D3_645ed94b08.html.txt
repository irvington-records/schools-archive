Student-Teacher Partnership Preserves Campus History
A unique collaboration between former Irvington High School student Jabari Domingo and history teacher Dr. Erik Weiselberg has led to the creation of a comprehensive guide to the memorial markers found throughout the Irvington High School-Middle School campus. The project catalogs and honors the commemorative markers dedicated to past students, staff and community members who have left a lasting impact.
What began in 2021 as a simple question – prompted by Domingo’s curiosity about a name engraved on a marker – developed into an in-depth research initiative. Domingo and Dr. Weiselberg explored the campus grounds to document each memorial, ranging from engraved stones and benches to plaques and flagpoles. They examined old yearbooks, delved into archives of a former local newspaper, and spoke with staff and community members to uncover the stories behind each name.
“The project has had many rewarding aspects,” Dr. Weiselberg said. “It offered a meaningful opportunity for an enthusiastic student, uncovered the history of the people named on the memorials, and elicited the response of community members who recalled the people. Seeing the renovated memorial markers in place by the new trees of the campus redesign has been fulfilling.”
Their efforts have helped preserve the stories behind these memorials and helped inform the restoration and reinstallation of the markers during the district’s campus renovation, completed in 2025.
“The earliest record of memorial markers we found during our research was that in 1966 trees were planted in memory of three students from different class years,” Dr. Weiselberg said. “Thus, many of these original markers were probably placed right after the building and campus opened in 1965.”
The documentation and renovated markers will ensure the individuals and their stories continue to be remembered by future generations in the Irvington school community.
