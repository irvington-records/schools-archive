Long Range Planning
**Reprinted here with the kind permission of the Irvington Viewpoint, first published in the January 2000 issue as a "School Matters" column, by Janice Silverberg.
Two years worth of study, deliberation and hard work resulted in the Board of Education's formal adoption of a bond proposition on Dec. 14. The bond would fund renovation to our existing buildings as well as construction of a new middle school and a combined auditorium/gymnasium, both on the high school campus.
All five members of the Board declared their strong support for the bond proposition at earlier meetings. As well as expanding our facilities to accommodate the continuing increase in student enrollment, the proposed master plan would strengthen our science program by adding labs and research facilities, substantially increase our library resources, and accommodate the art and music programs. The plan also includes a cafeteria, updates our technology program, and renovates the district's playing fields and tennis courts. In short, this proposed master plan would do far more than add "bricks and mortar" - necessary as the new construction and renovation are to reducing the overcrowding at Dows Lane and preserving the three grade middle school program.
The bond proposition, which the community will vote on, would authorize the district to borrow $47 million. This amount includes approximately $10million in renovations to the high school, $3 million for renovating the other buildings, and $34 for new construction. The bond is part of a comprehensive financial plan for the district. For example, a capital line of $500,000 will be incorporated in the annual budget, ensuring that we are able to adequately maintain our facilities in the future.
The impact of the bond on Irvington's homeowners will be greatly reduced by several factors. First, bonds passed prior to June 30, 2000 will receive State aid of 18%. Including our much-needed renovations as part of a new building project secures this level of aid for the renovations also. Second, BOCES will both contribute to the capital costs and pay rent for classroom/office clusters in the district. Finally, our strong cert reserve fund will allow us to anticipate retiring this annual budget line; annual debt service on our prior bond is also decreasing substantially.
The community vote on the bond will be held on February 3, 2000. This is the earliest possible vote date following the State Education Department approvals process. It is essential to conduct the vote as early as possible for two reasons. First, the percentage of state building aid drops to 10% after June 30 (costing our district approximately a quarter of a million dollars annually over the course of the bond). In addition, the construction timeline would need to be moved back if we do not pass the bond in a timely fashion.
During the month of January, the Board and administration will schedule a number of opportunities, including tours of our existing facilities, for the community to learn more about the master plan. We encourage all those interested to participate in these activities or to contact us directly for more information.
