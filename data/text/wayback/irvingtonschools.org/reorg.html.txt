ADMINISTRATIVE REORGANIZATION PLAN
1997/98
INTRODUCTION:
As most of you are aware, we have been in the throes of a financial crisis caused by the costs to the district of tax certiorari settlements. While we are not entirely out of the woods yet, certainly the worst is over, and the district is now on a sound financial footing. We owe our current stability in large measure to Richard Hajek, who has, since January, 1996, added the role of District Superintendent to his long term responsibilities as High School Principal.
Mr. Hajek provided able leadership during this difficult time, crafting annual budgets that addressed the certiorari issues while maintaining our educational programs. In addition, his very willingness to do two demanding jobs with a modest salary increase saved us the considerable cost of paying a separate Superintendent's salary and benefits during this time.
CURRENT SITUATION:
Mr. Hajek has informed the Board of his intention to retire in January, 1999, at the completion of three years in his current position. The original contract between Mr. Hajek and the Board of Education provided for the possibility of his remaining until the end of the 1998/99 school year. However, his thinking, in which we concur, is that an earlier departure date will best serve the needs of the district.
In essence, we have accomplished what we set out to do: weathered the financial crisis and maintained the integrity of our district's educational programs. At this time, we face a different set of challenges, such as dramatically increasing enrollment, and looming revisions of State educational standards and related changes in graduation requirements. These are long range issues, properly requiring the attention of administrators whom we can anticipate will be with the district for the long haul.
STATUS OF ADMINISTRATIVE POSITIONS:
We must begin by recognizing that the joining of the two positions of High School Principal and Superintendent was a temporary measure, requiring a variance from the State. It was a highly unusual move; indeed, no other Westchester district combines these two positions.
In fact, it is extremely doubtful whether anyone other than Richard Hajek, with his three decades of experience in the Irvington school system and unique leadership skills, could have succeeded in this dual role. At this time, we will need to reestablish these two essential positions as separate and distinct. We firmly believe that these positions are so demanding that it is unrealistic to expect any one person to do justice to both over any extended period of time.
The need to select candidates for these two critical positions is part of an ongoing administrative reorganization that poses the greatest challenge to our district. During the past two years, we have replaced both our Director of Special Education, and our Assistant Superintendent for Business. At some point, we can anticipate the resignation of Dr. Kerfut as Middle School Principal as well. During this process, the Board has and will continue to be guided by the need to balance the introduction of new perspectives and ideas offered by candidates from outside our system with the expertise and continuity represented by candidates from within. Given the number of administrative changes we face, this balance is critical to the optimal functioning of the district. The Board is also mindful of the need to provide for an appropriate transition period between Mr. Hajek and the incoming administrators, in order to effect an orderly transfer of responsibilities with no disruption to the program.
ANTICIPATED TIMEFRAME:
- 1997/98 school year with current administrative complement;
- early 1998: begin search process for new Superintendent;
-July, 1998: appoint new High School Principal
-September - November, l998: appoint new District Superintendent
-January 1, l999: Richard Hajek resignation
FINANCIAL IMPACT:
Obviously, the district has enjoyed a "two for the price of one" situation since January of 1996. Mr. Hajek's compensation was increased $19,500. annually when he assumed the Superintendent's role. We estimate that this arrangement will have saved the district over $400,000 at the completion of the three years.
However, we do need to anticipate an additional salary line when the new Superintendent is appointed. ( It should be noted that there is no impact to the 1997/98 budget).
The Board is committed to evaluate salary structure and particular candidates with the aim of securing the best possible Principal and Superintendent for our district in the most financially prudent manner.
