The mission of the Irvington Schools District is to help each student discover and achieve his or her highest potential academically, athletically, artistically, and socially. Each year, our graduates are recognized nationally and statewide. This year we had 3 National Merit scholarship winners, 14 scholar-athlete teams and 4 with the highest academic average in NY in their sport, several All-State and All-Eastern musicians and singers and winners of community service awards. (Go to http://www.irvingtonschools.org/Achievements.html for a complete listing of student achievements). Irvington placed 197 out of all high schools nationally in this year's Challenge Index (number of Advanced Placement exams taken divided by number of graduates), has maintained its overall strong performance on Regents exams and has seen an increasing number of graduates earning Regents Diploma and Regents Diplomas with distinction.
While we are justifiably proud of our graduates' achievements, we always review the academic results of our students so that we may improve our program K-12 and identify students who need extra help. In recent years, our children have been subject to an increasing number State Education Department tests designed to raise standards across NY State while holding districts accountable for their students' performance. All 4th and 8th graders (including special needs students) are now required to take math and English Language Arts (ELA) exams. Students are graded as performing at Level 1 (showing major deficiencies), Level 2 (not meeting state standards), Level 3 (meeting state standards) and Level 4 (highest - exceeding state standards). It is the goal of every district to have the highest percentage of students in Levels 3 and 4 and as many students as possible in Level 4. The results of the 4th and 8th grade ELA and math exams are published in newspapers around the state. Parents and district residents compare results from year-to-year and from district-to-district in an effort to determine which districts are "better" or "worse", which districts are improving or which are not.
What do these tests tell us about a district and a specific set of students? Several cautions are in order. First, most school results represent relatively small samples of students: a particular grade's performance can be skewed by a few students and so small differences from year-to-year or between districts are often not statistically significant. Second, the exams are still relatively new - annual changes may be the result of differences in the exam. Finally, yearly score variations in an exam are possible since they are measuring different groups of kids: one grade may do well and the next grade may not even though the program is unchanged.
Irvington's students have always done well on these exams. Dows Lane 4th grade students attaining Level 3+4 on the ELA exam have increased slowly over the last 4 years to 86% in 2003, a level that is comparable to many similar districts in our area. A continued emphasis will be placed on improving this result. Dows Lane 4th grade math students also did well, with 91% reaching Level 3+4 - in line with other similar Westchester districts. IMS 8th grade math students have improved markedly over the last 4 years. In fact, IMS was the #1 performing school in NY State in 2002 with 96% scoring at or above standards (Levels 3+4) and 54% attaining Level 4. We are awaiting the results of the 2003 exam. However, IMS 2003 8th grade ELA results saw a decline from 84% at or above standards in 2002 to 68% in 2003. While other similar area districts saw Level 3+4 declines, none saw such a dramatic decline year-over-year.
The Board of Education and administration believe that the performance of this year's 8th grade on the ELA should have been better and is not representative of the capabilities of these students. Several steps are being taken so that we may understand the results and assist children who did not meet the standards.
- We are reviewing each student's test to see where errors occurred. Reviewing the "item analysis" for both individual and groups of students will help us target instruction more directly during the next school year.
- We are comparing each student's 8th grade ELA results against their 4th grade ELA scores from 4 years ago.
- We are reviewing any gaps in our 4th and 8th grade ELA curricula. While we do not plan on "teaching to the test", we will insure that our students receive appropriate forms of preparation.
- We will provide extra academic support to students who did not meet or exceed standards as they enter high school to help them prepare for the required English Regents exam.
The Irvington Schools community measures its success largely by our students' academic performance. Your Board of Education places great emphasis on maintaining and improving the quality of our educational program. The Board conducts monthly public meetings focusing on specific aspects of our program. In recent years, the district has embarked on a multi-year curriculum review cycle for each subject K-12. (View the entire Curriculum Review Cycle on our website). Finally, early in the new school year a public Board meeting will be held to present the results of these exams and analyze the results. As always, the Board of Education welcomes your feedback: contact us at board@irvingtonschools.org.
