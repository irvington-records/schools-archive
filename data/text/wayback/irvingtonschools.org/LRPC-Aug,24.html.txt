Long Range Planning Committee
Meeting No. 4: Minutes
August 24, 1999
- Don Marra opened the meeting and noted that the previous meeting had addressed:
- the vision statement;
- space requirements;
- grade configuration options and locations.
- Vincent Beni told the committee that:
- he would like to bring the LRPC’s feedback to the Board in early September;
- a review of the enrollment projections will take place after the actual enrollment in September is determined.
- At the previous meeting, a discussion took place about the advantages and disadvantages of the following options:
- a 4-6 elementary school on the east field at the High School;
- a 4-6 elementary school on a new site;
- b 6-8/9-12 school on the High School site (alternate options of this configuration were presented and discussed).
- Peter Gisolfi reviewed the drawings presented at the last meeting, highlighting the following points:
- Based on the traffic study conducted by Frederick Clark Associates, the High School site is the only site which can take additional traffic. Efforts are underway to have a traffic light installed at the intersection of Broadway and the High School drive.
- In the option of a 4-6 elementary school on the east field with grades 7-8 at Main Street and grades 9-12 at the high school:
- The High School would require an addition of 50,000 sf to meet future needs;
- The 4-6 school would require 75,000 sf and would eliminate the east field.
- The 6-8/9-12 option would require 120,000 sf of additional space,
- A new diagram for the 6-8/9-12 option was presented which included:
- the existing high school building for classrooms and administration space
- a new middle school classroom building
- new special spaces for science and art
- a new cafeteria
- conversion of the existing auditorium and gym to a new shared library and a middle school gym
- a new building with an auditorium and a high school gym which would be used by the community
- parking for more than 300 cars.
- A discussion of the advantages and disadvantages raised the following points:
- Could the buildings be made taller so that the footprint could be made smaller? At one location in the current diagram, there would need to be a four-stop elevator because of the change in the grade across the site. However, the architects will continue to look into the idea of adding to the height of building.
- Could there be limits on student parking? This is a viable way to keep parking requirements lower. There could be senior privileges only or a lottery system.
- Is a second gym needed? The second gym would be for the high school and would provide two gym floors for instructional use which would be one competition floor. There would be bleachers for spectators and support space (lockers, offices, etc.) It is preferable to keep distinct gym spaces for the middle school and high school respectively.
- The 4-6 school on east field would eliminate the field entirely and would create a hardship. The 6-8/9-12 option would include renovation of east field.
- The middle school concept is important. There can be different configurations that support a middle school concept. The 6-8/9-12 option keeps the middle school as a distinct entity, with clusters of classrooms for 6th, 7th, and 8th grade and facilities for art, music, science and phys ed.
- On costs: the 4-6 school option would require building 125,000 sf for all its components and would incur costs for site acquisition and development; the 6-8/9-12 option would require building 120,000 sf.
- Could there be parking under any of the new buildings? The cost of building parking on-grade is about $1500 per space. This jumps to approximately $10,000 per space because of structure, insulation, fire protection and utilities. It might be possible to stack parking on a sloped part of the site where, if there is enough open space, mechanical ventilation might not be required.
- Is there a way to get more field space? Vincent Beni has met with the mayor to discuss the possibility of using Village fields; a spirit of cooperation exists.
- Is the construction of a pool an option? There is a lot of community interest in this. The idea of a pool that can be shared with the community is being considered and will be discussed further with the Village.
- Could tennis courts be built? Tennis courts might be an option at the existing basketball courts at Dows Lane.
- What class size is expected in the High School? At one time there were classes at the High School with 10 or fewer students. While the High School isn’t "crunched" yet, the population in the lower grades is burgeoning and the High School will see larger classes each year. The space requirements were based on 20-22 students per class.
- Will the school be adequate for future growth? There are different ways to look at capacity. Capacity can be defined for:
- classroom utilization at 75-80% efficiency
- utilization with BOCES spaces converted to classrooms
- additional classrooms if needed at a later date. Reminder: Using high end enrollment projections.
- Has any attempt been made at utilizing the existing High School space more efficiently? The current diagram assumes the conversion of the existing library, cafeteria, and District offices into classrooms to meet projected population increases. The District offices might be moved to the Main Street school.
- Should we revisit the creation of a new Middle School on a separate site? Would require approximately 100,000 sf of construction, site acquisition costs, site preparation costs, and still require an additional 50,000 sf at the High School.
- Benefits of the 6-8/9-12 school option:
- Economy of long-term staffing since several positions could be shared between schools
- Better library and science space
- Better opportunities for art and music
- More opportunities for enrichment for all students
- Community center with 365-day per year use
- Possibility of a satellite lunch program to serve the other schools and keep the students in the building for lunch
- Land acquisition possibilities were discussed:
- Don Marra and Vincent Beni have talked to neighboring property owners about acquiring land adjacent to the high school site. No interest in this has been demonstrated.
- The Unification Church owns 40 acres adjacent to Lyndhurst, 35 acres across from Kraft, and 188 acres in Greenburgh. All have the potential to be developed and could generate an additional 500 students in the District in the future. The District will continue to work with the various planning boards to insure that school issues are addressed in future developments.
- New sites have cost implications for acquisition, development, and staffing.
- Costs:
- A total financial package will be developed to present to the public to explain costs and tax implications.
- Dr. Beni noted that the debt service will be declining significantly in the next two years.
- There is an opportunity to take advantage of BOCES financing for new construction. BOCES will also lease space from the District if there is space, until that space is needed by the District.
- Building Assessments: Capital Improvement Projects
- J. Michael Orifici distributed a spreadsheet which listed capital improvement projects for all three existing schools which have been identified by the architects and engineers, the school administrators and the District.
- The State requires a capital assets preservation program to show how the District intends to address maintenance issues and extend the useful life of its buildings.
- These projects will be prioritized into work that could be done under a bond or through the District’s capital improvements program over five to ten years.
- The projects listed under Space Alterations assumed that modifications were needed at each school. This list will be edited if the decision is made to pursue the 6-8/9-12 option.
- Next Steps:
- Dr. Beni asked for and received the committee’s recommendation to pursue the various 6-8/9-12 options with the Board of Education.
- The major concerns that need to be addressed are parking and field space.
- George Huzinec noted his observations of the process: the LRPC has been effective; the information is getting narrower and more focused; the trade-offs are getting clearer.
- A tour of all three buildings will be organized to take place soon. Three alternative dates will be proposed shortly after this meeting.
- There will be a report on the LRPC work made to the Board at its meeting on September 14th and a Board work session on this subject is scheduled for September 28, 1999.
