Back to School 2004
**Reprinted here with the kind permission of the Rivertowns Enterprise, first published in the August 2004 issue as a contribution to the "Back to School" Issue.
The Irvington Union Free School District is off to a great start for the 2004-05 school year. Approximately twenty new teachers have been hired, and several new faces can be seen on the administrative team. Besides the new Superintendent, Dr. Kathleen Matusiak, and the new Assistant Superintendent for Instruction, Dr. Ralph Napolitano, there are two other changes in key leadership positions. Mr. Scott Fried began his role as the principal of Irvington Middle School in July. Mr. Fried was an assistant principal in the Plainedge, New York school system before coming to Irvington. Ms. Raina Kor, who has been the interim principal at Irvington Middle School, will return to her former role as assistant principal. Dr. Lola Garcia will be assuming a new principalship in New Haven, CT where she resides. No appointment has yet been made to fill this vacancy, but it is anticipated that a decision will be made at a special meeting of the Board of Education on July 27, 2004.
This summer the entire Central Office staff relocated from 90 North Broadway to 6 Dows Lane. Anyone needing information about the school district can visit the offices which are situated next to Dows Lane Elementary School.
The Irvington Schools offer rich educational experiences to all students, K-12. Student achievement continues to be strong, as reflected in results from recent State tests, Regents, and Advanced Placement tests. There was a very notable gain on the 2004 eighth grade State English Language Arts test, which had an increase of fourteen points from 2003. Grade 4 results remained consistent.
This year, 165 students participated in the Advanced Placement testing program, reflecting an increase of 34 students from the previous school year. More significantly, 303 exams were taken this year in comparison to the 241 completed last school year. Despite the 25% increase in student participation and test completion, our scores remained consistent.
Additionally, 953 Regents examinations were completed in 12 subject areas this June. With 904 papers receiving grades of 65 or better, 95% of the papers scored attained passing grades. More impressive, 57% of those papers received a mastery distinction indicating scores of 85-100.
English Language Arts (ELA) has been a major focus in the district in recent months as staff members prepare for the October 20-22, 2004 Tri-State Consortium visit. Several individuals have received training, and a committee of teachers and administrators have been meeting this summer to evaluate our new ELA program, collect and organize students' work, and make decisions about how a myriad of details will be coordinated to ensure a successful experience for the visiting team. Dr. Scott Mosenthal, Irvington High School principal, and Ms. Eileen McGuire, assistant principal of Dows Lane Elementary School are chairing the Tri-State evaluation process. Students, teachers and parents will receive information this summer about surveys and interviews that will be conducted. Strengths and considerations cited in the report will be used in the district's future planning.
The Board of Education also has one new face, following the recent elections. Replacing George Huzinec is Ms. Carol Keller-Gutman who will be serving a three-year term. Also beginning a new three-year term is Ms. Diane Mundree who has served on the Board since 2001. Other members are President Penny Delany and Trustees Paul Mandel and Janice Silverberg.
Students and their parents can look forward to several new programs and initiatives during the 2004-05 school year. At Dows Lane, the new Trailblazers mathematics program will be implemented in grades K-3. This program is an excellent combination of inquiry-based learning and practice in mathematic computation. Trailblazers builds upon previous student learning and engages students in a variety of age appropriate activities. The program's scope and sequence is closely aligned to the seven key mathematical ideas as outlined in the New York State curriculum. It has a wide variety of activities and materials to meet the needs of students of varying abilities. Teachers who piloted the program enthusiastically endorsed its adoption.
Main Street School is looking forward to the implementation of a new character education anti-bullying program called No Put Downs. Children and their parents will be receiving further information about this initiative in the fall.
Forty-one fifth graders at Main Street School will be taught in a collaborative teaching model next year. Mr. Philip Levine and Ms. Amy Panitz have been working hard for many months to plan some very exciting and creative lessons for their students. In future years, Main Street School looks forward to implementing this model in other classrooms as well.
Irvington Middle School will also be introducing several new initiatives. This year world language will be introduced into the sixth grade. Last year students in the fifth grade were introduced to Spanish and French and are now able to make a decision about which language they wish to pursue in Middle School. Our seventh and eighth grade students will have a humanities block, in which English and social studies will be scheduled in a two period block. This will also allow the two teachers more flexibility and integration of curriculum so they can meet the needs of all students. In addition, Irvington Middle School is piloting a new program in character education using Social Design Making/Problem Solving, a program created in partnership with Rutgers University and our nation's schools, as well as Facing History and Ourselves, which also focuses on decision making but through the lens of the social studies curriculum.
A number of new courses have been added to the curriculum at Irvington High School including AP Human Geography, Interior Design, Sports Marketing, Creative Writing, Journalism, AP Music Theory, and AP Computer Science. In addition, a ninth grade mentoring program will pair every entering freshman with a senior in order to help the younger student adjust to the high school. Finally, a new guidance counselor has been hired to accommodate the increase in student enrollment as well as to provide additional guidance programming.
The population in the Irvington Schools will increase by approximately 200 students this fall. Our administrators and teachers look forward to welcoming these newcomers and their families.
The school district continues to be the grateful beneficiary of very generous donations from the Irvington Education Foundation. In June 2004, over 19 different items totaling $157,729 were approved by the IEF Board. In addition, the PTSA continues to give tremendous support to all of our schools. Co-Presidents for the 2004-05 school year will be Ms. Francie Malina and Ms. Deb Weintraub.
The 2004-05 school year is marked with great enthusiasm and a continued commitment to excellence. We look forward to welcoming each and every student and to provide the challenges necessary to quality educational experiences for all members of the school community.
Dr. Kathleen Matusiak
Superintendent
