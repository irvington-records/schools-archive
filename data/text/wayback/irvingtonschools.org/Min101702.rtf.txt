The meeting began with a review of the current curriculum areas under review.  The areas beginning the process of “Needs Assessment,” the first year of curriculum review include:


Physical Education, K-12 – Joe DeGennaro is leading the review of this content area.  He reported that the group was meeting that day, incorporating the Health Department for the initial phases of discussion.  He indicated that the members of both the PE and Health departments will be basing their work on the New York State standards for their area, and will be visiting selected schools to gather new ideas for the programs. 

Health - As noted above, this review will be combined at selected point with the Physical Education area because New York State has defined common standards for them. As this work proceeds a sub-group will develop a curriculum for Health instruction K-12.

Business Education – Scott Mosenthal is chairing this committee which will also include other staff members as well as a community member.  Their purpose will be to see where the Business Education curriculum should go, mindful of the decreasing number of student requests in this department.  In conjunction with this work Scott will also be leading a high school review of the elective program to determine what other courses will be added to the high school course offerings to provide opportunities for students in areas in addition to business.  

Art – Nadine McDermott is leading this review which began last year but was extended because of the music review which was also led by Nadine.  One area of focus for the art program has been to determine specific goals for required art instruction at all grade levels through Middle School, and to define the course opportunities available at the High School when art becomes an elective option.  


The following areas are in the “Decision-making” phase of the Curriculum Cycle, each of them having begun their work last year.  

Music - Nadine McDermott indicated that the General Music work is complete for grades K-8 and the work for choral and instrumental instruction should soon be completed.  The next part of this group’s work is to develop a music sequence for students at the high school level so they will be able to work towards graduation requirements and the development of a 3-year sequence in this area.  

Library – The review in this area has focused on what the instructional program should be as it relates to student needs in using our library/media facilities.  The committee is also considering what structures will be appropriate for next year when the buildings will be reconfigured.  At that time there will be a joint library for the Middle and High School students and two separate libraries for Dows Lane K-3 and the new Main Street School (for Grades 4-5). 

World Languages – Linda Boyce discussed the fact that the first charge of this committee was to work on developing the written curriculum for Grades 7 – 12 in Spanish, French, and Latin. (A draft of that document will be discussed later in the meeting.) One change in the current program which has already been implemented is that the students will now take the New York State Regents exam at the end of 10th grade rather than 11th as is done in other high schools.  This will enable students to participate in a 4th year of language study for students before they enter the AP program and will better prepare them for the AP.  This will also enable us to consider other options for students not choosing the AP route such as starting a new language in Grade 11.         



Mathematics – Jennifer Dolan-Waldman and Lisa Urban have been working with this committee since last year.  The first need for the group was to develop a written K-12 curriculum. (A draft of that document will be discussed later in the meeting.)  After defining a curriculum based on standards from both New York State and from the National Council of Teachers of Mathematics, the committee will determine what materials, instructional approaches, and grouping practices will be appropriate to support the curriculum.  The choices for programs at the elementary level (K-5) have been narrowed to three, and members of the committee will visit three local schools currently implementing those programs to see which one looks like the best match for Irvington.


The following are in the “Implementation” phase of the Curriculum Cycle, having begun their work in September 2000. 

English Language Arts (ELA) - Each of the principals spoke of how ELA instruction in their buildings is supporting the revised curriculum.   At the High School, Scott said, the social studies and English departments have been working to develop a shared rubric for writing expectations so that students receive more consistent messages about expectations.  Additionally, the High School staff is looking at increasing elective options for students, particularly at the 12th grade after State requirements have been met by taking the Regents exam. He noted that student performance in this area has gotten better each year based on a review of the last 4 years’ data.
Lola Garcia-Blocker indicated that she and Lauren Allan are sharing a consultant who is working with staff on reading and writing skills.  The consultant will be modeling strategies in class with students and working directly with teachers as they implement the ELA document.  Joe Rodriguez also indicated that a consultant has been working with staff to assist them in working with new assessment tools that can be used by teachers in class with students on a regular basis.  Jennifer noted that many of our K-3 teachers have chosen to augment their involvement in this work by attending summer sessions led by the district’s consultant.  
    
Athletics – Because of the growing student population and its impact on the athletic program, this review was completed early in the process.  Artie McCormack indicated that new teams had been added as a follow-up to the review process.    
 

Summer Curriculum work – It was noted in the presentations on each of the curriculum areas above that a good deal of work had been done during the summertime.  The funding for the summer work is determined by a sub-group of this Curriculum Committee.  Jill Rembar, who had been a member of the review committee, described the group’s process and the insights gained through the discussions.  Jennifer encouraged members of the group to consider serving on the sub-committee which will meet for one full day in the spring to review submissions for summer work, and a follow-up briefer meeting in the fall to review the work submitted and recommend payment.   

 
Assessment of Student Progress – This topic will be the primary focus of the Superintendent’s Conference Day on October 24th.  At that time student performance in many content areas will be considered in light of how performance can inform our professional development efforts and lead us to a deeper understanding of student needs.  Teachers will also be asked to consider a variety of ways that information about students might be collected and useful to efforts to enhance instruction. 


Review of Math and World Language Drafts – The committee group worked in three smaller groups to review the drafts submitted and to make comments and suggestions for the leaders of those areas.  The consensus was that the math document was easier to read in early stages but did get more complex in higher grades.  With an acknowledgement that the issue of grouping is of high interest in this area, Jennifer and Lisa indicated that there is first a need to define the curriculum, then the program(s) that will be used to implement the curriculum, and then to consider how to change the structure of the delivery of the content before the issue of grouping can be decided.
The vast amount of material covered in language class – speaking, listening, reading, and writing as well as elements of culture and history relevant to the language – was more comprehensive than some committee members had realized.  The next steps of defining an elementary program and determining what additional language(s) might be introduced at the high school level will be addressed during the course of this school year.   

 
Additional areas of focus – As noted in the minutes, there are several possibilities for new courses to be developed during the course of this year. Such proposals should be brought to this committee at our January or May meetings.  Recommendations for implementation would then reflect the overall goals of the Curriculum Committee in relation to the various needs and challenges that the district is facing.
