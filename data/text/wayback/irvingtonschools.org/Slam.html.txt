To help kick off "Teen Read Week" (Oct. 13- 17), Irvington Middle School will hold a "Book Slam" event in which every single student in the school will dramatically read aloud to their classmates a three-minute passage from a book of their choice.
Teachers will then select the top three students in each class who will compete later this month in a special school-wide assembly. Prizes (gift certificates for books), will be awarded to the winners in each grade.
"The whole school is abuzz about the contest," said Middle School Librarian Joann Molloy. "The students really enjoy the exercise which helps students focus more on leisure reading and gets them interested in books that maybe they dismissed or didn't know existed before."
To help kick off the event, the Irvington PTSA sponsored a visit by a professional dramatic storyteller --- Jim McKenna, who spent the day at the school on Monday, Sept. 30, using voices, emotional expressions and acting to read stories and books to students using his talents to make the books come alive.
"Teen Read Week" was started four years ago by the Young Adult Library Services Association (YALSA), a division of the American Library Association (ALA) to forge a stronger link between teens, libraries and reading.
