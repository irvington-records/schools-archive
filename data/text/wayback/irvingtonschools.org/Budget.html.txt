Several factors have influenced the development of the Superintendent's recommended budget:
- New York State Standards
- Enrollment Increase
- Technology Plan
- Facilities Needs
- Special Education
- Teacher Contract
- State Aid
The recommended budget reflects the impact of these issues.
New York State Standards - The district will focus on increasing staff development with an emphasis on K-12 curriculum development. A Summer Academy for students who need extra support will be provided for identified students in grades 3-12. (A major portion of this program will be paid for by a grant.) New high school courses will be offered, and the multi-sensory program will be expanded to include 1st and 2nd grades.
Enrollment Increase - Projections indicate that we will have nearly 1700 students next year, an increase of 5.5%. Nine new teachers and support staff will be hired to insure that class size remains equal to or slightly less than 1998-99 levels.
Technology - The infrastructure for the High School network will be installed, and a new computer lab will be built there also. A major emphasis will be on staff development focusing on incorporating technology into the curriculum.
Facilities - The district will be narrowing down its options to address the enrollment/space issues as identified in the Long Range Planning Report. (Call 591-8501 to receive a copy.) As a short term measure, the district will provide two additional modular classrooms at Dows Lane next year.
Special Education - As the overall enrollment grows, the number of students requiring special education also increases. This budget provides programs for students with intensive needs.
Teacher Contract - The new teachers' contract provides the district with an opportunity to realize savings in the budget. The savings generated by the retirement of 8 veteran teachers will pay for the salaries of the 9.2 new teachers. The district also has the opportunity to provide significant new staff development without additional cost.
State Aid - Irvington continues to receive a disproportionately low share of aid from New York State.  In fact, we receive less than 7% of our budget from the State.  The average district receives 43% of its budget from State funds. 
The budget focuses on instructional needs and actually reduces the amount of money spent on non-instructional items. Although the tax rate increases by 3.97%, any homeowner who applied for the STAR program will see a reduction in taxes. For example, an owner of a home assessed at $18,000 will see a school tax reduction of $287.
The Board will continue to discuss the budget at its meeting on April 6 and will adopt a budget on April 20 which is the last date for budget changes. A hearing will be conducted on May 4, with the public budget vote and Board election on May 18.
