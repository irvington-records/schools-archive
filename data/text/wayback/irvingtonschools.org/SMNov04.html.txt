School Matters, November 2004
By Janice Silverberg
As our High School continues to grow, in population and programs, the Middle School begins its second year on the shared campus. Both schools are engaged in an enthusiastic exploration of the potential the new facilities offer.
High School Principal Dr. Scott Mosenthal gave the Board an overview of the class of 2004, and a preview of this year's program. Of last year's graduates, 95% earned Regents diplomas. This achievement is significant since it was only 4 years ago that IHS did not award any Regents diplomas. As a result, IHS is ahead of many other area high schools in this regard. Also of note, 6 students were named National Merit Scholar finalists, giving IHS one of the highest number of finalists in the area as a percentage of class size. Remarkably, last year IHS claimed 16 NYS Scholar Athlete teams, two of which had the highest grade point averages in the State. 43 students participated in NY State School Music Association, 16 were named area All-State musicians. Every single senior participated in the Senior Internship program, and fifteen earned credit for community service.
This year, 540 students are registered at the High School, 138 of whom are in the senior class. The number of Advanced Placement courses has grown steadily from 5 in 1999 to 17 last year. This year, the High School offers 4 additional AP classes in computer science, human geography, music theory, and language and composition. There are more elective offerings as well, including women's studies, journalism/creative writing, interior design and sports marketing. Extracurricular offerings range from academic challenge, key club, band, drama and yearbook to comedy improvisation, gay/straight alliance, community service and the audience club.
IHS has challenges as well as successes: Assistant Principal Joe DeGennaro focuses on student management efforts enforcing strict guidelines for student behavior. While regents results are generally excellent, the administration and staff are always looking for ways to improve student outcomes. To meet the full range of student needs, priorities for the 2004/05 school year include teacher mentoring, an evaluation of alternative programs, and a process of goal setting to enhance student achievement.
The Middle School story is enrollment, enrollment, enrollment, as reported by Principal Scott Fried. Clearly the driving force behind the new construction, the number of IMS students has increased from 378 in 2000 to 482 this year. With numbers comes opportunity as well as challenge: this year, world language was introduced to the 6th grade class, focusing on culture, history and literacy; collaborative education (general and special education students in one class, with team teaching) has been implemented in all three grades for English, social studies and science; character education has become a cornerstone of the middle school mission, with a focus on "enduring understandings" and "essential questions".
In order to assist students with varying abilities to achieve their best, IMS offers a wide range of student supports. These include a school based support team (guidance counselor, psychologist, social worker and student assistance counselor); instructional support teams, workshops in reading and writing; homework help; study skills training and daily team meetings. There is a solid schedule of fall, winter and spring modified sports and an extracurricular "menu" that includes knitting and stock market clubs, Amnesty International, Junior Red Cross as well as Science Olympiad, jazz and chamber ensembles and year book. On the horizon this year are town meetings, interdisciplinary teams, block scheduling for English/social studies, and winter concerts and arts festival.
Changes in scheduling, bus procedures, and cafeteria use have eased some of the initial strains of sharing a joint campus with IHS, as Assistant Principal Raina Kor, who served as Interim Principal last year, reported. Committees with representatives of both schools continue to address scheduling, the transition process, and joint ventures such as Veterans' Day lunch, peer tutoring and building beautification projects.
Overall, both the High School and Middle School give the joint campus a resounding cheer. We look forward to ongoing reports from this exciting front.
