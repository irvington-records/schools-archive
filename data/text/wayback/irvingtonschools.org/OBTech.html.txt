SOURCE http://www.irvingtonschools.org:80/OBTech.html | DATE 2003-10-20 | TITLE The View On Board: Technology

Technology is now an integral part of our children's daily learning experience in the Irvington Schools. Achieving this goal took years of diligent work and a major investment in equipment, software and staff training. The District takes great pride in this accomplishment.
In 1998, the Board adopted a Long-range Instructional Technology Plan whose #1 goal is to integrate technology in the daily curriculum of our children's education K-12. When the Board's Technology Committee began its work, it was done against a backdrop of outdated, non-networked, non-functional technology (available only in Dows Lane), a lack of teacher training and failed prior efforts. The committee recommended an ongoing commitment to funding; emphasis on staff development; hiring a Director to administer the program; timely technical assistance to keep it all running; fair distribution of technology resources and, most importantly, integrating technology with our curriculum.
These goals were not met overnight. However, we have reached the point where we can say that our students are using technology to enrich their daily learning experience. We have made a major investment in hardware, software and networking resources. Since 1998, the District has invested $1.3 million in hardware and networks, purchasing 400 workstations and networking each school. Additional funds have been spent on staff development, technology salaries, maintenance, a student information system, and software resources. Each classroom has networked workstations and every school has at least 1 computer lab. Peripheral devices (including several cutting-edge electronic whiteboards funded by IEF grants, are now widely available. Our 3 school buildings now have active and useful web sites.
It's not enough to buy computers and simply tell teachers and students to go use them. As envisioned by the Plan, the District embarked on an effort to educate our teachers on how to best utilize technology. Since 1998, teachers have spent over 500 days learning how to use hardware, software and the Internet and to integrate them into our daily curriculum. When hiring new teachers, we place emphasis on technological capabilities. Our Assistant Superintendent for Instruction, Dr. Dolan-Waldman, is deeply involved in all technology staff development. Technology now infuses curriculum development, such as the newly completed English and Language Arts K-12 Curriculum. This past year, technology was ended as a "Special" in Dows Lane. As a result, the emphasis on technology has increased: instead of classroom teachers dropping students off in the lab, they now use technology to lead lessons related to the daily curriculum. This teaching model, requiring extensive staff development, was a key goal of the Technology Plan.
Administering our Plan is Technology Director, Mrinalini Rajwar who purchases new hardware, keeps it running and trains our staff to make the best use of it. She is developing benchmarks for student technology skills and staff development K-12. Most importantly, she leads a committee to keep our Technology Plan up to date. Come to a Board technology presentation tentatively scheduled for January 21, 2003 to review our progress.
The Board of Education welcomes your feedback regarding our Technology program.
