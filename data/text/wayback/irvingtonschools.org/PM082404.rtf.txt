 V	PROGRAM AND POLICY ITEMS

Superintendent’s Report:

Mentoring Program (Supplemental Log – Appendix A)

Dr. Matusiak introduced Dr. Napolitano for his report on the Mentoring Program and Teacher Induction Academy.  Dr. Napolitano explained that mentoring is not new to school districts but that the Commissioner’s Regulations now require that all teachers, certified after February 1, 2004, must meet new requirements:
Complete a mentored experience in their first year of teaching
Complete 175 hours of professional development every five years to maintain certification
Document that requirements have been achieved
Among the various purposes of a mentoring program are to provide support for new teachers, provide resources and increase student achievement.  Mentor training has begun with a two day training session for 12 Irvington teachers as part of the District-wide Professional Development Plan (PDP).  The New Teacher Academy, a bridge program for experienced teachers, provides information about the school community and culture.  The Academy reviews building initiatives, testing protocols and school as well as district report cards and goals, professional development options and regulations. 

Mr. Mandel said that mentoring of new staff can make the difference between success and failure.  Dr. Matusiak thanked Dr. Napolitano for his report.  

Superintendent’s Entry Plan 

Dr. Matusiak outlined the following goals in her proposed entry plan:



Become familiar with the school district and community for the purpose of collection of information to be used in future planning.  
Ensure facilitation of long-range planning.
Develop systems, protocols and initiatives to address needs.
Ongoing mentoring to new and existing administrators.
Promote continued high expectations for student achievement and involvement.

Dr. Matusiak will expand on this plan in more detail after additional information gathering and  welcomes suggestions.  

3.    AP Scores  (Supplemental Log – Appendix B)

Dr. Napolitano reported on the Advanced Placement examinations given in 2003-2004.  76 students participated in the 2000 AP testing.  In 2004, 170 students participated in 376 examinations, reflecting an increase of 124%.  This year the High School is adding AP courses in Human Geography, Computer Science and Language & Composition.  Dr. Napolitano reported that the number of courses offered, number of students participating, number of exams being taken and test scores are all increasing. 

Dr. Matusiak thanked Dr. Napolitano for his report.
