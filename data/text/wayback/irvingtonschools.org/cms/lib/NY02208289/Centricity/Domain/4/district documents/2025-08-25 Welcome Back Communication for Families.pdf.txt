                                Welcome Back!

Dear Irvington School Community,

Welcome to the 2025-26 School Year!

As an educator, I have always felt privileged to celebrate the start of a new year twice.
The celebration marking the beginning of a calendar year is accompanied by hats and
noisemakers, while the start of a new school year brings fresh bulletin board paper,
brightly colored post it notes, stiff backpacks that will need breaking in, and perhaps a
special first day of school outfit. Resolutions are often common as well. For example,
this year I will raise my hand more often in class and make new friends. It is indeed a
special occasion worthy of celebration for educators, parents, and children. As part of
the new year celebration, we have been preparing all summer for our students’ arrival
including updating facilities, hiring additional faculty and staff, and updating
curriculum.

Facilities
The upgrades to the facilities that took place over the summer included the painting of
multiple spaces, the addition of new partitions in the IMS bathrooms, stripping and
waxing floors, as well as sanding and recoating gymnasium floors. Also, instructional
spaces have been given a deep clean. Lastly, a Genius Bar has been added to the
Campus Library, where students can work with campus staff to troubleshoot
Chromebooks.

I want to thank Rich Pittore, Director of Facilities, and the head custodians of each
building Doug O’Dell, Angel Aviles, and Neil Cole, our maintenance team of Mat Dorfman
and Joe Mack, as well as the entire custodial staff for their hard work in preparing our
facilities for the start of the new year.

Cell Phone Policy
On July 1, 2025, after much discussion and feedback from a variety of constituents, the
Board of Education adopted Policy 5695 to comply with Governor Hochul’s Distraction
Free Schools initiative and New York State law. This mandate eliminates the use of cell
phones, smart phones and watches, and tablets that can connect to the internet, for all
students from “bell to bell” including free periods and lunch. Internet enabled devices
provided by the school are not included in the ban.

Each individual school will communicate with students and families in terms of building-
based practices. In general, elementary school students are discouraged from bringing
such devices to school while students at IMS and IHS will be required to silence their
cell phones, smartphones and watches, and store them in their locker.

We recognize this will be a change for many students. Therefore, we intend to educate
them on the benefits of distraction free environments at school and support them
through the transition.

Universal Meals
We’re pleased to announce that beginning this 2025-26 school year, Irvington UFSD will
participate in the New York State Child Nutrition Universal Free Meals Program. Through
this initiative, all students will receive one full breakfast which is available only at the
Campus, and one full lunch meal daily in all schools at no cost, regardless of household
income. For more information, please see this document to determine what is
considered a “full meal”. If the student does not take all of the items listed as a “full
meal” their selections will be charged as ala carte items. Families that were previously
eligible for free or reduced lunch are encouraged to continue to complete the Education
Benefit form. For more information click here.

Program Updates
As you are aware, Mr. Scott Wynne, our new Assistant Superintendent of Curriculum,
Instruction, and Assessment started July 1st. Under his direction, this summer, a large
portion of our teachers participated in summer curriculum work addressing a wide array
of curricular and pedagogical topics. Among the highlights, beginning at the start of the
school year we will be launching the Arts & Letters Literacy Curriculum, across grades
K-5. This is a vertically aligned, authentic, knowledge rich program, aligned with the
Science of Reading, that blends literature, non-fiction and fine arts. We look forward to
sharing updates on this new curriculum throughout the year.

In terms of PPS, the District has long provided a broad continuum of services to students
which include Special Class, Integrated Co-teaching, Resource Room, and Related
Services. This year, we added Resource Room at Main Street School, a service that was
not previously offered in that building; and are transitioning from ICT classes to a
Consultant Teacher model in select courses for Juniors and Seniors. This model will
better accommodate students at that age level by allowing more flexibility to ensure
all students receive the necessary support while engaging with rigorous academic
content alongside peers in a less restrictive environment.

Throughout the fall, the IHS Scheduling Committee, including students, parents, and
teachers will be visiting multiple schools to learn more about various scheduling models
and their impact on teaching, learning, and the student experience. We will be
communicating frequently regarding what we are learning and gather feedback from
various constituency groups. The timeline includes presenting recommendations in
December and utilizing the winter and spring to prepare for the transition in the
​2026-27 school year.
