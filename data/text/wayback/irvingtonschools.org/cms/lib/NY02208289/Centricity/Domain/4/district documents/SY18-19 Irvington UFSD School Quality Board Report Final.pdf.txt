Results and Analysis

Irvington Union Free School District
School Year: 2018-2019

    Overview
    Positive school climate is an essential component of successful and effective schools. It is defined as shared beliefs, values, and attitudes that
    shape interactions between students, teachers, and administrators, while setting the parameters of acceptable behavior and norms for the
    school.

    The Irvington Union Free School District (UFSD) School Quality and Climate Surveys asked parents, community members, staff members, and
    students in grades 6-12 for feedback on school climate, district leaders, and district initiatives. Results will be used to inform decisions at the
    campus and district levels.

    K12 Insight partnered with district team members to develop the surveys, which addressed the following topics:

               Learning experiences
               Support for students
               School leadership
               Family involvement
               Safety and behavior
               Communication
               Board of education
               District Leadership Team
               Strategic objectives

    Details of the Study and Understanding the Results
    The surveys for parents, community members, and staff members were open from April 12 to May 9. The survey for students was open from
    April 22 to May 9.

    Email invitations with unique survey links were sent to parents, community members, staff members, and students in grades 6-12. Parents and
    community members could also participate via a public link on the district’s website. Parents with children attending more than one district
    school were able to take the survey again. Reminders were sent on April 18, 23, 26, and 30 and May 2 and 7.

    The survey was translated into Spanish. Paper surveys were available on request.

    Pre-survey communications included emails, a phone script, a district website description, and social media posts.

    This report summarizes survey results and and breaks them down by participant group. Results do not reflect random sampling; therefore,
    they should not be generalized to all Irvington UFSD parents, community members, staff members, and students in grades 6-12. Rather, results
    reflect only the perceptions and opinions of survey participants. School-level reports are also included in this project.

    Findings for each item in the report exclude participants who did not answer. In charts and graphs, data labels less than 5 percent are not
    shown. Percentages may not total 100 due to rounding.

     The school net promoter score (NPS) serves as a proxy for public confidence in district schools and can potentially be connected to district
     growth. It is calculated by subtracting the percentage of detractors from the percentage of promoters, which gives a value between -100 to
     +100. A positive score means there are more people promoting district schools than detracting from them.

     Key Insights
        •   86% of parents and 77% of students who took the survey rated their school as excellent or good, which is a slight decline from last
            school year for both participant groups. However, 92% of participating staff members rated their school as excellent or good, which
            is a 7 percentage-point increase from last school year. The school and district net promoter scores for parents and staff members
            followed a similar trend, with scores declining for parents but increasing for staff members.

        •   81% of participating staff members agreed or strongly agreed that students are shown how lessons relate to life outside of school
            compared with 33% of participating students. This is a 5 percentage-point increase for staff members and a 3 percentage-point
            decrease for students from last school year. It may be beneficial to explore why this perception gap is expanding.

        •   92% of participating staff members agreed or strongly agreed that students and staff members treat each other with respect
            compared with 77% of participating parents and 73% of participating students. Agreement on this item has declined by 3
            percentage points for parents but has increased by 2 percentage points for staff members and 8 percentage points for students
            from last school year.

        •   The family involvement dimension had the highest percentage of agree and strongly agree responses from participating parents
            (81%) and participating students (77%). Engaged parents have a positive impact on student achievement.

        •   Though 92% of participating staff members agreed or strongly agreed that the school has high learning standards for all students,
            24% of participating parents disagreed or strongly disagreed that students are appropriate challenged by their schoolwork.
