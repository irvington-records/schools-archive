  A Message from the Superintendent of Schools -
                Reopening Update

July 30, 2020


Dear Irvington School Community,

I would like to begin by thanking the 500 community members who attended last
night’s virtual Board of Education meeting focused on the reopening of school.
For those of you who were not able to attend the meeting, you can review the
presentation materials by clicking here and watch the video by clicking here.

As you can imagine, developing the District’s reopening plan has been a very
challenging process, as we seek to balance the health and safety needs of our
students and staff by following all applicable guidance, while maintaining our
commitment to providing the very best educational experience possible. I wish
we were not facing this terrible pandemic, and I also wish there were
opportunities to have all of our students physically in school daily.
Unfortunately, based upon our current inability to meet social distancing
requirements and still provide for the safe operations of each school, this is
simply not possible.

By design, we expect that our plans will evolve with changes in health and safety
guidance as well as from our own experiences. Throughout the school year, we
will closely monitor the success of our learning plans and will seek feedback from
the school community. This information will help to inform any modifications
that may be considered necessary.

In developing our reopening plan, that was presented to the Board of Education
last night, we maintained our focus on four guiding principles that are clearly
addressed throughout the IUFSD reopening plan:

      Ensure the health and safety of students and staff
      Promote equity and accessibility to learning for all students
      Provide instruction that meets the needs of all students
      Support the social and emotional needs of all students

The reality of the situation that we find ourselves in dictates that health and
safety must be the top priority. As a result, barring any changes in guidance from
the New York State Department of Health (DOH) and the New York State
Education Department (SED) and directives from Governor Cuomo (Andy), the
Irvington schools will begin the 2020-21 school year in a hybrid-learning model
that will reduce density in the classrooms to meet social distancing
requirements.

Hybrid-Learning Model Implementation:
In the Irvington UFSD hybrid-learning model, students will attend school for in-
person learning two consecutive days each week, either Monday and Tuesday or
Thursday and Friday, and will participate in distance learning the other three
days. Wednesday will consist of distance learning for all students.
Students will be assigned to a particular cohort based upon the following:
Cohort A: Last names beginning with A-L
Cohort B: Last names beginning with M-Z

It is expected that students will follow the same curriculum and pacing, regardless
of whether they are engaged in in-person learning or distance learning. To
support such an approach, live or synchronous learning experiences will be
provided for students engaged in distance learning. While specific details of
student schedules and learning experiences will be further outlined in future
communications, I assure the school community that the distance and hybrid
approaches to learning will far exceed those that were experienced in the spring.
Additionally, I want to advise the community that in order to meet DOH
guidelines, some class and schedule changes may be required. If your child is to
be affected by any such changes, school staff members will personally contact
you. Below, please find a graphic representation of the hybrid-learning model.




September School Calendar Modifications:
As a school community, we are committing to facilitating a safe and well-
organized opening to the school year. This year, there is great need to provide
training to students and staff on essential health and safety requirements,
changes to common school routines and potential modifications to school
facilities. Therefore, to ensure everyone is comfortable and prepared for the
adjustments that will be made, the District will adjust the opening days to the
school year for staff and students to have the necessary time to adapt to these
shifts. The school year will begin with four days of 100% distance learning that
will be followed by one week of modified in-person attendance, which will be
followed in week three by the full implementation of hybrid learning. Below is an
illustration of the modified school calendar.
I fully understand that this transition to school, and through such an unfamiliar
schedule, will be challenging. However, we believe that it is essential to
approach the first days of school and the start of the school year in such a
manner as to ensure that the school facilities are as safe as possible.

Transportation To/From School:
The District will continue to provide transportation for all eligible students.
However, based on survey data, we anticipate that the vast majority of students
will either walk to school or be transported via car. As a result, each school will
develop and communicate specific procedures for arrival and dismissal times to
control traffic flow and reduce congregating to maintain social distancing. In the
near future, the District will survey the parent community to collect more data on
the use of school buses. This information will be utilized to plan our bus routes
as efficiently and safely as possible. Please be reminded that just as it will be in
school, students will be required to wear a face covering while riding the school
buses and at bus stops. The District will make all reasonable efforts to provide as
much distancing as possible on the buses.

Travel Restrictions:
In response to increased rates of COVID-19 transmission in certain areas within
the United States, and to protect New York’s successful containment of COVID-19,
the State has joined with New Jersey and Connecticut in jointly issuing a travel
advisory for anyone returning from travel to states and countries that have a
significant degree of community-wide spread of COVID-19. Families, students and
staff members are asked to avoid travel to these areas and to avoid contact with
those that have. If one has traveled to, or has been in contact with others who
have traveled to states identified in the travel advisory, they are required by New
York State to quarantine for 14 days. During that time, they will not be permitted
to be on school grounds. It is very difficult for me to say this, but please avoid
traveling outside of our region when possible. For an up-to-date listing of states
currently falling under the travel advisory, please visit the New York State Travel
Advisory website as well as the CDC’s guidelines for international travel.

Future Communications:
In the coming weeks, each principal and I will continue to provide on-going
updates and more specific details related to the opening of school. It is essential
that you follow our communications closely because directives from the Governor
and shifts in DOH guidance can alter the District’s plans. All communications will
be posted to the Reopening Irvington UFSD website for your reference.

These are difficult times for all of us in our school community, but together we
must continue to do our part to prevent the spread of COVID-19. I urge you to
require your child(ren) to always wear face masks when leaving your home and
ensure that your child does not participate in large social gatherings and adheres
to social distancing requirements. By doing so, we will prevent further spread of
illness and will help to accelerate the pace by which we will have the opportunity
to return to a traditional school schedule.

I greatly appreciate your continued support and partnership.

Sincerely,

Kris

Dr. Kris Harrison
