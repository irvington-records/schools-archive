           Irvington High School Environment
The Irvington team works to create a safe and orderly environment that supports
student success, both in and out of the classroom. In this environment we strive to
help you build strong habits in the areas of respect, responsibility, empathy, and
kindness in school, at school related events, and in the community. We will support
you as you:

    ❖​ Work to the best of your ability by being on time to all classes, paying
       attention, and completing all work by clearly established deadlines.
    ❖​ Treat others respectfully at all times, through both your actions and the way
       in which you speak to and about others in all forms including social media.
       Use of profanity, language that is vulgar or offensive to others, or words that
       are derogatory or make fun of others are unacceptable at IUFSD.
    ❖​ Are truthful at all times, both as a student and in your relationships, and
       encourage your friends to be truthful as well.
    ❖​ Accept responsibility for your actions at all times.
    ❖​ Respect school property, i.e. lockers, textbooks, Chromebooks, uniforms,
       etc.
    ❖​ Ensure that fellow students feel safe and comfortable at IUFSD, and actively
       work to help those who feel uncomfortable or threatened in any way
       (physically, emotionally, etc.) by confiding in an adult who can intervene
       appropriately.
    ❖​ Inform an adult in school if an incident begins outside of school and you
       believe it may continue in school.
    ❖​ Use technology responsibly and for academic pursuits; you may only record,
       video/audio, or take pictures of students or staff if given specific permission
       to do so.
    ❖​ Understand that being in possession of or sharing images or videos of
       people, even if if you did not create them or obtained them through social
       media, without their permission is also a violation
    ❖​ Understand and follow all the rules set forth in this handbook, as well as
       those established by your teachers. If given a directive, follow it in the
       moment and please follow up with an adult after if you have concerns or
       questions.

                  Student Rights & Responsibilities

You Have the Right to:
●​ Address the Board of Education on the same terms as any other resident of the
   district;
●​ Take part in all district activities on an equal basis regardless of race, gender,
   sexual orientation or expression, or national origin; and
●​ Present your version of the facts and the circumstances in all disciplinary
   concerns and to be suspended from instruction only after your rights, pursuant to
   Education Law, have been observed.
●​ In cases where there is ongoing threat of danger and/or disruption, suspension
   may be immediate. Rest assured that students’ rights pursuant to Education Law
   will be observed.


You Are Responsible to:
   ●​ Accept responsibility for your actions;
   ●​ Work to the best of your ability and attend class regularly and on time;
   ●​ Conduct yourself in an appropriate manner at school and at all
      school-sponsored events;
   ●​ Participate appropriately in all Fire Drills/Emergency Drills:
               Classroom teachers provide supervision and take attendance during
               emergency drills. Stay with your class and follow your teachers’
               directions. The use of cell phones during emergency drills is
               prohibited.
   ●​ Contribute to an environment conducive to learning;
   ●​ Show respect to other people and to property;
   ●​ Report fairly and truthfully the circumstances of school-related issues;
   ●​ Be familiar with and abide by all rules of conduct;
   ●​ Encourage others to do the right thing; and
   ●​ Take care of school property and equipment.

Student Issued Resources:
Our residents/taxpayers pay for your school property. Please take care of it. Students will have
the opportunity to hand in school-issued books, technology and other resources at the end of the
school year. Students must keep their receipt as confirmation that they have returned a book. We
will not issue additional school resources, transcripts and/or diplomas until all previously issued
resources have been returned and all fines or obligations have been satisfied.

                             The School Day
Busing
The school day begins at the bus stop. Students who live 1½ miles or further from the
high school are eligible for school bus transportation. Because safety is the first
priority on the bus, students can enter or exit the bus only when it has come to a
complete stop. All passengers must be in their seats when the bus is moving. The
driver has authority on the bus and will report any unacceptable behavior, which can
result in a suspension of bus privileges and/or additional consequences in line with
the Code of Conduct.

Senior Parking
Parking is a privilege for Seniors only. To be eligible for parking privileges seniors
are to complete a Parking Permit Registration form, and obtain a parking permit,
which must be displayed. Seniors may only park in the Designated area in the
Immaculate Conception Church/John Cardinal O’Connor School campus. Seniors
disregarding the parking policies will have the following consequences

    ●​   The signed registration is the first warning/notice
    ●​   The 1st violation will result in 1 detention and a hold on the IC account
    ●​   The 2nd violation will result in 2 detentions and a hold on the IC account
    ●​   The 3rd violation will result in the suspension of parking privileges, the car
         will be booted, the IC account will be put on hold and a guardian will be
         contacted to take possession of the vehicle

UNDERCLASSMAN ARE NOT PERMITTED TO PARK ON CAMPUS. IF
AN UNDERCLASSMAN IS FOUND TO BE PARKING ANYWHERE ON
CAMPUS DURING THE SCHOOL DAY THEY WILL HAVE THEIR IC
ACCOUNT LOCKED AND WILL JEOPARDIZE THEIR SENIOR PARKING
PERMIT PRIVILEGES.

Access for Students with Handicaps
Irvington High School is accessible to all people.




                                          12
Lockers
Every student is assigned a locker. We encourage students to use their assigned
locker to store their personal items. As per our cellphone policy, all cell phones must
be stored in lockers during the school day.

Lockers (Gym)​
Gym lockers will be assigned at the beginning of the school year by the Physical
Education Department. Students will be provided with a school issued combination
lock. It is very important for students to store their belongings in a locker secured
with a lock during PE classes and after- school practices and games.

Inclement Weather
We encourage you to register for automated email or phone messages regarding
changes to the school day schedule in the event of school cancellation or delay due to
snow or for another emergency. Please contact Leann Serao in the high school office
at leann.serao@irvingtonschools.org to register for Constant Contact.

Lost and Found
If you’ve lost something or found something you should check the lost and found
racks located in the hallway on the second floor, in the cafeteria or in the library. It is
suggested that any electronic devices, such as cell phones, air pods, jewelry etc.,
should be brought to the main office.

Working Papers
Students between the ages of 14 and 18 need working papers in order to obtain an
after-school job. Applications are available in the Counseling Office or on the School
Counseling website. A Parent or Guardian must sign Part I of the form for consent. If
the nurse has a copy on file of a physical exam that has been given within 12 months
prior to the issuance of the employment certificate, the student can have the nurse
sign the Physical Fitness Certification page. If the nurse does not have a current
copy on file, the form must be completed by the student's physician.

          IHS Cell Phone Rules: Student Guide
The Basics​
You can’t use your cell phone or any internet-connected device during the school
day. This rule is based on state law and Board Policy 5695. It includes cell phones,
tablets, laptops, smartwatches, headphones, gaming devices, and anything else that
can go online. Devices the school gives you for learning are allowed for schoolwork.

Where Your Phone Goes​
When you arrive at school, turn off your cell phone and put it in your locker.** You
can’t keep it in your backpack or pocket. It must stay there until dismissal. Other
electronics like headphones or internet-enabled calculators should also be stored in
your locker** or backpack and kept silent unless your teacher says you can use them.

**Every IHS student has been assigned a locker. The locker number and
combination will appear on the bottom of your schedule.

When You Can Use Your Phone​
You may only use your phone or other internet-connected devices:

    ●​   On a school trip if your principal or a staff member says it’s okay
    ●​   If you leave campus for lunch or an appointment
    ●​   After school for sports, clubs, or activities
    ●​   If it’s in your IEP or 504 Plan
    ●​   For translation help that’s been approved
    ●​   For medical needs (with documentation)
    ●​   In rare cases approved by the MTSS team

How Parents Can Contact You​
If your family needs to reach you:

    ●​ Urgent: They should call the Main Office, and you’ll be called down.
    ●​ Not urgent: They can call or email the Main Office and leave a message.

What Happens If You Break the Rules

   ●​ Using your phone when you shouldn’t: Your teacher will tell an
      administrator, your phone will be taken until the end of the day, and your

   ●​ Lying about storing your phone: You’ll get a disciplinary referral, your
      phone will be held until the end of the day, and the situation will be

   ●​ Repeated issues: Your parents will meet with an administrator, and you

   ●​ Refusing to hand over your phone: The situation will be treated as

      include suspension.

                                 Attendance
School attendance is both a right and a responsibility. Irvington High School is an
active partner with students and parents in the task of ensuring that all students meet
or exceed the expectations of the New York State Learning Standards. As regular
school attendance directly correlates to academic success and contributes
significantly towards attaining a high school diploma, the high school has
developed, and will revise when necessary, the following comprehensive Student
Attendance Policy.

    9th & 10th graders are not allowed to leave campus during the school day.

 11th & 12th graders are allowed to leave campus only during their free periods.


Absences
Parents/guardians must contact the Attendance Office, 914-269-5483 or email Ms.
Adinaro at venus.adinaro@irvingtonschools.org to inform the school of their child’s
absence with details of the absence before or the morning of the child’s absence.
Calls or emails from the student themselves are not allowed.

You will not receive a confirmation call back or email when you leave a message;
however, all parents are encouraged to check their own child’s attendance on IC
Portal throughout the day. Parents will automatically receive a daily email
attendance report at the end of each school day if and when there is a coded
unexcused absence. Parents must have a registered IC account in order to receive
attendance emails.




                                          16
                      Medical/Personal Appointments
Medical and personal appointments should be made before or after school hours.
However, when a student must miss school due to a medical/personal appointment, a
verification note/receipt must accompany the student when they return to school.
When arriving late due to an appointment, the student needs to sign in at the main
office with their note/receipt. When a student needs to leave school early for an
appointment, the parent/guardian needs to call the attendance office and the student
needs to sign out at the main office and bring in their note/receipt the following
school day.

Lateness
If a student will be arriving to school late, the parent will need to call the Attendance
office and inform the attendance office of the time their child is expected to arrive
with the reason for their lateness; when the student arrives at school he/she must sign
in at the attendance office. Students MUST sign in for verification and upon their
arrival their attendance will be updated.

Road Tests
Students are allowed up to 4 consecutive periods to take their ROAD TEST - not
their learner’s permit. Attendance will be coded as Road Test. Classes missed over
and beyond the given 4 periods will be marked as Unexcused.


College Visits:
Up to 3 days per year are excused for college visitations for Juniors and Seniors only–
and only upon prior knowledge and approval by the parent/guardian to the attendance
office. If a sibling will be going along on the trip, their absence will be marked as
Vacation/Personal.




                                           17
Students That Become Sick While at School
Students who feel sick while at school need to inform their teacher and go to the
nurse’s office. The nurse will make her assessment and if necessary call the parents
to come pick up the student. Students who leave school because they do not feel well
without checking out through the nurse will be considered unexcused for any missed
classes and will be considered cutting their classes. A letter, phone call or email
from a parent after the fact will not excuse the absence.


                                  If you are injured:
If you are hurt in any way, even if it seems like a minor injury, report to the nurse to
file an accident report.


Extended Illness:
During the first week of an extended illness, a parent or guardian must contact the
attendance office. Parents must provide a doctor’s note, detailing the nature of the
illness as well as the potential length of absence.


After School Activities:
Students that participate in after-school activities such as sports, plays, concerts,
dances, exhibitions, field trips, etc. need to be in school the day of the activity. If a
student will be late for any reason, they need to be signed in at the attendance desk by
the end of 2nd period (10:05) and attend the remainder of the day’s classes to get credit
for attending school that day and to be allowed to participate in their after school
event. Otherwise, they will not be permitted to participate in their after school
activity. Likewise, if a student goes home early for whatever reason, they will not be
permitted to participate in an after school activity.


Field Trips:
Signed parental permission forms are required for all field trips. There are no
refunds for missed trips or change of personal plans. Students are responsible to
communicate with all of their teachers in advance of the trip and must make up for
all missed work.

              The IHS Counseling Department
The School Counseling Department in Irvington UFSD is committed to providing
every high school student with a comprehensive program that delivers academic,
social-emotional and college and career development. Student centered, goal
focused, individualized and personalized services describe the commitment of the
school counselors.


Mission
The mission of the Irvington School Counseling Program is to develop and deliver a
comprehensive school counseling program that cultivates in students the knowledge
and skills necessary to experience success in academic pursuits, social –emotional
development, and career and post-secondary aspirations. We are committed to
building strong relationships with students, families, teachers, and the Irvington
Community as we foster student self-awareness and self-reliance through
collaboration, support, advocacy, and education.


Vision for our Students’ Success
It is the vision of the Irvington School Counseling Department that students from
Irvington will become independent, socially conscious, civically minded members of
society. With the help of school, family, and community students will strive for their
best selves having developed the skills needed to maximize their academic success
and social/emotional development.


Counselor Assignments
The IHS Counselors follow Irvington's Comprehensive School Counseling program
to assist all students. Each 9th-grade student will work with a dedicated counselor
who specializes in the high school transition. For students in grades 10-12, a team of
three upperclass counselors are assigned based on a proportional distribution of each
class to ensure consistent support throughout the students' final three years. This
model allows for specialized attention for incoming students while maintaining
continuity for upperclassmen.

Direct Services…
provided by the School Counselors include in-person interactions between School
Counselors and students. These activities include and are not limited to: short-term
individual counseling, group counseling, classroom curriculum, crisis intervention
and management, prevention activities, student-family conferences and meetings,
career development activities, educational planning, goal setting, and post secondary
exploration.


Indirect Services…

referrals for additional assistance, and working with other educators and community
organizations. These services also include participation on school committees and
student support teams, collaborating with college admissions counselors, organizing
evening workshops for parents and family members, and community outreach.

A great resource to get information regarding academic success, post-secondary
planning, social & emotional support, as well as our Comprehensive School
Counseling Program and Plan is the IHS School Counseling Website, which can be

Grades
Course grades reflect a student’s class participation, completion of daily assignments,
unit tests, quiz marks, class work, projects, and final or Regents exam grades. Local
Midterm, Final and Regents Exams count for a percentage of the final course grade.
Grades are updated regularly and may be reviewed through the IC Portal. All courses
conclude with a final assessment.

There is a grade floor of 50% for every quarter EXCEPT the last quarter in the
course, for which there is no grade floor.


High Academic Achievement
To earn Honor Roll distinction, students must achieve a GPA from 3.25-3.63. To earn
High Honor Roll distinction, students must achieve a GPA at or above 3.64. The
student must be enrolled in at least three courses and must not have a grade below 70
for any course.

                                Academic Integrity:
Honesty is the ability to tell the truth, even under pressure, and integrity is the dedication to do
what is right. It is expected that all members of the Irvington High School community will act in
an honest and ethical manner, displaying academic integrity at all times.

Plagiarism:
Plagiarism is using someone else’s words or ideas and presenting them as your own. This is a
form of academic fraud. Failing to give credit to others by indicating the source of the ideas or
work in a proper citation is an example of being dishonest and cheating. This also may violate
the legal rights of others. Plagiarism is more than simply copying compositions or papers, it also
includes using, referencing, or copying parts or all of another person’s work into your own
assignment without crediting the source.


Plagiarism includes:
    ●​ copying and pasting from the Internet or any other source–including from AI–without
         citing the source and including quotation marks around these borrowed words. This can
         include, among other things, the following: text, music, drawings/pictures, video clips,
         maps, ideas, slide shows, tables/charts/figures, and interviews. Possible sources include
         books, magazines, newspapers, movies, web pages, letters, advertisements, TV
         programs, blogs, other people, or any other medium or source;
    ●​ copying and editing someone else’s words by reordering the words in the sentence or by
         using synonyms to express the other person’s ideas/words. To paraphrase a source, we
         must present the ideas entirely in our own words, not simply change a few words;
    ●​ copying or taking from another person’s homework or assignment, either in part or in its
         entirety.
    ●​ copying someone else’s ideas without crediting the source (this includes copying from
         Spark Notes, AI, or other study guides)
    ●​ submitting papers written by others (this includes siblings, friends, etc., with or without
         their knowledge)
    ●​ submitting the same paper for more than one class
    ●​ downloading entire or parts of research or term papers from the Internet

All work or ideas taken from other sources, even if you paraphrase the words, must be properly
cited in accordance with your teacher’s requirements.
If you are not sure how to do this, it is your responsibility to ask your teacher to explain how
he/she wants work cited. The school librarian can also assist you with this.


                                          35
Other Examples of Academic Dishonesty include:
   ●​ copying from other people during exams
   ●​ obtaining examination materials prior to test distribution
   ●​ using or possessing cheat sheets or other written resources
   ●​ using technology or seeking to obtain, store, transmit or copy information or material to
        assist you or another person during a quiz, test, exam, or any other assessment
   ●​ allowing others to copy your work, give others answers to use as their own, or obtain
        answers from a tutor, sibling, family member, etc. on a graded essay or assignment.
   ●​ While authentic collaboration is encouraged, sharing work with peers as a shortcut is
        prohibited

All instances of academic dishonesty will be reported to school administration.
Consequences for academic fraud may include:
     ●​ A grade of “0” on the assignment or exam
     ●​ Completion of reflection assignments
     ●​ In-school suspension
     ●​ Community service
     ●​ Restriction from participation in extracurricular activities and organizations
     ●​ Dismissal from an Honor Society

Repeat offenders of academic dishonesty will face more serious consequences.

The Board of Education of the Irvington Union Free School District (7310.vi) believes that
learners should dress with the intention to promote and engage in a positive learning environment.
The primary responsibility for this resides with the student and parents or guardians. Student attire
must ensure the health and safety of all students, and it should support a student’s ability to access
their education without substantially disrupting school activities. This policy is intended to

and other activities where unique hazards exist.

controlled substances.

pornography, nudity or sexual acts. Clothing with slogans that create hostile work environments
are similarly prohibited.
• Clothing may not use or depict hate speech, or images or language that creates a
hostile or intimidating environment by targeting groups based on race, ethnicity,
gender, sexual orientation, gender identity, religious affiliation, weight, or any other
protected groups.
• Clothing, including gang identifiers, must not threaten the health or safety of any
other students or staff.

                DISCIPLINE RELATED TO DRESS CODE VIOLATIONS
Students who do not adhere to these requirements will not be allowed to attend class.
Administrative personnel have the authority to require a student to change their attire if it violates
this dress code. Parents will be called if appropriate clothing is not available or the student refuses
dress-code appropriate clothing.

         Prohibited Student Conduct and Disciplinary Consequences

When self-discipline fails or self-control falters, a disciplinary response, if imposed,
is designed to protect the rights of others and modify inappropriate behaviors. The
nature of such a response is determined following the evaluation of several factors
including: the severity of the offense, the age of the involved student(s) and each
student’s prior disciplinary history. Listed below are prohibited student behaviors
along with possible disciplinary consequences. The detailed infractions and their
consequences do not compose a complete list of possible violations but merely serve
as examples to highlight the three different categories of poor student conduct:
Actions That Are Disorderly, Actions That Are Disruptive and Actions That
Endanger.
                             Actions That Are Disorderly:

Incidents at this level are those that create brief distractions or disturbances to the
climate of a classroom or the school building. Students who witness events that fall
into this category should report them to a teacher or staff member for an
investigation. The frequency and severity of these behaviors will impact the
disciplinary response and could potentially raise them to the level of a Disruptive
Action.

           Enforcement of Rules & Regulations

The principal is responsible for deciding if a rule has been broken. Students who
break rules may be suspended for up to five days and referred to the Superintendent
of Schools, which can result in a long-term suspension from school. Hearings before
the Superintendent are closed to the general public and all records of them are kept
confidential. At Superintendent’s Hearings, persons charged with violating the rules
described on the preceding pages may be represented by an attorney, may be
accompanied by a parent or others, have the right to present witnesses and other
evidence, and may cross examine witnesses who testify against them.
Administrators, faculty, staff, or visitors who break any of the aforementioned rules
or violate the IUFSD Code of Conduct in any way may be disciplined under the
appropriate Education Law or Civil Service Law or in appropriate criminal or civil
proceedings.
