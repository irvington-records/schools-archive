Irvington Union Free School District
                                 FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS:
                                     THE 2023-2024 SCHOOL BUDGET

WHAT ARE BASIC BUDGET FACTS?
● 2023-24 proposed budget amount: $73,079,120
● The proposed budget is compliant with the calculated tax levy cap.
● The tax levy increase is 3.41%, consistent with prior years.
● The budget-to-budget increase is 6.72% versus 3.18% last year which is primarily due to a large increase in state aid
  and, aside from the tax levy increase, does not affect taxpayers.
● The estimated tax rate is $20.30 per thousand of assessed value, a decrease of 1.87%. This is based on the March
  2023 assessment roll provided by the Town of Greenburgh Assessor and may change by August when the assessment
  roll is finalized for tax billing.
● Voter approval: for the budget to be authorized it must receive 50+% majority of the votes on Election Day

WHAT FACTORS INFLUENCE BUDGET INCREASES?
● District legal or contractual obligations such as employee salaries, pensions, health insurance, and BOCES fees
● Unfunded or underfunded state mandates such as transportation, new curriculum standard implementation,
  guidance counselor requirements, ELL mandates and special education services
● Increase in State Aid (see below)

HOW DOES THE INCREASE IN STATE AID IMPACT THE 2023-24 BUDGET?
● For the first time since it was created, Foundation Aid will be fully funded by the State. Foundation Aid is the state’s
  main funding source for school districts, basing its allocation on a formula that includes factors such as
  socioeconomic need and student counts in various categories. Additionally, other State Aids, such as Transportation
  Aid and Building Aid, increased for the District. Overall, the District’s total State Aid increased 45.1% year over year.
  This allows the budget to increase, while keeping the tax levy increase in line with past years.

WHAT STEPS HAS THE DISTRICT MADE TO CONTAIN COSTS?
● In creating the 2023-24 budget, the District analyzes every line of the budget to ensure we are maximizing our
  budget dollars. In doing so, efficiencies have been realized through supplies and maintenance. Detailed, historical
  budget information is available on the District website.
● Due to the expectation of receiving an increase of State Aid in 2023-24, the District is able to include one time
  purchases including:
                   o Technology purchases
                   o Facility projects
                   o Athletics equipment

WHAT IMPACT WILL THE 2023-24 BUDGET HAVE ON INSTRUCTIONAL PROGRAMS AND FACILITY MAINTENANCE?
● There are no instructional programs or staffing cut in the budget. The proposed budget adds important positions for
  student and staff support including an additional Special Education teacher, School Psychologist, Supervisor for
  Special Projects and Instructional Innovation Support Specialist. It also supports funding to provide for additional
  student clubs at all school levels. The proposed budget also supports on-going buildings and grounds maintenance
  such as painting, carpet replacement, preventive maintenance, and necessary repairs. Additionally, two new buses
  will be added to improve early student arrival times at the high school on select routes.

HAS THE IRVINGTON SCHOOL DISTRICT TAX BASE GONE UP? HOW DOES THE ASSESSED VALUE AFFECT THE TAX RATE?
● Based on the most recent valuation from the Assessor’s office, the total assessed valuation increased $196,775,235
   to $3,108,138,667 or a 7% increase. Although the tax levy increased by 3.41 % (fully compliant with the tax cap with
   capital exclusions), the higher assessed valuation resulted in a decrease in the tax rate of 1.87%.
WHAT IS THE ADDITIONAL PROPOSITION ON THE BALLOT?
● The proposition would allow the District to increase the Capital Reserve. The current Capital Reserve was approved
  by voters in May 2015 in the amount of $1,500,000 plus earnings, with a probable term of 15 years or until funds are
  exhausted. The current reserve balance as of June 30, 2022 is $1,117,300. Increasing the Capital Reserve would
  lessen the need for future bonds, thus saving the District/school community interest expenses. Additionally, funds
  accrue interest while in the reserve which are currently greater than 4%. Voters must approve expenditures from a
  capital reserve as they would for a bond project. There are no funds budgeted to go into the reserve fund annually;
  however, surplus at year end could go toward this reserve or one of the other existing reserves based on need.

DOES MY VOTE MATTER?
● Yes. The budget for the public schools impacts all residents of the Irvington school community. Casting your vote
   makes your voice heard on the budget, which ultimately relates to the type of programming the District is able to
   provide our students. Property values are also often affected with school budget vote outcomes.

WHAT HAPPENS IF THE BUDGET IS NOT PASSED?
● In the event that the budget does not receive the required majority of yes votes on May 16th, State law provides the
  Board of Education with three options: resubmit the budget to voters without any modifications, present a revised
  budget for one re-vote or adopt a contingency budget. If the budget is not approved on the second attempt, the
  District MUST operate on a contingency budget. When operating on a contingency budget, the total tax levy must be
  no greater than the current level and must eliminate certain categories of expenditures, such as equipment. This
  would require a budget reduction of approximately $2,080,400. To accomplish this would require severe reductions
  to programs and services provided district-wide and would limit community use of District facilities.

HOW DO I REGISTER TO VOTE, AND CAN I GET AN ABSENTEE BALLOT?
● Residents of the Irvington School District must be registered with either the school district or the Westchester
  County Board of Elections. If you are not registered, you may register online with the Department of Motor Vehicles
  https://dmv.ny.gov/more-info/electronic-voter-registration-application as soon as possible in order to receive a ballot
  by mail. Additionally, there is a voter registration event held on May 4 at the Main Street School from 3:30-7:30pm.
  Absentee ballots may be obtained from the District Clerk. Please view https://www.irvingtonschools.org/Page/4497
  for more information.
