     Co n t in g e n t Bu d g e t
• If there was to be a majority “no” vote on May 20th and a subsequent “no”
  vote (if the Board chose to have a second vote in June), a Contingent
  Budget would result.

• A Contingent Budget requires the District to levy no more than the
  previous year’s levy. This would require cuts of approximately $2.6 million
  and result in a budget increase of only $918,838 from the 2024-25 budget.

• A Contingent Budget would result in no new equipment, no community
  use of buildings and no capital improvements funded through the
  General Fund (unless emergency). Other necessary cuts would affect
  staffing, curriculum/professional development, technology purchases and
  student programs.

• The District does not have sufficient undesignated fund balance to offset
  these cuts. Even a partial offset could result in a position of “significant
  fiscal stress” by the Office of State Comptroller.

     Capital Reserve Spending Proposition
What is a Capital Reserve?

 • The Capital Reserve was established to fund larger capital expenditures
   that could not be supported in the annual general fund budget and are
   typically smaller than projects that are included in a capital bond project.
   This reserve is one of our several reserve funds, each having a specific
   purpose.

 • The District established our capital reserve in 2015 to fund up to $1,500,000
   and increased it to $10,000,000 in 2023. The establishment of the reserve
   fund was voter approved.

 • The reserve is funded through annual budget surpluses that are not
   allocated to other reserves, appropriations for following year budget,
   encumbrances or our undesignated fund balance.

 • These funds can only be used for capital improvements.
 • As of June 2024, the Capital Reserve amount is $4,034,571 which includes
   funding and interest revenue.

What Did The District Envision Using This Reserve For?
The District initially planned on using this reserve for the turf and track
replacement knowing they have an approximate 10 year life span. Funds
were also added to tackle other projects that are part of our long range
facilities plan. The use of a reserve saves the District money as it eliminates
the need to borrow and pay interest. Instead, the District is accruing interest
on the funds in the reserve account.


How Can The Capital Reserve Fund Be Accessed?
To spend from the capital reserve, voters must approve the spending via a
proposition on a ballot. The vote can occur at any time of the year. We
propose adding this proposition to the may 20th Budget/Board Trustee
election vote versus holding a separate vote for this purpose to not incur
additional vote costs.

Will Spending From This Reserve Increase Taxes?
This spending will strictly come from the funds set aside in the Capital Reserve
and will not impact the tax levy or tax rate. Unlike a Capital Bond Project/Proposition,
there is no borrowing associated with using the funds in the Reserve.


When Will The Work Be Completed?
After a successful vote, the District will work with our Architects to create the
specifications needed to submit to the State Education Department so we can
receive building permits and eventually state aid on projects that are aidable. This will
take several months. Our best estimate is to plan on work commencing the Summer of
2026. It is possible that once we have permits and can bid out the work, some of this
work might be able to get done over a break period.


We will look to minimize construction cost which also means grouping similar work
together (such as all paving and concrete repair) to get a favorable bid.
