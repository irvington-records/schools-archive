Jay Mathews, the author of the book, "Harvard Scmarvard,'' a book that offers an interesting view of the competitive college acceptance process in our country, spoke to Irvington parents last week in a presentation cosponsored by Irvington High School's guidance department and the PTSA.
Many parents, whose children are juniors and seniors, heard Mathews tell them that "while Americans are obsessed with name brand colleges, it is more important to pick a college "that fits the child's desires and needs."
"Research shows us that a child's success if life, depends on large part by traits of character, persistence, charm, inner strength, .. all of which they have acquired, long before they take the SAT's," said Mathews, who is now a "Washington Post" writer, and self-described "college obsessed" parent. "Many of these traits come from good parenting,'' he added.
In his presentation, Mathews tried to deflate the "Ivy" league college myth, that he said has created parents who he termed, "Ivyholics" who think that success will follow any student who attends top colleges. Mathews compiled research that showed many CEO's of the top American companies, as well as government leaders and accomplished celebrities, attended state universities and smaller, private colleges, not Ivy League colleges.
"Remember, you don't need a college that's famous, just that fits," added Mathews, who tried to ease parents' stress over the process by reminding them that "America has the greatest choice of colleges anywhere in the world."
