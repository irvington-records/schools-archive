Virtual Technology Tour
Mrinalini Rajwar, Director of Technology
**Based on the April 2000 "School Matters" column from the Irvington Viewpoint.
Throughout the past two school years, the Irvington School District has undergone a major expansion of its technology program, based on the premise that technology will increasingly affect every aspect of life and work. If the future colleagues of Dows Lane 3rd graders happen to be in
Sydney, Australia, our students will already know that "Oz" has nothing to do with wizards! That’s because of an exciting project that integrates their computer curriculum with the study of Great Barrier Reef in Australia. Students use the "G’Day Mate" WebQuest, an online lesson developed by Irvington teachers Betty Carle and Chris Cullen, to understand more about Australia’s culture and animal life, and the geography and ecosystem of the Great Barrier Reef. Projects like this offer great learning experiences for kids because they are complex, inter-disciplinary, multi-sensory and fun.
This is just one of the ways that district teachers use technology to provide learning opportunities for students. In the
Middle School, 8th grade students surf the Internet to research extreme environments and then build models of homes to shelter them in one of those environments. Our 6th grade students work through a Middle Ages Webquest to learn more about what it was like to be a serf, knight or other member of medieval society.
Using a new computer lab at the High School, students create brochures for the Board of Education and learn how to express themselves creatively through "Life in a Can" projects in graphic design class. High School students have also been working on programming in Visual Basic applications in Denise Hamlin’s math class. Congratulations also goes out to our students (Jeff Hung, Nick Mahon, Noah Spitzer-Williams and Jeff Weinberger),and their advisor (Anthony Pizzolla) who won 4th place at the Mercy College Kellogg Computer Competition this year.
The desire to provide such learning opportunities led the BOE to adopt the District’s Long-range Instructional Technology Plan in June 1998. In that plan, the BOE outlined 6 broad goals to fully integrate technology into our daily curriculum. The District has made great progress in meeting these goals. Most significantly, we’ve made substantial investments in staff development; Irvington faculty devoted over 170 days of technology training so far this year, a large increase over previous years. The new position of Director of Technology insures coordination and oversight of the program while technical assistants greatly reduce "downtime". Significant progress has also been made in acquiring and upgrading hardware, software, networks and Internet connections so that our students and staff can take full advantage of new educational resources that are becoming available. BOCES leases for this equipment allow the District to recover roughly half our hardware costs in state aid. Financial assistance from the Irvington Education Foundation and PTSA has helped in acquiring a more comprehensive software library.
The District has also used its technological resources to increase communications between parents, the community, the schools and the district. Dows Lane has an award winning web site and the Middle School has a new web site, which contains information about the school and activities of its students. The District’s web site contains useful information about our school community and links to school-related organizations. The District’s e-mail newsletter reaches over 750 community residents on a weekly basis. And a pilot test of Thinkwave was conducted at the high school this year to allow students and parents access to online information on student assignments and grades.
Our technology investments have paid off in reliable, up-to-date resources and vibrant programmatic offerings for our students. Not only is Irvington doing well by its own standards but it also scores high on national assessments. An analysis of our technology program using benchmarks developed by the CEO Forum on Education and Technology, a partnership between top business and education leaders, finds Irvington scoring in the High-Tech category, which puts us in the top 24% of schools surveyed nationally.
While we are proud of our accomplishments, the District continues to look to the future. The 2000-2001 school budget will continue our investment in educational technology: we will be installing a computer and network connection in each High School classroom allowing students to use these resources throughout the day. We will also be adding mini-labs of 12-15 computers at the High School and Dows Lane and replacing obsolete equipment in the Middle School. Finally, because using technology effectively in the classroom is a skill that takes time to develop, we will continue to provide our teaching staff with sustained and appropriate professional development. We have made great progress over the last two years but the District will continue its focus on technology to enrich our students’ educational experience and to insure that our students have the skills that they will need to succeed in the 21st century
