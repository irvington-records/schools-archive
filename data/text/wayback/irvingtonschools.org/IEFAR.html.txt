Annual Report ~ June 1998
The Irvington Education foundation, a not-for-profit organization, was founded in 1996, to enhance the quality of our public school education. Since its inception, thanks to the enthusiastic response by our community, we have raised more than 60 thousand dollars. This has enabled us to fund a range of innovative and exciting programs.
The Dramatic Arts Flourish
The first wave of generous donations allowed us to fund the high school musical play (absent for four years to due to budget constraints) with the presentation of "Dames at Sea" in 1997 and "The Pajama Game" in 1998. We also funded the original musical, "Rip Van Winkle", at Dows Lane.
The Visual Arts Get a Boost
At IMS, students constructed museums which reflected the art of the time periods studied in "Art Through The Ages". At Dows Lane this spring, we funded 7 Artists-In-Residents through the Westchester Arts Council. The projects included: 1st grade paper making; 2nd grade construction of birds in their sanctuary: 3rd grade print making and soap stone carving: 4th grade photo heritage composites: 5th grade quilts connoting American inventions. In addition we funded "Art and the Elements" (a weather education program for 1st grade) and "Painting of Famous Masters (2nd grade).
Innovative Architectural Course Instituted
At IHS, we helped to establish a new architectural drafting and design course that gives students the opportunity to build models evaluated by professional architects.
Science and Social studies Brought to Life
At IMS, 8th graders experienced "The Jason Project", an interactive course in oceanography using a worldwide computer hook-up. For 7th graders, "Urban Orienteering" gave them exposure to the history of New York City.
Dows Lane 5th graders had a Civil War re-enactment incorporated into their studies and 4th graders had a Native American storyteller and colonial crafts workshop; 2nd graders studied "Birds in their Habitat"; kindergarteners learned about our fragile ocean environment through "The Spectacular Seas" Puppet Show.
Acquisitions
The IEF co-funded the purchase of new bleachers for the high school athletic field to provide seating for the home team and to improve spectator viewing at athletic events.
Dows Lane received a new printer for the publishing center that has assisted the production of children's books.
The academic life of every Irvington public school student was enhanced through these awards which were not affordable within the schools' operating budget.
The Community Rises to The Challenge
The IEF's first annual fundraising event was an extraordinary community effort by a core of dedicated volunteers, businesses, individuals, administrators, and high school students. The magic of the "Legend of Sleepy Hollow" and the "Headless Horseman" will never be forgotten. Most important, through the generosity of contributors, it raised a significant amount of money to fund this year's curriculum enhancing projects. This effort won honorable mention in USA Weekend's "Make a Different State". We wish to thank all of the donors and volunteers whose effort got effort to such a successful start. We hope those who join us in the coming year will help to expand upon these beginnings. We must build upon our momentum and deepen our commitment to innovative, quality education, as together, we reach for excellence.
