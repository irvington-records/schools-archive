I. Reconfigure Grades
- Relieves space pressure to a degree on Dows Lane
- Greater flexibility in teacher scheduling due to less Middle School/High School staff sharing.
- Early adjustment to High School for 8th graders
- Potential for 5 year sequences
- Additional electives possible in High School
- 5-7 would allow for a gradual transition from self-contained classroom to semi-departmentalization in 6th to departmentalized in 7th.
- Curriculum coordination between 5th and 6th grade would be strengthened
- 8th grade students with ability could more easily accelerate in science, music, language and art or other subjects
B. Move 5th Grade to Middle School and 8th Grade to High School (K-4, 5-7, 8-12)
- Increases space pressure in High School
- Science lab space, currently almost at full capacity in the High School, would not be sufficient
- Redefines Middle School concept
- 8th grade might lose advantage of team approach
- Does not accommodate projected enrollment increases

C. Move kindergarten out of Dows Lane to High School
- The space is not available at the High School
- Appropriate bathrooms and cubbies would be required in each classroom
- It would not be an appropriate environment for kindergartners
- Support services available at Dows Lane would not be readily available at the High School
- Kindergartners might feel isolated
- Lack of gym/playground facilities
- Does not accommodate projected enrollment increases in the High School or Middle School
II. Temporary Measures
- Would provide classroom space quickly at a variety of locations in the District
- Would provide some flexibility in temporarily meeting future needs
A. Buy more modulars
- Would be an expensive short term solution to a permanent problem
- Would present a "patchwork" image of education in Irvington
- Specialized facility needs, such as science labs, can not be met by modulars
- Sites for additional modulars are limited at the Middle School and Dows Lane
- Does not directly address long range problems of the District
- Would partially relieve the space problem at Dows Lane
- Would not require adding space
B. Larger class size at Dows Lane
- Does not accommodate projected enrollment increases in the High School or Middle School
- Would lower the quality of our program and student services
- Would make introduction of new, small group teaching methods and use of technology less effective
- Less space would be available for learning centers and computers in each classroom
- Could potentially increase the costs of remediation in future years
- Children will not receive the necessary attention and direction which they require
- Would partially alleviate the space problem at Dows Lane
C. Eliminate full-day kindergarten at Dows Lane
- Does not accommodate projected enrollment increases in the High School or Middle School
- Would lower the quality of our Kindergarten program
- Most area districts offer full-day kindergarten programs
- Full day kindergarten is a recent and successful innovation in our District
- Need to prepare for universal pre-kindergarten, which may be required by the state in the near future
- Would temporarily alleviate the space problem at Dows Lane
- Would temporarily alleviate the space problem at the Middle School
D. Program modification: put art/music on wheels at Dows Lane and cut programs at Middle School (such as shop/home economics/museum/music lab)
- Would lower the quality of the art/music programs
- Does not accommodate projected enrollment increases in the High School or Middle School
- Would not add science lab space in the Middle School and High School
- Would not respond to the challenge of running Dows Lane as the largest elementary school in the area
- Would not enhance our program in any of the schools
- Would not provide a long term solution to our problems nor provide the District with any flexibility if our actual student population tops our high projections (i.e., over 1,000 students in Dows Lane and over 500 students in the Middle School in only 5 years)
- Would indicate to children that the arts are secondary to/not as important as other academic programs
III. Reconfigure Administrative Offices
- Would create additional room in the High School
A. Relocate Superintendent’s Office, Business Office and CSE Office out of High School to modulars or another location
- Would not address Dows Lane or Middle School space challenges
- Space may not be available nearby or at a reasonable cost
IV. Scheduling
- Would create extra space at the Middle School and High School at no extra cost
- Would provide an immediate response (although less desirable from an educational standpoint)
A. Split sessions at the Middle School and the High School
- Would not address Dows Lane space constraints
- At High School, would conflict with sports program
- Would not address field space needs
- Difficulties in scheduling
- Sharing of rooms would be necessary
- Would impact after-school activities of students
- Difficult to administer
- Students’ sense of belonging to a school is diminished
- May create difficulties for working parents
- Would create extra space at no extra cost
- Would provide an immediate answer
- Would allow for more flexible family scheduling of vacations
B. Run District year-round
- Not allowed by current state regulations
- State aid is not available for students attending school in the summer
- Buildings are not air-conditioned
- Difficulty in fairly assigning students and staff to the summer session
- Would destroy community atmosphere of our School District
- Significant negative impact to ongoing maintenance programs
V. Expansion/Renovation
- Could create a science/technology wing, or
- An entire annex housing 4th and 5th grade
A. Build a second level over the Middle School gym
- Would have to be many stories due to small building "footprint"
- Building up is often more difficult than building a new structure
- Disruption of athletic program during construction phase
- Gym may have to be completely demolished and rebuilt from scratch
- Does not help alleviate High School space challenge
- Parking and playing field needs at the Middle School not addressed
- Would add space to High School
B. Add a floor to the High School
- Building up is often more difficult than building a new structure
- Potential disruption of educational program during building phase
- Does not address Middle School space challenge
- Does not address the fact that Dows Lane would be the largest elementary school in the area
- Would add space to Middle School
C. Add a floor to the Middle School
- Building up is often more difficult than building a new structure
- Does not address High School space challenge
- Does not address the fact that Dows Lane would be the largest elementary school in the area
- Handicap access would be difficult without large expenditures
- Would disrupt Middle School during construction
- Parking and playing field needs at the Middle School not addressed
Cons
- Would add space to Dows Lane
D. Add a third floor to Dows Lane
- Does not address High School or Middle School space challenge
- Does not address the fact that Dows Lane would be the largest elementary school in the area
- Would add to safety/crowding issues on Dows Lane campus
- Would disrupt Dows Lane during construction
- Parking and playing field needs at Dows Lane not addressed
VI. Build a School
- Responds to all District space challenges
- Alleviates pressure at Dows Lane making it a more educationally sound environment and a more manageable size for administrators
- Would enable the return of the Dows Lane library to its original main floor location
- Excess space could be leased, providing the District with additional revenue
- Would possibly alleviate need for Assistant Principal at Dows Lane
- Is cost effective: sharing resources between the High School and Middle School
- Would require upgrading of High School Drive (including traffic light)
- Maintains District’s educational excellence
- Would give the District flexibility for years to come
- The new building could be either a Middle School or a High School depending on the final analysis of district needs
A. Build a new 6th to 8th Grade Middle School adjoining the High School: share High School gym/auditorium and build new library-multi-media center connecting building. Convert High School Library into additional classrooms. Downsize Dows Lane to be K-3; convert Main Street School into 4th to 5th grade building
- Would take 2 years to complete
- Creates additional safety/access concerns on High School hill for bus and car traffic, especially if Westwood receives access through High School Drive and if we can not get a traffic light at Broadway
- Would lessen field space available to the District
- Would require an additional Principal or the shift of the Assistant Principal from Dows Lane
