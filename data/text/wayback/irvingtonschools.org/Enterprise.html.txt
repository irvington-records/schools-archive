**Reprinted here with the kind permission of the Rivertowns Enterprise, first published in the July 2, 1999 issue as a letter to the editor.
To the Editor of the Rivertowns Enterprise:
	Thank you for your editorial supporting the Irvington School District’s Long Range Planning effort.  This matter is of immense importance to our community and will be one of the major focal points of the Board of Education during the next school year.
	By way of clarification, the Board has been investigating our space challenge for over 1 year.  In January 1998, the Board established a Long Range Planning Committee (LRPC) to analyze the District’s space needs and options.  The LRPC presented its findings at a January 1999 meeting attended by over 250 community members.  Subsequent meetings allowed the Board to receive community input and further analyze options.  At a May 4 public work session, the Board, with input from its administration and architectural consultant, eliminated options which did not meet the District’s long term needs and directed its consultants to investigate adding to our building space.  This work will proceed over the summer, as will the work of the expanded LRPC, which includes administration, staff and community residents (parents and non-parents alike).
	Irvington’s space challenge is clear and pressing.  Our new demographer (Focus Consulting) replaced another firm whose projections for 1998 were about 5% too low.  Focus, a local company with impeccable credentials and years of experience, worked to understand the distinct conditions facing our District: a continuing construction boom and significant housing turnover (empty nesters leave as families with children arrive).  Our demographer predicts that by 2002, total enrollment will exceed our all time high.  By 2006, total enrollment will likely be between 2,049 and 2,319 (a 28% to 45% increase vs.1998-99).
Our schools are already at or near capacity and the kids are already here: when the lower grades reach the Middle and High School, we will be hard pressed to find room.  The time to respond to this challenge is now.  This Board will carefully study our options and continue to solicit community input in order to build a broad-based consensus.  We will then decide how to best maintain our quality educational program.  In the meantime, we invite our entire community to be involved and stay informed!
 
Paul Mandel
President, Board of Education
