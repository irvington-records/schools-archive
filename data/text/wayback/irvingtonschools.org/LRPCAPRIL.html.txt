Long Range Planning - Report to the Community
April, 1999
As we have informed the community in prior mailings, the Board-appointed Long Range Planning Committee developed six options for the district to consider as we face a large and continuing increase in student enrollment - which may be as much as 45% greater than our current student population of 1,597 by the 2006/07 school year.
Option I. Reconfigure Grades
- A. Move 8th Grade to the High School
- B. As above and move 5th Grade to the Middle School
- C. Relocate Kindergarten
Option II. Temporary Measures
- A. Acquire more modulars
- B. Increase class sizes at Dows Lane
- C. Eliminate full-day Kindergarten
- D. Modify or eliminate programs
Option III. Reconfigure the Administrative Offices
- Move Superintendent’s Office, Business Office and/or CSE Office
Option IV. Effect Scheduling Changes
- Split sessions or year round school calendar
Option V. Expansion/Renovation
- A. Build a second level over the Middle School gym
- B. Add a floor to one or more of the existing school buildings
Option VI. Build a School
- A. Build a new Middle School on the High School campus; downsize Dows Lane to K-3; convert the Main St. building to a 4-5 school.
- B. Build a Dows Lane annex for grades 4 and 5 where the basketball courts are currently located.
- C. Build a new gym on the Middle School field and convert the current gym into a 4th and 5th grade annex.
- D. Buy land to build a new 6 to 8 Middle School; downsize Dows Lane to K-3 and convert the Main St. building to a 4-5 school.
The next step in the planning process is to present an analysis of the pros and cons of each of these options to the community. Our objective, in this meeting, will be to eliminate options which will not meet the long term needs of the district. Subsequently, will will narrow our focus to that option or combination of options which best meet the challenges posed by our space and facilities constraints. We must rise to these challenges in a way which preserves and enhances the academic excellence of the district as we also meet more rigorous educational standards and technological requirements. We must, as well, identify the solution which maintains a focus on the needs of the district as a whole, looks ahead ten years and allows maximum flexibility for the future. Finally, we must carefully consider the costs and benefits of each option.
The Board has, in conjunction with our Superintendent, Dr. Vin Beni, already acted on the immediate recommendations of the Long Range Planning committee. These included developing a short term plan for the coming two to three academic years as part of the budget development process, and engaging an architect to provide more specific information on renovation and building.
All residents are encouraged to attend the next Board of Education meeting at which these Long Range Planning options will be discussed.
The decisions we make now are critical to the future of our schools.
