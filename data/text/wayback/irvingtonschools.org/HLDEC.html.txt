IRVINGTON BOARD OF EDUCATION
HEADLINES DECEMBER, 1997
	PERSON IN THE NEWS:  DR. ANDREW KERFUT.  This month, the Board recognized the contributions of our Middle School principal, Dr. Kerfut, as we regretfully accepted his resignation.  Dr. Kerfut has truly embodied the Middle School philosophy and program in our district, as he has served as the first and only principal of IMS.  We will be looking to the community to offer suggestions as to criteria for a new Middle School principal.
	WESTWOOD UPDATE:  It is essential for community members to become educated about the stake the district has in the developer’s proposal to construct new homes behind the High School campus, with legal  access over High School Drive. The district’s traffic and safety consultants will be presenting their report to the Board and the community within the next few weeks.  The issues affecting the High School campus will be reviewed at the next Board meeting (Dec.16), in preparation for a full public discussion at the January 6 and 20 meetings. The Board encourages everyone to attend these meetings, as the issues to be decided will have a long term effect on the safety of our school grounds.
	TECHNOLOGY CONSULTANT ON BOARD:  The Technology Committee is pleased to welcome Peter Reilly, Director of Education Technology at BOCES.  Mr. Reilly will be working with administrators and faculty to develop a broad based plan for full integration of technology into the curriculum at all grade levels, with a further emphasis on training faculty to accomplish this goal.  It is the shared view of the Board, the Technology Committee and Mr. Reilly that specific purchases must be in the context of such a plan.
	REGENTS REALITY:   The New York State Board of Regents has officially adopted a far reaching revision of Regents graduation requirements.  Students will be required to complete more credit hours and demonstrate competency in more Regents exams than is now the case in order to graduate.  While the goal of the revised standards is the enhancement of education in the State, the implications for local districts will be profound.  Irvington is already planning curriculum revisions, scheduling adjustments, and program additions that will position our district to successfully adapt to the new standards. A case in point is a proposed multi sensory approach to reading for certain first graders.  This innovative program will be presented at the next Board meeting, on Dec. 16
