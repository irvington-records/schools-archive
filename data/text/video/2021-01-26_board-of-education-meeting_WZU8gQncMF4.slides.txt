Why Vinnie Bagwell
• Vinnie Bagwell is a leading artist, activist and educator who
has won prestigious commissions all over the country bringing
to light this important part of our collective history.
• Vinnie's figurative art expresses humanity and thus invites a
visceral connection between the viewer and this important
subject.
• Vinnie's reputation may attract sizable art grants.
• Vinnie's art will help make Irvington a destination for local
slavery acknowledgment.
• Members of this committee have visited Vinnie in her Yonkers
studio on numerous occasions.

Educational Plaque
• The Commemorating Enslaved Africans Committee
(CEAC) has created the first draft for the educational
plaque-slide 11.
• A new generation of scholarship has evolved and
changed the way we understand American
history-particularly as it relates to all aspects of diversity
and inclusion. The CEAC has expertise in writing
enlightened and multicultural text that is at the same
time accurate and sensitive to all readers of our history.
• The IUFSD and the Irvington Board of Education will
have final approval of the language on the educational
plaque.

Why Main Street for Artwork and Plaque
• Main Street is where we proudly display our historical monuments, such
as the war memorials and the Rip Van Winkle statue.
• Last year, the Revolutionary War Marker was installed in Veterans'
Memorial Plaza to commemorate the local patriots.
• The long-ignored history of enslaved Africans right in Irvington is of
equal historical importance and as such, merits a prominent location on
Main Street.
• The prominent placement of this monument to these enslaved Africans
is critical in order to serve as a contextual and educational counterpoint
to the existing display of Irvington founders' Eurocentric history.
• The IUFSD and the Irvington Board of Education will be taking a
leadership role in making Irvington a welcoming community for ALL
residents and visitors. Sponsoring this public commemoration links
with the District's anti-racist policy, educational and cultural
advancements in the schools.
• Sponsoring this public art will ensure that students will learn about ALL
of Irvington's history for generations to come.
