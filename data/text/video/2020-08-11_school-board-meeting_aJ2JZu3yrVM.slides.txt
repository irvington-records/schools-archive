Distance Learning Design Review
While participating in Distance Learning:
• All students will participate in regular, scheduled synchronous learning following
an assigned schedule
• Instruction will follow the pacing of on-site learning with similar assignments
targeting the same learning objectives
• Small group and whole group synchronous learning will be scheduled
• Students at home may be remotely interacting with the teachers and classmates
during the lesson

IRVINGTON
What Does Distance Learning Look Like?
• All students will have a structured school day
• All students will be required to attend all classes on their schedule (elementary and
secondary) - attendance will be taken and participation will be monitored
• Students will interact with the teacher and classmates as well as view live streamed
instruction, watch recorded videos, complete independent work, etc.
• Small group sessions will be scheduled at all levels based on student need and
instructional objectives
• Some elementary specials will be delivered remotely; PE will be outdoors when possible
• Flex day, Wednesday, will provide opportunities for direct instruction, small group
sessions, individual support and independent work

For Students:

Distance Learning Guidelines
ON FREE SCHOO
For Parents and Guardians:
• Provide a quiet space, free from distractions.
• Encourage your child to be as independent as possible.
• Let your child work through challenges and frustration. Remember that
they do this on their own every day that school is in session!
• Allow the teachers to work with all students. If your child needs support
beyond what is offered, have them sign up for time during the teacher's
scheduled extra help.
• Respect the privacy of all students, including your child, and refrain from
participating in the lesson or discussing anything you may overhear during
a live class.
• If you need to speak with the teacher, reach out to them using email.
No recording of any kind is permitted by students or parents

IRVINGTON
Challenges with 100% Distance Learning as an Option
UNION
• Distance learning does not offer the same experience as in-person learning
Distance learning is documented to have negative effects on social and emotional
development due to no personal interaction
• Significant limitations on learning that is interactive, hands-on and engages students
physically
a. arts, science labs, PLTW, PE, etc
• Very limited flexibility with scheduling at the secondary level
• Introduces further inequities in the learning experience
• Questions exist regarding permissibility and impacts regarding NYS requirements and
State aid
• Limits teachers' ability to meet the needs of all students
a. due to the need to provide in-person learning there will be limited availability to
tend to increased number/disproportionate number of distance learners
b. simply not enough time in the day/contractual limits in the day to provide the
necessary attention to student learning and to plan appropriately

NEW: IUFSD Will Offer a 100% Distance Learning Option
At the onset of the planning process, the District indicated that our planning would be fluid and
evolve. This opportunity represents one such example. In order to implement a 100% distance
learning option, it is essential to remind the community of the following:
• Must understand that it will not equate to the same level/quality of experience if the entire
District was facilitating 100% distance learning
• Students must fulfill all school attendance requirements and participate in all learning
experiences - as required by New York State
• Students will be held accountable for all curricular learning standards and will be assessed in
the same manner as students attending in-person schooling
• Must understand the limitations that are inherent to the experience in contrast to the richer,
in-person learning
• Would not be able to switch back and forth between distance learning and the hybrid model

NEW: Exploration of K-1 Four Half-Days/Week In-Person Learning
Through its planning process, the District did not advance a model that would have
students in school for in-person learning due to:
Transportation limitations: cost, social distancing, routing needs
• In-school social distancing requirements
• Staffing and supervision requirements necessitated a significant increase in staffing
Since the initial planning process, having heard significant feedback from parents and
further exploration, the District is now investigating the possibility to have Kindergarten and
Ist grade attend school for four (4) days per week. This would include:
•
Four (4) half days: Monday, Tuesday, Thursday, Friday
Wednesday will be distance learning
•
Maintain two cohorts through separate AM & PM sessions
